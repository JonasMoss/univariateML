# Existence atlas

A fit regime fixes the held coefficients and the fitting domain, including endpoint conventions and restrictions. Fix the sample and take the limsup of its **joint** log likelihood along feasible parameter vectors approaching a point in a compactification. This gives the sample likelihood envelope. Taking a separate limsup at each observation may combine incompatible paths.

On a compact metrizable space with a dense feasible set, the upper semicontinuous envelope attains its maximum. Its maximum equals the original supremum. Upper semicontinuity of the original likelihood on the feasible set makes the two agree there. When only boundary points maximize the envelope, every maximizing sequence has a subsequence approaching one of them. A proof may instead confine the relevant sequences to a compact region. Identification with a named model also requires a compatible path and equality of its joint likelihood limit with the face value. [Likelihood closure lemma](../ml-behaviour.tex#lem:closure)

The supremum can be attained, approached at a named limit, infinite along a collapse path, or finite and unattained without a named model. Attainment leaves a separate cardinality question. On a singleton feasible domain the supplied parameter vector is the unique maximizer because there are no other feasible points.

Each limit keeps its path grade (`dossier_grade`) and winning-condition
grade (`detect_firmness`). The nested path and detector proofs carry the
corresponding dossier references and any narrower evidence from the atlas.
A scoped atlas claim retains its own scope and status. The base family's
all-free row also carries the modifier configurations in `modifier_limits`,
with their own grades and references. The base-regime proof totals use the
unmodified likelihoods.

The table is generated by `python3 dev/tools/existence_atlas.py --overview`. A one-sided theorem settles only the cases in its hypotheses. For individual paths and detectors see the structured `limits` column. The overview groups by the coverage of the audited proof, so an open cell can contain a proved special case. [Atlas](atlas.csv) · [Lean statements](lean-map.csv) · [Proof references](proofs.csv)

Each Lean map row names a dossier and its exact `held` mask, or uses the
`generic` regime for declarations without a single atlas mask. The reserved
`generic` family holds reusable lemmas and declarations without a dossier.
The generator reads the main map and every CSV under `lean-map.d/` with the
same schema. The optional `coverage` column is a JSON object keyed by
`criterion`, `failure`, `uniqueness`, or an existing `case:…`, `path:…`,
or `limit:…` key. Leave it empty for inventory-only rows. Use `full` for
theorems that together establish the audited claim as stated, and `partial`
for a restricted branch or weaker conclusion. The atlas records full theorem
names in `evidence` and partial theorem names in `lean_partial` fields. Rows
with a proof marker supply no verified Lean coverage.

For a family theorem listed under `generic`, prefix each reviewed coverage
key with its exact hold mask and a slash, as in `max/path:1`. This supports
theorems that quantify over held values while keeping each specialization
explicit. Legacy labels in fragments are resolved by
[the alias table](lean-regime-aliases.csv).

`proofs.csv` supplies claim wording, direction, and written or cited
references. A blank status and evidence preserve the wording of a claim whose
proof is supplied by the Lean map. Run
`python3 dev/tools/existence_atlas.py --check` to check map scopes and
theorem references in the saved atlas. It compares the saved and regenerated
statuses with the [pre-derivation baseline](proof-status-baseline.csv).
An intentional downgrade requires the exact claim, replacement status and
reason in [the exceptions list](proof-status-exceptions.csv).
