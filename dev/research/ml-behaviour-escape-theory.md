**Main correction:** unrestricted GED and SGED have unbounded likelihood for every nonconstant sample. Their huge-shape uniform fits are local solutions, not global MLE escapes. A second correction: SGED's infinite-shape limit is ordinary uniform, not a density with a jump at the mode. For Student-t, kurtosis gives a necessary boundary condition and a sufficient *strict local* condition, not a global escape rule.

## Coordinates, probability and scope

Assume a finite, nonconstant, distinct sample, n >= 2. Use mode c and left/right base scales a,b > 0: `f(x)=2 g((x-c)/a)/(a+b)` to the left, and the same expression with b to the right, where g is a symmetric unit-scale density. Here `xi=sqrt(b/a)`; its endpoints are `a/b -> 0` (right half-law) and `b/a -> 0` (left half-law). If the code's standardized base has absolute first moment M and skew standardizing moments m_u,s_u, then `c=mu-sigma*m_u/s_u`, `a=sigma/(s_u*xi)`, `b=sigma*xi/s_u`. Free mean and sd therefore allow arbitrary c,a,b. For t calculations below g is ordinary `t_nu`, with its extra scale absorbed into a,b; for GED use `g_p(z)=exp(-|z|^p)/(2 Gamma(1+1/p))`, p=nu.

“Positive probability” means under an iid continuous sampling law assigning positive probability to the relevant open sample configurations; it does not mean probability one or every n. Local boundary attraction and global nonattainment are different. Strict global comparisons survive small perturbations. Gaussian-boundary, heavy-tail lower-boundary, and one-sided configurations occur numerically below; a numerical search is evidence, not a proof of an exhaustive existence theorem.

| Family/direction | Nondegenerate limit with location/scale allowed to adjust | Global escape probability / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| std, nu -> infinity | normal | Positive; not all samples. |
| std, nu -> 2+ | ordinary location-scale t with df 2, infinite variance | Positive; jointly sigma -> infinity. At fixed code mu,sigma the weak limit is a point mass instead. |
| snorm, xi -> infinity / 0 | translated/scaled right / left half-normal | Positive in either orientation; endpoint is estimated, not fixed at zero. |
| sstd, nu -> infinity | Fernandez–Steel two-piece normal (snorm), including its half-normal faces | Positive; score gate below is only local. |
| sstd, xi -> infinity / 0 | translated/scaled right / left half-t, nu >= 2 in closure | Positive configurations are possible; compare all competing shape and skew faces, not just a fixed-nu half-law. |
| sstd, nu -> 2+ | two-piece ordinary t2; corners are half-t2 | Positive lower-boundary configurations; jointly diverging code sd. |
| ged / sged, nu -> infinity | uniform on an interval | A local maximum at infinity; **zero probability of being the global supremum in the unrestricted models**, because their likelihood is unbounded elsewhere. |
| sged, xi -> infinity / 0, fixed nu > 0 | translated/scaled right / left half-GED | Local boundary candidates; no global MLE escape to these bounded-likelihood fits in unrestricted SGED. |
| ged / sged, nu -> 0+ | no regular density limit on the likelihood-divergence path; a collapsing spike with remote tails | Likelihood unbounded for every nonconstant sample (probability one); this is not a finite-likelihood escape to another density. |

Joint infinite-shape/extreme-skew SGED limits are also uniform. Normal symmetry xi=1 is interior, not an escape. Scale collapse gives the usual degeneracy for constant samples; ties need separate checks: for t at fixed nu, placing k coincident observations at c and shrinking both scales gives `L proportional to scale^{nu(n-k)-k}`. Thus large ties can make even the nu>2 t likelihood unbounded; this has probability zero under continuous sampling but matters for rounded data. The table concerns the listed shape/skew boundaries, not a formal classification of every arbitrary joint parameter sequence.

## std: the normal score and the lower df boundary

At the normal MLE let `z_i=(x_i-xbar)/s`, `s^2=n^{-1} sum (x_i-xbar)^2`, `K=n^{-1} sum z_i^4`. Expanding the **variance-standardized** density at eta=1/nu gives `log f_eta(z)=log phi(z)+eta*(z^4-6z^2+3)/4+O(eta^2)`. The envelope theorem removes first-order nuisance refitting, so `S_infinity=ell_profile'(0+)=n(K-3)/4`. Equivalently the ordinary-t expansion is `(w^4-2w^2-1)/4`; its fitted scale has `sum w^2=n`, producing the same score.

`K>3` rules out the normal boundary as a local or global maximum. `K<3` proves a strict local boundary maximum; `K<=3` is necessary for a global normal-boundary maximum. Equality needs higher orders. **K<=3 is not globally sufficient:** the distinct sample `c(seq(-1e-4,1e-4,length=30),seq(.9999,1.0001,length=10))` has K=2.33333337, normal logLik -23.27801305, and an admissible t2 boundary fit with logLik 54.07217335. By continuity, finite nu just above 2 already beats normal by almost 77.35. Hence the failure is on an open set, not an equality pathology.

For the lower face fit ordinary t2 in location c and scale s. With `w_i=(x_i-c)/s`, define `D_v(w)=0.5[psi((v+1)/2)-psi(v/2)-1/v-log(1+w^2/v)+(v+1)w^2/(v*(v+w^2))]`. Then `S_2=sum D_2(w_i)` is the inward derivative in `nu-2`. `S_2<0` is sufficient for a strict local lower-boundary maximum and `S_2<=0` necessary globally; it is not a global certificate. Code sd is `s*sqrt(nu/(nu-2))`, so a detector inspecting only small `nu-2` while requiring bounded sd misses this limit.

**Recommendation:** `NormalBoundary` -> “Student-t supremum is the normal fit; use mlnorm”; `DfTwoBoundary` -> “supremum is a location-scale t with df 2; finite sd parameterization has no MLE.” No existing mean/sd t fit can represent the latter. Cheap gates are the scores above; use them to nominate faces, never to stop unconditionally. An exact global trigger is: the fitted face value exceeds every finite interior stationary maximum and every other face, and no interior point attains the same value. Interior candidates obey `sum (nu+1)w_i/(nu+w_i^2)=0`, `sum (nu+1)w_i^2/(nu+w_i^2)=n`, and `sum D_nu(w_i)=0`. Exhaustive root/global-profile comparison is required; this report does **not** establish a cheap globally sufficient moment inequality. A finite grid or one BFGS fit cannot certify the trigger.

## ged: uniform boundary is nonregular; shape zero dominates globally

For fixed p,c, put `M_p(c)=n^{-1}sum |x_i-c|^p`. Profiling the exponential-power scale gives `a^p=p M_p(c)` and `ell_p(c)/n=-log 2-lgamma(1+1/p)-[log(p M_p(c))+1]/p`. Optimize c by minimizing M_p: for p>1 the signed power-sum equation has a unique root; for 0<p<=1 a minimum occurs at an observed value (at p=1, also possibly an interval of medians). This is a cheap exact reduction of the nuisance optimization.

Let `R=(xmax-xmin)/2`, c the midrange, `U=-n log(2R)`, eta=1/p. Only the two extrema contribute at leading order, so after profiling c, `ell_p=U+n*eta[log eta-log(2/n)+gamma_E-1]+o(eta)`. Indeed `M_p=(2/n)R^p(1+o(1))`, with exponentially small corrections from strictly interior observations. Thus `ell_profile'(0+)=-infinity`, **for every distinct sample**. There is no finite kurtosis-like score at the uniform boundary. It is always a strict local boundary maximum, but often not even the best finite-likelihood branch. Pointwise endpoint density limits depend on the boundary layer (the factor is exp(-1) if the natural scale a=R is fixed); substituting the uniform MLE into finite-shape densities and taking pointwise limits is wrong. Slight support inflation in the profiled sequence resolves that issue.

Now put c=x_j, with k observations equal to c, `q=(n-k)/n`, and `A=mean_{x_i!=c} log|x_i-c|`. As p -> 0, `log M_p=log q+p A+O(p^2)`. Stirling in the exact profile gives `ell_p/n=-log(q)/p + 0.5 log p - A-log 2-0.5 log(2*pi)+O(p) -> +infinity`. This reproduces the nonexistence theorem of Tumlinson [1]. For distinct data k=1; no rare-sample condition is needed. Unit-variance standardization cannot repair an invertible reparameterization of a free location-scale family. Fixed standardized GED converges weakly to a point mass as p -> 0, not a regular density with the same variance.

**Recommendation:** exact pre-fit trigger for unrestricted GED is simply any nonconstant valid sample: `UnboundedShapeZero`, “GED likelihood is unbounded as shape tends to zero with location at an observation; no global MLE.” Do not say “use mlunif” as a statement about its global supremum. If policy explicitly changes to a constrained shape range, e.g. p>=1, report `UniformBoundary` / “use mlunif” only after comparing U against the entire constrained profile. The infinite negative derivative alone never establishes that comparison. Numerical local fits may still be useful, but must be labeled local fits to an ill-posed global ML problem.

## snorm: an exact one-dimensional boundary rule

For a trial mode c define `A(c)=sum_{x_i<c}(x_i-c)^2`, `B(c)=sum_{x_i>=c}(x_i-c)^2`, and `Q(c)=[A(c)^(1/3)+B(c)^(1/3)]^3`. Differentiating the likelihood gives `a/b=(A/B)^(1/3)`, `a=A^(1/3)*sqrt((A^(1/3)+B^(1/3))/n)` and the analogous b. Consequently `ell_profile(c)=n log sqrt(2/pi)-(n/2)log(Q(c)/n)-n/2`. It suffices to minimize Q over `[xmin,xmax]`: outside that interval its one-sided expression improves toward the nearest endpoint.

**Exact global rule:** compute the minimum of Q at the two endpoints and every interior stationary point (and observation knot); an exclusively endpoint minimizer means escape. At a smooth interior point solve `sum_{x<c}(c-x)/A^(2/3) = sum_{x>c}(x-c)/B^(2/3)`. Put u=sum_{x<c}(c-x), v=sum_{x>c}(x-c). On each observation interval the stationary equation is the polynomial `u^3 B^2-v^3 A^2=0`, of degree at most seven; A,B are quadratic and u,v linear there. Positivity makes cubing equivalent to the original equation. Prefix counts, sums and squared sums provide its coefficients after sorting. Enumerate/isolate all real roots within every observation interval; a single bounded minimizer or grid is not exhaustive. If an interior minimizer ties the endpoint minimum, a finite MLE exists too: do not claim compulsory escape.

At the right half-normal face `c=xmin`, `b^2=mean (x-xmin)^2`; left is the reflection. Set `r=a/b=xi^-2`. Reopening the empty side at fixed c,b changes logLik by `-n log(1+r)`; after local profiling, the inward derivative remains **-n** (the optimizing mode displacement is O(r^2)). Thus every sample has both half-normal faces as strict *local* boundary maxima. An eta=1/xi score is zero because r=eta^2; log-xi gradients also vanish automatically near escape. Neither is a useful global classifier.

**Recommendation:** exclusive xmin winner -> `RightHalfNormalBoundary`; exclusive xmax winner -> `LeftHalfNormalBoundary`. Message: “supremum is a translated/scaled [right/left] half-normal.” Do not refer users to a zero-location Nakagami or half-normal fitter unless it also estimates the endpoint. The Q comparison is the exact trigger; use a certified interval/root search if exact classification is required.

## sstd: fit and compare a boundary graph, not one giant nu

Use the same two-piece coordinates with ordinary t_nu. At each snorm boundary fit, form sidewise `w_i=(x_i-c)/a` or `/b`. The common scale score gives `sum w_i^2=n`; therefore the inward df score at eta=1/nu is again `S_infinity=(sum w_i^4-3n)/4`. This holds also on half-normal faces with c at the appropriate extreme and fitted b or a. Negative score gives a strict local maximum in the df direction; a snorm face already escaping in xi must be represented by its half-normal corner, not a huge finite skewness. At nu=2 use `sum D_2(w_i)` at the fitted two-piece t2 face, including nuisance refitting, exactly as for std. Equal scores need higher orders; competing tied face fits require considering all directional branches.

At xi infinity/zero with fixed nu, the decreasing half-t density puts c at xmin/xmax; fit its single scale and nu, including nu=2 and infinity. With `r=a/b` or its reflection, the inward skew score is again -n: t is smooth at its mode and local nuisance refitting contributes only higher orders. This is a cheap **local** test that accepts every half-t candidate, not an existence criterion. Shape and skew boundary tests must be checked jointly at corners.

**Recommendation:** compare the best finite interior fit, snorm closure, the two half-t closures, two-piece t2 closure, and all corners. Use `SnormBoundary` (“use mlsnorm,” unless snorm itself escapes), `Right/LeftHalfTBoundary` (name the translated half-t model and df), `DfTwoBoundary` (name two-piece t2), or a corner-specific half-normal/half-t2 status. Exact trigger: an exclusive boundary maximizer over this graph and the full interior; local scores are necessary filters only. No cheap global sufficient sstd rule is proved here. If a fitted limit exceeds the returned finite fit, that **does** rigorously invalidate the finite fit as a global MLE, but by itself does not prove which face wins globally. Retain an unresolved-boundary status when global comparison has not been established; never infer escape from nu=343 or an arbitrary parameter cap.

## sged: same skew faces, but the global problem is ill-posed

For fixed finite p its skew limits are half-GED, with endpoint at the relevant sample extreme. Reopening the empty side gives inward score -n in r: for p>1 the optimizing displacement is O(r^(p/(p-1)))=o(r), and for p<=1 the local cusp pins the mode. This is local only. At p -> infinity, the density on `[c-a,c+b]` is the **single constant** `1/(a+b)`; c and xi describe a redundant split of an ordinary uniform interval. Thus the reported xi=.065 is not evidence of a separately identifiable skew-uniform law. The profiled uniform boundary has the same negative-infinite leading shape score (`n eta log eta+O(eta)`), including half-uniform corners.

Setting xi=1 embeds GED exactly, so [1] and the explicit shape-zero path prove SGED likelihood unbounded for every nonconstant sample. **Recommendation:** the same unconditional `UnboundedShapeZero` status as GED; no global “use mlunif” or “use half-GED” status for unrestricted ML. If a lower shape bound is explicitly adopted, compare uniform, both half-GED closures, the lower-shape face and interior; only an exclusive global boundary winner warrants the corresponding limit-model message. A fixed lower shape bound is a statistical model change, not a numerical tolerance.

## Numerical derivation checks (not package tests)

Python used `default_rng(20261028)`. For 30 normal samples of n=30, ordinary-t nuisance optimization used BFGS from mean/sd and median/half-sd starts on `nu={2} union geomspace(2.001,1e4,65)`, plus exact normal. **15/30** had K<3; all 15 selected normal on this grid; the other 15 selected finite df. This small experiment does not make the local score sufficient. First three analytic scores / profile secants at nu=1e5: **-0.220434/-0.220625**, **-4.672108/-4.672130**, **3.107598/3.107499**; the third sample's best grid nu was 8.64909, gain 0.199581 over normal. The explicit two-cluster counterexample above supplies the essential failure case.

For `x=c(seq(-.02,.02,length=18),-10,10)`, t2 inward score **-9.483545** versus profiled secant **-9.483550** at nu=2.00001; df=2 won the grid. For n=60 normal/exponential draws following the 30 draws above, snorm Q search (801 mode points plus refinement of each nominated minimum) selected an interior mode -0.413150 for normal, versus xmin=0.0138765 for exponential. Q winner / endpoint Q values: normal **140.926 / (185.060,231.893)**; exponential **142.303 / (142.303,488.793)**. Right-half inward skew secants at r=1e-4 were **-59.8532** and **-59.9044**, approaching -60 even when that half-face loses globally.

For the next n=40 uniform draw, optimized GED profile gaps per observation below uniform at p=50,200,1000,10000 were **-0.0273338,-0.0136474,-0.00433563,-0.000663747**; asymptotic predictions were **-0.0267815,-0.0136268,-0.00433481,-0.000663739**. On that same sample, c=x[1] and exactly profiled scale gave logLik **-39.5551,-39.0505,6.76945,219.087,872.285** for p=.1,.03,.01,.003,.001: the competing zero-shape divergence is already visible.

A subsequent n=60 normal draw gave sstd's snorm-face analytic score **-11.373814**, profiled secant **-11.374006** at nu=1e5. A separate seed 901 search used `{2} union geomspace(2.01,2000,32) union {infinity}`, two Nelder–Mead interior starts plus exact half-face location and scalar scale optimization at every nu. For n=35 draws, normal selected the interior snorm face (**-52.821399**, t2 face -55.221589); uniform selected the right half-normal corner (**-3.510435**, t2 face -8.584907); exponential selected the two-piece t2 face (**-36.393993**, normal face -43.949183). These are multistart grid evidence, not certified global proofs. An R check against `fGarch::dsstd`, nu=5, xi=1.7, mean=.3, sd=1.2, x=(-1,-.2,0,.7,2), verified the two-piece coordinate conversion to maximum absolute density error **8.33e-17**.

## Literature and Nakagami convention

**[1]** Tumlinson, S. E. (2015). *On the non-existence of maximum likelihood estimates for the extended exponential power distribution and its generalizations*. Statistics & Probability Letters **107**, 111–114. **DOI [10.1016/j.spl.2015.08.009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167715215002886)**, seen on publisher index/abstract. Its theorem states unbounded likelihood for every sample size in the free location-scale positive-shape model, including invertible reparameterizations and asymmetric extensions. This directly resolves the GED/SGED existence question; numerical local MLE literature must be read subject to this obstruction.

Conventional radio-fading usage attributes m>=1/2 to Nakagami; that attribution is not a checked quotation from the 1960 chapter.

For the density `2(m/Omega)^m x^(2m-1) exp(-m*x^2/Omega)/Gamma(m)`, integrability requires only **m>0**, since `X^2 ~ Gamma(m,scale=Omega/m)`. The user-owned R [`nakagami` manual](https://jonasmoss.r-universe.dev/nakagami/doc/manual.html) explicitly allows m>0 and identifies m>=1/2 as a radio-channel convention. The author’s [`VGAM` manual](https://www.stat.auckland.ac.nz/~yee/VGAM/doc/VGAM.pdf), nakagami section, likewise specifies positive shape and log links. [`scipy.stats.nakagami`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.nakagami.html) specifies nu>0 (SciPy scale is sqrt(Omega), not Omega). **Recommendation:** retain m>0 and document the generalized convention; m=.275 is valid under all three software conventions. If m>=1/2 is deliberately required, set `Omega_hat=mean(x^2)` and constrain the unique gamma-shape solution to `max(1/2,m_unconstrained)`; m=1/2 is an attained half-normal boundary, not an escape.
