# Transformed beta (GB2): an escape-aware ML design

Research evaluation, 2026-10-29; no implementation. Start points: the free-scale
Burr/inverse-Burr/beta-prime discussion in `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`, and the
corresponding distribution READMEs. Evidence labels below distinguish derivation,
numerical observation, and unresolved global questions. Scratch: `/tmp/umvl-opus/gb2/`.
## 1. Convention, members, and closure

Write GB2 as `(a,b,p,q)`, all positive, with

\[
f(x)=\frac{a}{x B(p,q)}\frac{(x/b)^{ap}}{[1+(x/b)^a]^{p+q}},\qquad x>0.
\]

The requested reference is
`actuar::dtrbeta(x, shape1=q, shape2=a, shape3=p, scale=b)`;
`rate=1/b` is alternative syntax. `dpearson6` is an identical R alias.
This agrees with the [actuar documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/actuar/html/TransformedBeta.html).
Let `Y=log X`, `m=log b`, `t=a(Y-m)`, and `s(t)=log(1+exp(t))`. Then

\[
\log f(x)=\log a-\log x-\log B(p,q)+pt-(p+q)s(t),\quad
U=\operatorname{logistic}(t)\sim\mathrm{Beta}(p,q),\quad
X\overset d=b(G_p/G_q)^{1/a},
\]

where the unit-scale gammas are independent. Reciprocal symmetry is
`1/X ~ GB2(a,1/b,q,p)`; it is not a second parameter representation of X.
Moments: `E X^r=b^r B(p+r/a,q-r/a)/B(p,q)`, precisely for `-ap<r<aq`.
The two tail exponents are `ap` at zero and `aq` at infinity.

| Restriction | Member and existing fitter |
|---|---|
| `p=1` | Burr XII / Singh–Maddala: `mlburr`, shape1=q, shape2=a, scale=b |
| `q=1` | inverse Burr / Dagum: `mlinvburr`, shape1=p, shape2=a, scale=b |
| `p=1,q=a` | `mlparalogis`, shape=a, scale=b |
| `q=1,p=a` | `mlinvparalogis`, shape=a, scale=b |
| `p=q=1` | Fisk/log-logistic: `mlllogis`, shape=a, rate=1/b |
| `a=1` | beta prime: `mlbetapr`, shape1=p, shape2=q, scale=b |
| `a=p=1` | Lomax: `mllomax`, lambda=1/b, kappa=q |
| `a=q=1` | `mlinvpareto`, shape=p, scale=b |

Actuar calls the whole `a=1` beta-prime family “generalized Pareto”
(`dgenpareto`); this is not the repository's location/threshold GPD fitter.
Its `dpareto` is Lomax, whereas repository `mlpareto` is Pareto I.
Numerical rerun (`param.R`): at x=(.01,.3,1,2.5,40), a=2.3, b=1.7,
p=.8, q=3.1, log-density error was 3.55e-15 and CDF error 3.52e-19;
all listed member densities agreed within 4.45e-16.
**Derived proper continuous faces.** In the table, parameters not varied stay
fixed; θ, α, β, σ are positive. GG means `θ G_k^(1/c)`; inverse GG means
`θ G_k^(-1/c)`. These are distributional faces, not finite GB2 members.

| Path in GB2 | Limit | Fitter |
|---|---|---|
| `q→∞, b=θ q^(1/a)`, a,p fixed | GG(k=p,c=a,θ) | planned `mltrgamma` |
| `p→∞, b=θ p^(-1/a)`, a,q fixed | inverse GG(k=q,c=a,θ) | planned `mlinvtrgamma` |
| first path with a=1 | gamma(p, scale=θ) | `mlgamma` |
| second path with a=1 | inverse gamma(q, reciprocal-gamma scale θ) | `mlinvgamma`, alpha=q, beta=θ |
| first path with p=1 | Weibull(a,θ) | `mlweibull` |
| second path with q=1 | inverse Weibull(a,θ) | `mlinvweibull`, shape=a, rate=1/θ |
| `p=q=K→∞, a=sqrt(2 ψ₁(K))/σ, b=exp(μ)` | lognormal(μ,σ) | `mllnorm` |
| `a→∞, p=α/a, q=β/a, b=θ` | double Pareto(α,β,θ) | **none** |
| `a→∞, q=β/a`, p fixed, b=θ | Pareto I(β, minimum θ) | `mlpareto` |
| `a→∞, p=α/a`, q fixed, b=θ | power(α, maximum θ) | `mlpower` |

Exponential and inverse exponential are the a=p=1 and a=q=1 gamma faces
(`mlexp`, rate=1/θ; `mlinvexp`, rate=1/θ).
Uniform(0,θ) is the power face α=1; `mlunif` has no held-lower-endpoint
interface, so its unconstrained fit is unsuitable for this comparison.
The double Pareto density is

\[
f_D(x)=\frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)x}
\begin{cases}(x/\theta)^\alpha,&x<\theta,\\
(x/\theta)^{-\beta},&x\ge\theta.\end{cases}
\]

Proofs: use the gamma ratio for GG; use the gamma CLT on log gammas for
lognormal. For double Pareto, `a B(α/a,β/a)→(α+β)/(αβ)` and
`s(a log(x/θ))/a→max(log(x/θ),0)`. The one-sided paths follow by keeping
one beta shape positive. Every path has pointwise density convergence a.e.
to a unit-integral density; Scheffé gives L1 and hence Hellinger convergence.
Pareto/power are also obtained from double Pareto by α→∞ / β→∞.
Support endpoints and density values exactly at them require separate care.
**Exhaustiveness argument (continuous, nondegenerate limits).** On the log
scale these are location-scale log-beta odds. Pass to subsequences on which
each shape tends to 0, a positive finite value, or infinity. Two finite
positive shapes require finite positive a, yielding GB2 itself; one infinite
shape and one finite positive shape gives log-gamma or reflected log-gamma;
two infinite shapes, after centering and scaling, give normal. A vanishing
shape contributes an exponential fluctuation of size 1/p or 1/q in log odds.
If it dominates, the limit is one-sided exponential; if both contribute,
it is asymmetric Laplace. Log-gamma fluctuations from an infinite shape
are asymptotically normal of size 1/sqrt(shape), so cannot balance a
vanishing shape's diverging fluctuation. Other scalings collapse or fail
tightness. These cases give precisely the table and its nested faces.
A fully formal convergence-of-types proof would strengthen this argument;
no additional continuous face was found. Point masses and mass at 0/∞ are
weak degenerations, not proper Hellinger limits of these densities.
In particular neither GB1 nor a shifted two-sided bounded power law is a GB2 face.
## 2. Existence: bounded escape is different from an unbounded likelihood
**Derived unbounded path.** For constant observations x₀, take b=x₀,
p=q=1 and a→∞: `f(x₀)=a/(4x₀)`, so log likelihood diverges.
This covers n=1. Alternatively the lognormal corner can concentrate there.
There is no freely movable lower endpoint with a negative-power singularity
that can spike at one observation while preserving a separate broad component.
**Derived boundedness for nonconstant positive finite data.** The density g
of Y is strictly log-concave: `(log g)''=-a²(p+q)U(1-U)<0`.
For any such unimodal log-concave density with maximum M at mode v,
its e^-1 superlevel interval has length ≤e/M. Concavity then gives the
loose but sufficient envelope `g(y)≤M exp(1-M|y-v|/e)`.
For log-data range d>0, `Σ|y_i-v|≥d`; hence
`Σ log g(y_i)≤n log M+n-Md/e`, bounded above over M>0.
Subtracting `Σlog x_i` proves bounded GB2 likelihood. Thus nonconstant-data
escapes concern a finite supremum, not an arbitrarily high density spike.
**Derived local escape diagnostics.** At the GG face set ε=1/q and
`b=θ q^(1/a)`, `v_i=(x_i/θ)^a`. Expansion gives

\[
\ell_{GB2}=\ell_{GG}+\epsilon D+O(\epsilon^2),\qquad
D=\tfrac12\sum_i[v_i^2-2pv_i+p(p-1)].
\]

At the fitted GG scale, `Σv_i=np`, so `D=(Σ(v_i-p)^2-np)/2`.
The profiled derivative has the same value by the envelope theorem.
D<0 means the face is locally preferable to entering GB2; D>0 excludes
that face as a local maximum. Use reciprocal data for inverse GG.
At p=1 this becomes the Burr/Weibull discriminant `Σv_i²-2n`;
at a=1 it becomes the beta-prime/gamma condition
`p̂ CV_ML²<1`. These are local signs, not certificates against another basin.

For double Pareto at a strict interior fitted kink m=log θ, set p=α/a,
q=β/a. Normalization differs from the double Pareto by O(a^-2), while an
observation at the kink costs `(α+β)log(2)/a` at fixed m. Optimizing m on
the O(1/a) scale changes the coefficient but retains a strictly positive
smoothing penalty when the kink has strict left/right likelihood slopes.
Thus this face too can be approached from below. Hellinger convergence
alone does not guarantee likelihood convergence at an estimated endpoint:
for Pareto/power, shift b slightly outside the sample, with
`a|log(b/x_extreme)|→∞` and `log(b/x_extreme)→0`, to recover the full
one-sided endpoint density. Naively setting b exactly to the extreme can
lose a nonvanishing factor, e.g. the Burr Pareto path loses log 2 there.
**Positive probability: scope of the evidence.** A proper GB2 has positive
density on every open positive-data configuration. Strict boundary score
inequalities therefore occur with positive probability when their sample
sets contain an open neighborhood. At a GG population model, the efficient
extra-shape score is centered, nondegenerate, and asymptotically normal;
negative signs persist with nonvanishing probability under contiguous
interior GB2 alternatives. This establishes a local boundary phenomenon,
not a finite-sample global nonexistence theorem. Numerical searches below
support global escapes to GG, inverse GG and double Pareto, and inherited
probes also suggest lognormal and Pareto/power. A proof that an open sample
set has its **global** supremum on each named face is still missing here.
In particular Burr nonexistence does not imply GB2 nonexistence: freeing p
can open a better finite fit. Do not present simulation face counts or
negative D as a proved positive-probability global classification.
## 3. Identifiability and practical flatness
**Derived:** for finite parameters the two log-tail slopes identify ap, aq;
the exponentially small corrections to either tail identify a, then p,q,
and the tail constants identify b. Thus the four positive parameters are
identifiable. Allowing signed a would introduce `(a,p,q)↔(-a,q,p)` redundancy.
Near GG only `b/q^(1/a)` stays stable; near inverse GG only `b p^(1/a)` does.
Near double Pareto only ap, aq and b survive. Near lognormal only
`μ=log b+(ψ(p)-ψ(q))/a` and `σ²=(ψ₁(p)+ψ₁(q))/a²` survive.
These losses explain small information eigenvalues and wildly different
parameter vectors with nearly identical likelihoods. Report tail exponents,
log-location/log-spread and profile intervals; a small optimizer step or
relative objective change is insufficient evidence for an interior MLE.
Fresh expected-information quadrature (`fisher.R`), in coordinates
(log a,log b,log p,log q), gives correlation-scaled condition numbers
1411 at (a,p,q)=(3,2,1.5) and 89113 at (.5,10,10). At n=1000 the latter
has asymptotic SE(log a)=.858 and SE(log p)=1.644, but SE(log SD(Y))=.023.
These are local expected-information calculations, not finite-sample coverage.
## 4. Fitter design and small probes

Use standardized log data z, keeping its Jacobian. For fixed p,q, maximize
in A>0 and c with t_i=A z_i-c:
`h=n log A+p Σt_i-(p+q)Σs(t_i)-n log B(p,q)`.
This is strictly jointly concave for nonconstant z. The c score is
`Σlogistic(Az_i-c)=np/(p+q)`; solve by bracketed Newton, then maximize the
strictly concave profile in A. Its Hessian is the Schur complement of
`H_AA=-n/A²-(p+q)Σz_i²w_i`, `H_Ac=(p+q)Σz_i w_i`,
`H_cc=-(p+q)Σw_i`, where w_i=U_i(1-U_i). Search only two outer shape axes.
Concavity in A does not imply concavity in log A: safeguard those steps.

Alternative for fixed a,b: U_i=logistic(a log(x_i/b)) and fit ordinary beta
shapes by their strictly concave likelihood using log U and log(1-U).
With a fixed but b free, transforming x^a gives a free-scale beta-prime
problem, with the known Jacobian `Σ[log a+(a-1)log x_i]`. Reuse `mlbetapr`
only if powers are representable; preferably work on a log-data interface.
With b fixed, profile over a, fitting whichever beta shapes remain free; with p,q fixed,
solve the concave (a,c) problem, restricting c=a log b if b is also held.
Held p or q removes one outer shape axis; holding all parameters just evaluates
the likelihood. All 16 subsets need explicit routes and their constrained faces;
fixed arbitrary shapes generally preclude the unconstrained escape paths.
For example fixed a>0 precludes lognormal and double Pareto, but permits
GG/inverse GG; fixed a,b leaves beta fitting and no proper shape escape.

Compare maximized GG, inverse GG, lognormal, double Pareto, Pareto and power
faces first; gamma/Weibull and their inverses are nested GG cases, not extra
unrestricted GB2 faces to fit independently. Reuse planned GG fitters and
existing endpoint fitters, correcting `mlpower`'s positive epsilon offset
when computing the exact face supremum. There is currently no double-Pareto
fitter to call: D1 must name that missing model honestly.
For log-data kink m, let A=Σ(m-y_i)_+, B=Σ(y_i-m)_+; its profiled value is
`n log n-2n log(sqrt A+sqrt B)-n` (before the X Jacobian).
Check every order-statistic kink plus the one-sided endpoint faces;
α=n/[sqrt A(sqrt A+sqrt B)], β=n/[sqrt B(sqrt A+sqrt B)].

Start the outer search on log p/log q grid, at Burr and inverse-Burr fits,
and near each face; refine multiple basins and extend the search when an
edge improves. Check profile scores, outward derivatives and competing
face values; do not call a box edge a fit. Use the repository tie rule
`1e-8*n`, but flag unresolved searches separately from proven escapes.
Cost is O(n) per inner evaluation, times c-root/Newton iterations and
outer evaluations; grids are much more costly than existing 1D profiles.
Use compensated sums, softplus, log-scale parameters and asymptotic beta
normalizers near faces to avoid cancellation.
**Numerical evidence, not global certification.** The stopped agent's
`probe3.csv` contains 288 samples (8 generators × n=20,100,1000 × 12).
Its profile comparison exceeded the existing Burr/inverse-Burr/beta-prime
fit likelihoods by at most 1.66e-11, 1.14e-11, 8.30e-12 respectively;
these are inherited results, not a fresh complete test suite.
Fresh slot-locked prototype reruns (`codex-probe.R`, seed 13 independently
for each n=100 sample) gave these log-likelihood comparisons:

## 5. Reference accuracy and reproducible defects

Fresh checks used actuar 3.3.7, R 4.6.1. The independent density formula
above (softplus evaluated as `pmax(t,0)+log1p(exp(-abs(t)))`) agrees at
x=1e-300,1,1e300 with a=5,p=3,q=2; log densities are
-9666.763, .628609, -7594.436. The inherited mpmath quadrature grid
(`ref_mp.py`, 25 digits) flagged 224/548 d/p rows, mostly CDF saturation;
that count is exploratory, not a verified accuracy bound. Its tail
integrals are separate from actuar but lack rigorous error enclosures.

Fresh deterministic R one-liners (qualify calls with `actuar::`):

```r
ptrbeta(.4,.003,1000,.002)                         # 0; true ≈ .09600094
ptrbeta(1e300,2,5,3,lower.tail=FALSE,log.p=TRUE)    # -Inf; true ≈ -6905.964
qtrbeta(.5,.01,1,1)                              # Inf; true expm1(log(2)/.01)
qtrbeta(.001,.06,50,.04,lower.tail=FALSE)          # Inf; finite quantile
```

For the first result, independently integrate log-odds density using v=pt:
`integrate(function(v) exp(v-.005*(pmax(v/.002,0)+log1p(exp(-abs(v/.002))))-log(.002)-lbeta(.002,.003)), -Inf, 2*log(.4))$value`.
This avoids forming an underflowed beta argument. For the second,
`-10*log(1e300)-log(2)-lbeta(3,2)` gives the tail log probability, with a
negligible relative correction O(1e-1500). For the third, p=1 gives the
exact Lomax quantile 1.2676506e30. For the fourth, the small upper beta
argument is about exp(-100), so the X quantile is only about 7.4.
The failure is inversion through a beta value rounded to one, not overflow
of the requested X quantile. Density appears considerably more robust
than p/q in these probes; this is not a universal density guarantee.
The inherited purported `pbeta(log.p=TRUE)` workaround is invalid: log.p
controls output, not logarithmic input. Its unequal-large-shape lognormal
probe also overflowed b and is discarded. No reference fix is installed.
## 6. Recommendation, implementation order, and literature boundary

Proceed only with a face-aware research prototype first. Finish GG/inverse
GG with their own nested limits; add/test a double-Pareto face evaluator;
then implement stable log-data inner profiles, analytic gradients and
constrained fixed-parameter dispatch. Validate face expansions and endpoint
paths, expand-box searches, reciprocal/scale equivariance and independent
likelihood oracles. Resolve global escape certification before promising
an exact GB2 MLE status. Add runtime-checked actuar p/q fixes only after
agreeing a stable reference-based replacement; ordinary `pbeta` on a
rounded logistic argument is insufficient. No package code changed here.

Existing three-parameter fitters provide useful certified subproblems and
starts. Their good inherited likelihood comparisons are reassuring, but
the containing GB2 exposes two limitations of generic fitting: excellent
likelihood accuracy need not identify parameters, and nonexistence in a
subfamily can disappear when another shape is freed. The new double-Pareto
face makes a four-parameter optimizer plus the old three-parameter face
list inadequate.

Literature supplies family definitions/context, not the new global claims:
[McDonald (1984), *Econometrica* 52, 647–665](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913469)
introduces generalized income-size families;
[McDonald & Xu (1995), *J. Econometrics* 66, 133–152](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(94)01612-4)
introduces the larger five-parameter GB family (not just this GB2), with
[erratum, 69, 427–428](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(94)01680-X).
[Kleiber & Kotz (2003), *Statistical Size Distributions*](https://doi.org/10.1002/0471457175)
is the family/naming reference. For the pre-existing Burr escape problem,
[Shao (2004), *CSDA* 45, 675–687](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-9473(02)00367-5)
and [Shao, Ip & Wong (2004), *CSDA* 46, 317–334](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-9473(03)00171-3)
are directly relevant; the latter describes embedded-model likelihood
expansions. Publisher abstracts/records were checked; full-text theorems
were not verified. Closure arguments, boundedness proof and discriminant
algebra above are this evaluation's derivations, not attributed theorems.
