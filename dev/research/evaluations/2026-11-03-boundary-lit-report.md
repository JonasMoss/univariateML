## Findings

The proposed regular/singular/degenerate classification is not a valid trichotomy. Its first two definitions can both hold vacuously when normalized Hellinger secants have no strong limit. Its proposed degenerate case is coordinate-dependent and overlaps the proposed regular case. The claimed fixed-sample likelihood consequences are false under the stated hypotheses, including on a three-atom sample space. These are mathematical counterexamples, not failures to locate sufficient literature.

The replacement separates support leakage, DQM at a specified speed, the contingent Hellinger cone, and intrinsic dimension collapse. It provides a narrow parametrization-free definition of the last property, with an example and explicit exclusions. It does not give ordinary corners, singular information and all geometric singularities one common name.

Two package-specific conclusions sharpen the draft:

- At a fixed Pareto law the **full free Burr model has leakage-ratio cluster set `[0,1]`**. One dominant-leakage path cannot label the entire boundary point.
- For **BB approaching Poisson with its positive mean held fixed, the full contingent cone spans two dimensions**. The cancelling diagonal exposes a cubic count direction. Collinear first derivatives in `(1/a,1/N)` are correct, but are not the full tangent cone.

## Literature: what is established

The closest established phrase for a proper law reached as the original parameters diverge is **embedded model** or **embedded distribution**. Cheng and Iles explicitly discuss selecting the lower-dimensional limiting model when it fits best. Cheng and Traylor distinguish unbounded likelihood, embedded models and indeterminate parameters. Cheng's book gives the embedded-model problem its own chapters. These precedents cover the motivation for the draft, rather than its proposed Hellinger trichotomy. Sources: [Cheng and Iles, publisher summary](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/52/1/135/7027883), [Cheng and Traylor, publisher summary](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/57/1/3/7035899), [Cheng, book contents](https://academic.oup.com/book/43941).

The additional directly relevant references are **Watkins (1999)** on Burr/Weibull fitting and **Shao, Ip and Wong (2004)** on embedded-distribution discriminants. Their local comparisons precede the note's face-slope argument. The publisher abstract of the latter describes a likelihood difference near the fitted embedded distribution; it does not justify transferring a universal global existence criterion to every family. The repository's BB and Burr counterexamples independently rule out such a transfer. Sources: [Watkins](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947399000249), [Shao, Ip and Wong](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947303001713).

**DQM, scores, tangent sets and local experiments** are established in Le Cam, van der Vaart and Bickel et al. DQM is a statement about an integral remainder at a stated parameter speed. **Constrained likelihood and cone projection** are the relevant terminology for regular boundaries. Chernoff and Self–Liang require probabilistic and geometric assumptions; Drton's Theorem 2.6 explicitly assumes a regular ambient model, Chernoff regularity and consistency. Neither an arbitrary contingent cone nor negligible leakage alone supplies these hypotheses. Sources: [Le Cam, publisher record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-4946-7), [van der Vaart](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511802256), [BKRW author record](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/jaw/RESEARCH/BOOKS/book3.html), [Self–Liang full text, pp. 605–607](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/763-2013/week06/papers/self-liang-1987.pdf), [Drton, Section 2, Theorem 2.6 and Remark 2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703360).

**Locally conic models** are another close antecedent. Dacunha-Castelle and Gassiat study likelihood tests with nonidentifiability using a local reparametrization and derivative directions. Their setting reinforces the need to examine directions exposed after cancellation. It is not an outside-support taxonomy. Source: [full text, introduction and Section 2](https://www.numdam.org/item/PS_1997__1__285_0.pdf).

**Nonregular support problems** already have a substantial rate-sensitive theory. Smith considers densities proportional near an unknown endpoint to distance raised to `alpha-1`; his summary distinguishes finite-information classical behavior for `alpha>2`, a critical case at `alpha=2`, and nonclassical cases below it. Thus a hard support edge alone is not a classification. Ibragimov and Has'minskii have separate chapters on densities with jumps and classification of singularities. Sources: [Smith, publisher abstract](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/72/1/67/242523?login=true), [Ibragimov–Has'minskii, contents, Chapters V–VI](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4899-0027-2).

**Singular information** and **singular model sets** have established meanings different from the draft's outside-support definition. Rotnitzky et al. study singular Fisher information; Drton studies geometric singularities within regular ambient families. Watanabe's singular learning theory addresses nonidentifiability and singular information in a broader analytic setting. Prentice's extended log-gamma parametrization is a concrete antecedent for placing a limiting normal model inside an enlarged family. Sources: [Rotnitzky et al., DOI identity](https://doi.org/10.2307/3318576), [coauthor's institutional bibliography](https://www.imm.ki.se/biostatistics/singularinformation/), [Drton](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703360), [Watanabe, publisher introduction](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/64671/excerpt/9780521864671_excerpt.pdf), [Prentice, publisher summary](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/61.3.539).

For exponential families the established terms are **completion**, **extended family**, **accessible face**, **generalized MLE**, and **direction of recession**. Csiszár and Matúš distinguish variation, weak and information-divergence closures; their introduction identifies earlier closure discussions in Barndorff-Nielsen and Brown. Geyer develops likelihood inference in the Barndorff-Nielsen completion. Hellinger and total variation induce the same topology on probability laws, but this does not identify either with weak or divergence closure. These theorems need exponential-family hypotheses; they do not automatically classify Burr or BB. Sources: [Csiszár–Matúš, introduction and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0503653), [Geyer, Sections 3.3–3.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.0455), [Geyer's definitive bibliography](https://www.stat.umn.edu/geyer/8931expfam/biblio.html), [Barndorff-Nielsen, publisher description](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9781118857281).

No matching published trichotomy or established use of “seam/cusp/corner” for these three proposed properties was found. This is a bounded search finding, not a proof of absence. The ingredients are established; the leakage descriptor, narrow intrinsic definition and explicit BB/Burr calculations are the contribution of this revision within the repository. Priority for those particular formulations or calculations is **not established**. “Cusp” and “corner” already carry geometric meanings that do not match the original proposal.

## Mathematics changed in the note

1. **Boundary and domination.** A designated accumulation law replaces an unqualified ambient topological boundary. Finite-dimensional models usually have empty interior in the ambient space of laws. Weak limits outside the dominated Hellinger closure are kept separate.
2. **Missing directions.** Uniform laws on `[-t,1]` and `[t,1]` approaching `U[0,1]` have no strong normalized secant limits. Their weak limits are zero. The note proves the support decomposition of existing strong tangents, including the nonnegativity of the outside component. It states precisely when relative compactness makes direction-based tests equivalent to leakage tests.
3. **Leakage descriptor.** With `S={g>0}`, write `H²=A+sigma`, with `A` the inside-support squared distance and `sigma=p(S^c)`. The ratio `sigma/H²` and its cluster set remain meaningful without tangent directions. Their invariance under coordinates, dominating measures and invertible data transformations is proved.
4. **DQM and fixed samples.** The path speed is explicit. Negligible leakage is necessary at the DQM speed, but needs an inside-support expansion too. Pointwise likelihood Taylor expansions have their own assumptions. A bounded `t^(3/2)` remainder disproves the claimed quadratic remainder; a shrinking density bump disproves even the pointwise linear conclusion from DQM alone.
5. **Dominant leakage.** The three-atom counterexample has an actual purely outside-support direction and `H²~sigma`, but likelihood increases at the first atom. On a compact segment of that path, the limit is the MLE exactly when the sample sum of the two-valued inside perturbation is nonpositive. Its probability tends to one-half, directly disproving the proposed probability-one conclusion too. A replacement theorem adds a conditional-density remainder `o(sigma)` at the observations. Exact disjoint mixtures furnish a model for which the boundary is indeed always optimal under data from the limit.
6. **Boundary probability.** The one-half statement is restricted to a regular extension, one smooth scalar inequality, positive efficient information, regular nuisance parameters and localization. An additional local profile sign argument establishes *exact* boundary attainment probability. Weak convergence of scaled estimates with an atom at zero does not by itself establish convergence of the finite-sample atom. No universal probability-one escape theorem is retained.
7. **Intrinsic degeneracy.** Dimension collapse means the span of the strong contingent cone has smaller dimension than the stable local Hausdorff dimension of the square-root model, with compact normalized secants assumed. A normal mean model restricted to `a>=0, |b|<=a²` proves this can happen independently of support leakage. A regular quadrant and a semicubical cusp demonstrate its limitations. Arbitrary coordinate rank loss, as in normal mean `theta=t³`, is excluded as a definition of a property of the set of laws.
8. **Burr.** An explicit dominant-leakage path has outside mass of order `k log(1/k)`, but no strong unit tangent because mass concentrates at the endpoint. Shape perturbations of order `sqrt(sigma)` give all intermediate leakage ratios, and larger vanishing perturbations give zero. Thus the all-free cluster set is exactly `[0,1]`. The report's entropy proof of sample-profile stability is retained as a separate likelihood argument. Endpoint density traces remain additional structure, not a property of a law in Hellinger distance.
9. **BB.** The diagonal log-pmf coefficient is `A2=-C3/3-(mu+1)C2/2`, where `C2,C3` are Poisson–Charlier polynomials. They are independent. The full fixed-mean cone is `{(a C2+b A2)sqrt(g): a real, b>=0}`. The proof supplies a uniform exponential-moment bound from BB factorial moments, making the expansion an `L²` statement rather than just a fixed-count calculation. Integer `N` is retained throughout. Local Hausdorff dimension is one for the countable fixed-mean union over sizes; treating it as a smooth two-coordinate chart is not justified. With free mean a further independent mean direction appears.
10. **Evidence discipline.** The earlier unarchived simulations and quadrature table are replaced by retained scripts/data and explicit proofs. The note's family table distinguishes one-sided DQM, discrete secants, negligible leakage, and coordinate cancellation. It does not assign a global type from a selected path or from a few likelihood coefficients.

## Precise open entries

- **Priority beyond the bounded search.** No claim that the leakage terminology, dimension-collapse descriptor or BB cone formula is historically new. A priority claim would require broader full-text discovery, including the inaccessible source proofs. It is unnecessary for the corrected note's conclusions.
- **BB transverse DQM at a fixed NB law.** Negligible leakage is immediate because a proper NB has full counting-measure support. A transverse `1/N` DQM assertion additionally needs a square-integrable NB score and uniform tail/remainder control for that fixed positive shape. The note no longer equates the fixed-count expansion in the closure report with that proof.
- **Other Burr held subsets and other support limits.** The all-free Burr cluster set is proved here. Analogous descriptors with endpoint/shape held, skew-normal approaching half-normal, and other threshold/truncation limits require their own approaching sets and rate analysis. The original note's proposed 99-dossier inventory is not claimed as completed by this focused review.
- **Global escape calibration.** A probability-one Burr escape claim and a Lomax one-half claim for the actual global fitter require consistency/localization, uniform likelihood bounds and control of competing regions, followed by procedure-specific calibration checks. The prior unarchived simulation is insufficient. The note retains only the conditional regular-half-space result and exact mixture example proved or justified under their stated assumptions.

These entries delimit the revised statements. None is left as a hypothesis needed by its proved leakage, counterexample, intrinsic-definition or BB-cone results.
