## Grouped-gamma tests

**Proved.** In dgamma the coefficient domain is $k,r>0$. Use $C_j=\int_j^{j+1} y^{k-1}e^{-ry}dy$, $j\ge0$. Every cell has positive mass; the effective integer set is $L,\ldots,U$. The normalizer is the integral on $[L,U+1]$, with infinity unchanged. A singleton has likelihood one at every compatible coefficient vector. Real nominal lower cuts with effective lower cell $m>0$ range over $(m-1,m]$ and if $m=0$ over all cuts $\le0$. For estimated upper cell $M$, real cuts range over $[M,M+1)$. The parent has no last cell.

For free shape and rate on a nonsingleton, constants have the excluded occupied-cell atom. Exactly two occupied adjacent cells have the empirical Bernoulli limit, and attain iff the two cells exhaust the window. The limit follows from a gamma CLT at the shared cell boundary with its mean shifted by order $1/\sqrt{k}$. On two retained cells the rate odds at each fixed shape decrease strictly to zero because the upper cell has greater conditional mean $y$. At rate zero the grouped-power odds diverge as shape grows. Thus all sufficiently large shapes yield a unique matching positive rate, giving a continuum. The optimum height for empirical odds $o$ is the Bernoulli product in the two conditional probabilities $1/(1+o)$ and $o/(1+o)$. If any two occupied cells are separated by one or more whole cells, strict concavity of $\log y-(r/(k-1))y$ implies loss at one occupied cell along $k\to\infty$. A steep decreasing rate also gives loss at the upper occupied cell. Compare finite fits with the integrable grouped shape-zero kernel $y^{-1}e^{-ry}$ if $L>0$, the rate-zero power $y^{k-1}$ if $U<\infty$, and the corner $1/y$ if both conditions hold. Equality at a finite value suffices. This covers data using three or more cells as well.

At held shape use the maximum of the grouped power height ($U<\infty$; zero height on an unbounded ray) and the first-cell atom height ($r\to\infty$). At held rate use the maximum of the grouped inverse-power exponential ($L>0$) or the zero-cell atom ($L=0$) at shape zero, and the last-cell atom at infinite shape ($U<\infty$; zero height with mass escaping on a ray). Apply the scalar root comparison with finite equality. On two cells occupied at both ends, at held shape the empirical odds must be strictly between zero and the grouped-power endpoint odds. At held rate they must exceed the shape-zero endpoint odds (zero if $L=0$), with no finite upper threshold. These bounds follow from strict ordering of the two cells' means of $-y$ and of $\log y$, respectively. A scalar coefficient fitting these two cells is unique. For more than two retained cells a general count of winning roots is open.
