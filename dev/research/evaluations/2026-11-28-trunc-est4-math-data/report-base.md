## Corrections and scope

**A tight event can be null even when the nominal interval has positive width.** For the exact sample $(1)$, hold triangular $(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)$ and fix the upper cut at 2. The tight window $[1,2]$ retains only the endpoint. But with lower cut $1-\varepsilon$ the likelihood is $2/(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)$, which diverges, and the retained laws tend weakly to $\delta_1$. This is a qualification to the estimated-bound reduction if it is applied merely by checking the nominal widths. On the same held interval with mode 0, the density at 1 is zero, so these loosenings have zero likelihood. With GPD $(\mu,\sigma,\xi)=(0,1,-1)$ the first example instead has height $1/\varepsilon$. With the same GPD and sample $(0)$, the lower-estimated design with fixed upper cut 1 has height one at every lower cut at or below zero. Thus even a unique, all-held parent can have a continuum of optimal nominal cuts.

**A divergent GPD likelihood can have escaping mass on a right ray.** The known-window failure list requires the qualification already identified in the independent review. For $(1,2)$, let $\mu=1$, $s\downarrow0$, $h=s^{1/4}$, $\xi=1/h$ and $\sigma=s/h$. On the lower-estimated design with fixed upper cut $\infty$, retention is one and the likelihood is $(h/s)^2(1+1/s)^{-h-1}\sim s^{-1/2}$. The conditional survival at each positive excess tends to one. On $[1,2]$ the same path has mass asymptotic to $h\log(1+1/s)$ and the conditional CDF at each excess in $(0,1)$ tends to one, so the tight bounded laws instead converge to $\delta_1$. The same divergence witness does not identify a common limit law for the three regimes. [Known-window review](2026-11-27-mod-rev9-report.md#gpd-and-pareto-iv-joint-escapes)

## Joint profiling lemma

**Proved.** Write $m=\min_i x_i$ and $M=\max_i x_i$. Denote fixed cuts by $A,B$ and the tight nominal window by $W_*=[\ell_*,u_*]$, one of $[m,B]$, $[A,M]$ or $[m,M]$. Require $A\le m$ and $B\ge M$ wherever those cuts are fixed. For each admissible parent coefficient vector $\theta$, set $S_\theta$ to its support and $C_\theta=\prod_i f_\theta(x_i)$. For every admissible nominal containing window $W$ we have
$$
 W_*\cap S_\theta\subseteq W\cap S_\theta,\qquad
 L(\theta,W)=\frac{C_\theta}{P_\theta(W)^n}.
$$
The inclusion is at the *same* coefficient vector. In particular, if $\theta_j$ moves the support, it applies separately at every $\theta_j$. On positive finite numerator and positive tight mass,
$$
 L(\theta,W)=L(\theta,W_*)\left(\frac{P_\theta(W_*)}{P_\theta(W)}\right)^n\le L(\theta,W_*).
$$
The tight pair itself is available. These two inequalities identify the joint supremum on these slices with the supremum of the tight problem. A tight maximizer gives a joint maximizer, and every finite positive joint maximizer must have maximizing tight coefficients and must add zero parent mass. An escaping tight sequence already gives a joint sequence at the same heights with fixed tight cuts. The proof uses neither an exponential-family form nor a fixed support. A zero sample density cannot give a positive finite maximum. A singular positive sample product with positive event mass gives infinite likelihood under the inside-density convention. If an observation has zero density, the positive-carrier arguments do not apply, including when a different observation is singular. Outside-support observations are incompatible.

For a continuous parent with positive density on the interior $(d_\theta,e_\theta)$, the conditional effective ends are
$$
 L_\theta=\max(\ell_*,d_\theta),\qquad U_\theta=\min(u_*,e_\theta).
$$
At a positive finite fit the nominal lower cut must equal $m$ if $d_\theta<m$, and can be any cut $\le m$ if $d_\theta=m$. The nominal upper cut must equal $M$ if $e_\theta>M$, and can be any cut $\ge M$ if $e_\theta=M$. Apply each condition only to an estimated side. Compatibility rules out $d_\theta>m$ or $e_\theta<M$. The strict statements follow by integrating strictly positive density on the open interval between the changed effective endpoints. With both cuts estimated the allowed lower and upper intervals form a Cartesian product for each winning $\theta$. Coincidence with a fitted support endpoint gives a plateau even when the parent coefficients themselves are unique.

**Proved, null tight slices.** For an atomless parent with interval support in this study, a compatible null tight slice can occur only at a constant sample $c$. For every such coefficient vector test whether a looser allowed window retains a nonempty support interval and whether $0<f_\theta(c)<\infty$. If so, take the loosened window back toward $W_*$ while keeping that vector fixed. The positive mass decreases to zero and the likelihood diverges. The effective interval shrinks to $c$, so the conditional law tends weakly to $\delta_c$. For the finite positive endpoint densities here the conditional height per observation is asymptotic to inverse effective width, by the inside limit for the density. If a looser window has positive mass and the occupied endpoint is singular, the extended likelihood is already infinite. Shrinking that window also gives a weak point mass, with infinite heights throughout that fixed-coefficient trace. If no loosened window has positive mass, the null slice is infeasible. If loosenings have positive mass but density zero at $c$, they give no positive fit. Check **all** compatible null slices first, even if some other coefficient vectors retain positive tight mass.

For $c=m=M$ there are only the following null geometries. In each test require that the candidate support and endpoint density are feasible under the stated holds.

| Design | Null geometry | Side into which loosening must reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower estimated, $B>c$ | $e_\theta=c$ | Left |
| Lower estimated, $B=c$ | $(\ell_*,u_*)=(c,c)$ | Left, so $d_\theta<c\le e_\theta$ |
| Upper estimated, $A<c$ | $d_\theta=c$ | Right |
| Upper estimated, $A=c$ | $(\ell_*,u_*)=(c,c)$ | Right, so $d_\theta\le c<e_\theta$ |
| Both estimated | $(\ell_*,u_*)=(c,c)$ | Either side within the support |

For example when $B=c=d_\theta$ a lower cut cannot gain mass at that coefficient vector. A different vector may be feasible because an endpoint is free. The null test examines that vector with its own support. When both cuts are estimated on constants the nominal tight slice is null for every continuous parent. The null test thus decides the complete constant-sample case for that design.

**Proved, positive finite escape heights.** After the null test, the global comparison for the tight window applies pointwise to its positive-mass slices, with the original holds unchanged. For $0<S=\sup L(\theta,W_*)<\infty$, any sequence with joint heights tending to $S$ must have tight heights tending to $S$ and $P_\theta(W_*)/P_\theta(W)\to1$. The conditional law on the looser window is a mixture of the tighter law and the normalized removed part, with removed weight $1-P_\theta(W_*)/P_\theta(W)$. Thus their total variation distance tends to zero. This proves transfer of competitive limiting laws at a finite positive supremum. At an infinite supremum the identity gives no such ratio limit. A divergence witness can settle nonattainment without exhausting divergent paths. At attained height, an excluded face tying the fit does not displace it.

## Applying the fixed-window proofs

All comparisons below are in exact arithmetic. The windows displayed in the regime sections are held fixed **for the realized sample**. In a monotone transformed coordinate, change both the observations and the cuts and reverse the cut order when the transform is decreasing. The Jacobian at the observations cancels in comparisons if the transform is fixed by the holds. If the transform uses a free coefficient, retain its Jacobian or compare conditional likelihood in the original coordinate. A bounded nominal window does not imply a bounded log window, and clipped support ends remain active in every limit.

The scalar tilt test uses the statistic $T$ and carrier written in the linked proof. If the tilt is positive, the empirical mean must be strictly between the zero-tilt mean and the supremum of $T$ on the displayed window. Its derivative is positive variance on every positive-width interval where the statistic varies. The two-shape beta tests use $\log z,\log(1-z)$ and the gamma tests use $\log u,-u$. Their Hessians are negative definite by linear independence modulo constants on the retained interval. Their coercivity comparisons use a positive-length subinterval where the limiting directional exponent exceeds its empirical mean. For distinct observations that mean is strictly below the exponent maximum, including when observations occur at both cuts. Apply the inward tests only on integrable faces permitted by the holds. In a stationary-height comparison evaluate the linked kernels and reachable excluded heights on the displayed window, retaining exact equalities, feasible joins and inside-density sample traces. Pareto and GPD comparisons at threshold contact use the occupied threshold factors, not the positive-excess formula evaluated by dropping those observations. For a pure tilt $e^{tz}$ on a transformed interval, the integral is finite on bounded intervals at every finite $t$, on right rays at $t<0$, and on left rays at $t>0$. The $t=0$ law is uniform in $z$ only on a bounded interval. In log coordinates it pulls back to log-uniform, and other slopes pull back to powers. In physical coordinates a nonzero slope is a truncated exponential. Its uniform limit on $[m,M]$ is admissible as an excluded comparison only when the specified path can reach it under the hold. In particular it is an attained conditional law for the GPD uniform submodel and cannot be imposed generically on GB2 or triangular. The grouped kernels in dgamma are integrated over the underlying unit cells, not evaluated at their endpoints. The narrow-window weak limits on null continuous slices are point masses, excluded from these continuous parent models.
