## Explicit moving-endpoint submodels

**Proved, GEV at held $\xi=-1$.** The conditional kernel is $e^{tx}$ for $t=1/\sigma>0$, supported below $e=\mu+\sigma$. At fixed $t$, decreasing $e$ to $M$ has no effect on the numerator once the common factor $t e^{-te}$ cancels, and strictly decreases the denominator when the effective upper cut decreases. Nonconstant data therefore optimize at effective upper cut $M$. The strict mean range is $((\ell_*+M)/2,M)$ for finite $\ell_*$, and $(-\infty,M)$ otherwise. At or below the finite lower mean the excluded face is uniform. At the upper equality, either the interval already has zero width or the increasing exponential with rate tending to infinity concentrates at $M$. A constant at $M$ can alternatively put $e=M$ and loosen a lower cut when that cut is estimated. On attainment the rate is unique. The set of $e$ is $\{M\}$ if $u_*>M$, and $[M,\infty)$ if $u_*=M$. For each such $e$, apply the cut plateau rule at that same endpoint. For sample $(1/2,1)$ with $\ell_*=0$ use the mean $3/4$ on $[0,1]$ to obtain continuum many parent winners when $u_*=1$. With both cuts estimated the sample $(0,1,1)$ gives the same strict interior test. The only way a constant can attain an ordinary finite maximum in this submodel would be to reach the upper statistic limit at finite rate and positive width, which strict positivity of the conditional carrier precludes.

**Proved, GPD at held $\xi=-1$ with threshold and scale free.** For $m<M$ every containing interval after clipping has width at least $M-m$. The support $[\mu,\mu+\sigma]=[m,M]$ attains that lower bound. Every winner has
$$
 \max(\ell_*,\mu)=m,\qquad \min(u_*,\mu+\sigma)=M.
$$
For lower-estimated $B>M$ this means $\mu\le m$ and $\mu+\sigma=M$. If $B=M$, any $\mu+\sigma\ge M$ is allowed. For upper-estimated $A<m$ it means $\mu=m$ and $\mu+\sigma\ge M$; if $A=m$, any $\mu\le m$ is allowed. With both cuts estimated any parent support containing the range wins. The conditional law is uniform on $[m,M]$ in all three cases, with likelihood $(M-m)^{-n}$. The nominal cut fibers follow from parent support contact, so a fitted threshold $\mu=m$ or upper end $\mu+\sigma=M$ permits a plateau on the corresponding estimated side. Constants diverge to $\delta_m$ by taking a support interval of length tending to zero on a side allowed by the fixed cut or by loosening an estimated cut into the support at a null slice.

**Proved, selected triangular masks.** At nonconstant data the retained height is rational on an order stratum of the support endpoints, mode, data and finite cuts. Clearing positive denominators gives $Qw=P$ for the sample height $w>0$. Substituting the tight cuts before eliminating the free coefficients gives a semialgebraic height set. A finite included rightmost height gives all tight winners by taking its fiber; absence of such a height identifies an excluded limit or incompatible data. At positive retained width the conditional density is at most twice the inverse width, by integrating a triangle under the concave height kernel. Nonconstant data therefore cannot diverge. A finite excluded law is a normalized one- or two-sided affine kernel from the limiting strata in the reviewed proof, with a separate point trace if a side shrinks at a sample cut. Exact algebraic projection also detects excluded constant-sample heights, which need a width check first. For $a=-1,b=2$ and sample $(0,1)$, choose $B=1$ or $A=0$ if there is a fixed cut. The maximum is $8/9$, achieved at every mode in $[-1,0]\cup[1,2]$. On either plateau the conditional shape is affine. In between, with $D=(c+1)(2-c)>2$, the height is $4D/(9(D-1)^2)<8/9$. The nominal estimated cuts are strict here because the optimum retained interval is strictly inside the parent support. [Triangular algebraic proof](2026-11-27-trunc-misc-math-report.md#triangular)

## Zero observations in exponentiated Weibull and PGW

**Proved.** When zero is observed and the design admits positive mass at the tight or an expanded window, it retains a right neighborhood of zero. In exponentiated Weibull, if shape or exponent is free, one can choose their product below one and obtain infinite inside density with the other sample factors positive. In PGW the same holds if shape is free. If the endpoint power is held strictly above one, every valid window instead has zero likelihood. A lower-estimated sample of all zeros with fixed upper cut zero has no positive-mass expansion because the support starts at zero. Both-estimated constants and upper-estimated all-zero data with a consistent fixed lower bound can expand upward.

If exponent and shape are held at endpoint power one and scale varies, the broad-scale face on finite upper cut is uniform in physical coordinates. The same expansion of the CDF and density that gives the positive-observation power limit includes the endpoint density. On an infinite upper cut the likelihood tends to zero as scale grows. At small scale the occupied-zero density is proportional to inverse scale, while any fixed positive observation has exponentially small density. This proves that every nonconstant zero-containing sample is decided by the scalar scale score against the broad face, and an all-zero sample on a valid fixed positive-width tight window diverges as scale decreases. For a constant on the null tight slice use expansion first.

For PGW with scale and shape held at $S>0$, shape one, the same reasoning applies at exponent infinity. The zero density grows linearly as $\mathrm{exponent}/S$ and each positive density tends to zero faster than any inverse power of exponent by the survival $\exp(1-(1+x/S)^{\mathrm{exponent}})$. The retained mass tends to one when the positive-width interval starts at zero. At exponent zero the normalized broad kernel is proportional to $(S+x)^{-1}$ on the finite window, including at zero, and on an infinite upper cut the likelihood tends to zero. Thus the scalar exponent comparison extends to zero-containing nonconstant samples in this mask. An all-zero sample on a positive-width window diverges at exponent infinity, with weak limit $\delta_0$.
