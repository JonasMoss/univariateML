## Shape declarations and remaining proof obligations

- **Exponentiated Weibull.** The held-exponent and held-shape iff statements cover nonconstant positive data. The held-scale iff covers every positive sample, including constants below the held scale. The two held-scale failure declarations cover complementary branches. The power theorem has the actual `log(e−1)` loss per contact and the optimized power theorem separately shows strict separation from every finite point. The maximizing-set theorem gives a compact superlevel and a finite root list with the conditional exponent and height test. It leaves the almost-everywhere uniqueness clause of that cell open. The all-free statements in this fragment cover concentration on constants. The nonconstant two-face exhaustion and the exact replicated crossing remain written. [Lean source](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Shape2ExpWeibull.lean)
- **PGW.** The narrow Gompertz theorem takes an upper bound on the sample and the powered-ratio hypothesis. Choosing the sample maximum provides the exact written sufficient branch, including constants. Its conclusion supplies an attainable face height and strict inequality for every finite fit under that hypothesis. The held-scale concentration theorem handles constants at or below scale. The complete failure cell also involves the threshold comparison, including contacts and constants above scale. The held-shape criterion still needs a joined finite stationary list, its retention rule and equality against the fitted face. [Lean source](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Shape2PGW.lean)
- **Fatigue.** The density path treats each tied minimum and gives a finite trace off the minimum. The unbounded-image theorem supplies joint unboundedness, and the no-max theorem supplies nonattainment. At held shape alone, the new path handles constants. [Lean source](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Shape2Fatigue.lean)
- **Generalized gamma.** The fixed-`Q` results include the normal seam. At fixed location the unique scale is conditional on an arbitrary supplied `Q`. ## Endpoint declarations and remaining proof obligations

- **Structural masks.** The Feller–Pareto threshold spike takes a subunit lower power. The generic `unbounded_of_threshold_path` also assumes an arbitrary model has the entire feasible path and agrees with the kernel along it. That assumption is the embedding step, so the declaration is supporting evidence only. The option-valued holds in `structural_unbounded` enforce the actual held coefficients and its disjunctive hypothesis can be discharged for the mapped structural masks. In Pareto IV the substitution is lower beta shape one, upper beta shape `shape1`, and power `shape2`. At held scale and held power, the subunit spike covers only one branch. The constant branch and the regular compact profile with upper shape free need separate results. Several atlas failure claims share wording about vanishing scale and upper beta shape. I credited structural unboundedness when the statement supplies the disjunction appropriate to the actual mask. The single-path rows give narrower evidence where the wording describes more than that path. [Threshold kernel](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2FellerPareto.lean) · [Author scope notes](2026-11-27-lean-endpoint2-report.md#scope-and-source-discrepancies)
- **Fixed shapes with threshold and scale free.** The criterion captures the sample multiplicity through the sign of the collapse exponent and includes the unit-power contact. The negative-exponent theorem and the finite zero-exponent envelope cover individual failure branches. The latter states existence of a strict supremum rather than its analytical value. The criterion quantifies over existence only. It gives no joint uniqueness for positive exponent. The fixed-scale-and-shapes theorem gives the threshold criterion and singleton, including a maximizer at contact when the lower power is one. Its statement omits identification of the root above one. The separate interior theorem identifies that root on the strictly superunit branch. A joined declaration would close the complete cardinality claim. [Free scale](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2FellerParetoFreeScale.lean) · [Fixed scale](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2FellerParetoRegular.lean)
- **Feller–Pareto with threshold held.** The conditional power–scale theorem quantifies over supplied positive beta shapes. It settles the conditional fit, including cardinality when both shapes are held, while comparison over free shapes remains for the wider masks. The physical concentration path fixes the beta shapes and reaches the common excess. At held scale and power a separate beta transfer provides the constant unboundedness and the iff. The open wider claims need the fitted face envelope, finite stationary-component heights and exact crossing transfers. - **Pareto II and III.** Pareto II all-free divergence couples a vanishing threshold gap and scale. With threshold held its iff explicitly excludes zero excesses. With scale and shape held, its public singleton result does not identify the threshold as the sample minimum, although the proof uses the monotone contact kernel. This is the required test when the gaps are measured from an observed minimum, and would be insufficient for arbitrary distances from a held threshold. The fixed-shape result covers all three collapse-exponent signs. The three-level tie leaves the two-level and almost-everywhere clauses open. Pareto III constant paths preserve the exact common-excess scale. Its fixed-shape scale–threshold uniqueness needs a joint score argument. [Pareto II](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2ParetoII.lean) · [Author open targets](2026-11-27-lean-endpoint2-report.md)
- **Pareto IV.** The translated Weibull path uses the declared product exactly. The held-scale escape uses the penalized trace, with `−log 2` per contact, and supplies the finite supremum or unboundedness in the stated branches. It supplies no explicit fixed-product path. The held-threshold failure join discharges existence of the best Weibull parameter and gives the strict two-face supremum when no finite point attains. The argmax transfer by itself neither constructs the named crossing nor establishes generic uniqueness. The unit-power tie is exact at the stated sample and still leaves the other cardinality clauses. [Author path and contact notes](2026-11-27-lean-endpoint2-report.md#scope-and-source-discrepancies)
- **Nonstandard beta.** The reflected width chart covers either orientation of the fixed endpoint. Its `heldCompatible` predicate excludes negative distances and held-end contact unless the held-end shape is one. Extended contact likelihood distinguishes zero, finite positive and infinite factors, but a fixed-width evaluation does not give the mixed width–shape limits. The free-end-shape no-fit result is on the finite positive domain. The affine theorem applies on normalized data with both observed extremes and fixed shapes at least one, including uniform. Its transported form gives the unique finite pair on that branch. Joint profiling with a free held-end shape still needs compactness, constant concentration, and the strictly downward profile-score crossing. Where the written structural failure text mentions a simultaneous scale and upper-shape collapse, the new Lean result establishes an endpoint spike. [Width chart](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2NonstandardBeta.lean) · [Affine chart](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/Endpoint2NonstandardBetaAffine.lean)
- **Generalized beta.** The free-second-shape result assumes the available sets of first shape and power contain a positive member. That is automatic for each mapped free or positive singleton hold. It proves unboundedness and an empty maximizing set by a subunit spike. It does not exclude other maximizing sequences. The fixed-shape endpoint theorem gives the criterion, including closed contact at `q=1`. Its companion locates stationary maximizers when `q>1`; the two statements leave a joined root-and-contact declaration and proof of the complete failure classification. The fixed-beta-shapes joint criterion treats the constant, spike and regular branches, but its uniqueness clause does not identify the `q=1` optimizer as a contact point. The two `q=1` ridges are exact sets in their respective domains. They leave the other shape branches and maximizing sequences along the ridge open. The constant contact-rate ray fixes the power to one and cannot supply the claimed failure classification across arbitrary held powers. The gamma and moving-power path cells expressly name laws, so their remaining work includes distributional convergence and the applicable joint contact trace.
