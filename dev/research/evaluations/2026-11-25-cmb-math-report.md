# Conway–Maxwell–binomial existence, uniqueness and faces

TASK-355 · 2026-11-25 · source inspected at `f22e71d9`

**Proved.** The declared size domain $0\le N\le 10^7$ gives an attainment rule for all eight hold masks. With size and probability held and dispersion free, an upper-central singleton at an odd size has a two-point limit. The current fitter identifies the limit type and can report the wrong support and likelihood. With size free, the exact sample $(0,6)$ at held $(p,\nu)=(1/2,1)$ has exactly two maximizing sizes, 7 and 8. With sample $(1,2)$ and only probability held, the previously reported numerical crossing is an exact global finite/face tie at one irrational probability. Two adjacent binary64 holds give opposite global outcomes.

**Proved.** For unrestricted integer size the full list of nonzero pointwise limits consists of the laws and subprobabilities below. Those limits can inform bounds on the supported problem. For example, with $(0,4)$ and held $\nu=1$, the unique supported maximum is at $N=10^7$, while the unrestricted problem has an unattained Poisson supremum.

## Supported model

Write $n$ for sample length, $M=\max x_i$, $B=10^7$, $m=\overline{x}$, $\eta=\log(p/(1-p))$, $r=e^\eta$, and $h_N(k)=\log\binom Nk$. The interior weights are $e^{k\eta+\nu h_N(k)}$. Comparisons below can use likelihood products or their logs. A sample is feasible if it is nonempty, consists of nonnegative integers within the applicable size domain, and is compatible with the held endpoints. With $N>0$, $p=0$ requires all zeros and $p=1$ requires all counts equal $N$. With $N=0$ any $p$ gives the zero atom. Infeasibility is an input failure.

### Held size

**Proved, using the fixed-size hull and scalar score results in the dossier.** At $N=0$, each free $p$ ranges over $[0,1]$ and each free $\nu$ over $\mathbb R$. At $N=1$, the law is Bernoulli with $p=m$ when probability is free, or with the feasible held value. Free dispersion ranges over $\mathbb R$. At positive size with held endpoint probability, free dispersion is again arbitrary. For $N\ge2$, the remaining cases are as follows.

| Held coefficients | Exact condition and outcome |
|---|---|
| $N,p,\nu$ | Evaluate the held law. The maximizer set is the single specified point. |
| $N,\nu$ | Always attained and unique in $p$. Use the endpoints for $m=0,N$, otherwise solve $E[X]=m$. |
| $N,p$ with $0<p<1$ | Sample entirely on $\{0,N\}$ gives `EndpointLimit`, with high mass $r^N/(1+r^N)$. Sample entirely on the center set $\{\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil\}$ gives `PointMass` at even size or `AdjacentLimit` at odd size, with upper mass $p$. This includes either central singleton at odd size. All other samples have a unique finite dispersion root $E[h_N(X)]=\overline{h_N(x)}$. |
| $N$ | An endpoint constant is attained with that endpoint probability and arbitrary dispersion. An interior constant gives `PointMass`. Two occupied consecutive counts give `AdjacentLimit`. Two occupied endpoints give `EndpointLimit`. The two-point faces use the empirical proportions. All remaining samples have one finite maximizer, matching both moments. |

Indeed the log normalizer has positive variance or positive definite covariance on the respective minimal charts. The scalar dispersion score spans the two extreme statistics, and the joint log likelihood is coercive for an empirical point strictly inside the hull. The hull criterion uses its **interior** for a finite natural-parameter solution. Boundary edges expose the laws listed in the table. The endpoint probabilities supply the attained endpoint vertices. This is the prior fixed-design proof, expressed as integer predicates. For example, at size 3, $(0,1,3)$ gives a finite joint fit, $(0,3)$ an endpoint limit, and $(1,2)$ an adjacent limit. At size 3 and $p=3/4$, the singleton $(2)$ has supremum $3/4$, from the central law on $\{1,2\}$. Source inspection of [the face construction](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/cmb/mle.hpp#L334) shows it instead passes $\{2,3\}$ and $p=3/4$ to the two-point witness, so it uses likelihood $1/4$. The limit status itself is correct.

### Free size

**Proved.** On all zeros with free probability, the attained set is $N=0$ with any $p$, together with $1\le N\le B$ and $p=0$. Held $p=0$ on all zeros leaves every size maximizing. Held $0<p\le1$ on all zeros fixes $N=0$. A positive constant $k$ with free probability or held $p=1$ attains likelihood one only at $N=k,\ p=1$. Every free dispersion in these attained sets is arbitrary. Held $p=0$ with a positive count, and held $p=1$ with a nonconstant sample, are infeasible.

| Held coefficients | Attainment on the remaining feasible samples |
|---|---|
| $p,\nu$ | Always attained. Each feasible size supplies one value. At $\nu=0$ the normalizer strictly increases with size, so $N=M$ is unique. |
| $\nu$ | Always attained. Each feasible size supplies the unique fixed-design probability fit. At $\nu=0$ the same increasing-normalizer argument holds at the optimum of any larger size, so $N=M$ is unique. For positive or negative held dispersion, the size comparison is separate. |
| None | Any mixed sample on $\{0,1\}$ attains the saturated Bernoulli likelihood at $N=1,p=m$ with arbitrary $\nu$. Its excluded adjacent face has the same value. A mixed sample on $\{0,M\}$ with $M\ge2$ gives `EndpointLimit`, and two occupied adjacent positive counts give `AdjacentLimit`. Both are unattained saturated values. All other samples attain a maximum. |
| $p$ with $0<p<1$ | Apply the finite candidate rule below. If $0<k\le B/2$ is the only count, the size $2k$ point mass gives `PointMass`. With at least three occupied counts, or with no compatible face in the list below, attainment is guaranteed. |

For the all-free two-point exceptions the corresponding edge is available already at size $M$, including adjacent positive counts near the ceiling. Equality to the empirical likelihood requires matching the whole empirical distribution, by relative entropy. The finite interior law puts positive mass at unobserved counts in these cases. A Bernoulli sample with both outcomes has no second finite size that attains its empirical law. Every other nonconstant sample on the all-free mask is interior at every feasible size, by the held-size predicates. Finitely many attained profiles therefore give attainment. An explicit unique all-free example is $(0,1,1,2)$, whose empirical law is $N=2,p=1/2,\nu=1$. Saturation excludes larger sizes, and the fixed-size fit is unique.

**Proved per-input rule for held interior probability.** For each size $M,\ldots,B$ look up the held-size predicate. Put attained maxima in $A$ and the excluded profile maxima in $D$. Each excluded profile is strictly increasing or strictly decreasing in dispersion toward its listed value. For a nonconstant sample the only possible members of $D$ are:

- An endpoint face at $N=M\ge2$ if the occupied counts are $\{0,M\}$. With counts $z,o$ at the lower and upper points its value is $r^{Mo}/(1+r^M)^{z+o}$.
- A center face at $N=2k+1$ if the occupied counts are $k,k+1$, $k\ge1$, and $2k+1\le B$. Its value is $(1-p)^z p^o$.

For a positive constant $k$, the compatible centers have $N=2k$ or $2k\pm1$ when feasible, and the compatible negative-dispersion face has $N=k\ge2$. In the center list, the sizes must be at least 2. Use the held-size weights, including the unobserved member of an odd central pair.

The exact test is $\max A\ge\max D$, with an empty maximum set to zero on the product scale. If it holds, return the entire subset of $A$ at $\max A$, retaining any equal faces as diagnostics. If it fails, return the winning limit or limits. This proves the rule because the scan includes the supremum at every feasible size, with its attainment bit. It reduces equality to comparisons of specified profile values, whose finite roots are unique on their identifiable coordinates. Signed moment brackets and the existing entropy bound can enclose these heights. Exact equality on general free-dispersion transcendental profiles still requires a separate predicate. On the held-$\nu$ masks with binary64 holds an exact, potentially expensive, procedure exists: all $\binom Nk^\nu$ are algebraic, a free odds root solves the polynomial mean equation $\sum_k(k-m)\binom Nk^\nu r^k=0$, and each profile likelihood product is algebraic. Isolate the positive root and compare the likelihood numbers as real algebraic numbers, including endpoint reductions. This also handles both canonical coordinates held. At sizes 2 and 3 with dispersion free, the log-binomial statistic is a single positive log times an indicator. Using its corresponding positive power as the variable gives algebraic moment equations for algebraic held odds and rational sample frequencies. The identifiable solution is isolated by the same strict concavity. These procedures concern exact arithmetic and may exceed the implementation work allowance.

There are simple shortcuts on the held-probability mask:

- If an endpoint or central face matches the empirical proportions, its saturated value wins strictly over every finite model. For the endpoint predicate use $r^M=o/z$. For the center predicate use $p=o/(z+o)$. These are exact tests for dyadic holds. Sample $(0,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2,2)$ at $p=3/4$ gives `EndpointLimit`, and $(1,2,2,2)$ at $p=3/4$ gives `AdjacentLimit`. The latter uses size 3. For the mixed binary sample, size 1 always exists and no excluded finite-design face is compatible.
- For a constant $k>B/2$ and $1/2\le p<1$, the endpoint at $N=k$ strictly wins. For every $k<N\le B$, reflection pairs $k$ with $N-k<k$ and has odds $P(k)/P(N-k)=r^{2k-N}\le r^k$. The two reflected cells have mass strictly less than one at any finite dispersion. Larger feasible sizes have neither a center nor an endpoint face on this sample. This proves `EndpointLimit` for $(5000001)$ at $p=1/2$, for example. At $k=B$ the endpoint is the only size and wins for every interior $p$. The result cannot extend to all $p<1/2$: take $k=5000001,\ p=2^{-30}$. At $N=k+1,\nu=1$, the probability at $k$ divided by the endpoint face value is $(k+1)(1-p)^{k+1}(1+r^k)>(k+1)/2$, by Bernoulli's inequality. The only compatible face therefore loses, and a finite maximum exists.

## Exact comparisons and ties

**Proved, structural and binary64.** The previous [binomial tie theorem](2026-11-25-binom-ties-math-report.md#held-probability-and-free-size) applies on $\nu=1$ with $p$ held: successive size ratios are $(1-p)^n\prod_i(N+1)/(N+1-x_i)$, strictly decreasing if any count is positive. Apply its exact integer ratio test through $B-1$ and use $B$ if all increments are positive. For $(0,6)$ at $p=1/2$ the ratio is $(N+1)/(4(N-5))$, equal to one at $N=7$, above it at size 6 and strictly below it thereafter. Both maxima have likelihood $7/16384$. Return both complete parameter points with `unique = FALSE`. The [current shortcut](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/cmb/mle.hpp#L1470) is limited to constant samples.

**Proved, no equality in the held-$1/2$ binary comparison.** For positive counts $z,o$ on $\{0,1\}$, the dossier gives the only two relevant sizes, 1 and 2. Size 2 wins exactly when $2^o z^z o^o>(z+o)^{z+o}$, and size 1 wins under the reverse inequality. For $N\ge3$, both observed categories have distinct reflected partners, so their total mass is at most $1/2$. Their likelihood is bounded by $2^{-(z+o)}$ times the strictly subunit saturated binary likelihood, strictly below size 1. If the two values were equal, dividing the counts by their gcd and taking the corresponding root would give $2^b a^a b^b=(a+b)^{a+b}$ for coprime positive $a,b$. An odd prime dividing any of $a,b,a+b$ contradicts this identity. Thus all three numbers are powers of two, which by coprimality forces $a=b=1$; this gives $2\ne4$. The winning size is unique. At size 1 the free dispersion is a full line. At size 2 its unique estimate is $\log(2o/z)/\log2$. For $(0,1)$ size 1 wins, and for $(0,1,1,1,1)$ size 2 wins. An overlapping numerical enclosure cannot be an exact equality on this slice.

**Proved, real crossing with exact binary64 neighbors.** For the earlier numerical example $(1,2)$, hold $p\in[2/3,3/4]$ and free both $N,\nu$. At $N=2$, $P(2)/P(0)=r^2$; optimizing $P(1)P(2)$ sets $P(1)=1/2$, or $\nu=\log((1+r^2)/r)/\log2$. Its height is $p^2/[4(p^2+(1-p)^2)]$. At size 3 the central face gives $p(1-p)$ and the finite profile is strictly below that value. At size 4 reflection pairs 1 with 3, with $P(3)\ge P(1)$, so $2P(1)+P(2)\le1$ and the product is at most $1/8$. For every size at least 5 the reflection partners of 1 and 2 are distinct and have at least as much mass, so the product is at most $1/16$. These bounds are strictly below the face throughout the interval since $p(1-p)\ge3/16$. They also cover larger-size excluded limits.

The difference of the two competing heights has sign opposite to $C(p)=4(1-p)(p^2+(1-p)^2)-p$. The derivative of $C=-8p^3+16p^2-13p+4$ is everywhere negative, and its endpoint signs are opposite. Its unique root $p_*$ in the interval is irrational by the rational root test, since a reduced positive root would have numerator dividing 4 and denominator dividing 8. For $C>0$ return `AdjacentLimit`. For $C<0$ return the unique attained size-2 estimate. At $C=0$ retain that certified singleton and the equal excluded face. This is an exact real finite/face equality, with integer observations and an irrational hold. The exact rational signs at `0x1.65881e52d3418p-1` and `0x1.65881e52d3419p-1` give the limit and finite outcomes respectively. **Numerical**, 80 digits: their total log likelihood gaps (finite minus face) are approximately $-1.3481\times10^{-16}$ and $2.3998\times10^{-16}$. A fixed likelihood tolerance cannot select the correct outcome on both inputs.

## Unrestricted integer size

**Proved.** The following table lists all possible nonzero pointwise limits of masses at fixed counts as $N\to\infty$. The zero function is possible except where the specified fixed law path prevents it. A geometric law $G_q$ has masses $(1-q)q^k$, $0<q<1$. The table treats held $0<p<1$; held endpoint probabilities give the zero atom for $p=0$ and mass escaping to infinity for $p=1$.

| Held coefficients | Nonzero pointwise limits |
|---|---|
| None | Positive-dispersion CMP laws, full point and adjacent two-point laws at arbitrary finite counts, and $\alpha G_q,\alpha\delta_0$ with $0<\alpha\le1$. |
| $\nu>0$ | CMP with that dispersion, and $\delta_0$. |
| $\nu=0$ | $G_q$ and $\delta_0$. |
| $\nu<0$ | $\alpha\delta_0$, $0<\alpha\le1$. |
| $p$, free dispersion | For $p<1/2$, $G_q,\delta_0$. For $p=1/2$, $\tfrac12 G_q,\tfrac12\delta_0$. For $p>1/2$, none. |
| $p,\nu$ | For $\nu>0$, none. For $\nu=0$, $G_r$ if $p<1/2$, otherwise none. For $\nu<0$, $\delta_0$ if $p<1/2$, $\tfrac12\delta_0$ if $p=1/2$, otherwise none. |

Proof of exhaustion and reachability. Take a pointwise convergent subsequence and, where needed, subsequences for extended real limits of $\nu$ and $a=\eta+\nu\log N$. The adjacent log ratio at $k$ is
$$
d_{N,k}=a-\nu\log(k+1)+\nu\log(1-k/N).
$$
With finite $\nu$ and finite $a$, positive mass at any one count forces positive mass at every finite count. The shape is then proportional to $e^{ka}/(k!)^\nu$. For positive limiting $\nu$ it is summable with uniform domination on a compact neighborhood of $a,\nu$, so the limit has mass one. For negative limiting $\nu$ it is nonsummable. At zero limiting dispersion, summability requires $a<0$, and domination need not hold when $\nu$ approaches zero from the negative side. If $a\to+\infty$ with finite $\nu$, each fixed count loses to its successor. If $a\to-\infty$, only count zero can keep mass. Nonnegative dispersion gives full mass there by the decreasing ratio bound. This also proves full mass for the $\nu=0$ geometric branch when $a<0$.

When $\nu\to-\infty$, the discrete second difference of log masses at every fixed positive count tends to $+\infty$, so the product of its neighboring masses bounds its squared mass times a factor tending to infinity. Hence every positive count loses its mass. When $\nu\to+\infty$, consecutive log ratios decrease by a quantity diverging to infinity at each fixed count. If $P(k)$ stays bounded away from zero, $d_{N,k}$ is bounded above and, for $k>0$, $d_{N,k-1}$ is bounded below. Monotonicity bounds the entire tail beyond $k+1$ by a geometric tail with ratio tending to zero. Counts below $k-1$ vanish by the lower bound and the diverging ratio differences. The masses at $k-1$ and $k+1$ cannot both stay positive. This gives a proper point or adjacent limit; to attain any adjacent weights, choose $a$ to fix the log ratio at that pair, and for a point choose the adjacent ratios on either side to diverge with opposite signs (only the right ratio at zero is needed). The positive fixed-dispersion CMP and exact $\nu=0$ paths are those in the earlier holds analysis.

For free probability, to reach $\alpha G_q$ with $0<\alpha<1$, choose $a=\log q<0$, $W=1/\alpha-1$, $\eta=\log W/N$, and $\nu=(a-\eta)/\log N$. At fixed $k$ the unnormalized left weights tend to $q^k$, and the weights at $N-k$ tend to $Wq^k$ by reflection. Up to $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ from each endpoint, the ratios toward the middle are bounded by $e^{a/3}<1$ for large $N$, from the displayed formula and the reflected odds. Log weights are convex for negative $\nu$, so the central block weighs at most $N$ times the larger of its two bordering weights, which is exponentially small in $\sqrt N$. This proves the claimed normalization. For a fixed negative dispersion $\alpha\delta_0$ follows with the same $\eta$ and fixed $\nu<0$. To see the interior sum vanish, use a fixed integer $K>2/|\nu|$. In the central block $[K,N-K]$, the binomial coefficient is at least $\binom NK$, and the odds factor is bounded by $\max(1,W)$. The finitely many other interior weights tend to zero. Full mass follows by using $p=0$.

For fixed $\eta<0$, a finite $a$-limit requires $\nu\to0$ and gives the geometric law when $a<0$. If $\nu<0$, domination follows from $e^{k\eta}\binom Nk^\nu\le e^{k\eta}$; with $\nu\ge0$ use the decreasing ratios for negative $a$. Every $a<0$ is reachable by $\nu=(a-\eta)/\log N$, including when $a<\eta$. If $a\to-\infty$ at held $\eta<0$, the negative-dispersion weight bound gives full mass at zero. At $\eta=0$, reflection gives two equal blocks. For negative $\nu$ and $a\to a_0<0$, the same $\sqrt N$ argument proves half a geometric law; for $a\to-\infty$, it proves half an atom. For $\nu\ge0,\ \eta=0$, every fixed count is bounded in mass by a reciprocal growing number of cells between it and the center. For held $\eta>0$, the right endpoint dominates every fixed count when $\nu\le0$ or when $\nu\log N$ stays bounded. If $\nu>0$ and $\nu\log N\to\infty$, each fixed count loses to its successor. These cases cover fixed odds, including the restrictions imposed by additionally holding dispersion.

**Proved decision in terms of profiles.** Products for a fixed sample are continuous under pointwise mass convergence. Diagonal subsequences of near-maximizers over sizes tending to infinity show that the limsup of the size profiles is the best value $I$ over the table (zero if every path has zero likelihood). Each reachable value gives the reverse inequality. With $m>0$, the held-$p$ geometric branch maximizes at $q=m/(1+m)$, and the half-mass branch subtracts $n\log 2$ on the log scale. With free probability and held positive dispersion solve the CMP mean equation. With both canonical coefficients free and sample span at least two, proper CMP or geometric weakly dominates every compatible subprobability. To choose between them, at the matched geometric compute $E_{\rm emp}[-\log(X!)]-E_{G_q}[-\log(X!)]$. If this is nonpositive, concavity gives the geometric maximum, uniquely even at equality of the score by strict concavity. If positive, there is a better positive-dispersion CMP maximum. For attainment of that CMP maximum, use the adjacent-ratio argument with $d_k=a-\nu\log(k+1)$: a sequence leaving compact parameter sets can only retain positive likelihood here at a proper geometric law, since point and adjacent limits cannot cover the sample. A finite optimum is unique by positive covariance. On a free-$p,\nu$ sample with constant or adjacent data the reachable point or adjacent laws give the saturated value directly.

Compare the finite-design candidates, with their attainment bits, and $I$. If some finite-design height strictly exceeds $I$, only finitely many sizes can compete with it, and the best of those sizes decides attainment. The dossier's truncation and block inequalities are constructive sufficient covers on the routes where they close. If all finite-design heights are at or below $I$, the unrestricted supremum is $I$, and it is attained exactly when an attained finite-design candidate equals $I$. A proof that no finite size reaches $I$ is essential for an unrestricted escape stop. For the supported model the same large-size inequalities can exclude blocks inside its domain, but the value of a path to infinity is outside the set of feasible lower witnesses.

**Proved example distinguishing the domains.** For $(0,4)$ and $\nu=1$, the finite probability optimum is $2/N$. The difference between its **mean** log likelihood and the Poisson value of mean 2 is
$$
\frac12\sum_{j=1}^3\log(1-j/N)+(N-2)\log(1-2/N)+2
=\sum_{s\ge1}\frac{2^{s+1}/(s+1)-(1^s+2^s+3^s)/2}{s N^s}.
$$
For $s\ge1$, the integral of $u^s$ on $[0,2]$ is strictly less than $1+2^s$, by the unit right endpoint sums. Also $3^s\ge1+2^s$ by the binomial theorem. Every coefficient in this convergent expansion for $N\ge4$ is negative. The finite profiles therefore increase strictly to the Poisson value. Return the unique size-$B$ fit with probability $2/B$ on the stated domain, or a proved Poisson limit if size is deliberately unrestricted. The numerical total log likelihood gap at $B$ is about $2.00000043\times10^{-7}$.

## What this changes for the fitter

- Dispatch the exact finite-design faces by integer counts and use their actual support to compute witnesses. In particular the held-probability central witness at odd size always uses the lower and upper central indices, even with only the upper one observed. Extend the held-probability saturation checks to all matching odds, and the positive-constant endpoint shortcut above half the size ceiling to held $p\ge1/2$, with the sole-feasible-size case for all interior $p$.
- Use the declared size domain as the comparison domain. Drop infinite-size witnesses from its feasible lower bound. Search or bound every supported block that can still win. A fit at the declared ceiling requires evidence, as in $(0,4)$ with held $\nu=1$. For a separately defined unrestricted route, classify and compare the reachable infinity laws before using a limit status.
- Use exact size increments on the held-$p,\nu=1$ submodel. Use the integer comparison for held $p=1/2$ on $\{0,1\}$ and the signed cubic on $(1,2)$ with $p\in[2/3,3/4]$. Report a complete typed maximizer set for proved multiplicities, including flat coordinates. At proved finite/face equality, retain the attained estimate and attach the face. Strictly winning excluded faces yield the specific limit status after the finite cover closes.
- Compare separate coefficient and height enclosures at each surviving size. Overlapping heights without a proof of equality stay unresolved. If an accuracy-checked search estimate survives and only its global certificate allowance expires, return it with `certified = FALSE, unique = NA` and retain the bounds. If a feasible point or an in-domain face strictly refutes it, return a checked surviving estimate or a proved limit as appropriate. Preserve failure handling for searches that never produce an accuracy-checked candidate.
- Apply the local accuracy test at the final represented $p,\nu$ after any refinement. Use the fixed-design moment system and covariance or signed root inclusion and check the likelihood at the represented point. Keep size exact. The two-point witness fix and the new comparisons belong on the fitter side. They require no change to the probability kernels.

## Open

- The general held-interior-probability, free-dispersion predicate is an exhaustive finite comparison. A practical effective equality test for arbitrary transcendental profile heights, and a cheaper rule for the remaining $p<1/2,\ B/2<k<B$ constant samples, are open. A proof of strict ordering, algebraic reduction on wider slices, or exact tie predicates would resolve equality cases that numerical brackets alone cannot settle.
- General cross-size cardinality with dispersion free remains open beyond the exact slices and the per-input comparisons. Exact rational-held algebraic elimination at fixed dispersion terminates in principle. Its cost for large binary64 denominators and many designs needs a practical strategy.
- Exhausting the unrestricted limit list supplies $I$, but a practical proof that every finite size is below or equal to $I$ on general positive-dispersion CMP comparisons remains open. It requires a signed tail bound and a finite cover, or a direct strict inequality. The $\nu=1$ Poisson example supplies such a comparison on its stated sample.
- Fresh public-output checks for the current base at tighter tolerances, especially the post-refinement path and severe endpoint projection, require an implementation run. The archived replay and exact representation example establish the scoped results above.
