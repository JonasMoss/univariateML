## 1. Triangular: full likelihood geometry

**Reference and convention.** extraDistr supplies `dtriang`, `ptriang`, `qtriang`, `rtriang`, with endpoints a<b and mode a≤c≤b. Use the continuous one-sided density at an endpoint-mode: f(a)=2/(b−a) when c=a, and f(b)=2/(b−a) when c=b. This density version matters for sample-dependent endpoint fits. For a<c<b,

$$f(x)=\frac{2}{b-a}\begin{cases}(x-a)/(c-a),&a\le x\le c,\\(b-x)/(b-c),&c<x\le b.\end{cases}$$

**Literature scope.** [Oliver (1972), DOI 10.2307/2682867](https://doi.org/10.2307/2682867), establishes the order-statistic oddity for the mode with known endpoints. [Huang and Shen (2007), DOI 10.1016/j.jspi.2006.06.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jspi.2006.06.044), explicitly restrict their displayed model to support [0,1]; it is not a general three-free-parameter existence theorem. [van Dorp and Kotz (2002), DOI 10.1111/1467-9884.00299](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9884.00299), embeds the triangular in the two-sided power family (power 2). Their [2004 book, *Beyond Beta*, DOI 10.1142/5720](https://doi.org/10.1142/5720), develops bounded-support likelihood methods; the publisher's sample chapter was blocked. The author's accessible [course slides](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~dorpjr/BEYONDBETA/1%20-%20BEYOND%20BETA%20-%20TSP%20FRAMEWORK.pdf) give family context. The existence proof and convex endpoint algorithm below are **derived here**, not attributed to those inaccessible portions. Freeing the two-sided-power exponent would change the problem; no uniform limit arises by moving only a,b,c.

**Boundedness and attainment, all three free (derived).** Let m=min x, M=max x, R=M−m>0. Any positive-likelihood fit has a≤m≤M≤b, hence width w=b−a≥R and f(x_i)≤2/w. Therefore L≤(2/R)^n: no three-parameter divergence for nonconstant data. Also L→0 if w→∞. Choose any positive-likelihood fit; all competitors above that level have bounded width, and therefore bounded endpoints and mode. On that compact set the chosen density is upper semicontinuous, including at endpoint-mode corners: an approaching endpoint factor is at most 2/w, the value assigned at the corner. Thus L attains a positive maximum. Constant data instead allow w↓0 around their common value and L→∞. Excluding c=a,b artificially removes legitimate members and can remove attainment.

**Can endpoints equal sample extremes?** If c stays strictly above m, sending a↑m sends the factor at m to zero; similarly b↓M is impossible at a positive maximum with c<M. Consequently an attained a=m requires c=a=m; an attained b=M requires c=b=M. Both endpoints cannot simultaneously equal distinct sample extremes at positive likelihood. If a and c jointly approach m, the factor at m is

$$\frac{2}{w}\frac{m-a}{c-a}\le\frac{2}{w},$$

so such a path cannot beat the endpoint-mode member at the same limiting width. This is why “estimate the endpoints by min/max, then estimate the mode” is incorrect. In particular `bound: lower/upper` must **not** be attached to general triangular endpoints.

**The endpoint-mode fits are attained submodels.** On c=a, f(x)=2(b−x)/(b−a)^2 for a≤x≤b. Increasing a up to m improves every observation, so a=c=m, while b>M solves

$$\sum_i\frac1{b-x_i}=\frac{2n}{b-m}.$$

Its unique root follows by putting v=b−m: the equivalent equation ∑(x_i−m)/(v−(x_i−m))=n has a strictly decreasing left side from ∞ to 0. Reflect for c=b=M, a<m. These are decreasing/increasing triangular laws, **not** uniform or another `mlX` escape. Compare them as members of the fitted family. For x=(0,1), the only mode candidates are the extremes; the two global fits are (a,b,c)=(0,1.5,0) and (−0.5,1,1), both L=16/27. This is an explicit failure of global uniqueness with all parameters free.

**Why enumerate the mode (derived).** With a,b fixed, between observations let k=# {x_i≤c}. Apart from terms independent of c,

$$\ell(c)=-k\log(c-a)-(n-k)\log(b-c),\qquad \ell''(c)=\frac{k}{(c-a)^2}+\frac{n-k}{(b-c)^2}>0.$$

Each gap is convex, so its maximum is at an observed value. In the tails outside [m,M], moving c toward the nearest observation improves L. Hence every global maximum has an equivalent candidate c among the **distinct observations**, including m,M. For distinct data with known endpoints, observation x_(j) can be a local maximum only when

$$\frac{j-1}{n}\le\frac{x_{(j)}-a}{b-a}\le\frac{j}{n}.$$

There can be several local maxima; x=(0.2,0.8), a=0,b=1 gives equal global maxima at both observations, separated by a minimum. Thus a single smooth scalar optimizer is not a valid mode solver. Ties require multiplicities in the one-sided score conditions; return a documented maximizer and flag equal optima rather than claiming uniqueness.

**The endpoint subproblem is better than an arbitrary smooth 2-D search (derived).** Fix c with observations on both sides. Put u=c−a, v=b−c, α=1/u, γ=1/v; let d_i=c−x_i for x_i<c and e_i=x_i−c for x_i>c. Then

$$\ell_c(\alpha,\gamma)=n\log\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{\alpha+\gamma}+\sum_{x_i<c}\log(1-\alpha d_i)+\sum_{x_i>c}\log(1-\gamma e_i).$$

The domain is the rectangle 0<α<1/(c−m), 0<γ<1/(M−c). Each log-affine summand is concave. Moreover log α+log γ−log(α+γ) is strictly concave: its Hessian has negative diagonal entries and determinant 2/[αγ(α+γ)^2]>0. Thus there is exactly **one** endpoint solution for each interior candidate c, obtainable by safeguarded Newton or a concave one-dimensional profile. At c=m or M use the analytic endpoint-mode reduction above. This also proves uniqueness for a fixed interior mode, although it does not imply uniqueness across mode candidates.

**Exact algorithm and cost.** Sort and compress ties; enumerate each distinct c; solve its unique concave endpoint problem or boundary root; compare log likelihoods, preserving ties. This is exact as a global reduction, with scalar/convex solves computed to tolerance. No multi-start BFGS is needed. With K distinct values, a naive pass costs O(n log n+KnT), T endpoint iterations, O(n) storage: worst-case O(n²T), with fixed 2-D linear algebra. Histogram aggregation replaces n by K in each evaluation. A nested scalar profile can maximize α uniquely at each γ before maximizing the concave profile. Establishing an iteration bound in requested accuracy is separate from the finite-enumeration result.

**Known endpoints: especially inexpensive.** Sort once; prefix sums of log(x−a) and suffix sums of log(b−x) give every candidate likelihood in O(1), making the scan O(n) after O(n log n) sorting. The local-max inequalities can prune candidates. This is the useful PERT-style case when elicited endpoints are treated as fixed; fixing them is a statistical assumption, not estimating them by observed extremes. An endpoint observation forces the corresponding endpoint-mode face; observing both known endpoints gives zero likelihood throughout this density family. Such incompatible data need an explicit diagnostic.

**Other useful fixed subsets.** Make **a,b,c individually fixable**, with joint constraints a<b and a≤c≤b checked after combining fixed and fitted values. A known interior mode leaves the unique convex endpoint problem, O(nT), without enumeration. If the known mode lies at or beyond one sample extreme, all observations are on one side and the opposite unused side shrinks to zero: a=c for c≤m, or b=c for c≥M, followed by a scalar width root. One fixed endpoint plus free c uses the same mode scan and a one-coordinate concave endpoint solve. Fixed a,b,c is evaluation only. Known mode and one known endpoint leave a scalar problem; known endpoints and fixed mode leave no fitted coefficients. Fixed values can make the data incompatible, even though the fully free model always fits nonconstant data.

**Asymptotics: the order statistic does not imply an n-rate (derived).** For known a,b and true a<c<b, the score is −1/(c−a) on the left and +1/(b−c) on the right. It has mean zero and Fisher information I_cc=1/[(c−a)(b−c)]. The square-root density is differentiable in quadratic mean; crossings of the continuous kink contribute only third-order squared-Hellinger remainder. The ordinary local likelihood expansion therefore gives

$$\sqrt n(\hat c-c)\ \Rightarrow\ N\bigl(0,(c-a)(b-c)\bigr).$$

Although curvature is positive *inside individual gaps*, the downward score jumps at observations supply the negative macroscopic curvature. The selected order statistic has a random rank; nearest-observation spacing O_p(1/n) is smaller than estimation error. At true c=a or b the regular information formula is inapplicable: the endpoint-mode local experiment has scale 1/n (the near-mode density is positive), giving non-Gaussian boundary behavior. With **unknown endpoints**, scores contain 1/(x−a) and 1/(b−x), with logarithmically divergent second moments at a true interior triangle; ordinary joint finite-information inference is invalid. The expected endpoint scale is (n log n)^−1/2 and the mode retains the root-n scale, since the endpoint/mode cross-information is finite. This last joint-rate statement is a local asymptotic prediction from the score integrals, **not a verified joint-MLE limit theorem**; validate before exposing Wald standard errors. No inference implementation is needed to supply the likelihood fitter.

**Reference edge checks (checked).** At a=0,b=1 and c∈{0,0.4,1}, density agrees with the piecewise formula, including d(0;0,1,0)=2 and d(1;0,1,1)=2. At ordinary endpoints the density is zero. Over p={0,10^−12,0.1,0.5,0.9,1−10^−12,1}, p(q(p)) errors were ≤2×10^−16 **except** `ptriang(0,0,1,0)=NaN`, so p(q(0)) also fails at c=a. Correct value: 0. The source takes its x≤c branch and forms log(0)−log(0); the reflected upper corner works because x≥b is handled first. Small interior p at c=a is otherwise accurate in this check. Source: [triangular implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/triangular-distribution.cpp). Add an upstream fix or the repository's runtime-checked reference workaround before exposing this attained face. Scale checks with a=0,b=s,c=.4s, s∈{10^−150,10^150}, gave the correct mode log-density log(2)−log(s), and q(p)/s agreed across scales at p={.1,.5,.9}; larger magnitudes were not exhaustively tested.

**Recommendation:** add now, M for known-endpoint fitting and L for the full fixed-subset/boundary-aware API. The full optimizer is mathematically tractable; the main work is endpoint conventions, constraints, ties, metadata, and validation of nonsmooth scores. Neither estimated endpoint is generically a sample extreme, and no arbitrary “padding” of the observed range is an ML solution.

## 2. Poisson–inverse Gaussian

**Parameterization and literature.** actuar mixes Pois(Y) with Y∼IG(μ,λ), where μ=`mean`, λ=`shape` and φ=`dispersion`=1/λ; Var(X)=μ+μ³/λ. These are **two**, not three, independent parameters; the R wrapper uses dispersion when supplied. `d/p/q/rpoisinvgauss` and aliases `d/p/q/rpig` exist. [Willmot (1987), DOI 10.1080/03461238.1987.10413823](https://doi.org/10.1080/03461238.1987.10413823), is the relevant two-parameter treatment. [Stein, Zucchini and Juritz (1987), DOI 10.1080/01621459.1987.10478520](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1987.10478520), concerns the larger three-parameter Sichel family. Bibliographic identities were verified; their full likelihood theorems were not accessible, so the following reduction is derived independently.

**Exact 1-D reduction (derived).** Put A=2+λ/μ² and z=√(λA). Integrating the Poisson kernel against the IG density gives

$$p_k=\frac{\sqrt{2\lambda/\pi}}{k!}\exp(\lambda/\mu)\left(\frac\lambda A\right)^{(k-1/2)/2}K_{k-1/2}(z).$$

Set t=λ/μ, so λ=(z²−t²)/2, 0<t<z. At fixed z the t-dependent log likelihood is nt+S log[(z²−t²)/2], S=∑x. Its second derivative is negative for S>0; the unique maximizer satisfies λ=t·x̄, i.e. **μ̂=x̄**. Thus global fitting reduces exactly to λ>0 with μ=x̄. This identity need not hold when λ is fixed: then holding z fixed is no longer an allowed variation. Start λ at μ³/(v−μ) when empirical v=mean((x−μ)²)>μ, otherwise start near the Poisson face; bracket all scalar peaks and compare the exact `mlpois` log likelihood. No global uniqueness theorem is claimed.

**Existence and limits (derived).** Along this profile, λ↑∞ gives Pois(x̄); λ↓0 gives p_0→1 and p_k→0 for every fixed k>0. Thus for any sample containing a positive count the profile tends to −∞ at λ↓0 and has either a finite maximum or its supremum at Poisson. Expanding at δ=1/λ=0 gives

$$\ell(\delta)-\ell(0)=\frac{n\mu}{2}(v-\mu)\delta+O(\delta^2).$$

Consequently **v>μ guarantees a finite maximum** beating Poisson. Underdispersion makes Poisson a local boundary maximum; the report does **not** establish that v≤μ rules out every separated interior maximum. A correct first implementation can avoid that unproved shortcut by searching the full scalar profile and comparing Poisson globally. For x=(1,…,1), Jensen's inequality p_1=E[Ye^−Y]≤e^−1 proves the Poisson supremum globally, giving a positive-probability nonexistence event. Equality v=μ requires more than the first-order expansion. All-zero data have point-mass supremum; mean zero is rejected by the reference density, so diagnose separately.

The PGF is exp{(λ/μ)[1−√(1+2μ²(1−s)/λ)]}. Besides Poisson and δ_0 it has the **proper infinite-mean Poisson–Lévy face** μ↑∞, λ fixed, PGF exp{−√(2λ(1−s))}; actuar accepts mean=Inf. It cannot be the maximizing escape with both parameters free and finite positive sample mean: at fixed z, the finite t maximizer above strictly improves the t=0 face. With mean fixed finite, that face is unavailable; with shape fixed and mean free, it must be compared. Other non-tight paths can lose mass at infinity; do not call them proper Hellinger limits. At fixed μ, shape→0 gives δ_0, **not** another heavy-tailed proper law.

## 3. Discrete Weibull, type I

**Reference and history.** extraDistr `d/p/q/rdweibull` uses `shape1`=q∈(0,1), `shape2`=β>0, support {0,1,…}, p_k=q^(k^β)−q^((k+1)^β). This is [Nakagawa and Osaki (1975), DOI 10.1109/TR.1975.5214915](https://doi.org/10.1109/TR.1975.5214915). The later [Vila, Nakano and Saulo (2019), DOI 10.1080/02331888.2018.1550645](https://doi.org/10.1080/02331888.2018.1550645), discusses conditional uniqueness; no joint uniqueness theorem from it is assumed here.

**Profile and finite existence (derived).** Write t=−log q, a_k=k^β and d_k=(k+1)^β−k^β. Then

$$\ell(t,\beta)=\sum_i[-t a_{x_i}+\log(1-e^{-t d_{x_i}})],\quad \ell_t=\sum_i[-a_{x_i}+d_{x_i}/\operatorname{expm1}(t d_{x_i})].$$

For each β>0 this is strictly concave in t. If any count is positive, its score decreases from +∞ to a negative limit, giving a unique t root. Profile β by a scalar global search; aggregate repeated counts and use `log(-expm1(-t*d))`. For β=1, p_k=(1−q)q^k is the **attained geometric member**, q̂=x̄/(1+x̄), equivalent to `mlgeom` success probability 1/(1+x̄). No escape declaration is needed at β=1.

**All parameter escapes and sample characterization (derived).** As β↓0, positive-count probabilities vanish uniformly over t: for k≥1, d_k/a_k=exp(β log(1+1/k))−1→0 and e^−t a_k(1−e^−t d_k)≤(d_k/a_k)/e. At fixed t this leaves mass 1−e^−t at zero and e^−t escaping to infinity: a defective pointwise limit, not a proper mixture on the integers. With β bounded away from zero and infinity, t↑∞ (q↓0) gives δ_0, and t↓0 (q↑1) loses all mass to infinity.

As β↑∞, coordinate with t so that t(k+1)^β→h∈(0,∞): S(k+1)→e^−h, S(k)→1 for k≥1, S(k+2)→0. Hence **every adjacent two-point law on {k,k+1}** is a proper closure member, with mass e^−h at k+1; k=0 uses t→h. Other threshold paths yield point masses at any integer, or non-tight paths. Fixed q with β↑∞ yields the Bernoulli law on {0,1}, mass q at 1. These exhaust tight proper boundary limits by considering the threshold t^(-1/β).

Therefore a nonconstant sample has a finite MLE **iff max x−min x≥2**. For a range-one sample the adjacent two-point face attains the saturated empirical likelihood; every finite parameter assigns mass elsewhere and has strictly smaller likelihood. A constant sample escapes to its point mass. For range ≥2 every escaping parameter sequence makes at least one observed probability tend to zero; the remaining positive likelihood level set is compact, proving finite attainment. This is a complete escape characterization, not a numerical heuristic. It also gives positive probability of nonexistence at every finite n. No joint uniqueness guarantee is needed for the profile search.

**Reference audit (checked).** At (q,β,k)=(.7,1.3,4), log pmf agrees with the stable expression within 5×10^−16. At (1−10^−12,.2,10), extraDistr gives −31.1200294720 versus −31.1210366293, a log error ≈0.001007; at (.999,2,1000) it gives −Inf versus finite **−1000.6454341855**. The [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/discrete-weibull-distribution.cpp) subtracts powers then logs. Also `pdweibull(.5,.7,1)=.4143379814`, but the integer-support CDF must equal `pdweibull(0,.7,1)=.3`: the argument is not floored. Ordinary generalized-inverse tests passed for (.7,1.3) and (.2,10); at (.999999,.2), quantiles reach 10^36, beyond exact integer representation, and the inequalities failed. Do not promise distinguishable adjacent counts beyond 2^53.

**Recommendation:** add now, M, once stable external log-pmf/CDF behavior is available or a runtime-checked reference fix is accepted. Fit β with the unique inner t root, starting at the geometric fit; cost O(K·T_inner·T_outer). Fixing **β** is especially useful and gives a single strict-concavity solve; fixing q leaves a scalar β search and changes which two-point faces are reachable. With β fixed finite and any positive observation, the finite t MLE exists; all-zero samples still escape. With q fixed, β↓0 cannot maximize samples containing positives, and the only non-point tight β↑∞ face is Bernoulli(q). A shifted adjacent two-point escape has no direct existing `mlX` identified here: name it honestly; only k=0 maps to `mlbinom(size=1)`, with success probability equal to the observed fraction of ones.

## 4. Huber density

**Reference and model (derived from the definition).** extraDistr `d/p/q/rhuber(mu,sigma,epsilon)` uses c=epsilon>0, σ>0, z=(x−μ)/σ, ρ_c(z)=z²/2 for |z|≤c and c|z|−c²/2 otherwise:

$$f(x)=\frac{e^{-\rho_c(z)}}{\sigma Z(c)},\qquad Z(c)=\sqrt{2\pi}[2\Phi(c)-1]+\frac{2e^{-c^2/2}}c.$$

**Fixed epsilon: correct joint ML, not arbitrary robust scale.** Its equations are ∑ψ_c(z_i)=0 and ∑z_iψ_c(z_i)=n, where ψ clips at ±c. Conditional on σ, μ is exactly the Huber M-estimator at that scale; jointly ML must use the second equation, not MAD or the usual consistency-corrected robust-scale equation. Set η=1/σ and h=μ/σ: ℓ=n log η−∑ρ_c(ηx_i−h)−n log Z(c) is concave in (η,h), strictly concave in η. For every nonconstant sample it attains a finite maximum. Location can have an interval of maximizers when a balanced sample lies entirely in the linear tails; do not promise joint strict uniqueness. Constant samples have unbounded likelihood as σ↓0. A concave inner solver or profiling location and solving the scale score costs O(nT); starting at median/MAD or the normal fit is sufficient with collapse guards.

**Free epsilon and correct faces (derived).** As c↑∞ with σ fixed, Z→√(2π) and f→N(μ,σ²): **use `mlnorm`** if that face wins. As c↓0 with b=σ/c fixed, cZ(c)→2 and ρ_c((x−μ)/(bc))→|x−μ|/b, giving Laplace(μ,b): **use `mllaplace`**. Taking c↓0 with σ fixed instead spreads mass to infinity; it is not a fixed-scale Laplace limit. No extra heavy-tail/shape-zero divergence appears: after reparameterizing by b for small c, the boundary is an ordinary Laplace location-scale law. Nonconstant data have bounded likelihood, with attainment after adding normal and Laplace faces.

**Finite MLE exists with positive probability, but not always.** For two distinct observations, translate/rescale them to (−1,1). Symmetry and concavity of the fixed-c inner fit give μ=0 and σ=1 for c≥1, σ=c for c<1. For c≥1 its log likelihood is −2 log Z(c)−1, strictly below the normal optimum because Z(c)>√(2π). For c<1 it is −2 log[cZ(c)]+c²−2; differentiation shows it increases up to c=1 and remains below the normal supremum. Thus **every distinct n=2 sample has no finite free-c MLE**, and a finite large c can look numerically identical to normal without attaining it mathematically. Conversely a true finite-c Huber law differs from both faces; KL separation plus sufficiently rich finite empirical approximations yields open sets with an interior maximum, hence positive probability. This is not a theorem that every sample with n≥3 has a finite MLE.

Laplace must also be compared: at its median fit, if no residual is zero, a sufficiently small Huber core avoids the observations and actually improves its likelihood, since 2e^(c²/2)/(cZ(c))>1. With an observation at the fitted median, its central factor instead incurs an O(c²) loss while nonzero residuals gain only O(c⁴); Laplace can therefore be a local profile maximum. This shows why selecting a face from kurtosis or one-sided optimizer drift is insufficient; global scalar profiling is still required.

**Checks versus claims.** Direct-R normalization/piecewise log-density checks at c={10^−8,.2,1.345,10,40}, μ=0,σ=1 and x={−2,0,2,50} agreed within 2×10^−16. For p={10^−8,.1,.5,.9,1−10^−8}, p(q(p)) errors were ≤2×10^−16 at the same c values. A separate extreme-tail check found **`phuber(c(-50,50),epsilon=40)` returns `(NaN,NaN)`**. The [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/huber-distribution.cpp) multiplies overflowing `exp(c*c/2)` by an underflowed tail exponential. This needs a reference fix even though its log density and central p/q tests passed. A small profile probe using 80 midpoint quantiles of Huber(c=.8) found c≈.84446, ℓ≈−156.11340, beating normal (−159.24083) and Laplace (−156.36835); this supports interior existence, not a certified optimum. On (−10,−.1,0,.1,10), a small-c scan approached Laplace from below; it does not prove that Laplace is the global face for that sample.

**Recommendation:** fixed epsilon now (M), free epsilon later (L). Profile c with the globally solved concave location-scale fit; compare both analytic face fits and handle finite-c numerical plateaus. Make epsilon fixable with default `NULL` only if free fitting is implemented; a first restricted fitter should declare an explicit fixed tuning constant, e.g. 1.345. μ and σ can also be fixable. Holding σ prevents the rescaled Laplace face, while the normal face remains; holding epsilon prevents both shape escapes. Keep limit declarations conditional on which coordinates are free. No inaccessible literature theorem is being used for Huber existence here.

## Implementation order and fixed-parameter machinery

1. **Triangular first**, especially known a,b, then the certified endpoint solves. Use `fixable: true` for a,b,c; joint domain validation, exact closed faces, tied maximizers and nonsmooth-mode validation are essential. Do not misuse `bound` for a,b. The quality harness needs an explicit nonsmooth-mode exception rather than an ordinary Hessian certificate.
2. **Fixed-epsilon Huber**, then discrete Weibull once reference defects are addressed. Huber needs epsilon fixed/optional, a scale-dependent location solve, and free-coordinate face eligibility. Discrete Weibull needs fixable β and optionally q, automatic holding during bootstrap, correct coefficient names/`r.args`, and the exact sample-range escape test for the fully free case.
3. **PIG after numerical repair**, then free-epsilon Huber. PIG needs one shape/dispersion representation, fixable shape/mean, and solver dispatch when the exact mean reduction no longer applies; free Huber needs normal/Laplace comparisons under each fixed subset. For all four, apply the schema's `fixed` attribute to logLik degrees of freedom, AIC/BIC and bootstrap refits; list unattained proper faces under `limits`, naming only existing package models. The current `limits.fixed` annotation alone does not express every “these parameters must be free” combination: adapters must enforce those eligibility rules explicitly.
