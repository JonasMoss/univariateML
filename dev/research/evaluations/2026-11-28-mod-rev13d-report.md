## Scope and review standard

The retained event is the closed nominal window intersected with support at the trial coefficients. With sample minimum $m$, maximum $M$, and fixed cuts $A,B$ where applicable, the tight windows are $[m,B]$, $[A,M]$ and $[m,M]$. The mass inequality holds at each coefficient vector, including along sequences of moving thresholds. Supremizing over those same vectors and using feasibility of the tight pair gives equality on positive-mass slices. Repeated extremes enter the unchanged numerator with their multiplicities. At a positive finite winner the mass equality decides the complete nominal cut fiber at that vector. At a finite positive supremum the removed mass fraction tends to zero along any maximizing sequence, so the loose and tight conditional laws approach one another in total variation. At an infinite supremum the fraction can stay positive, which affects the failure grade. [Joint profiling and null-slice proof](2026-11-28-trunc-est4-math-report.md#joint-profiling-lemma)

Each coefficient comparison uses the realized tight window as fixed data in the linked known-window proof. I checked the transform and its cut order, the observation Jacobian, the admissible domains under the holds, and the integrability conditions on the resulting bounded intervals or rays. The scalar comparisons use the full statistic mean range. The two-coordinate gamma comparison retains inward equality and uses coercivity for distinct observations even at the cuts. The stationary-height comparisons allow a feasible candidate to equal the excluded supremum, because any higher positive height then confines a maximizing sequence to the feasible region. Each applicable joined criterion, failure and uniqueness row has a reviewed written known-window predecessor. The additional case rows have separate support, density, tilt or divergence arguments. ## Proof errors and scope corrections

### Inverse Pareto uses two complementary coordinates

The held-scale sections display $z=x/(S+x)$ and correctly derive a tilt with statistic $log z$, then state the criterion using $log(1-z)$. The known-window proof uses $w=S/(S+x)=1-z$ instead, so its $log(1-w)$ agrees with the forward derivation. The three submitted criteria now use $log z$ and the carrier $dz/z$. For $S=1$, sample $(1,2,2)$ and tight window $[1,2]$ in any design, write $z_1=1/2$ and $z_2=2/3$. The zero-tilt mean is $(log z_1+log z_2)/2$. The correct empirical mean is strictly above it and strictly below $log z_2$, so the unique finite fit exists. Substituting the empirical mean of $log(1-z)$ into the displayed forward test puts the mean below the zero-tilt threshold. The two inequalities reduce respectively to $(z_1z_2^2)^2>(z_1z_2)^3$ and $((1-z_1)(1-z_2)^2)^2<(z_1z_2)^3$. [Estimated-window section](2026-11-28-trunc-est4-math-report.md#invpareto-scale-truncatelower-estimated) · [Reciprocal proof](2026-11-27-trunc-gb2-math-report.md#invpareto-scale-truncateknown)

### Fixed shapes remove zero-shape and gamma ends

The generic beta-prime face wording in the inverse Pareto, paralogis and inverse paralogis held-shape rows names zero-shape and gamma ends. With both beta shapes and the power fixed, the only free coefficient is scale. In $u=y^d$ with $y=x$ for paralogis and $y=1/x$ for the two inverse families, the kernel is $(1+t u)^{-1-q}$. At $t\downarrow0$ it approaches the constant kernel on bounded $u$ windows. At $t\to\infty$, after multiplication by $t^{1+q}$, it approaches $u^{-1-q}$, integrable precisely when the lower $u$ cut is positive. Fatou eliminates positive heights on the other windows. The scalar criterion compares against these two heights and retains equality. For all-free inverse Pareto, the lower beta shape stays fixed at one. The reachable list then also includes the upper-shape-zero edge and the broad exponential, while the narrow end remains a reciprocal power. The revised rows state the hold-specific lists. [Fixed-power derivation](2026-11-27-trunc-gb2-math-report.md#fixed-power-beta-prime-escapes)

The proposed all-free paralogis rows already incorporate the earlier review's missing finite-power escapes. At finite $d>0$, the forward log slopes are $d$ and $-d^2$ as log scale escapes to the two ends. Reflection reverses the slopes and cuts for inverse paralogis. At $d\downarrow0$ the absolute log slope is bounded by $d\max(1,d)$, giving the bounded log-uniform. At $d\to\infty$ the opposite slopes away from the projected mode rule out a positive nonconstant height. Thus the integrable tilt list, including the zero slope only on a bounded log interval, applies with samples at the realized cuts. [Prior analytic correction](2026-11-27-mod-rev10-report.md#finite-power-with-escaping-scale)

### Pareto III requires the inside density and the joint cut geometry

**Divergence on the tight window does not exhaust the divergent joint paths.** Hold scale $s=1$ and shape $p=1/2$, use sample $(0,1)$, and take the threshold $d=-\epsilon\uparrow0$. With the upper cut estimated and the lower cut fixed at zero, use $[0,u]$ for any fixed $u\ge1$. With both cuts estimated the same windows are admissible. The likelihood diverges at order $\epsilon^{-1/2}$ as the other sample factor and event mass converge to positive limits. The retained laws converge to the threshold-zero parent conditioned on $[0,u]$. Distinct $u$ give distinct laws. The exact tight-window failure list is therefore one-sided for the upper- and both-estimated fixed-scale fixed-shape rows. At a finite positive supremum the mass-ratio argument still transfers the remote uniform comparison. With the upper cut fixed, adding mass below zero on this example's threshold approach has probability tending to zero. The general lower-estimated scalar argument uses the same fact at $m$.

The fixed-pole argument in P4 requires threshold **and** scale to vary with $s-d$ fixed. The repeated prose about preserving the pole does not apply to the held-scale masks. Their scalar criterion instead uses the actual threshold score and its included join at the fixed cut and contact at $m$, with singular or zero density treated before comparison. For P3, at shape one and $k$ threshold ties the small-scale likelihood has exponent $n-2k$. This distinguishes the finite critical trace from divergence and zero height.

For sample $(1)$ with threshold, scale and shape all held at one, the upper-estimated tight nominal window $[0,1]$ has zero mass. The admissible expansion $[0,1+\epsilon]$ has conditional height $(1+\epsilon)/\epsilon$, so the supremum is infinite and the retained law tends to $\delta_1$. With shape above one the inside density at the sample stays zero on every expansion at that vector. Below one it is infinite on every positive-mass expansion. With only the lower cut free and the fixed upper cut equal to the held threshold, every containing window has zero mass.

On the positive-width branch at a finite winner, a lower-cut plateau can occur only when the occupied minimum equals the fitted threshold and shape is one. For an exact example outside the proposed general uniqueness claims, hold shape one and use lower estimation with fixed $B=\infty$ on $(0,1,1)$. For $d\le0$ put $t=s-d>0$. Profiling the window to $[0,\infty)$ gives likelihood $t/(1+t)^4$. The gap satisfies

$$
Thus $(d,s)=(0,1/3)$ and $(-1/6,1/6)$ both attain the global height $27/256$, and so does the full segment $-1/3<d\le0$, $s=d+1/3$. At $d=0$ every lower cut at or below zero ties. For $d<0$ the only winning lower cut is zero. This is a one-sided nonuniqueness witness for one design and hold, with an exact global comparison for that sample. ### Fatigue contact is excluded from positive fit fibers

The shared plateau sentence is conditional on a finite positive optimum. For this family, density at the threshold is zero for every positive shape and scale. For example, hold threshold $1$ and take sample $(1)$ with upper estimation and fixed lower cut zero. The tight intersection is a singleton, and every positive-mass expansion still has zero likelihood. With a free threshold, a different trial threshold below the sample can yield divergence on the null nominal interval. Every positive finite winner has threshold strictly below $m$ and has strict estimated cuts. The submitted cut fibers state that explicitly. ### The generalized-gamma normal seam has both slope signs

Holding $Q=0$ frees no signed transformed-gamma branch. In the held-$Q$ failure comparison the zero-quadratic exclusion is the normal linear tilt, with either slope on a bounded log interval and only the inward slope on a ray. Holding nonzero $Q$ uses the fixed-gamma-shape signed comparison. A finite positive-variance lognormal is an included candidate where the seam is admissible.
