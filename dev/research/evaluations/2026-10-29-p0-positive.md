# P0 positive families: MLE existence, 2026-10-29

Classifications are proved by the arguments below, except where a numerical search is explicitly identified. The R7 treatment of singular and improper parameter endpoints remains a design question; the tables describe the positive continuous parameter domains unless stated otherwise. Numerical optimization supports the analysis but does not certify global maxima.

Conventions: samples are finite, nonempty, and strictly inside the README support unless explicitly stated; held values are finite and in their stated domains. H is the set held; only subsets with at least one free coefficient are listed. `A` means a finite MLE under the stated condition; `D` means a sample-dependent boundary/degeneracy; `N` means no MLE for the specified subset for all such samples. Ties are permitted. “Constant” means all observations equal. An excluded support point held outside the data produces zero likelihood and is not a meaningful fit. A `yes` in the master column means the combination is implemented and the finite fits checked agree within 1e−6 n; `no` means the hold argument is not implemented. `Partial` means accepted as an optimizer start, leaving the coefficient free, and does not agree with the held oracle. Fits at non-existent boundaries are evaluated separately. See the evidence section for sample coverage and exceptions.

## exp

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A for positive sample sum; all zeros: rate→∞ | yes |

For positive sum, rate = n/Σx. With all zeros likelihood is unbounded. The row is D if zero observations are included in the scope.

## gamma

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; constant: point-mass collapse | yes |
| shape | A | scale = mean/shape | no |
| scale | A | unique shape solution, including constant data | no |

The planned scale is reciprocal to master's `rate`; holding either fixes the same coordinate. At fixed rate the shape score is strictly decreasing from +∞ to −∞. Jointly, Jensen's gap log(mean x) − mean(log x) is positive precisely for nonconstant data.

## weibull

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; constant: collapse | yes |
| shape | A | scale = (mean(x^shape))^(1/shape) | partial: `shape` matches `shape0`, ignored as hold |
| scale | D | A unless every x equals held scale; there shape→∞ | no |

The shape score is strictly decreasing in shape: its derivative is −n/shape² − Σ(x/scale)^shape log²(x/scale). At a constant sample equal to the held scale its likelihood grows as shape^n.

## lnorm

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | On sdlog>0, A iff log data nonconstant; otherwise sdlog→0 | yes, on sdlog>0 |
| meanlog | D | On sdlog>0, A unless all log(x) equal held meanlog; otherwise sdlog→0 | no |
| sdlog | A | For held sdlog>0, meanlog = mean(log x); held sdlog=0 is singular, below | no |

Normal likelihood on log(x) proves the rows on sdlog>0. The reference also accepts sdlog=0 as a point mass: a constant sample at exp(meanlog) has probability one under that member; nonconstant samples have zero probability under every held-sdlog=0 member. Its singular likelihood cannot be compared to continuous densities without the R7 decision below, and master rejects constant samples.

## invgamma

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; point-mass collapse otherwise | yes |
| alpha | A | beta = alpha / mean(1/x) | no |
| beta | A | unique alpha, even for constant samples | no |

Apply the gamma argument to 1/x; beta is the reciprocal-data gamma rate.

## invgauss

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; shape→∞ otherwise | yes |
| mean | D | finite mean: A unless all x equal held mean; then shape→∞. At mean=∞: A, shape = n/Σ(1/x), Lévy | no |
| shape | A | mean = sample mean, including constant data | no |

For finite fixed mean, shape = n / Σ((x−mean)²/(mean²x)). The actuar reference also accepts mean=∞, giving the proper Lévy law; profiling its shape yields n/Σ(1/x). For fixed shape, differentiating in 1/mean gives the unique mean x̄; the Lévy boundary at mean→∞ never wins for a finite positive sample.

## invweibull

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; collapse otherwise | yes |
| shape | A | reciprocal-Weibull scale fit | partial: `shape` matches `shape0`, ignored as hold |
| scale | D | A unless all x equal held inverse-Weibull scale; shape→∞ otherwise | no |

Apply Weibull to 1/x. The proposed scale is reciprocal of master's actuar `rate`; the criterion is invariant under this one-to-one reparametrization.

## invexp

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | A | positive sample; scale = 1/mean(1/x) | yes |

The proposed inverse-exponential scale is reciprocal to master's rate. This is an exponential fit to 1/x.

## llogis

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; constant: shape→∞ with scale at datum | yes |
| shape | A | logistic location fit on log(x) | no |
| scale | D | A unless all x equal held scale; shape→∞ otherwise | no |

The logistic log likelihood on y=log(x) is strictly concave in location for fixed scale. With fixed location it has shape score n/shape − Σ(y−location)tanh(shape(y−location)/2), strictly decreasing. Planned scale = 1/rate; existence is invariant.

## rayleigh

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | A | positive observations; sigma² = mean(x²)/2 | yes |

Zeros have reference density zero for every sigma, so samples containing them have zero likelihood everywhere and require an explicit convention; all zeros do not yield the displayed estimate. Master returns a fit with logLik=−∞ for mixed zero/positive data and rejects all-zero data.

## naka

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; shape→∞ otherwise | yes |
| shape | A | scale = mean(x²) | no |
| scale | D | A unless all x² equal held scale; shape→∞ otherwise | no |

For fixed scale ω, the large-m log likelihood is (n/2)log m − m Σ[z−1−log z] + O(1), z=x²/ω. The bracket is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at z=1. This is not the same held-parameter problem as holding the rate of the gamma law of x².

## lomax

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff ψ(u) ≤ 0 for some 0<u<ū; otherwise exp | yes, approximate face test |
| lambda | A | kappa = n/Σlog(1+lambda x) | partial: `lambda` matches `lambda0`, ignored as hold |
| kappa | A | unique positive lambda root | no |

Here y=x/x̄, ψ(u)=mean(log(exp(−u)+u y)), ū=x̄/geometric_mean(x). The exact criterion and its proof appear in `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`, Theorem lomax; equality is an attained co-maximum. The implementation deliberately treats gaps ≤1e−8 n as the exponential face. With kappa fixed, the rate score multiplied by lambda is n−(kappa+1)Σlambda x/(1+lambda x), strictly decreasing from n to −n kappa.

## gompertz

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A for nonconstant positive data if mean(x²)<2 mean(x)²; otherwise exp; constant positive: collapse | yes |
| a | D | A iff Σx > (a/2)Σx²; otherwise b→0, exponential with rate a | no |
| b | A | a = n b / Σ(expm1(bx)), provided Σx>0 | no |

For fixed a, the b score at 0 is Σx − aΣx²/2 and its derivative is strictly negative for positive data, proving the condition. Jointly, profile out a to obtain, up to constants, bΣx − n log[Σ∫₀ˣⁱ exp(bt)dt]. The logarithm is a strictly convex log moment-generating function, so the profile is strictly concave. Its derivative at 0 has the sign of 2 mean(x)²−mean(x²). For nonconstant data it tends to −∞ as b→∞, proving the criterion. With constant positive data it grows like n log b. All-zero samples have unbounded likelihood if a is free; with a held, likelihood is constant in b.

## sgomp

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A for nonconstant nonnegative samples; constant: collapse | yes, numerical search |
| b | A | eta score strictly decreases; eta=0 allowed and attained | yes |
| eta | D | A if Σx>0; all zeros: b→∞ | yes |

For fixed b, the score at eta=0 is Σ(1−2 exp(−bx)); a nonpositive score gives the attained exponential member. For all zeros the score is constant and negative, so eta=0 still maximizes. For b free and eta fixed, the likelihood goes to −∞ at both ends when Σx>0. For joint free and nonconstant data, the `sgomp/mle.hpp` likelihood envelopes bound the search; the eta=0 face is included. This proves existence but does not prove that the numerical grid finds the global optimum. Zeros are supported.

## lgamma

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | A iff nonconstant; collapse otherwise | yes |
| shapelog | A | ratelog = shapelog / mean(log x) | no |
| ratelog | A | unique shapelog even for constant data | no |

Apply gamma to log(x)>0. Holding ratelog is not the same as holding the mean of log(x).

## invpareto

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | Lomax ψ criterion on 1/x; otherwise invexp | yes, approximate face test |
| shape | A | unique scale root | no |
| scale | A | shape = n/Σlog(1+scale/x) | partial: `scale` matches `scale0`, ignored as hold |

Apply the Lomax argument to 1/x.

## pareto

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | b=min(x), a=n/Σlog(x/min(x)); A iff nonconstant | yes |
| a | A | b=min(x), including constant data | no |
| b | D | require b≤min(x); a=n/Σlog(x/b) unless all x=b, when a→∞ | no |

The reference includes x=b. For fixed a, likelihood increases strictly with b up to min(x); for fixed b, optimize n log a−aΣlog(x/b). This proves both joint and held-shape endpoint results.

## power

| H | Class | Condition / boundary | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | N | alpha↓max(x), excluded by reference; profile shape finite if nonconstant, diverges if constant | partial: returns max(x)+epsilon, not an MLE |
| beta | N | alpha↓max(x), excluded endpoint | no |
| alpha | D | require alpha>max(x); beta=n/Σlog(alpha/x) | no |

For beta>0, the likelihood decreases strictly in alpha above max(x); `extraDistr::dpower(max(x), alpha=max(x), ...)` is zero. Hence no free-alpha MLE under R7, even with beta held. With alpha held strictly above the maximum, the denominator is positive and the beta maximizer is unique. Holding alpha at or below the maximum gives zero likelihood for every beta. This contradicts the design's expectation that the endpoint can be estimated under the current reference support; changing support closure would change the model convention.

## Design and R7 domains

Agree with `endpoint` for pareto `b` and power `alpha`, and `regular` for the remaining coefficients. Endpoint kind alone does not imply attainment: power is a counterexample. No R5 restriction is listed or needed for any of these families under the positive continuous domains. Constant-data collapse is not an almost-every-sample pathology; a bound on power's shape does not include its excluded endpoint. C4 changes gamma, invexp, invweibull and llogis by one-to-one rate/scale transformations, so all existence conditions are invariant; held labels in these tables and YAML use proposed scales.

`domains.R` probes every coordinate at −∞, −1, 0 and +∞ at interior x; see `domains.csv`. The following README open domains differ from values for which the density returns without a NaN at interior x:

| Coefficient | Accepted edge and interpretation |
|---|---|
| gamma `shape=0` | `dgamma` gives zero at positive x; `rgamma` generates 0, an attained point mass outside the stated support. |
| lnorm `sdlog=0` | `dlnorm` gives ∞ at exp(meanlog), zero elsewhere; `rlnorm` generates the point. |
| invgamma `beta=0` | density gives zero at positive x; `rinvgamma` generates 0. |
| invgauss `mean=Inf` | proper, nondegenerate Lévy law; both density and CDF are finite and sampler works. |
| exp `rate=0`, gamma `rate=0`, invgauss `shape=0`, llogis `rate=0`, lgamma `ratelog=0` | density returns zero on all tested positive x. These are not positive continuous densities there; some p/r functions reject or put mass at infinity. |
| gamma `shape=Inf`, Weibull `scale=Inf`, lnorm `meanlog=±Inf` or `sdlog=Inf`, invgauss `shape=±Inf`, invweibull `rate=±Inf`, invexp `rate=±Inf`, rayleigh `sigma=Inf`, invpareto `scale=Inf`, pareto `b=Inf`, power `alpha=Inf` | density returns zero at tested finite interior points, not evidence of a proper finite-valued continuous law. |

`sgomp.eta=0` is already correctly closed in its README. These probes are empirical reference acceptance, not a claim that every numeric return defines a proper law. In particular, taking R7 literally raises a design decision: point masses cannot be combined with ordinary continuous densities using the same Lebesgue density, and the sums `Inf + (−Inf)` are undefined for mixed data. The finite-domain MLE classifications above do not resolve that singular likelihood convention. Shape-zero gamma and beta-zero inverse gamma put all mass at 0, outside the positive-data scope. Lévy is a proper extension and its held-mean fit is classified above.

## Numerical evidence and limitations

Scripts run from the repository root with `Rscript`; their CSV output is alongside them. `oracle.R` uses the installed master fitter only for comparison; multistart BFGS operates on reference log densities in transformed coordinates. Pareto's attained sample-minimum endpoint is handled explicitly, as is sgomp's attained eta=0 face; sgomp's curved large-eta ridge has an additional reparametrized search. Tiny Gompertz b evaluations are cross-checked against `expm1` to reject catastrophic cancellation in the reference density. The oracle is not a certificate of a global maximum.

Fixed seed 20261029: default draws at n=2, 3, 5, 20, 100, 1000; tied and constant samples for each family; held-value matching samples for the six relevant collapse cases; exponential, inverse exponential, Lévy and half-normal limit samples where applicable; heavy Lomax/inverse Pareto pairs; zero samples for supported families. `oracle-*.csv` contains all 445 subset/sample results, including fit errors and actual `fixed` attributes. `paths.R` records 19 divergent/boundary paths in `paths.csv`, including constant-sample collapses and the free power endpoint. `edges.R` tests reference boundaries, eta=0, Lévy and partial argument matching.

Of 157 finite fits with the requested holds actually implemented, 156 agree within 1e−6 n; the sole discrepancy is power on constant data, where the oracle follows an unbounded path and master returns a finite epsilon approximation. For nonconstant power data the approximation is numerically close but remains a non-MLE. Own `dnaka` and `dsgomp` agree with separately written README log-density formulas at the tested default parameters to 3.6e−15; the own density still supplies the optimized likelihood, so this is a numerical formula check rather than an independent library. Stored package logLik agrees with reference density at returned coefficients to 4.5e−9 absolute. `comparison-summary.csv` gives counts by family and implemented hold. Failures on escape and collapse samples are in the CSVs; the Lomax implementation intentionally uses a 1e−8 n face tolerance.

The arguments above prove the positive-domain classifications, using the existing Lomax theorem and shifted-Gompertz likelihood envelopes; the optimizer merely checks finite examples and cannot prove global optimality or validate every held value. The reference endpoint policy described above remains open, including whether singular point-mass limits should be modeled under R7. No library or published-source results beyond the local Lomax theorem are required by these proofs.
