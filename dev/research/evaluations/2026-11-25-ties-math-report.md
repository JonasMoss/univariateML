# Exact ties on binary64 data

TASK-324 · 2026-11-25 · Base `a2f9a313`

**Proved.** On rational data, an interior Lomax fit cannot tie the optimized exponential, and a finite Fréchet fit at shape one cannot tie the optimized Gumbel. The lower-only truncated-binomial finite/Poisson exclusion is also unconditional. The critical skew-Student example with held mean, df three and rational skew other than one cannot have an attained finite/collapse tie on rational data. Schanuel's conjecture would exclude the folded-normal half-normal/interior and held-threshold GPD uniform/interior ties on rational data.

**Numerical.** Exact binary64 neighbors give opposite branch rankings with differences as small as $4.91\times10^{-19}$ in mean log likelihood in the new continuous-data replays. The retained integer Skellam examples have gaps as small as $4.73\times10^{-22}$. A fixed likelihood tolerance cannot determine which separated coefficients maximize the likelihood. A certificate of the maximum value and a certificate of the maximizer set answer different questions.

## Classification of the constructions

Here **structural** means equality follows from an exact invariance, a flat likelihood, or an exact fit of a discrete empirical law. **Real crossing** includes max-defined thresholds and IVT constructions. **Dyadic** describes the exact inputs, including holds where relevant. All finite binary64 values are dyadics, but precision and exponent bounds matter in the other direction. A maximizing coefficient may be irrational even at integer inputs. Rational weights on an integer support produce an ordinary sample by clearing denominators. Real weights need an independent rationality proof before that argument applies.

## Arithmetic of likelihood equality

### What the transcendence results can decide

**Proved tools.** Lindemann–Weierstrass gives linear independence over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ of $e^{u_j}$ for distinct algebraic $u_j$. For a polynomial in exponentials of algebraic numbers, expand and combine terms whose exponent sums coincide before applying it. In particular $e^u$ is transcendental for nonzero algebraic $u$ (Hermite–Lindemann). Baker gives linear independence over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ of $1$ and logarithms of nonzero algebraic numbers whose selected logarithms are independent over $\mathbb Q$. For positive real algebraic arguments, the latter dependence is equivalently a multiplicative relation after clearing denominators. Thus an algebraic-coefficient linear height comparison with algebraic logarithm arguments can only vanish when its coefficients cancel after reduction to a rationally independent logarithm basis, including cancellation of any algebraic constant. The hypotheses concern the arguments and coefficients at the optima. Rational observations alone do not supply them. [Lindemann–Weierstrass proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14352) · [Baker's linear forms theorem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/linear-forms-in-the-logarithms-of-algebraic-numbers-iii/C3A7D8C6F454E237D6A3FBAD8DBD006D)

**Conditional tool.** The number-theoretic Schanuel conjecture says that for $m$ rationally independent complex numbers $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, the field generated by the $u_j$ and $e^{u_j}$ has transcendence degree at least $m$ over $\mathbb Q$. To use it for exclusion, find independent exponents whose exponentials and exponents lie in a field of smaller transcendence degree. Rational independence is a separate proof obligation. The differential-field version has a different scope. [Ax](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1971/93-2/p03)

**Proved applications and limits.** In held-shape fatigue the rational score makes the scales algebraic. After cancelling common factors, equality of the squared likelihoods has the form $e^{n(Q(b_1)-Q(b_2))/a^2}=R(b_1)/R(b_2)$ with algebraic exponent and right-hand side. Hermite–Lindemann forces $Q(b_1)=Q(b_2)$ and then $R(b_1)=R(b_2)$. In truncated binomial with finite size, including comparisons with a bounded-window Poisson face, all fitted odds or rates solve polynomial scores. The positive ordinary likelihoods are algebraic and exact equality is an algebraic-number problem. For Cauchy with held scale the same conclusion follows directly after removing the common factor. The all-free two-piece-normal profile is algebraic up to the common exponential factor in the optimized density product, so roots, joins and their values can be compared algebraically on that route. [Fatigue equality test](2026-11-25-fatigue-math-report.md) · [Truncated-binomial algebraic comparison](2026-11-25-tbinom-math-report.md) · [Two-piece profile](2026-11-25-sn-math-report.md)

For the Lomax finite/finite question, stationarity writes $A_i=\overline{\log(1+r_i x)}$ as a rational function of $r_i$ over the data field. The equality would give $\exp(A_1-A_2)=r_1A_2/(r_2A_1)$ in addition to $\exp(nA_i)=\prod_j(1+r_ix_j)$. The difference adds no independent exponent to the two $A_i$, and the two-rate field can have transcendence degree two. This reduction alone gives no Schanuel contradiction. For Burr, Fréchet with a free interior shape, and paralogistic, roots generally enter powers or logarithms through unknown coefficient-dependent arguments. The cited comparisons supply no algebraicity or independent-exponent bound sufficient to apply these theorems to the finite/finite ties. For held power $c$ in Burr with positive algebraic observations and rational $c$, the transformed observations are algebraic and the Lomax face theorem below applies.

For Skellam, the prior obstruction uses Siegel's Bessel quotient theorem, together with the order recurrence and a pole argument, to show a positive stationary argument is transcendental for rational weights and rational mean. In the mean-zero problem the tie would satisfy $e^{n(z_2-z_1)}=\prod_k[I_k(z_2)/I_k(z_1)]^{n_k}$. This supplies no algebraicity of the Bessel values at the two transcendental arguments, nor a field of small enough transcendence degree for Schanuel. Reflection and the integer near-ties leave this arithmetic question open. [Skellam arithmetic argument](2026-11-25-skellam2-math-report.md#an-arithmetic-obstruction)

### Unconditional exclusion of a finite Lomax/exponential tie

**Proved.** Let $x_i>0$ be algebraic, $r>0$ the Lomax rate, $m=\bar x$, and $A(r)=\overline{\log(1+r x_i)}$. The profiled mean log likelihood is $\log r-\log A-A-1$. Put $U(r)=\overline{r x_i/(1+r x_i)}$. Every interior stationary rate satisfies $1=U+U/A$, so $A=B(r)=U/(1-U)$. Equality to the optimized exponential, with log value $-\log m-1$, implies $e^A=rm/A$. Raising to power $n$ gives
$$
\prod_i(1+r x_i)=\{rm/B(r)\}^n.
$$
This rational equation is nonidentical. The left side diverges as $r\to\infty$, while $B(r)/r\to1/\overline{x_i^{-1}}$, and the right side has a finite limit. As $B>0$ at the rate, clearing denominators forces $r$ algebraic. Then $A=B(r)>0$ is algebraic, but $e^{nA}=\prod_i(1+r x_i)$ is algebraic, contradicting Hermite–Lindemann. The last contradiction also shows every positive stationary rate is transcendental on positive algebraic data, regardless of comparison to the face. For Burr XII with positive algebraic data and a held positive rational power, the exact power transformation gives the same exclusion against the matched, held-power Weibull face. The root in the transformed chart remains an interior root. [Lomax profile and transformation](2026-11-25-lomax-uniq-math-report.md) · [Burr held power](2026-11-25-burr-math-report.md#held-power-has-an-exact-global-tie)

### Unconditional exclusion of a Fréchet shape-one/Gumbel tie

**Proved.** Take nonconstant rational observations. Translate by their minimum and write their gaps as $d k_i$ with rational $d>0$ and integers $k_i\ge0$. At Fréchet shape one and gap $h>0$ from the minimum to the lower endpoint, put $z_i=h+d k_i$. The profiled scale is $s(h)=1/\overline{1/z_i}$ and the likelihood product is
$$
e^{-n}\,s(h)^n\prod_i z_i^{-2}.
$$
The coefficient multiplying $e^{-n}$ is a nonconstant rational function of $h$ (it tends to zero at infinity and is positive at finite $h$). Its derivative therefore has a nonzero polynomial numerator, so an interior optimum $h$ is algebraic, and its likelihood divided by $e^{-n}$ is algebraic.

For the Gumbel write $t=d/\beta>0$ with scale $\beta$, $Y=e^{-t}$, $Q(Y)=\overline{Y^{k_i}}$ and $S=\sum_i k_i$. Profiling location gives
$$
e^{-n}(t/d)^nY^S/Q(Y)^n,\qquad
1/t=\bar k-\overline{k_iY^{k_i}}/Q(Y)
$$
at the optimizing scale. Indeed, profiling location sets the mean exponential term in the log likelihood to one, and differentiation in $t$ gives the score identity. For nonconstant data the scale optimum is interior: the normalized likelihood tends to zero both as $t\downarrow0$ and as $t\to\infty$, since $S>0$ and at least one $k_i=0$. Thus $t=T(Y)$ is a rational function evaluated at $0<Y<1$. Were $Y$ algebraic, $t$ would be nonzero algebraic, contradicting $Y=e^{-t}$. Moreover $(T(Y)/d)^nY^S/Q(Y)^n$ is a nonconstant rational function. It diverges at $Y\uparrow1$ because $1/T(Y)$ tends to zero while $Y^S/Q(Y)^n$ tends to one. A nonconstant rational function over the algebraic numbers cannot take an algebraic value at a transcendental argument where it is defined, by clearing denominators. The Gumbel likelihood divided by $e^{-n}$ is therefore transcendental. This excludes equality for the shape-one finite maximum on rational data. It does not cover a finite optimum at an unrestricted interior shape. [Fréchet profiles and real crossing](2026-11-25-frechet-math-report.md)

### Two other unconditional exclusions

**Truncated binomial, proved.** For integer observations on a lower-only window $[L,\infty)$ with integer $L\ge1$ and empirical mean $\mu>L$, let $n$ be sample size, $S=n\mu$ the total, and $Z_L(\lambda)=\sum_{k\ge L}\lambda^k/k!$. The matched positive Poisson rate satisfies
$$
(\mu-\lambda)Z_L(\lambda)=\lambda^L/(L-1)! .
$$
Since $Z_L=e^\lambda-\sum_{k<L}\lambda^k/k!$, algebraic $\lambda$ would make $e^\lambda$ algebraic, contradicting Hermite–Lindemann. The matched Poisson likelihood equals $C\lambda^{S-nL}(\mu-\lambda)^n$ with positive rational $C=((L-1)!)^n/\prod_i x_i!$. This nonconstant polynomial in the transcendental $\lambda$ gives a transcendental likelihood. At any finite size, the score at an interior optimum is a polynomial equation in the odds with rational coefficients, with nonzero numerator since the conditional mean increases strictly when the support has at least two points. The attained likelihood is algebraic there and also at permitted closure endpoints. No finite size can tie the matched face. The untruncated $L=0$ case with $S>0$ follows from its nonzero algebraic exponent $-S$ in the Poisson likelihood. The zero-rate point-mass cases and bounded windows have different arithmetic. [Earlier proof and scope](2026-11-25-tbinom-math-report.md)

**Skew-Student critical collapse, proved.** Hold rational mean and rational skew $\xi>0$ with $\xi\ne1$, hold df $\nu=3$, and take rational data at critical multiplicity $k=3m$, with $m>0$ non-ties. In the earlier ratio formula use $u=|M|s$ and $q=3/M^2$, where $M=(\xi-\xi^{-1})2\sqrt3/\pi$. Write $d_i=x_i-\mu$ and $v=\operatorname{sign}(M)$. For fixed $u>0$ the factors of the inverse likelihood ratio before taking the common positive power are
$$
F_{d_i}(u,q)=
\frac{(d_i+v u)^2+q\kappa_e^2u^2}{d_i^2}
\frac{\kappa_{\operatorname{sign}d_i}^2}{\kappa_e^2},
\qquad
e=\operatorname{sign}(d_i+vu),\quad (\kappa_-,\kappa_+)=(1/\xi,\xi).
$$
At a join either assignment gives the same value. For every $u>0$ the product is strictly increasing in $q>0$, since each factor is positive with strictly positive slope. A finite/collapse global tie requires $\prod_i F_{d_i}(u,q)\ge1$ for every $u>0$, and equality for some $u>0$. The set of $q$ satisfying these two conditions has at most one point. If it holds at $q_1$, every larger $q$ gives strict inequality everywhere. It is semialgebraic over $\mathbb Q$ by real quantifier elimination, since the finite sign branches and all coefficients except $q$ are rational. Any singleton so defined is algebraic: away from the roots of its finitely many defining polynomials the Boolean signs are locally constant. But $q$ is a nonzero rational multiple of $\pi^2$, transcendental by Hermite–Lindemann applied to $i\pi$. Therefore the tie is impossible. In particular the real example's $d_*$ is irrational. The proof uses the held mean/df/skew mask with scale free and critical multiplicity, rather than assuming a similar representation on other masks. [Scale ratio and real example](2026-11-25-sstdsged-math-report.md#which-model-wins) · [Tarski's quantifier elimination](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/a-decision-method-for-elementary-algebra-and-geometry/epub-pdf)

### Schanuel and the GPD uniform boundary

**Proved conditional on Schanuel, with an unconditional exponential special case.** Hold a rational threshold strictly below all rational observations, divide the excesses by their maximum, and write the resulting values as $x_i\in(0,1]$. In the dossier's interior profile chart let
$$
D(b)=\overline{\log(1+b x_i)}/b,\qquad R(b)=\overline{x_i/(1+b x_i)},\qquad k=bD(b).
$$
Here $b>-1$, $D(0)=\bar x$, and $k>-1$ for the interior fit. Relative to the attained uniform the mean log likelihood is $h=-\log D-k-1$. At $b=0$ an equality would give $D(0)=1/e$, impossible since $D(0)$ is rational.

For $b\ne0$ one has $bD'=R-D$. Stationarity gives $0=bD h'=D-(1+k)R$. Thus $k=bR/(1-bR)$ belongs to $\mathbb Q(b)$, and $1-bR=\overline{1/(1+b x_i)}>0$. The rational function $bR$ is nonconstant, with derivative $\overline{x_i/(1+b x_i)^2}>0$. If $k$ were algebraic then $b$ would be algebraic by the nonconstant rational equation, hence also $D=k/b$. At equality $D=e^{-1-k}$, contradicting Hermite–Lindemann since $k>-1$. Consequently $1,k$ are rationally independent. Now
$$
e^{nk}=\prod_i(1+b x_i),\qquad e^{1+k}=1/D=b/k .
$$
Both right sides lie in $\mathbb Q(b)$. Therefore $e^k$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q(b)$, as is $e=e^{1+k}/e^k$. The field generated by $1,k,e,e^k$ has transcendence degree at most one. Schanuel would require two. This argument applies to any interior stationary point at the uniform value on this route, even before checking global dominance. [GPD profile and exact real example](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gpd/README.md#joint-maxima-need-not-be-unique)

For **folded normal**, the prior proof has a parallel rank obstruction. Write $x_i=d k_i$ with rational $d>0$, $a=\mu/\sigma^2$, $Y=e^{2ad}$. Stationarity writes $\mu,\sigma^2,a$ as rational functions in $Y$ and excludes algebraic $\mu$ by Hermite–Lindemann. Equality to the half-normal writes $e^{2na\mu}$ as another rational function in $Y$. The two exponents $2ad,2na\mu$ are rationally independent because their ratio is $n\mu/d$. So Schanuel requires transcendence degree two inside a field of degree one. Hermite–Lindemann alone supplies the intermediate parameter obstruction. [Folded-normal proof](2026-11-25-foldnorm-math-report.md#exact-and-near-ties)

## Open

- For rational samples, finite/finite ties in Lomax, Fréchet, Burr and paralogistic, and the all-free Burr finite/Pareto and finite/Weibull equalities, remain undecided. The IVT constructions force a crossing over the reals. Their endpoint inequalities do not give the arithmetic of the crossing. A rational witness with equality and a complete global cover, or an exclusion controlling the jointly fitted exponents, would settle the corresponding case.
- The folded-normal and GPD Schanuel exclusions leave unconditional rational equality open on the stated masks, aside from the GPD exponential subcase. The missing step is an unconditional obstruction to each displayed pair of exponential relations, or an exact rational construction. The GPD real three-point construction still leaves the cardinality of the positive part of its maximizing set open. The replay only follows its indicated branch.
- For Skellam, neither rational coexistence weights nor a proof of exclusion at transcendental stationary arguments are available. For nonconstant lower-only truncated binomial, an equality of two distinct finite sizes is algebraically testable per pair, but the bounded histograms and finite/Poisson theorem do not decide existence on all histograms.
- The numerical replay does not count all stationary points after rounding all fixed levels in the Burr or paralogistic paths. Large-weight Burr and Skellam histograms are mathematical regression inputs. A production test would need a weighted harness or smaller verified examples, and the arithmetic padding for the production likelihood comparisons requires its own measured or proved bounds.
