Reviewed [issue #52](https://github.com/JonasMoss/univariateML/issues/52) and [#7](https://github.com/JonasMoss/univariateML/issues/7). `gh api` could not connect; browser access supplied the issues. No Git writes, board edits, sub-agents, package builds or test suites. The session-start board listing was read only.

**Conventions.** Observations are iid nonnegative integer counts, with a common size/draw count where applicable; let h be sample size, mean u, population-form sample variance v, and maximum b. Open beta shapes and NB size are strictly positive. Boundary extensions must be declared explicitly. Every PMF is at most one, so **none of these models has an unbounded likelihood**: nonexistence concerns unattained finite suprema, not continuous-density spikes. Pointwise convergence to a proper count PMF gives total-variation and hence Hellinger convergence; mass escaping to infinity is not such a limit model. The derivations below identify relevant faces, not a general theorem certifying every possible maximizing sequence of each free-parameter family. S/M/L mean small/medium/large implementation and validation scope, including metadata, external-function agreement and boundary handling.

One R script and one Python/mpmath script ran sequentially, each below a second wall time, under `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 nice -n 10`. Independent log-PMF checks used 80-digit log-gamma/beta formulas, not the extraDistr implementation. Quantiles were checked with the discrete criterion F(q−1)<t≤F(q), not F(q)=t. These are spot checks, not a full numerical audit. Scratch: `/tmp/umvl-check.R`, `/tmp/umvl-check.py`, `/tmp/umvl-dens.tsv`.

**Dependencies and maintenance.** The installed [extraDistr DESCRIPTION](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/DESCRIPTION) matches the public 2026-07-13 release, maintained by Sigbert Klinke. ## Beta-binomial

**Reference, source evidence.** The [R interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/R/beta-binomial-distribution.R) supplies d/p/r but **no `qbbinom`** (also checked in the installed namespace). Parameters: integer N=`size`≥0, a=`alpha`>0, c=`beta`>0; X|P~Bin(N,P), P~Beta(a,c). PMF is C(N,x)B(a+x,c+N−x)/B(a,c). Use the shared extraDistr dependency/maintenance assessment above. A generated `qml` cannot simply assume a q function exists; package policy forbids filling this gap with a newly defined public quantile implementation.

**Checked locally.** For (x,N,a,c)=(3,10,2,5), independent log-PMF −1.80280930541464 agreed to rounding; (0,100,.01,100) agreed within 2e−16. At (5,10,10^12,10^12), extraDistr gave −1.40185546875 versus −1.40204271809053, error 1.87e−4. Over x=0:N, max |p−cumsum(d)| was 4.44e−16, 6.44e−15 and **0.003196**, respectively; the extreme PMFs summed to 1.000149. No p/q check is possible with this package. The [C++ source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/beta-binomial-distribution.cpp) subtracts large lbeta terms for d and uses a separate gamma recurrence for p, explaining their different cancellation. Do not use huge shapes as a substitute for an explicit binomial face.

**MLE behaviour: direct derivations.** First hold N≥2 fixed. Put p=a/(a+c), t=a+c and ρ=1/(t+1). Then E[X]=Np and Var[X]=Np(1−p)[1+(N−1)ρ]. Relevant closure members are:

- t→∞ with p fixed: Bin(N,p), **Use `mlbinom(x, size=N)`**. The fixed-size argument matters; an unrestricted binomial fit is a different comparison.
- t→0 with p fixed: mass 1−p at 0 and p at N. If all observations are endpoints and both occur, the empirical endpoint frequencies attain the unrestricted categorical upper bound only in this limit; there is no finite positive-shape MLE. This is scaled Bernoulli, not ordinary `mlbinom` on the original x when N>1; name the face explicitly.
- p→0 or 1 gives the endpoint point masses. All-zero/all-N samples have no positive-shape MLE unless a matching boundary extension is deliberately added. N=0 is a point mass; N=1 is Bernoulli and concentration t is completely unidentified, even with both outcomes observed.

At the binomial fit p=u/N, expansion in 1/t gives inward profile derivative h[v−Np(1−p)]/[2p(1−p)]. Thus underdispersion makes the binomial a local boundary maximum; positive derivative rules it out globally. A nonpositive derivative alone is **not used here as a proved global escape criterion**. Compare the actual faces and all competitive finite optima. A finite interior MLE does exist for some samples: at N=2, equal frequencies of 0,1,2 are fitted exactly by a=c=1, reaching the empirical categorical upper bound. Endpoint-only samples show nonexistence with positive probability under every interior model. Therefore existence is **sample dependent**, not always or never.

If **N is estimated**, enumerate integers N≥b and optimize the two shapes at each N. Additional limits: N→∞, a fixed, c/N→s>0 gives P small and NP→Gamma(a,rate=s), hence **NB(size=a, prob=s/(1+s))**, Use `mlnbinom`; binomial concentration followed by N→∞ gives **Poisson**, Use `mlpois`. Joint scales also reach the Poisson corner. There is no justified arbitrary finite upper bound for N in this report. Unlike the fixed-N problem, this needs comparison with a fitted unbounded-support family as well as all finite N candidates.

**Literature, distinguished from derivation.** [Gupta, Nguyen and Wang (1999), DOI 10.2307/3316115](https://doi.org/10.2307/3316115) explicitly reports upper bounds for beta-binomial size estimators and an example of a nonunimodal beta-binomial likelihood. I checked its abstract, not the full proof; its bounds have not been imported as a certified algorithm here.

**Fitter sketch and recommendation.** Start with **required fixed N**, optionally fix a or c; express optimization as logit(p), log(t), use BFGS with moment and dispersed starts, and compare binomial/endpoints explicitly. Moment start: p=u/N, ρ=[v/(Np(1−p))−1]/(N−1), t=1/ρ−1 when valid; otherwise use several interior starts. Each objective costs O(h), or O(number of distinct counts) after aggregation. No general closed-form joint shape profile. Stable rising-factorial sums or asymptotic differences are needed near concentration boundaries. Fixed individual shapes change which limits remain reachable and need their own checks. **Add later, M fixed size / L free size**: useful, but q coverage and reference accuracy precede integration; free-size global search is a separate project.

**Alternative source, documentation/source only.** [gamlss.dist BB](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/R/BB.R) has d/p/q/r with bd=N, mu=p, sigma=1/t. It is not currently a reference package; adding it needs a policy/dependency decision. Its source replaces sigma<10^−4 by binomial evaluation, so it is not an exact beta-binomial reference throughout the open parameter space; it also sets out-of-support log densities to zero. Thus presence of qBB alone does not resolve quality concerns.

## Beta negative binomial

**Reference, source evidence.** [extraDistr interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/R/beta-negative-binomial-distribution.R): d/p/r, **no q**; r=`size`>0 need not be integer, a=`alpha`>0, c=`beta`>0. X|P~NB(r,P), P~Beta(a,c); f(x)=Γ(r+x)/(Γ(r)x!) · B(a+r,c+x)/B(a,c). Same dependencies and maintenance as above. Do not interpret `size` as a finite support or force it to be integer.

**Checked locally.** At (x,r,a,c)=(3,2,3,4), log-PMF −2.26436388017385 agrees within 3e−15; at (1000,.1,.01,100), error is 1.6e−12. At (3,2,10^12,10^12), error is 9.58e−5. On x=0:100, max |p−cumsum(d)| was 1.89e−15, 6.09e−15 and **0.006670**. No external p/q pair exists. [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/beta-negative-binomial-distribution.cpp) uses gamma/beta differences and a cumulative recurrence, with O(q) work/storage for a scalar CDF request; heavy-tail large quantiles would be expensive even with a separate inversion routine.

**MLE behaviour: direct derivations and limitations.** For all three parameters free, writing the PMF as gamma factors shows the exact identity

`f(x; r,a,c) = f(x; c,a,r)`.

It was verified numerically on x=0:10 at (r,a,c)=(2,3,4), max error 2.36e−16. Thus **the ordered parameter vector is not identifiable**, and a finite optimum off r=c has an equally good swapped solution. Replacing c by the mean does not remove this distributional symmetry. Either constrain r≤c with a declared equality boundary, return equivalence classes, or require known r; an ordering convention must not be silently imposed on a scientifically fixed size.

For fixed r, a+c→∞ at fixed p=a/(a+c) gives NB(r,p): **Use `mlnbinom(x, size=r)`**. With free r, compare the unrestricted NB fit and its Poisson corner (**Use `mlpois`**); the swapped route r,a→∞ with a/r→s and c fixed gives NB(c,s/(1+s)) as well. These are not different fitted distribution families, but both routes matter for optimization and parameter diagnostics. c→0 at fixed a,r gives δ0: all-zero data have supremum one, unattained in the strictly positive-shape domain. a→0 with r,c fixed moves mass to infinity, not a proper Hellinger limit.

The tail is f(x) proportional to x^(−a−1). The mean rc/(a−1) exists only for a>1; variance only for a>2. These moment restrictions are **not** admissibility restrictions for likelihood fitting. Finite interior fits are possible and escapes/nonexistence occur with positive probability; no general samplewise existence or uniqueness theorem was verified here. In particular, the face list is a derivation of necessary comparisons, **not a completed closure/existence classification** for unrestricted size. This unresolved part is a reason to defer, rather than claim a multistart optimizer settles the question.

**Literature.** A [2022 count-time-series paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10184601/pdf/CJAS_50_2064439.pdf) explicitly notes the r–beta interchange and the NB limit. Its assertion that mean reparameterization avoids nonidentifiability should not be taken literally for the unrestricted iid family: the gamma-factor identity above survives a one-to-one coordinate change.

**Fitter sketch and recommendation.** Permit fixed positive fractional r; optimize log(a),log(c) by multistart BFGS, compare the fixed-r NB face, and handle all-zero data explicitly. For a free-r version use three logs plus an identification convention; start from several NB fits and several a values, including a≤1, since mean/variance starts omit valid heavy tails. Aggregate counts; each objective is O(distinct counts). Scores follow from digamma differences, but no closed-form joint profile was established. **Add later, L**, pending a closure/existence analysis, identification choice, quantile source and reliable extreme-shape reference evaluation.

**Alternative source, documentation/source only.** [gamlss.dist BNB](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/R/BNB.R) supplies d/p/q/r with r=1/nu, a=1+1/sigma, c=mu·nu/sigma: it covers **only a>1**. It therefore cannot supply q for the full extraDistr domain by argument mapping alone. [DESCRIPTION](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/DESCRIPTION) shows CRAN 6.1-11, released 2026-09-10, maintained by Mikis Stasinopoulos; upstream r-universe already lists 6.1-12. Recursive Imports are MASS, graphics, stats, methods, grDevices, utils (MASS's own Imports is methods); no VGAM. The same dependency assessment applies to BB. gamlss.dist was absent locally; these alternatives were source-reviewed, not installed or numerically validated. Recent [NEWS](https://gamlss-dev.r-universe.dev/gamlss.dist/NEWS) includes qBB corrections, evidence of maintenance rather than a numerical guarantee.

## Zero-inflated negative binomial

**Reference, source evidence.** [extraDistr interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/R/zero-inflated-negative-binomial-distribution.R) provides all four functions. Parameters r=`size`>0 (fractional allowed), p=`prob` in (0,1], π=`pi` in [0,1]; f(0)=π+(1−π)p^r, f(x>0)=(1−π)NB(x;r,p). Same dependencies/maintenance as above. The plain-text zero-case formula in its documentation contains a typo; the displayed formula and implementation use π as the extra-zero probability, matching the package's ZIP convention.

**Checked locally.** At (x,r,p,π)=(3,2,.4,.3), log-PMF −2.33543891786512 agrees within 5e−15. At (0,10^6,.999999,.999999), error is 1.9e−17. At (10000,2,.5,.1), however, `dzinb(log=TRUE)` is **−Inf**, versus independent **−6923.75302010925**. The [source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/extraDistr/master/src/zero-inflated-negative-binomial-distribution.cpp) exponentiates a log NB density before the outer wrapper takes its log. At q=100,r=2,p=.5,π=.1, upper-tail log CDF is −Inf instead of −66.17164 because it complements an ordinary lower CDF. Quantile inequalities passed for t={10^−10,.1,.5,.9,1−10^−10} at all three parameter sets; but π=1 gives `qzinb(1,...)=NaN` rather than 0 for the degenerate law. These are material `dml/pml/qml` defects; a stable C++ objective alone cannot fix external dispatch.

**MLE behaviour: direct derivation.** Let z be the zero fraction and f0=p^r. For fixed r,p, differentiating the binomial zero/positive likelihood gives

`πhat = max(0, (z−f0)/(1−f0))`, when f0<1.

On the branch f0≤z, the fitted positive probability is 1−z and optimizing r,p reduces to the **zero-truncated NB likelihood** for positive observations. When f0>z, the constrained optimum has π=0 and the ordinary NB objective. This is an exact profile, avoiding EM or unconstrained three-dimensional BFGS. If π is fixed instead, this profiling identity no longer applies.

- π=0 is an **attained boundary MLE**, the NB subfamily. Return r,p,π=0, as current `mlzip` returns π=0 for its attained Poisson boundary; do not report nonexistent MLE merely because a submodel wins.
- r→∞ with r(1−p)/p→λ yields ZIP: **Use `mlzip`**; π→0 at the same time yields Poisson: **Use `mlpois`**. Compare full maximized face likelihoods, including ZIP's own π=0 boundary, not a variance/mean test on the raw mixture counts.
- All zeros attain likelihood one at π=1 (any r,p) or p=1; parameters are nonidentified. Follow the current ZIP/NB warning convention, but check reference evaluation of the chosen representative at quantile endpoints.
- For samples with positives, r→0 at fixed p makes their NB probabilities vanish. A zero-truncated logarithmic-series limit by itself is **not** an extra ZINB face: maintaining a positive observed fraction would require negative inflation as f0→1. Likewise mass fleeing to infinity cannot be accepted as a fitted count law.

Finite MLE existence is **sample dependent**, even allowing π=0; ZIP/Poisson can win with no finite r maximizer. There is no continuous-mixture variance-collapse singularity. Away from the all-zero degeneracies, the positive-PMF ratio f(x+1)/f(x)=(x+r)(1−p)/(x+1) identifies r,p, then the positive mass identifies π. Sparse positives can nevertheless make the fit weak and nearly flat. Fixed finite r removes the infinite-r escape and leaves an attained fit when p=1/π=1 are included; a sample containing positives keeps p away from its degenerate endpoints at a maximizing sequence.

**Fitter sketch and recommendation.** Fit r,p with the exact π profile; optionally profile p with safeguarded one-dimensional optimization at each r, then search r including the explicit r=∞ face. Use stable log-sum-exp at zero and `log1p` for positive mass/truncation. Starts: existing `mlnbinom`, zero-truncated positive moments and several dispersions. The π profile is piecewise smooth: handle its join explicitly, or search its two branches separately. Each evaluation costs O(distinct counts); profiling adds iterations but only one or two numerical coordinates. Make r and π fixable; a fixed-r π=0 candidate has closed-form p=r/(r+u). With free r use the existing NB one-dimensional profile as a boundary candidate, including equality v=u, which the current `<`-only NB guard does not resolve safely.

**Add now as the next project, M**: good use of existing ZIP/NB machinery, all d/p/q/r available, no new package. Before release obtain an upstream extraDistr correction or another validated CRAN reference with an argument map; otherwise defer release under the ML-only policy. In particular, neither truncate r at an arbitrary ceiling nor silently dispatch to hand-written R probability functions. The derivation, not an external existence theorem, supports the proposed profile; a global comparison remains required.

## Hypergeometric

**Reference and literature evidence.** [stats documentation](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/Hypergeometric.html) provides all four functions. In R, m successes, n failures, k draws, N=m+n; all integer, m,n≥0, 0≤k≤N. Support is max(0,k−n)≤x≤min(k,m); PMF C(m,x)C(n,k−x)/C(N,k). No new dependency, bundled maintenance as above. The documented density uses Loader's binomial probabilities, CDF uses a density-ratio sum, and q uses inversion; this is a substantially stronger numerical starting point than raw factorials.

**Checked locally.** Independent log-PMF errors at (x,m,n,k)=(3,20,30,10), (0,1,10^9,1000), (500,10^9,10^9,1000) were 3.7e−15, 3.0e−16, 3.1e−15. Quantile inequalities passed on the same five probabilities as ZINB for all three sets. These include a rare-success case and a large-population binomial limit. This does not certify every enormous integer or tail; double precision also cannot represent arbitrary integers above 2^53 exactly.

**MLE behaviour and practical parameter choices: direct derivations.** Treat observations as independent repetitions from the same urn composition, with the urn restored between repetitions; dependent successive samples require a different likelihood. In most applications k is known by design. Prefer coordinates **N,m,k**, mapped to R n=N−m. Do not confuse h observations with R's `n` failures or `rhyper`'s `nn` sample length.

1. **Known N,k, estimate m.** Feasible integer interval is b≤m≤N−k+min(x). It is finite, so an MLE always exists (possibly tied or at a support edge). The adjacent likelihood ratio is

   `L(m+1)/L(m) = product_i [(m+1)/(m+1−x_i)] [(N−m−k+x_i)/(N−m)]`.

   Each factor is nonincreasing in m; find the crossing of one by integer binary search, evaluate adjacent candidates and preserve ties. Endpoint zero factors need direct log-PMF handling. k=0 gives complete nonidentification; k=1 identifies only a Bernoulli success proportion on the grid m/N. More generally a finite grid guarantees attainment, not a unique parameter.
2. **Known m,k, estimate N.** N≥m+k−min(x); also require b≤min(m,k). With at least one positive count and m,k>0, likelihood tends to zero as N→∞, so some finite integer maximizer exists. For all zeros, m,k>0, it increases toward δ0 and no finite N attains one. If m=0 or k=0, the all-zero law is already attained. A justified finite search bound or tail certificate is required; stopping a grid at a large N is insufficient.
3. **Known k, unknown N,m.** N→∞ with m/N→p gives Bin(k,p), **Use `mlbinom(x, size=k)`**. A concrete escape is k=2 with data (0,2): its likelihood is C(m,2)C(N−m,2)/C(N,2)^2 < [m(N−m)/N²]^2 ≤1/16 for every finite feasible N, whereas Bin(2,1/2) attains 1/16. The finite-population variance is kp(1−p)(N−k)/(N−1); matching empirical moments is a start, not a global existence criterion. With k=1, many (N,m) give exactly the same Bernoulli law, so a face tie need not imply nonexistence.
4. **All three unknown.** There is an exact interchange HG(N,m,k)=HG(N,k,m), so ordered parameters are nonidentifiable even before limits. Let m,k→∞, m/N,k/N→0, mk/N→λ: factorial moments (k)_j(m)_j/(N)_j→λ^j give the **Poisson** limit, **Use `mlpois`**. Poisson convergence alone does not prove that a given sample escapes there; no samplewise global escape criterion for this unrestricted model was established here. This distinction matters for D1 diagnostics.

**Literature boundary.** [Wang, DOI 10.1080/01621459.2014.966191](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2014.966191) distinguishes known-population/unknown-success and known-success/unknown-population inference. Its abstract supports these practical scenarios, not unrestricted iid three-parameter global MLE claims. [Hodgson and Bzdok (2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hodgson24a.html) studies unknown populations using a differentiable gamma relaxation; that relaxation is not an exact discrete optimizer and should not be adopted as an exact package fitter.

**Fitter sketch and recommendation.** **Add later, S–M for known N,k**: require those two values initially, estimate integer m exactly with the adjacent-ratio search, expose them as fixed parameters and map (N,m,k)→(m,n=N−m,k). O(h) preprocessing, O(distinct counts·log N) search. Report tied maximizers by a documented deterministic convention and warning where appropriate. Known m,k with free N is M once a certified tail bound is supplied. Fixed k with both N,m free needs nested discrete search plus the fixed-k binomial comparison (L). **Skip the unrestricted three-integer version** for now: poor identification, unbounded search, multiple limit scales and no natural generic iid application. Statistical design information is more valuable than making every parameter estimable.
