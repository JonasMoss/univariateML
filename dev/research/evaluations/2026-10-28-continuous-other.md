## Truncated normal

**Reference and maintenance — checked.** extraDistr has all four functions with `mean = mu`, `sd = sigma > 0`, `a`, `b` (defaults `-Inf`, `Inf`); these describe the *parent* normal, not the truncated mean/sd. Its installed metadata reports publication 2026-07-13, Imports Rcpp only (recursive nonstandard count **1**), LinkingTo Rcpp/RcppArmadillo; it is already a reference dependency. The density is normal divided by `Phi((b-mu)/sigma)-Phi((a-mu)/sigma)` inside the interval. The existing hnorm README explicitly documents exclusion of its lower endpoint by this reference. [Reference manual](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/extraDistr/html/TruncNormal.html).

**Numerics — checked installed package.** All examples use `mu=0,sigma=1`. Ordinary `x=.3,a=-1,b=2`: log-density agrees with mpmath to displayed precision (about `2e-16`). For `x=8.5,a=8,b=9`, package log-density is **-2.0988974328**, independent value **-2.0303199398**: about 6.6% density error. For `x=40.01,a=40,b=41`, package returns **NaN**; correct log-density **3.2894534805**. A narrow interval `[.5,.50000002]` at its midpoint has log error about `5.4e-9`.

Round trips used `p={1e-10,.01,.5,.99,1-1e-10}`. On `[-1,2]`, absolute CDF errors are below `2e-16`. On `[8,9]`, the first two quantiles are **7.99157395**, outside support, and the last two are **Inf**; on `[40,41]`, all five are **Inf**. The narrow interval has CDF errors around `8e-9` and rounds extreme probabilities onto endpoints. Stable C++ likelihood alone would therefore leave `dml/qml` inconsistent with it. `truncnorm` offers another all-four interface, no Imports, compiled C, release 1.0-9 (2023-03-20), but was not installed or numerically validated here; it is a follow-up candidate, not an established cure. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/truncnorm/master/DESCRIPTION).

**MLE geometry — derived.** For fixed finite bounds, write

`f(x) = exp(theta1*x + theta2*x² - A(theta1,theta2))`, `theta1=mu/sigma²`, `theta2=-1/(2*sigma²)<0`.

The log likelihood is strictly concave in these natural coordinates, so an interior solution, when present, is unique. It matches the first two empirical moments. Its proper continuous boundary at `theta2=0` is an **exponential tilt of uniform on [a,b]**, `exp(t*x)/integral_a^b exp(t*u)du`; uniform occurs at `t=0`. This is a Hellinger limit, since the normalized densities converge on a bounded interval. It arises with `sigma -> Inf`, `mu/sigma² -> t`, and need not have a finite parent `mu`.

An exact existence test is available. Fit the one-dimensional boundary tilt `t` to `E_t X = mean(x)`. Let `v` be the empirical variance with divisor n, and `v_t` this tilt's variance. The inward derivative in `theta2` at zero is `n*(v-v_t)`. Thus **v < v_t gives an interior MLE; v >= v_t gives only the boundary supremum**, assuming nonconstant observations and mean strictly between bounds. Equality also has no finite negative `theta2` solution. This follows from concavity after profiling `theta1`, not from a variance threshold against uniform irrespective of the mean. Samples concentrated near both endpoints produce nonexistence on an open set, hence with positive probability under a truncated normal.

For `[a,Inf)`, the boundary is `a + Exp(rate)`. Its fitted mean distance is `tau=mean(x)-a`; the analogous test is `v < tau²` for an interior solution, `v >= tau²` for exponential escape. Reflect for upper-only truncation. With no truncation, ordinary normal MLE exists for nonconstant data: use `mlnorm`. For finite fixed `[a,b]`, neither `mlunif(x)` with estimated endpoints nor `mlexp(x)` on `[0,Inf)` represents the general boundary law. Declare the named truncated-exponential limit without inventing an `mlX`; uniform is its `t=0` case. For one-sided escape, `mlexp(x-a)` fits distances but is not a translated fitted object on the original data.

**Unknown bounds.** At fixed parent parameters, shrinking the support while retaining all observations increases every likelihood factor. Therefore estimated bounds are `a=min(x)`, `b=max(x)`, or the corresponding one unknown bound. Then apply the same natural-coordinate analysis. These are boundary estimates, not four regular score equations. With a reference that excludes endpoints, they are only support-bound suprema: use and document adjacent outward floating-point values as hnorm does, or change reference/convention; do not claim exact attainment for that numerical object. Constant data allow support/scale collapse and unbounded likelihood. Two distinct observations with both bounds estimated already force the exponential-tilt boundary (the empirical second moment is maximal for its mean).

**Literature.** [Hegde's 1986 dissertation abstract](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/mathstat_etds/83/) explicitly treats necessary/sufficient observable-moment conditions for singly and doubly truncated normals and cites earlier nonexistence results. Only the abstract was accessible; the explicit criterion above is my exponential-family derivation, not a quotation of its theorem.

**Fitter sketch / decision.** Make `a,b` fixable, preferably required known bounds in the first API; allow fixed parent `mu` or `sigma` through reduced objectives. Use sample mean/sd starts, standardized data, stable log differences of normal CDF/survival probabilities, and safeguarded Newton on moment equations. Hessian is the covariance matrix of `(X,X²)`; solve the boundary tilt first to classify existence. Cost O(n) preprocessing plus small-dimensional moment evaluations. Unknown bounds add endpoint conventions and a different inferential problem. `mlhnorm` is the constrained `a=mu,b=Inf` slice, not a general one-sided truncated normal. **Add later (M/L)** until external evaluation passes tail tests and D1 has an honest boundary message.

## Folded normal

**Reference and maintenance — checked source.** A usable non-VGAM implementation exists: greybox `dfnorm(q,mu,sigma,log)`, `pfnorm`, `qfnorm`, `rfnorm`, for `abs(N(mu,sigma²))`. Restrict fitted `mu>=0`; `sigma>0`. Current mirror metadata: **2.0.11**, published **2026-10-20**, actively updated. The help formula omits `1/sigma`, but the code includes it. [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/greybox/master/R/fnorm.R), [metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/greybox/master/DESCRIPTION).

Imports: stats, generics, graphics, utils, pracma, nloptr, statmod, zoo, texreg, xtable, methods, Rcpp; recursive nonstandard count **16**, including texreg -> httr -> curl/jsonlite/mime/openssl/R6 -> askpass -> sys. LinkingTo Rcpp; nloptr adds a compiled optimization dependency. **No mandatory VGAM**. However, [texreg's metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/texreg/master/DESCRIPTION) lists VGAM under **Enhances**; this optional edge does not install/load VGAM. Thus greybox is appreciably heavier than its two-line density suggests. Other candidates such as generic folding packages were not validated; VGAM is excluded outright.

**Numerics — checked source transcription.** Independent density is `[phi((x-mu)/sigma)+phi((x+mu)/sigma)]/sigma`. Source log-density agrees within `3e-13` at `(x,mu,sigma)=(1,1,2),(40,0,1),(.001,10,1),(100,100,.01)`; in particular it preserves log-density **-800.22579135** at x=40, where ordinary density underflows. Source uses `dnorm(x,abs(mu),sigma,log=TRUE)+log1p(exp(-2*x*abs(mu)/sigma²))` on nonnegative support.

Scalar p/q round trips at the five probabilities above, `sigma=1,mu=0` and 10, have absolute CDF error below `1e-16`. CDF computes `Phi((q-mu)/sigma)+Phi((q+mu)/sigma)-1`, which cancels in the small lower tail: `pfnorm(1e-20,0,1)=0`, true value approximately `7.98e-21`. Quantiles use scalar root solving on `[0,abs(mu)+10*sigma]`, no log-probability or upper-tail interface. Source also exposes a separate array branch; it was not executed here. Acceptable ordinary evaluation, with explicit tail limitations; no claim of exhaustive validation.

**MLE — derived.** For nonnegative x, the stable log likelihood is

`ell = -n*log(sigma*sqrt(2*pi)) - sum((x-mu)²)/(2*sigma²) + sum(log1p(exp(-2*x*mu/sigma²)))`.

Sign symmetry makes unconstrained `mu` unidentifiable. With `mu>=0` and at least two distinct observations, a maximum exists: as `sigma -> 0`, the factor from an observation separated from `mu` kills any inverse-scale divergence; as `sigma -> Inf` the density vanishes; and `mu -> Inf` cannot retain a fit unless scales also diverge. Restricting to a compact level set then gives attainment, including `mu=0`. Constant data have unbounded likelihood at `mu=x,sigma -> 0`; reject negative data, allow zeros with the density's continuous endpoint version.

At `mu=0`, `sigma²=mean(x²)` is the **zero-endpoint half-normal**, an included boundary member if the domain is closed in mu. It is not the existing translated `mlhnorm` fit, whose endpoint is estimated; no D1 pointer to that unrestricted fit is justified. There is no proper normal Hellinger limit on the original x-axis as `mu/sigma -> Inf`: a nearly normal folded density is an approximation, not automatically an escape to `mlnorm`. At zero the mu score vanishes identically, so optimizing log(mu) alone can miss the boundary; information is singular there.

**Profile sketch.** Put `t=mu/sigma>=0`, `m2=mean(x²)`. At fixed t, the scale score is

`sigma² + t*sigma*mean(x*tanh(t*x/sigma)) - m2 = 0`.

It has a unique positive solution: each term `sigma*tanh(c/sigma)` is increasing in sigma, as is sigma². Profile a single t coordinate, include t=0 explicitly, and bracket rather than cap it silently. At interior joint stationary points, the scores also imply `sigma²=m2-mu²` and `mu=mean(x*tanh(x*mu/sigma²))`; these help validation but are not a nuisance profile valid for every mu. Fixed mu or sigma reduces to one dimension. Use half-normal and ordinary mean/sd starts; cost O(n) per nested scalar evaluation. Compare every located stationary maximum; no general uniqueness theorem for the joint fit is claimed.

**Literature / decision.** [Johnson (1962), DOI 10.1080/00401706.1962.10490008](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1962.10490008) studies accuracy of ML estimation; only its abstract was inspected, so the existence/profile statements above are derived here. **Add now (M)** if greybox's mandatory dependency cost is acceptable; retain mu=0 in the model and add numerical checks before integration. This is a real alternative to VGAM, not a claim that no other implementation exists.

## Generalized gamma

**References / parameterizations — checked.** All three candidates have d/p/q/r. For Stacy coordinates `(a,b,k)>0`, `X=a*G^(1/b)`, `G~Gamma(k,1)`, giving `f=b*x^(b*k-1)*exp(-(x/a)^b)/(a^(b*k)*Gamma(k))`. flexsurv `.orig` calls these `scale=a,shape=b,k=k`; ggamma calls them `a,b,k`. The latter's documentation has a misplaced scale inside its quantile formula, but code uses the correct `a*qgamma(p,k)^(1/b)`. [ggamma source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/ggamma/master/R/ggamma.R).

flexsurv Prentice coordinates are `mu=log(a)+log(k)/b`, `sigma=1/(b*sqrt(k))`, `Q=1/sqrt(k)`. Its `dgengamma` extends Q to all real values, with **Q=0 an explicit lognormal member**. For negative Q use a signed power `b=Q/sigma<0`, `k=Q^-2`, `a=exp(mu-log(k)/b)`, and `abs(b)` in the density. This changes the model relative to positive-power Stacy, rather than merely renaming parameters. [Official manual](https://chjackson.github.io/flexsurv/reference/GenGamma.html).

gamlss.dist `d/p/q/rGG(mu_g,sigma_g,nu)` has `mu_g>0,sigma_g>0`, signed nu, `k=1/(sigma_g²*nu²)`, power `b=nu`; map from Prentice as `mu_g=exp(mu)`, `sigma_g=sigma`, `nu=Q/sigma`. Source substitutes lognormal for `abs(nu)<=1e-6`. **mu_g is not generally E(X)**. [GG source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/R/GG.R).

**Maintenance/dependencies — checked.** flexsurv installed/mirrored **2.3.2**, publication 2024-08-17; live documentation identifies 2.3.3, so do not mistake this test for a test of that build. Recursive Imports: **58 packages including base/recommended, 43 excluding them**, including rstpm2/bbmle, deSolve, plotting/tidyverse packages; Depends survival, LinkingTo Rcpp. No VGAM in this installed mandatory tree. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/flexsurv/master/DESCRIPTION). ggamma **1.0.2**, 2025-12-08, pure R, **no Imports**, flexsurv only Suggested: very light. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/ggamma/master/DESCRIPTION). gamlss.dist **6.1-11**, 2026-09-10, compiled; Imports MASS plus base packages only, recursive non-base/nonrecommended count **0**; no VGAM. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/DESCRIPTION). Recent release dates establish activity, not future maintenance guarantees.

**Numerics — checked.** Installed flexsurv log-density against high precision: `(x,mu,sigma,Q)=(2,.2,.7,.6)` agrees at displayed precision; `(.01,.2,.7,-2)` within `3e-11`; `(1e100,.2,.7,-2)` within `3e-13`; Q=0 agrees with lognormal. But at `(1,.2,.7,1e-5)`, log error is **2.20e-5**, and at **Q=1e-8** package log-density is **128**, correct value **-0.6030799158**. This is catastrophic cancellation near the claimed smooth lognormal transition. Five-probability p/q round-trip errors are below `3e-17` for Q=.6,-2,0, about `2.54e-12` at 1e-5 and `1.06e-8` at 1e-8. A good p/q round trip does not validate d.

ggamma source transcription: log-density agrees within `4e-13` at `(x,a,b,k)=(2,3,1.8,.5),(1e-100,1,2,.1)`; at `(1,1,.0001,1e8)` absolute log error is about `5e-6` on a log-density of `-1.742e9`. Ordinary five-probability p/q errors are below `6e-17`. Upper tails use subtraction: `pggamma(100,1,1,2,lower.tail=FALSE)=0`, true gamma tail **3.7573e-42**. Density lacks support guards (`x<0` produces NaN for many powers); at x=0 the formula can also generate `0*log(0)`. Thus it needs upstream quality improvements before serving as the universal reference. gamlss GG was inspected but not executed: it forms powers before taking logs and has inconsistent tail/log handling in the near-zero-nu CDF branch; it is not a verified drop-in repair.

**MLE / limits — derived, with scope limits.** Assume strictly positive, nonconstant data; no additional location/threshold parameter. Gamma (`b=1`), Weibull (`k=1`), exponential (`b=k=1`) and zero-endpoint half-normal (`b=2,k=.5`) are finite members, so do not report D1 escapes merely for landing on them. In Prentice coordinates gamma is Q=sigma and Weibull Q=1; lognormal is Q=0. The positive-power Stacy family excludes lognormal and can approach it; use `mllnorm` on that face. The signed Prentice family includes it and should return Q=0 normally.

There are additional proper limits. Let `b -> Inf`, `k -> 0`, `b*k -> d>0`, `a -> A`: for x<A the density tends to `d*x^(d-1)/A^d`, and mass above A vanishes. This is the **power distribution**, suggesting `mlpower`. With signed `b -> -Inf`, `abs(b)*k -> d`, the reciprocal transformation gives **Pareto** on `[A,Inf)`, suggesting `mlpareto`. These are Hellinger limits (a.e. density convergence to a normalized density). At exactly x=A the naive sequence loses an `exp(-1)` factor; endpoint likelihood comparison must allow A to approach the sample extreme from outside at an appropriate rate. The current mlpower also uses an endpoint offset, so compare the theoretical supremum separately from that offset's likelihood.

The MLE is **not guaranteed**, unlike a fixed-shape gamma fit. Nonconstant data avoid ordinary scale-collapse divergence; the question is finite attainment versus boundary suprema. A complete necessary/sufficient sample classifier for all signed-Prentice escape paths was **not established here**. Do not interpret convergence of a three-parameter optimizer as proof of existence. A small positive-power profile scan for x=(1,2,3), b in `[exp(-3),exp(10)]`, rose toward power: best grid logLik **-2.7228064**, power supremum **-2.7204617**, lognormal **-3.6769786**. This is numerical evidence of escape, not a proof that every finite b is worse. Constant samples have unbounded likelihood; admitting x=0 introduces parameter-dependent singular endpoint densities and should be rejected.

**Exact profile / cost.** Write `y=log(x)`, `D(b)=log(mean(exp(b*y)))-b*mean(y)`. For fixed nonzero signed b, nuisance MLEs satisfy

`log(k)-digamma(k)=D(b)`, `log(a)=(log(mean(exp(b*y)))-log(k))/b`.

The k equation is strictly decreasing from infinity to zero, so has a unique root for nonconstant y. This reduces the full fit to **one outer scalar b search**, with one safeguarded k root, log-sum-exp, and asymptotic expansions near b=0/k=infinity. Include lognormal analytically; compare power/Pareto endpoints and all located profile maxima. Fixed b, k or a are natural options, with simpler profiles where possible. Starts from lognormal, gamma and Weibull fits; O(n) per profile evaluation. No generic three-dimensional BFGS is needed for the unrestricted Stacy/signed-power fit.

**Literature / decision.** [Noufaily & Jones (2013), DOI 10.1007/s00180-012-0314-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-012-0314-4) explicitly finds practical ML computation more favourable than older warnings suggest, while declining convergence/uniqueness theorems; abstract inspected. [Arslan et al. (2025), DOI 10.1007/s00500-025-10498-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00500-025-10498-y) discusses multiple roots and modified likelihood. Its approximated/bias-corrected score procedure is not an exact ML substitute for this package. **Add later (L)**: first choose positive Stacy versus signed Prentice, validate the exact profile/closure, and obtain a light external evaluator with a stable lognormal transition and honest support/tails.

## Exponentially modified Gaussian

**References — checked source.** Define `X=mu + sigma*Z + tau*E`, Z standard normal, E unit exponential, `sigma,tau>0`; `lambda=1/tau`. emg `demg/pemg/qemg/remg` uses `(mu,sigma,lambda)` with mu the **normal component mean**. It is a convolution, despite its package description calling it a mixture. Metadata **1.0.9**, 2020-06-20, pure R, **no Imports**, Depends stats/stats4/moments; moments has no Imports. This is light but shows no recent release. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/emg/master/DESCRIPTION), [manual](https://rdrr.io/cran/emg/man/EMG.html).

gamlss.dist `dexGAUS/pexGAUS/qexGAUS/rexGAUS` uses `(mu,sigma,nu=tau)`, all four present; maintenance/dependencies above. brms offers `dexgaussian/pexgaussian/rexgaussian`, **no q** in its inspected distribution source, and its mu is **E(X)=mu_normal+tau**, beta=tau. It brings Stan and a large regression stack; not pursued as a reference, and its full recursive dependency tree was not audited. [brms source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/brms/master/R/distributions.R), [metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/brms/master/DESCRIPTION).

**Numerics — checked source transcriptions.** Independent log-density is

`-log(tau)+(mu-x)/tau+sigma²/(2*tau²)+log(Phi((x-mu)/sigma-sigma/tau))`.

emg's log form agrees within `4e-13` for `(x,mu,sigma,lambda)=(1,0,1,1),(-40,0,1,1),(0,0,1,100)`. At lambda=1e6,x=0 it loses **1.31e-5** in log-density by cancellation. Critically, its ordinary density at x=0,lambda=100 is **0**, versus **0.398902**: it forms `Inf*0`, then replaces NaN with zero. Thus `log(demg(...))` and `demg(...,log=TRUE)` disagree. [Density source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/emg/master/R/demg.R).

emg p/q round trips for p=.01,.5,.99 and lambda=1,100 have max errors about `2.6e-8`, `3.1e-8`. Its q uses unconstrained minimization of absolute CDF error, not bracketing; **log.p is wrong**: it takes the logarithm of the resulting quantile instead of interpreting input log-probabilities. `qemg(log(.5),log.p=TRUE)` produces NaN in the transcription. Upper-tail CDF subtraction and extreme log-likelihood cancellation remain. [CDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/emg/master/R/pemg.R), [quantile](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/emg/master/R/qemg.R).

gamlss source substitutes `N(mu,sigma²)` whenever **tau <= .05*sigma**, omitting even the mean shift tau. At x=1,mu=0,sigma=1,tau=.01, its log-density is **-1.4189385332**, correct **-1.4089902017**. At x=-40,tau=1 it returns **-Inf**, correct **-804.6331046**, because it takes `log(pnorm(...))` rather than using a log-CDF. p/q share the normal substitution, so round-trip agreement there would validate the approximation against itself. Quantiles use default-tolerance `uniroot` after linear interval expansion; random draws invert it. [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/R/exGAUS.R). These are substantive model/numerical defects for an ML reference, not just optimizer issues.

**MLE / closure — derived.** As tau -> 0 (`lambda -> Inf`), X tends to normal: D1 **use `mlnorm`**. As sigma -> 0 it tends to **translated exponential** on `[mu,Inf)`; its fitted endpoint is min(x), tau=mean(x)-min(x). Existing `mlexp` fits only endpoint-zero distances, so say `mlexp(x-min(x))` for that calculation, not a nonexistent translated-exponential fitter. These convolutions converge in L1/Hellinger to the proper limit densities. At the moving endpoint, do not simply put mu=min(x) at positive sigma: that loses a factor 1/2 for each minimum. Let mu approach the minimum from below by many sigma but a vanishing absolute distance to recover the exponential supremum.

For nonconstant data the likelihood has no ordinary collapse divergence: both scales shrinking concentrates at a single point and forces a separated observation's density to zero; diverging sigma or tau spreads mass and cannot be repaired by a location shift. The closure including normal and translated exponential has the relevant finite candidates. The finite interior MLE is **not assured**; compare it globally with both faces. Constant data have unbounded likelihood with both scales collapsing. Interior parameters are identifiable from cumulants: mean=mu+tau, variance=sigma²+tau², third cumulant=2*tau³; identification becomes weak near normal.

Small multistart probes on standardized data illustrate the comparisons, **not a global theorem/classifier**. For x=(-2,-1,0,1,2), normal logLik is **-7.0946927**, but translated exponential is higher at **-6.7328680**; the tested interior starts remained near normal. For x=(0,.01,.02,.03,5), the optimizer reached its imposed numerical sigma floor and approached the exponential face (**-1.6088666**). A 30-point positively skewed quantile construction had a finite interior fit (**-40.654599**) above normal (**-42.568156**) and exponential (**-43.119203**). Negative empirical skewness gives a locally unfavourable inward normal perturbation, but neither that score nor these bounded multistart searches certify a global face winner. A full positive-probability classification theorem was not verified here; boundary escape is the implementation issue, not “MLE never exists.”

**Fitter sketch / decision.** Use `(mu,log(sigma),log(tau))`, allowing any of the physical parameters fixed; fixing positive sigma removes the exponential face, fixing positive tau removes normal. Starts: `tau0=cuberoot(max(sample third central moment,0)/2)`, capped below sample sd; `sigma0=sqrt(v-tau0²)`, `mu0=mean-tau0`, plus starts near each face. Fit with multistart BFGS, O(n) per evaluation, using log-normal-tail/Mills-ratio or scaled-erfc expansions to avoid cancellation; compare both analytic limit likelihoods. Supply explicit status enums and D1 messages, never exceptions in C++ or a silent shape cap. **Add later (L)** until emg's d/q defects are fixed upstream or another acceptable reference is validated, and the translated-exponential face has an accurate endpoint implementation. A stable C++ objective cannot compensate for broken external `dml/qml`.
