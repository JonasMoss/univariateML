# All-free EMG maxima and the normal face (TASK-346)

**Proved.** The all-free EMG has separated finite local maxima on an exact integer sample and exactly two finite global maxima on an exact real crossing. A finite maximizer can also tie the optimized normal in the closure. Skewness decides the initial direction from the normal face whenever it is nonzero. Zero skewness admits both directions, and even strictly negative skewness and negative excess kurtosis together leave the global normal comparison undecided. No fixed truncation of the sample moment sequence determines that global comparison on real samples.

This extends the earlier held-location, held-scale counterexamples to the all-free problem. The fixed-shape nuisance theorem, two escape values, and strict finite winner certificate are used below. ## Shape and the normal expansion

Use mean log likelihood throughout. For nonconstant data standardize by the empirical mean and the root mean square deviation, with divisor $n$. Write $m_j$ for the resulting empirical raw moments, so $m_1=0, m_2=1$. Let $s^2=\sigma^2+\lambda^{-2}$, $a=\mu+\lambda^{-1}$ and $t=1/(\lambda s)$. The standardized EMG is
$$
W_t=\sqrt{1-t^2}\,Z+t(Y-1),\qquad Y\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1),\quad Z\sim N(0,1),
$$
with independent components. The all-free profile $P(t)$ optimizes $a,s$ for $0<t<1$. Its dimensionless shape in the existing cover is $k=\sqrt{1-t^2}/t$. Write $N=-\tfrac12(1+\log(2\pi))$ and $E=-1-\log(-x_{\min})$ for the optimized normal and translated exponential values on the standardized data. The earlier escape proofs give the endpoint limits $P(0)=N, P(1)=E$.

**Proved.** Take the first nonzero entry of the following ordered list. Its sign is the sign of $P(t)-N$ for all sufficiently small positive $t$. Each row after the first assumes exact zeros in all preceding rows.

| Power of $t$ | Coefficient |
|---|---:|
| 3 | $m_3/3$ |
| 4 | $(m_4-3)/4$ |
| 5 | $m_5/5$ |
| 6 | $(m_6-17)/6$ |
| 7 | $m_7/7$ |

Positive sign excludes the normal as a global winner and provides finite witnesses arbitrarily close to it. Negative sign proves strict normal dominance over a neighborhood of the face. For example, $(-1,1)$ gives a negative quartic, while $(-2,0,0,0,0,0,0,2)$ gives a positive quartic. Both skewnesses are exactly zero.

For the proof let $H_j$ be the probabilists' Hermite polynomials. The log moment generating function of $W_t$ is $u^2/2+\sum_{j\ge3} t^j u^j/j$ near $u=t=0$. Coefficient extraction in the density ratio to $\phi$ replaces $u^j$ by $H_j$, since $\int e^{uz}\phi(z)H_j(z)\,dz=u^j e^{u^2/2}$. Equivalently one can differentiate the Gaussian convolution at $t=0$. The density-ratio coefficients at orders 3–7 are $H_3/3,H_4/4,H_5/5,2H_6/9,(1/7+1/12)H_7$. Taking logs subtracts $H_3^2/18$ at order six and $H_3H_4/12$ at order seven.

At the normal optimum $(a,\log s)=(0,0)$ the nuisance Hessian is $\operatorname{diag}(-1,-2)$. The leading perturbation to the nuisance gradient is $t^3(0,-m_3)$, by $H_3'=3H_2$. Thus optimization changes the log value by $O(t^6)$, or $O(t^8)$ when $m_3=0$. In the cancellation cases $\overline{H_6}=m_6-15$, $\overline{H_3^2}=m_6-9$, and $\overline{H_7}=\overline{H_3H_4}=m_7$. This gives the table, including the value 17 at the sixth-order equality. The nearby nuisance root is the global conditional optimizer by strict concavity in the existing fixed-shape coordinates.

Here is a regularity detail used both for the expansion and for the constructions. On dividing the convolution density by $\phi(z)$, its integral, apart from $1/\sqrt{1-t^2}$, is
$$
\int_0^\infty \exp\!\left(-y+\frac{z^2}{2}
               -\frac{(z+t-ty)^2}{2(1-t^2)}\right)\,dy.
$$
For bounded data, sufficiently small $t$ controls differentiated integrands by polynomials in $y$ times $e^{-y/2}$ on compact nuisance boxes. For the growing samples below, bounds polynomial in $|z|$ suffice instead. For $0<t\le1/4$, put $A=t(z+t)/(1-t^2)-1$. The normalized integrand in $y$ is proportional to $\exp(Ay-t^2 y^2/[2(1-t^2)])$ on $y\ge0$. If $A\le-1/2$, it is stochastically bounded by an exponential of rate $1/2$, by the decreasing ratio of densities. Otherwise $t|z|$ is bounded below by a positive constant. The tilted law is a truncated normal with untruncated standard deviation at most $1/t$. For a nonpositive untruncated mean it is stochastically bounded by the corresponding half normal. For a positive mean its moments are bounded by twice the absolute moments of the untruncated normal. The mean in absolute value is at most $(1+|z|+t)/t+1/t^2$. All the required tilted moments therefore admit polynomial bounds in $|z|$. Any fixed derivative of the log integral is a sum of products of such moments with polynomial factors in $z$ and bounded rational factors in $t$. The bounds also cover derivatives in $a,\log s$ on compact boxes. They justify differentiation at zero and uniform Taylor bounds over families with uniformly bounded empirical moments of every fixed order. A small common box about $(0,0)$ keeps the nuisance Hessian negative definite and its inverse bounded for small $t$. The score is small at its center, and implicit differentiation of the enclosed root gives uniform profile Taylor bounds, including differentiated remainders.

## Exact global crossings on real samples

**Proved.** An exact family of equal-weight samples is defined as follows. For integer $n$, take the $n$ points $z_i=\Phi^{-1}((i-1/2)/n)$, $i=1,\ldots,n$. Group the points in each of the five intervals of radius $1/16$ about $-2,-1,0,1,2$. Add an unknown common displacement to the observations in each group. Use the five displacements on the local inverse branch near zero that prescribes the first five moments. At target $(0,1,0,3,0)$ denote the resulting sample by $X_{n,0}$, and put $D=(17-m_6(X_{n,0}))/6$.

This inverse branch is well defined for all sufficiently large $n$. The midpoint quantile rules converge in all moments. On each monotone tail the sum of the absolute $j$-th powers is bounded by twice the corresponding tail integral, by integrating over the outer half of each cell. In the moment Jacobian the column for each group is an integral of $(1,2z,\ldots,5z^4)^{\mathsf T}$ against its empirical measure on the group. Multilinearity gives the determinant as an integral of a positive Vandermonde times $1\cdots5$, since one point comes from each successive disjoint interval. The limiting Gaussian group masses are positive, so the limiting Jacobian is invertible. The displacement-to-moment maps are polynomials and converge with all derivatives on a neighborhood of zero. The inverse function theorem, or contraction using the inverse limiting Jacobian, gives a common moment neighborhood with a unique local branch and displacements at $(0,1,0,3,0)$ tending to zero. In particular $D\to 1/3$.

For clarity, the global bound needed for this construction is
$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty} \sup_{t\ge\delta} P_{X_{n,0}}(t)<N\qquad(0<\delta<1).
$$
To see this, use the uniform density envelope from the original EMG analysis,
$f(x)\le C\rho^{-1}\exp[-|x-\mu|/(2\rho)]$, where $\rho=\max(\sigma,1/\lambda)$. The empirical mean absolute distance from any $\mu$ is bounded below uniformly by having positive mass in two separated intervals. The envelope then forces a compact range for $\rho$ on any likelihood superlevel. It also bounds $\mu$, using the empirical mean absolute observation. Consider a convergent subsequence of such parameters with $t\ge\delta$. The limiting delay scale is positive. If the limiting Gaussian scale is positive, the density converges pointwise under a quantile coupling $X_{n,0}\to Z$ almost surely. If the limiting Gaussian scale is zero, the same holds away from the exponential endpoint, an event of Gaussian probability zero. The log densities have a common upper bound on the parameter compactum, so reverse Fatou bounds the limiting mean log likelihood above by its expectation under $Z$ at that parameter limit. That expectation is strictly below $N$ by the strict likelihood inequality for the Gaussian population. An EMG with positive delay and positive Gaussian scale has nonzero third cumulant, and the translated exponential has half-line support. This argument precludes any sequence with scores tending to $N$ or above. The same compactness argument gives stability of any strict exterior bound at a fixed $X_{n,0}$ under sufficiently small changes to its observations. At an exponential endpoint of a discrete sample, the upper semicontinuous density uses the full one-sided value and is bounded by the optimized exponential face. In the neighborhood of $t=0$, the sixth coefficient at $X_{n,0}$ is $-D$, with the first five coefficients zero. The uniform Taylor bound therefore gives $P_{X_{n,0}}(t)<N$ on a common punctured neighborhood for large $n$. Together these arguments give a strict global normal winner at the base and a strict gap off any neighborhood of zero.

For the exact real crossing samples, prescribe the moment target
$$
\big(0,1,3D A\epsilon^3, 3+4D B\epsilon^2, 5D C\epsilon\big)
$$
on the same inverse branch. Write $X_n(\epsilon;A,B,C)$ for this multiset and $v=t/\epsilon$. At any sufficiently large fixed $n$, the scaled gain $(P(\epsilon v)-N)/(D\epsilon^6)$ converges to
$$
Q(v)=Av^3+Bv^4+Cv^5-v^6,
$$
with two $v$ derivatives and one moment-parameter derivative on bounded $v$ intervals. This follows by Taylor expansion through order six with the differentiated bounds above. The statement also localizes the global winners. For a bounded set of $A,B,C$, choose $M$ so large that the first three terms of $Q$ contribute in absolute value at most $v^6/4$ for $v\ge M$. The likelihood expansion then bounds $P(t)-N$ strictly below zero when $M\epsilon\le t\le\delta$ for small $\delta,\epsilon$, since the sixth-order coefficient stays close to $-D$ and the seventh-order remainder is $O(t^7)$. The compact exterior comparison excludes $t\ge\delta$, including the exponential face. Therefore every competitor at or above the normal is within $0\le v<M$.

### Two finite global co-winners

Take $B=-231/23, C=126/23$, and vary $A$ about $144/23$. At that central value,
$$
Q'(v)=-6v^2(v-1)(v-36/23)(v-2).
$$
The two peaks at 1 and 2 have height $16/23$. Indeed, $36/23$ is the mean of $v$ under weight $v^2(v-1)(2-v)$ on $[1,2]$, so the integrated derivative between the peaks is zero. The peaks are nondegenerate. In disjoint small windows about 1 and 2 the exact scaled profile for small $\epsilon$ has one root each, by the signed endpoint derivatives and strictly negative second derivatives. Outside the windows the limiting polynomial has a strict gap from the two equal peaks, and the comparison persists uniformly for $A$ in a sufficiently small closed interval about $144/23$. The difference of peak heights, right minus left on the scaled likelihood, has positive $A$-derivative near the crossing, with limiting derivative $2^3-1^3=7$. At the left and right endpoints of the $A$ interval the height differences have opposite signs. The endpoint signs persist at small $\epsilon$. Let $A_*(n,\epsilon)$ be the unique zero between these endpoints.

The exact tie sample is $X_n(\epsilon;A_*(n,\epsilon),-231/23,126/23)$, for every sufficiently large $n$ and sufficiently small positive dyadic $\epsilon$. Its only global maximizers are the two simple roots just described, with their unique nuisance fits. The nearby exact samples with $A$ below and above the crossing have a unique finite winner in the left and right window, respectively. This definition uses exact quantiles, moment equations, and a uniquely specified real height crossing. An equivalent specification of a single member is to take the first $n$ on the local moment branch with $D>0$, $E<N$, and every finite stationary height at the base below $N$. Take disjoint windows $(7/8,9/8)$ and $(15/8,17/8)$, and the first dyadic half-width $2^{-j}$, $j=1,2,\ldots$, about $144/23$ on which the polynomial has simple window maxima above zero, a strict exterior gap below both window maxima, positive height-difference derivative, and opposite endpoint height differences. Then take the first dyadic $\epsilon=2^{-l}$, $l=1,2,\ldots$, for which those same conditions hold on the scaled exact profiles and the moment branch exists throughout the interval. The proofs of strictness and uniform convergence ensure these choices exist. The finite stationary list used in the $n$ predicate is justified below. ### Finite/normal equality and samples with matching moments

Take $A=0, B=-1$, and vary $C$ about 2. At the central value $Q(v)=-v^4(v-1)^2$. The only positive-shape contact with zero is a nondegenerate local maximum at $v=1$. There is a uniform strict gap outside a small window about 1 and away from zero. To include the neighborhood of zero, divide the scaled gain by $v^4$. Its constant coefficient is exactly $-1$, with the remaining terms $O(v)$ uniformly. Thus all sufficiently small positive $v$ lose strictly. The root in the window has scaled $C$-derivative of height tending to 1. Its height is below and above zero at the two ends of any sufficiently small fixed interval about 2, for small enough $\epsilon$. The exact sample $X_n(\epsilon;0,-1,C_*)$ uses the unique $C_*$ in that interval where the window root's value equals $N$. This is a single finite MLE together with an equal, excluded normal face. For $C<C_*$ in that interval the normal is the strict winner, and for $C>C_*$ the finite root wins strictly. All these samples have identical moments through order four. One can also take $A<0$ fixed sufficiently close to zero and choose two fixed $C$'s on opposite sides of 2. The strict signs at the window roots persist by continuity, and the negative cubic and quartic jointly control small $v$. This gives both global outcomes at the same strictly negative skewness and excess kurtosis.

In fact any fixed moment order $r\ge4$ can be retained across the finite/normal switch on real samples. Use the $C_*$ construction with large $n$. Its number of distinct observations tends to infinity since those outside the five groups stay unshifted. At the finite tied root the differential of its optimized height with respect to the observations is the vector of spatial log density scores, divided by $n$, by stationarity. The fixed-first-$r$-moments constraint has full-rank Jacobian by a Vandermonde minor, with row span given by evaluating polynomials of degree at most $r-1$. A spatial EMG log density score is analytic and is never such a polynomial: it tends to $-\lambda$ at the right tail and grows without bound at the left tail, as follows from the Mills bounds or the convolution formula. The definability argument below applies to the one-variable equation equating this score to a polynomial. Its zero set is finite for each choice of EMG parameters and polynomial coefficients, by analyticity. Cell decomposition of the definable family gives a common finite bound on their number for fixed $r$, by counting the cells with point fibers. Choose $n$ so that the number of distinct observations exceeds this bound. Then the height differential has a nonzero component along the moment-constraint manifold. A local curve on that manifold moves the finite height through $N$, with the normal height fixed, the negative quartic neighborhood intact, and the exterior gap and window root persisting. This proves the claim for $r\ge4$, which also rules out smaller fixed cutoffs.

## Exact finite comparison and per-input certificates

**Proved.** For every nonconstant all-free input, the shape profile has finitely many stationary points. For $0<t<1$ it is real analytic. In the fixed-$k$ chart the Hessian in the location and inverse scale coordinates is strictly negative definite. Indeed $\log f_k(z)=\log k+k^2/2-kz+\log\Phi(z-k)$ has strictly negative second derivative in $z$, and the $(x_i,-1)$ span two dimensions. The nuisance score root is unique and the analytic implicit function theorem applies. The derivative $P'$ is definable in the Pfaffian closure of the real field. In that closure the exponential is definable from $y'=y$, and $\Phi$ from $y'=\exp(-x^2/2)/\sqrt{2\pi}$. The other operations and the graph of the unique nuisance root are first-order definable, as is the derivative by its limit formula. Speissegger's theorem gives o-minimality, so the zero set of the derivative on the shape line is a finite union of points and intervals. [Speissegger, The Pfaffian Closure on an O-Minimal Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9710220)

The derivative cannot vanish on an interval. Analyticity would give a constant profile, while the small-$k$ proof in the certificate report gives $P(t)<E$ for small positive $k$. More explicitly, in its free-endpoint argument with CDF argument $h\ge0$ retain the upper bound $E-k^2/2$ per observation. With $h<0$ the bound per observation is $E+(n-1)k^2/2-(\log 2)/n$. Both are strictly below $E$ for sufficiently small $k>0$, while $P(t)\to E$. Hence there are only isolated stationary points and the list is finite. This also implies a strict eventual sign of $P-N$ near zero even if all five tabulated coefficients vanish. Its exact sign in that case can be defined by evaluating $P-N$ at any positive point below the first stationary shape, or on $(0,1)$ if there is none. [Small-shape bounds](2026-11-07-emg-cert-report.md)

An exact decision rule is to compare $N,E$, and all heights in the finite stationary list. The largest value is the supremum. Every finite shape at that value contributes its unique nuisance fit. In particular, a strict normal winner requires $E<N$ and every stationary height below $N$. If $E=N$ with all stationary heights below it, both faces attain the closure value and no finite MLE exists. A stationary height equal to the highest face value gives finite attainment and may coexist with other winning stationary heights. A largest stationary height strictly above both faces gives finite attainment regardless of the local normal sign. The rule can be expressed using the first $n$ raw sample moments and $n$, since Newton identities reconstruct the monic polynomial whose multiset of roots is the sample. The finitely many comparisons are exact mathematical predicates. O-minimality supplies cardinality, while effective root isolation, tail cutoffs, and exact value equality require additional evidence.

For a practical signed normal-tail certificate on the listed moment strata, use the log-integral above and a box about the optimized normal in $(a,\log s)$. Choose $T<1/4$ so that the box's residual bound ensures $A\le -1/2$ throughout $0<t\le T$. Bound derivatives by integrating polynomials against $e^{-y/2}$, and use the contribution from $0\le y\le1$ as a strictly positive denominator bound for log derivatives. All coefficient signs in the table can use exact centered sums or padded sign enclosures, with equality tests for the preceding rows. Choose $T$ and box radius $R$ so that the Hessian is at most $-hI$, $h>0$, and the matched-normal score norm is less than $hR/2$. The maximum stays inside the box and the value is at most the matched-normal likelihood plus the squared score norm divided by $2h$. This correction is $O(t^6)$, or $O(t^8)$ on the exact zero-skew stratum. Thus the signed first nonzero coefficient through the applicable row can be made to dominate an explicit sum of higher terms, Taylor remainder and nuisance correction on $0<t\le T$. A positive sign can use the matched-normal value alone as a lower witness. A negative sign supplies pointwise strict exclusion on the punctured normal tail. The latter must be combined with the opposite-tail bound and a cover of the compact shape range. Padding needs to cover the sign and remainder computations and the original input transformation.

The per-input singleton certificate in the singleton audit remains valid for this family. The Schur complement gives a strictly concave profile there. Merge root regions only when the inclusions establish root identity. Cover both tails and all other finite shape intervals, requiring strict competitor exclusion or a proved equality to an excluded face. Restore the physical coefficients and compare on the original inputs with the existing arithmetic margins. ## What this changes for the fitter

- All-free nonconstant data require a global comparison even when skewness is positive. A positive signed normal-tail test rules out `NormalLimit`. A negative test closes the normal neighborhood when searching for a limit, with the compact and translated-exponential comparisons still required. Use the signed equality ladder only with validated preceding zeros.
- If the normal strictly beats all finite candidates and the exponential face, return `NormalLimit` and name `mlnorm`. A strictly winning translated exponential gives `TranslatedExponentialLimit`. If both excluded faces tie globally and there is no finite winner, report proved nonattainment with both limit witnesses. Constant all-free samples retain the unbounded outcome.
- For a proved unique finite winner use the singleton certificate and the public coefficient tolerance. At finite/face equality return the finite MLE, recording the face as an equal excluded candidate. For a proved finite/finite tie return the complete, coefficient-accurate maximizing set with known multiplicity. In particular the real two-peak crossing has a two-member set, while the real finite/normal crossing has one finite member.
- Where height bounds still overlap, preserve distinct root diagnostics. On exhaustion or unresolved comparison, return the accuracy-checked successful search result as uncertified with uniqueness unknown unless a separated feasible or face witness refutes it. An actual search failure retains its existing failure handling. [Comparison and outcome rules](2026-11-25-ties-math-report.md#what-this-changes-for-the-fitter)
