## 1. Student critical multiplicity

### Criterion and sharp value

**Proved.** For Student use denominator widths \(a,b\) equal to \(\sqrt{\nu}\) times the ordinary side scales. Write \(S=a+b,\ w_-=a/S,\ w_+=b/S,\ z=w_+-w_-\), and
\[
h_\nu=\frac{\Gamma((\nu-1)/2)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(\nu/2)},\quad K_\nu=(\nu-1)h_\nu.
\]
The density at a point on side \(e\) of the join \(c\) is \(K_\nu/S\) times \([1+((x-c)/(Sw_e))^2]^{-(\nu+1)/2}\). The half-kernel moments give
\[
\mu=c+h_\nu zS,\qquad
\sigma^2=S^2\left[\frac{1+3z^2}{4(\nu-2)}-h_\nu^2 z^2\right].
\]
Thus a held mean is not a held join. [Coordinates and prior closure analysis](2026-11-07-skew-theory-data/proofs.md), [stable moment arithmetic](2026-11-08-sstd-moment-report.md).

Let \(k\) be the largest atom count when mean is free, or the count at the held mean. Let \(m=n-k\) and \(q=\nu\) for held df, \(q=2\) for free df. With df and sd both held the total width cannot collapse. Otherwise the test is \(qm-k\). At fixed skew the likelihood contributes order \(S^{-1}\) per tied observation and \(S^\nu\) per separated observation. Negative test gives unboundedness, taking fixed sufficiently small df when free sd allows it, or taking \(\nu\downarrow2\), \(S\asymp\sqrt{\nu-2}\), when sd is held. Positive test makes the collapse likelihood tend to zero, uniformly in skew, since each side width is at most \(S\). Off the atoms, every residual is separated. For df diverging, use ordinary total scale \(S/\sqrt\nu\). If it vanishes on a nonconstant sample, one separated residual beats the height bound: \((1+u^2/\nu)^{-\nu/2}\) decreases in \(\nu\) at fixed standardized ordinary residual \(u\), so use any finite lower df greater than \(n\). The mode tends to the held mean along such an ordinary-scale collapse. These are the same multiplicity tests as in the current held search. [Shared search](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/sstd/hold_search.hpp).

At equality \(k=qm\) the critical atom \(b_0\) is unique. Put \(d_i=x_i-b_0\), let \(m_e\) count its non-ties on side \(e\), and define \(J_q(v,z)=[1+(v/w_{\operatorname{sign}v})^2]^{-(q+1)/2}\) with \(J_q(0,z)=1\). The sharp collapse likelihood is
\[
C=K_q^n\prod_{d_i\ne0}|d_i|^{-(q+1)}
\max_z\{w_-^{(q+1)m_-}w_+^{(q+1)m_+}J_q(v,z)^k\}. \tag{1}
\]
Evaluate the braces at the held skew if applicable. Use \(v=0\) for free mean and \(v=h_qz\) for held mean. A zero exponent contributes one. At skew endpoints the held-mean tied residual lies on the broad side, so its factor extends continuously. For free mean the maximizing free weights are \(m_e/m\).

For the proof, extract the powers \(S^{\nu m-k}\) and the side weights from the non-tie kernels. The remaining non-tie factors are \([(d_i+b_0-c)^2+S^2w_e^2]^{-(\nu+1)/2}\), converging uniformly even on the disappearing side. A weight approaching zero with a positive count only reduces the bound. The tied kernel factor is at most one for free mean and equals the factor shown for held mean. At free shape \(S^{(\nu-2)m}\le1\) eventually. It tends to one with held sd, and with free sd one can choose \(S\to0\) with \((\nu-2)|\log S|\to0\). The sharp value is achieved in the limit by the exact join \(b_0\) when mean is free and by the moment equation otherwise. This proves the value against all vanishing-width paths. A positive-width \(t_2\) face is a separate competitor only when sd is free.

### Which model wins

**Proved, symmetric fixed df.** For held mean, the log-scale score strictly decreases from zero at equality. For free mean, the earlier geodesic theorem proves the same unattained supremum, since \(q>2\) excludes the balanced Cauchy exception. For unit skew (1) becomes \((K_q/2)^n\prod|d_i|^{-(q+1)}\). At free df the symmetric \(t_2\) comparison is just the lower shape end. For example, the free std sample \((-1,0,0,0,0,1)\) selects collapse by its proved decreasing profile. The ordinary fitted normal on \((0,0,1)\) has likelihood \((3/(2\sqrt{\pi e}))^3>1/8\), so collapse loses there. [Fixed-df theorem](2026-11-07-std-held-data/derivation.md#joint-critical-atom-at-held-shape-at-least-one), [free-df comparison](2026-11-25-tcert-math-report.md), [std critical example](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/std/README.md).

**Proved, fixed df, free mean/skew, critical extreme atom.** Replacing the two widths by \(S\) bounds each density by twice the symmetric Student density of denominator width \(S\). The symmetric critical result strictly bounds the product below \(K_q^n\prod |d_i|^{-(q+1)}\). With all non-ties on one side, (1) reaches that bound by allocating the limiting width to that side. Hence collapse wins.

**Proved, fixed-df asymmetric examples.** Use ordinary common scale \(s\), \(\kappa_-=\xi^{-1},\kappa_+=\xi\), and \(M=(\xi-\xi^{-1})E|T_\nu|\). With mean held, \(c=\mu-Ms\). At equality the ratio to (1) is \(\prod_{d\ne0} F_d(s)^{-(\nu+1)/2}\), with
\[
 F_d(s)=\frac{(d+Ms)^2+\nu\kappa_{\operatorname{sign}(d+Ms)}^2s^2}{d^2}
 \frac{\kappa_{\operatorname{sign}d}^2}{\kappa_{\operatorname{sign}(d+Ms)}^2}. \tag{2}
\]
Its log ratio near zero is \(-(\nu+1)Ms\sum 1/d+O(s^2)\). For \((0,0,0,1)\) hold \(\mu=0,\nu=3\). With \(\xi=10\), \(F_1(s)>1\) for all \(s>0\), so collapse wins. With \(\xi=1/10\), \(M<0\), so small positive scale beats collapse. Continuity and the zero-likelihood large-scale end prove attainment.

For an exact tie, hold \((\mu,\nu,\xi)=(0,3,10)\) and use six zeros and \(-1,d_*\). Let \(G(s)=F_{-1}(s)\). On \(G(s)<1\) let \(v(s)>0\) solve
\[
 1+2Mv+(M^2+3\xi^2)v^2=1/G(s),\qquad d_*=\min_{G(s)<1}s/v(s).
\]
For small \(s\), \(G(s)=1-2Ms+O(s^2)\), giving \(s/v(s)\to1\). The function is continuous at the join and diverges at positive endpoints of \(G<1\), which is bounded. At \(s=1/M\), using \(M^2>100\), \(G(s)F_{1/2}(s)=3(9+1200/M^2)/(100M^2)<1\). Thus the minimum defining the sample is attained, positive, and below \(1/2\). For \(G(s)<1\), \(F_{d_*}(s)\ge1/G(s)\) with equality at a minimizing \(s\). For \(G(s)\ge1\) the product exceeds one. This proves a finite global maximum at the collapse likelihood.

**Proved, an attained winner with df free.** For \((0,0,1)\) hold \(\mu=0,\sigma=1/100,\xi=1/10\). At \(u=\nu-2\downarrow0\), \(s\asymp\sqrt u\), and (2), the extra power \(s^u\), and smooth moments give leading log difference \(-3Ms+O(u|\log u|)\) relative to (1), with \(M\) evaluated at two and negative. Thus the collapse limit loses. At the normal end the total ordinary width lies in \([\sigma,2\sigma]\), the join is less than \(0.008\) in absolute value, the peak is less than 80 and the density at 1 is bounded by \(80e^{-1200}\). But (1) gives \(C>1/(8\cdot101^3)\). Hence the normal face also loses, and the finite scalar df domain attains a global maximum.

**Proved, decision rule.** Compare (1) with the finite domain and every other reachable face. Report collapse if it attains the greatest limiting value and no finite parameter attains that value. A finite point tying collapse is an attained maximizer. Different equal-valued excluded faces are tied limits if there is no finite winner. At fixed df this has an exact sample test in real arithmetic. Raise likelihood divided by \(K_q^n\) to \(2/(q+1)\). Because \(n=(q+1)m\) the result is \(S^{-2m}\) times a product of rational factors. Split at the observation joins, add the affine mean equation and the linear held-skew equation if applicable, and clear positive denominators. The likelihood image, its supremum and attainment are real polynomial quantifiers in the data and model constants. Separate half and collapse witnesses label excluded ends. This is an exact decision in those constants, not a practical prescription for general root isolation. If only scale is free, the TASK-252 polynomial scale scores give the cheaper way to enumerate roots and joins before comparing (1). [Scalar score derivation](2026-11-25-tcert-math-report.md#fernandezsteel-sstd), [prior asymmetric scale counterexample](2026-11-06-sstd-cert-report.md).


## 2. SGED held width and a free mean

Let \(p=\nu,\ t=1/p,\ g_j=\log\Gamma(1+jt)\). Now \(S=a+b\) is the sum of the GED kernel side scales, \(w=a/S,\ z=1-2w\), and
\[
 k=\Gamma(2t)/\Gamma(t),\quad v=\Gamma(3t)/\Gamma(t),\quad
 D=3v-4k^2,\quad W=\sqrt{v+Dz^2}.
\]
The moments are \(\mu=c+kzS,\ \sigma=SW/2\), the log height is \(-\log S-g_1\), and the penalties are the \(p\)-th powers of the side-standardized absolute residuals. [Constrained coordinates](2026-11-08-sged-held-report.md), [earlier free-shape analysis](2026-11-08-sged-free-report.md).

**Proved, exact contact.** Let \(L,H\) be the sample extremes with counts \(A,B\). Write \(R=H-L=2\sqrt3\sigma>0\). Project the join onto \([L,H]\), which cannot increase the loss. If \(u_L=(c-L)/(Sw)\) and \(u_R=(H-c)/(S(1-w))\), then \(wu_L+(1-w)u_R=R/S\). Convex minimization with this constraint gives the minimum two-extreme penalty for \(p>1\),
\[
 P_p(w)=\frac{(R/S)^p}
 {[w(w/A)^{1/(p-1)}+(1-w)((1-w)/B)^{1/(p-1)}]^{p-1}}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the multiplier equations make \(u_L,u_R\) proportional to \((w/A)^{1/(p-1)},((1-w)/B)^{1/(p-1)}\). At fixed \(w\), the minimizing join tends to \(L+wR\), and each interior observation has limiting ratio strictly below one. The log-gamma series gives \(\log(R/S)=-\gamma t+C_z t^2+O(t^3)\), \(C_z=(2+3z^2/2)\zeta(2)\), uniformly in \(|z|\le1\). Substitution into (3) gives the sharp held-skew face
\[
 \ell_U(w)=-n\log R-e^{-\gamma}(A/w)^w(B/(1-w))^{1-w}. \tag{4}
\]
At unit skew this recovers the optimized GED cost \(2e^{-\gamma}\sqrt{AB}\).

With skew free the answer instead is
\[
 \ell_U^*=-n\log R-e^{-\gamma}\min(A,B). \tag{5}
\]
Weighted Jensen gives \(A u_L^p+B u_R^p\ge\min(A,B)(R/S)^p\), uniformly in skew. For sharpness let the side of the more frequent endpoint disappear, for example \(w=o(t)\) and \(c=L\) when \(B\le A\). The only non-vanishing penalty is at \(H\), with limit \(Be^{-\gamma}\). The symmetric construction applies when \(A\le B\). Equal counts admit both paths with the same limiting uniform law. Equations (4)–(5) are likelihoods **along** contact paths. The uniform density evaluated at both closed endpoints has log likelihood \(-n\log R\).

**Proved, uniform tail comparisons.** Put \(\Delta=g_3+g_1-2g_2\). The peak excess relative to \(-\log R\) is
\[
 C(t,z)=\tfrac12(g_3-3g_1)+\tfrac12\log[1+3z^2(1-e^{-\Delta})].
\]
The trigamma series gives \(0\le\Delta\le\zeta(2)t^2\) and \(0\le(g_3-3g_1)/2\le(9/4)\zeta(2)t^2\), the latter by the GED peak bound. Therefore \(C(t,z)\le(15/4)\zeta(2)t^2\). Also \(g''(u)\ge\zeta(2)/(1+u)^2\) termwise in the series, so \(\log(R/S)/t\ge-\gamma+2\zeta(2)t/(1+3t)^2\). The free-skew tail consequently satisfies
\[
 \ell-\ell_U^*
 \le(15/4)n\zeta(2)t^2-
 e^{-\gamma}\min(A,B)\,2\zeta(2)t/(1+3t)^2. \tag{6}
\]
A small explicit cutoff strictly excludes the whole finite tail against (5). [GED tail bound](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/ged/README.md).

For held skew set \(X=\log(w/A)\) with probability \(w\), and \(X=\log((1-w)/B)\) otherwise. Expanding the log of the weighted exponential sum in (3) gives
\[
 \log P_p=-\gamma-E X+t(C_z-\operatorname{Var}(X)/2)+O(t^2).
\]
The peak correction is order \(t^2\). A positive linear coefficient excludes the tail relative to (4). A negative one proves that a sufficiently large finite shape beats it. If the coefficient vanishes, use the first nonzero Taylor term of \(nC(t,z)-P_p(w)-[\ell_U(w)+n\log R]\). This analytic function is nonconstant. For equal values of \(X\) its linear term is nonzero. Otherwise, near \(t=1\), factor the dominant exponential from (3). The dominant part is positive and analytic at one, and it is multiplied by \((1+v_0e^{-\delta t/(1-t)})^{-(1-t)/t}\), where \(v_0,\delta>0\). This factor differs from one by a nonzero flat term. A constant relative profile would force the analytic part to be equal to that constant to all orders at one, and then identically near one, contradicting the flat term. Checked Taylor remainders turn separated signs into cutoffs. The endpoint-only profile bounds the full profile above, and evaluation at the endpoint-optimal join bounds it below up to an exponentially small interior penalty. This proves that the first nonzero Taylor term controls either comparison.

**Proved, selection is data-dependent.** One copy of \(L\) and 1000 copies of \(H\) at exact contact and held unit skew give \(C_z-\operatorname{Var}(X)/2=2\zeta(2)-(\log1000)^2/8<0\). There are no other escape faces at held skew, so a finite maximum is attained. To refute the face even for free skew, use \((-1,0\text{ repeated }100\text{ times},1)\), held \(\sigma=1/\sqrt3\), and the feasible normal member \(p=2,\xi=1,c=0\). Its log likelihood is \(51\log(3/(2\pi))-3>-102\log2\). In the opposite direction the complete comparisons in the numerical section certify that \((-1,1)\) at exact contact selects (4) with held unit skew or (5) with free skew. In general compare the sharp face against finite fits and, when skew is free, finite-shape half faces. A finite candidate at the same value is attained if no competitor is higher.

**Proved, binary input distinction.** For positive binary floating-point \(R\) and \(\sigma\) interpreted as exact dyadics (taking the range difference exactly), \(R^2=12\sigma^2\) is impossible because \(\sqrt3\) is irrational. Rounded equality can still occur. Compare the exact squared quantities, using a sign enclosure with exact fallback if desired. If \(R>2\sqrt3\sigma\), the extreme penalty tends uniformly to infinity as \(p\to\infty\). If \(R<2\sqrt3\sigma\), choose fixed finite skew and a join with all observations strictly inside the limiting support. Its exponentially small penalties are dominated by the positive order-\(t^2\) peak excess, so finite shape beats the unpenalized uniform height. Near contact that witness may require a very large shape. The sharp discontinuous contact penalty cannot be substituted under a rounding tolerance.

## 3. SGED held sd and two distinct observations

**Proved previously.** With shape and skew held, the two-point maximizing join for \(p>1\) is
\[
 j=\frac{\log(A/B)+2p\log\xi}{p-1},\qquad
 c=L+\frac{R}{1+\exp(j)},\qquad \mu=c+kzS .
\]
The positive derivative balance is \(A(c-L)^{p-1}/a^p=B(H-c)^{p-1}/b^p\). Strict convexity gives uniqueness. For stable evaluation use a displacement from the nearer endpoint of \(R/(1+\exp|j|)\) and log-space computation when needed. This is the existing TASK-150.9 solution, including for \((0,10^{100})\), \(\sigma=1,p=1.01,\xi=100\). The remaining archived stop had log likelihood of order \(-10^{513}\), outside binary64. [TASK-150.9 proof and validation](2026-11-22-sged-loc-report.md).

For \(p=1\), compare \(A/a\) and \(B/b\). The larger left slope puts the join at \(L\), the larger right slope at \(H\), and equality gives the entire interval. For \(0<p<1\), the loss is strictly concave between knots. Compare \(B(R/b)^p\) at \(L\) and \(A(R/a)^p\) at \(H\), retaining both when equal. These are two-knot specializations of the existing fixed-shape certificate. When either shape parameter is free, (3) and the formulas at or below one remove the join exactly. Cover the remaining scalar or two-dimensional profile including its eligible ends. As Section 2 shows, freeing shape may result in an unattained optimum. ### Compact boxes and half-face bounds

**Proved.** On compact shape intervals eliminate held sd using the moment variance, and held mean using the join equation. With free mean project the join into the sample range. Truncate free total width above using the inverse-width height bound. At small width with held mean, the join differs from it by at most a constant times width, so any unequal observation has a separated residual. With free mean on nonconstant data at least one extreme is half a range away. For SGED a single such power penalty beats the height, uniformly in skew on compact shape intervals. For Student, use disjoint atom neighborhoods and their non-tie distances, which give a positive power at strict masses. At critical masses compare (1) instead. Constant samples with free scale and attainable all-ties collapse are unbounded.

On a box enclose raw residuals, side scales and height. Use lower residual magnitudes and upper side scales for lower penalties, and take the lesser endpoint power at any nonnegative ratio when GED power varies. For Student use the lower shape exponent times the lower log penalty in denominator widths. Split at joins or use zero for a lower residual magnitude in crossing terms. On smooth boxes use the TASK-252 score, Taylor and root-inclusion scheme with side-scaled kernels and derivatives of the moment substitutions. Individual Student gradients agree at the joins and the Hessian has finite one-sided bounds away from zero widths. For GED use the convex conditional score to enclose all conditional roots over a cell. At subunit shape with free join use the knots. With held mean include the join strata in the value cover. Recover the whole winning coefficient enclosure in public units.

For a vanishing side on a compact positive total-width and shape interval, a feasible incumbent and the upper height bound bound each individual penalty. Thus the absolute residual on that side cannot exceed its width times a computable constant. If \(P=n\log(\mathrm{height}_{\max})-I\ge0\) for a feasible log likelihood \(I\), use the maximum of \(P^{1/p}\) over the GED cell, or \(\sqrt{\operatorname{expm1}(2P/(1+\nu_{\min}))}\) for Student. At the right half face impose \(c\le L+O(a)\) before dropping the left penalty and bounding the broad objective. For free mean the best right half join is \(L\), by kernel monotonicity. For mean held and sd free keep \(c=\mu-kS\) at the half face (use \(h_\nu\) for Student). At its support contact, increasing total width by a vanishing positive amount moves the join to the supported side. Reflection gives the left result.

For both moments held and shape fixed, the right half contact is forced. At \(c_0=\mu-k\sigma/\sqrt{v-k^2}\) the observation at \(c_0\) has limiting narrow-side ratio \(kv/[2(v-k^2)]\). To bound it, with \(c(z)=\mu-kzS(z)\) divide \(c(z)-c(1)\) by \(1-z\) using the integral of \(-c'\) along the segment. Since \(S=2\sigma/W\) and \(-c'=2k\sigma v/W^3\), the endpoint ratio is \(2kv/W(1)^2\), as asserted. The same formula applies to Student with \(v=1/(\nu-2),k=h_\nu\). This removes the apparent zero over zero and bounds the contact score too. A datum strictly unsupported at the half face forces zero limiting likelihood. If shape varies with skew and both moments are held, a moving contact can reach the broad side or approach at any allowed width rate. Dropping its penalty gives an upper bound, and sharp classification can require a coupled comparison. [Earlier fixed-shape contact formulas](2026-11-07-sged-skew-report.md), [SSTD contact analysis](2026-11-08-sstd-free-report.md).

### Additional signed shape tails

**Proved, SGED with both moments held.** Let \(r=\sqrt3\sigma,\ U=\sqrt3W,\ V=2k\). Since \(\Delta\ge0\), \(U\ge\sqrt{3v}\ge V\). An exact contact \(x-\mu=er\) has ratio
\[
 (U+ezV)/(1+ez)=V+(U-V)/(1+ez)\ge V .
\]
The trigamma lower bound gives \(\log V=g_2-g_1\ge-\gamma t+3\zeta(2)t^2/[2(1+2t)^2]\). Each contact therefore costs at least \(e^{-\gamma}\) plus a uniform positive linear correction in \(t\), while peak excess is order \(t^2\) by (6). With \(N_c>0\) contacts and no exterior points, the sharp face is \(-n\log(2r)-N_c e^{-\gamma}\), attained in the limit at any fixed skew, and an explicit small cutoff excludes every finite shape in the tail. If a point is outside, its ratio has the additional term \((|x-\mu|/r-1)U/(1+ez)\), bounded away from zero as \(t\to0\). The tail vanishes. If all points are strictly inside, large finite shape beats uniform. This settles the upper likelihood bound even though individual joint paths can have different penalties. [Earlier coupled-corner calculation](2026-11-08-sged-free-report.md#held-moments-joint-endpoint-penalties-depend-on-the-path), [stable GED arithmetic](2026-11-14-sged-tail-report.md).

**Proved, SGED with mean and skew held, sd free.** Let \(\rho=\max |x_i-\mu|>0\) and use a farthest observation with sign \(e\). If \(P\) is the total penalty, its signed residual divided by its designated width is at most \(P^t\), including a negative signed residual. Hence \(\rho/S\le w_eP^t-ekz\) with \(w_e=(1+ez)/2\). Relative to the proposed uniform face \(-n\log(2\rho)\), the likelihood is at most
\[
 n\log(2w_eP^t-2ekz)-ng_1-P
\]
on the positive-argument domain. For \(P\le\sqrt t\), bound \(P^t\) by \(\exp[(t/2)\log t]\). With fixed \(w_e>0\) the negative term of order \(t|\log t|\) dominates the \(O(t)\) peak terms. For \(\sqrt t<P\le1\), the peak excess is at most \(O(t)\), dominated by the penalty. For \(P>1\) use \(P^t\le1+t(P-1)\) by concavity and \(\log(1+y)\le y\), so the penalty dominates uniformly for small \(t\). Taylor bounds on \(k,g_1\) give cutoffs. The face is reached by \(S=2\rho(1+d)\) with \(d\downarrow0,\ d/t\to\infty\), keeping mean and skew fixed.
