## Proof criterion

**Proved.** Take a feasible likelihood lower witness L. A global maximizer must have value at least L. It cannot belong to any cell whose upper bound is strictly below L, or any cell where a necessary condition for a maximum fails. A smooth interior maximum must be stationary even at an artificial subdivision boundary. Support knots, seams and attained domain faces require their own necessary conditions or value comparisons. A nonempty compact superlevel and upper semicontinuity give existence. A strict upper bound below L on every escape end suffices for compact containment if those ends exhaust the ways of escaping. When attainment is already proved, pointwise strict loss of every finite competitor suffices even if an unattained limit has the winner's value.

If all possible maximizers lie in a convex region with strict concavity, at most one exists by the strict inequality on the segment joining any two distinct maximizers. A contraction of an invertibly preconditioned score map into a closed box proves one stationary point there by the fixed-point theorem. A coefficient box that only localizes stationary points does not count them. For a profile, these arguments carry to the full problem when each surviving slice has one nuisance optimizer. A positive left score, negative right score and strictly negative upper curvature give one profile root by continuity and strict monotonicity. Two such concave intervals that overlap have the same root because the derivative decreases strictly on their union. An endpoint of the same continuous profile inside a signed concave slab loses strictly to its interior root.

For distinct root regions, compare each losing maximum-value upper bound against the winning feasible lower bound. A point-value evaluation error alone does not bound the change from a numerical representative to the root. For a representative m, root radius r, bound S on the absolute score at m, and curvature upper H on the bracket, Taylor's theorem bounds the root value by the upper point value plus S r + max(0,H) r²/2. Strict concavity allows the last term to vanish, or one can use the two endpoint tangents. The same proof applies to arithmetic comparisons widened by the repository's practical margins. A positive allowed objective gap has a different meaning.

## GPD

**Proved.** With threshold held, let M be the positive maximum excess, v the excesses divided by M, b=expm1(t), and D the mean of log(1+bv)/b with its continuous zero value. The joint conditional fit is (sigma,xi)=(MD,bD), and the gain per observation relative to the fitted uniform is h=−log D−bD−1. The exponential at b=0 is an interior profile member. For b<0 with A=−bD≥1, the constrained conditional optimum is instead uniform with sigma=−M/b>M for finite t. Its value is strictly below the attainable uniform at sigma=M, including at A=1. Consequently the omitted negative tail can have upper bound zero and still lose strictly at every finite point. The certificate establishes that branch from a padded lower bound on A. If the omitted tail has a positive upper, the finite winner must exceed it. The analytic positive-tail and held-scale negative-tail score signs exclude maxima there. [Prior report](2026-11-05-gpd-cert-report.md) · [Profile, tails and exact construction](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gpd/README.md)

A per-input sufficient condition is a closed cover with one signed strictly concave root enclosure, and a feasible lower witness strictly beating every other root upper, the applicable omitted tail upper, and the uniform whenever attained. In the joint fit, a positive witness in the winning concave interval also proves root feasibility. If the root lay on the infeasible side of A=1, the intervening crossing would lie uphill from that witness by strict concavity and stationarity, yet its gain is negative. For the uniform winner require all retained root uppers strictly below zero and the pointwise negative-tail exclusion above. A fixed scale at least M gives the unique uniform exactly when M>0: xi>−1 gives density at most 1/sigma everywhere and strictly smaller at each positive excess in support. For fixed scale below M the scalar signed-cover rule applies with no uniform candidate. With all excesses zero and scale held the entire fitted shape interval maximizes. Supported constant-sample free-threshold fits give an interval of thresholds, with an additional shape interval at the observation when shape is free. These are typed sets. With shape held, the scale is unique by the previous monotone-score or endpoint proof.

The exact counterexample is (a*,a*,1), where a* is the attained maximum over k>−1 of

    a(k) = expm1((3k − log(1+b(k)))/2) / b(k),
    b(k) = k exp(1+k),    a(0) = (3/e−1)/2.

The dossier proves attainment at a finite nonzero k*. The equality h=0 gives the above expression. For a two-point sample (a,a,1), D strictly increases with a and h strictly decreases with D whenever 1+bD>0. This is because the respective derivatives are 2/[3(1+ba)] and −(1+bD)/D. Hence every admissible profile value at a* is at most zero, including b=0 by continuity and the strict inequality a*>a(0). The uniform and the interior member (exp(−1−k*),k*) both have total log likelihood zero. The certificate's strict root-versus-uniform comparison therefore cannot succeed on that exact sample. This proves at least two maxima and does not require a unique k*. It also gives an independent sample condition: for a*<a≤1 the uniform is unique, by strict inequality on every admissible slice and the strict pointwise inequality on all constrained-uniform slices. For sufficiently small decreases below a*, the old interior witness beats the uniform by strict monotonicity and persistence of feasibility. Its dominance over other roots needs the cover.

**Numerical.** At 80 decimal digits the reproducer checks k=−0.995,−0.990,…,8.000 and refines the largest grid peak. It checks gain and score to 10⁻⁷⁰ and strictly negative second derivative. The resulting k, a and sigma are approximately 0.580819888441923564, 0.078806755923837291 and 0.205806290813611985. Moving the smaller excesses by −10⁻⁵ and +10⁻⁵ gives local stationary total log likelihoods 0.00012568462038 and −0.00012565925482 relative to uniform zero. The numerical grid and refinement do not count the exact maximizers defining a*.

## Fatigue

**Proved.** With beta held and threshold fixed, the positive RMS expression is the unique alpha maximum. A zero RMS gives unbounded likelihood. With beta free, the cover excludes score-definite intervals and positive score-derivative crossings. For a covered decreasing crossing, signed endpoints and a strictly negative derivative of the scaled score isolate one maximum. A complete cover with one such root gives uniqueness, provided the exclusion also accounts for endpoints. With several roots, the final comparisons use point likelihoods with a 10⁻¹⁰ n margin and log-beta distance at most 10⁻⁹ as a proxy for identity. Neither checks the whole competing root value or counts distinct roots. Save the signed brackets, use the Taylor bound with the score and curvature of the actual log likelihood, and compare the upper bounds. The near-constant offset rescue remains local.

For an exact constant shifted sample y>0 and held alpha, put u=log(beta/y), w=cosh(u). Up to a constant the mean log likelihood is ½log(w+1)−(w−1)/alpha². This uses cosh²(u/2)=(1+cosh u)/2 in the Jacobian and 2 sinh²(u/2)=cosh u−1 in the exponent. The objective is strictly concave in w≥1 and its derivative is 1/[2(w+1)]−1/alpha². For alpha≤2 it uniquely maximizes at w=1, giving beta=y. For alpha>2 the unique optimum in w is alpha²/2−1>1, giving exactly the two scales y exp(±arcosh(alpha²/2−1)). The equality alpha=2 gives one maximizer even though its curvature in u vanishes there. Near-constant data do not satisfy this exact test.

## Value-gap covers

**Proved limitation.** L≤ell*≤L+epsilon for positive epsilon allows a strictly better finite point, as well as tied maxima. Even an exact optimal value alone does not count its maximizing points. Tukey lambda's scalar boxes and shape-only cover and the half-t shape cover use deliberately positive value gaps. Skellam's joint cover may select a zero-rate face within a gap.
