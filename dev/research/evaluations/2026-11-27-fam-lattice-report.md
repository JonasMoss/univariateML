## Skellam interior fits and stationary Poisson faces

The old joint search could find an interior maximum and check its local coefficient accuracy without settling global optimality. It also left the zero-score Poisson face for `(0, 3, 3)` uncertified and stopped on the `wide` snapshot when the paired-count polish could not handle the rates. The new certificate covers the mean-constrained profile with interval scores and curvatures. It evaluates positive Bessel continued fractions at moderate rates and centered Riccati bounds for large rates. A negative tail score excludes the infinite tail. Strict signs exclude stationary points or identify minima, and strict negative curvature with opposite score signs isolates each interior maximum. Positive paired-count series with geometric remainder bounds compare the isolated peaks and any feasible face that can still win. The certificate uses outward binary64 padding for arithmetic and practical allowances for library evaluations. [Profile bounds and exclusion proof](2026-11-25-skellam-math-report.md#2-an-interior-certificate)

The face derivative is classified by checked rational arithmetic when the integer products fit. An exactly zero derivative is resolved locally only when the curvature has strict sign on a cell reaching zero. For `(0, 3, 3)`, the curvature per observation is `-1/5` and the certificate excludes the rest of the profile, giving the unique Poisson face at `(2, 0)`. Seven zeros, forty ones and nine eights also give an exact zero derivative at mean two, but their inward curvature is `1/90` and refutes the face. For the mean-zero, single-positive-magnitude case, the Lean uniqueness theorem allows an isolated interior root to close the certificate. The atlas wording now includes the single-magnitude condition explicitly. ## Discretized normal at tiny held scales

For `(-2, -1, 1)` at held scale `1e-151`, the old projected score's error allowance of \(n\sigma\) was too wide for the location tolerance. The remote projected edges cancel exactly, which can be checked by accumulating integer edge totals on either side of zero. In units \(q=\mu/\sigma\), remote cells then contribute \(-n_{\rm remote}q\), with error at most \(2n_{\rm remote}\sigma\) on the bracket. For a remote edge at distance \(d\), the conditional inward displacement is at most \(\sigma^2/d\). Indeed, the standardized normal excess above \(a=d/\sigma>0\) has survival bounded by \(e^{-ay}\) at excess \(y\), and truncating at the opposite edge can only reduce its mean. The two cells at zero contribute half-line normal scores with negligible outer-edge terms on the bracket. Strict concavity and endpoint signs enclose the unique maximizer. The original projected search remains available when these comparisons cannot close.

The regression above gives \(q\approx-0.306713042463949369\), the root of \(-2q-\phi(q)/\Phi(-q)=0\) for the limiting score. Its reflection gives the opposite sign. At the smallest subnormal scale, the same histogram proves a tolerance failure. The shared binding now sends the same binary64 location-resolution message to R and JavaScript. At an exact cell edge with subnormal scale, the standalone density now standardizes the endpoints directly, returning a half-cell mass. The fitter uses that same density. The C++ and R tests cover the density, the near-zero estimate and the representability failure. The public WebAssembly smoke check confirmed the shared message. All 79 dnorm snapshots per mode match the baseline. ## Open

- Dnorm still needs a uniform floating-point enclosure for endpoint probabilities and derivative ratios. For the binary-exact interval \([32,32+2^{-20}]\), a 100-decimal reference gives a log cell mass near \(-526.781897403154\). The compiled native and double-only densities have relative natural-mass errors of about \(1.94\times10^{-9}\) and \(-1.78\times10^{-9}\). Reconstructing the endpoint score subtraction gives relative errors of comparable magnitude. These results show the amplification from subtracting closely spaced tail values. A uniform certificate needs validated tail-primitive errors and a cancellation-aware enclosure for the interval mass and its derivatives. The existing coefficient checks retain their practical margins. [Reproduction](2026-11-27-fam-lattice-data/dnorm-endpoints.py)
- General Skellam uniqueness on integer samples remains open. The implemented cover needs strict comparisons, so it can leave stationary points with vanishing curvature or nearly equal candidate values unresolved. At very large competing peaks the positive-series comparison can also exhaust its budget or numerical range. Resolving those cases requires a sharper score or value enclosure, or an exact equality proof for a tie. [Integer near-ties and open equality cases](2026-11-25-skellam2-math-report.md#open)
