# Exact maximising sets for discrete data

TASK-413 · Base revision cb3a93e2

All likelihoods are joint over a nonempty integer histogram. Let \(n\) be its total frequency, \(M\) its largest count for nonnegative data, \(\mu\) its mean, \(V\) its variance with divisor \(n\), and \(f_j=E_{\rm emp}[(X)_{\underline j}]\) its falling factorial moments. Use \(L\) for a likelihood product and \(\ell=n^{-1}\log L\) for the average log likelihood. Nonattainment gives an empty finite maximizer set. A face that matches an attained value supplies no extra finite coefficient vector. Sizes are unbounded unless an explicit domain cap is specified. A working search limit is not a domain cap. ### binom held all free

**Proved.** For an all-zero histogram take the union \(\{N=0\}\cup\{p=0\}\). For a positive constant the only winner is \((M,1)\). Otherwise \(p_N=\mu/N\). The Lean dispersion theorem gives the empty set at \(V\ge\mu\). When \(V<\mu\), strict profile branches from DeRiggi's continuous-size result make the first nonpositive forward increment the smaller winner. For \(S=n\mu,\ F=nN-S,\ G=F+n\), compare the integers
\[
N^{nN}G^G,\qquad (N+1)^{nN}n^nF^F\prod_i(N+1-x_i).
\]
Their ratio is \(L_{N+1}/L_N\). A strict negative comparison ends the search at \(N\), while equality returns both sizes with their own fitted probabilities. No nonadjacent ties are possible. This terminates because a finite mode exists and the decreasing branch is strict. For a declared finite cap stop at its upper end if no crossing has occurred, including for a positive-mean histogram whose unbounded fit escapes. [Lean existence and profile graph](2026-11-27-lean-binomsize-report.md) · [Integer comparison and prime valuations](2026-11-25-binom-ties-math-report.md#which-adjacent-sizes-can-tie)

**Proved exclusion at prime-power \(N\).** In the prime-valuation test of the preceding reference, let \(t=N+1\), let \(\mu=a/b\) be reduced, and put \(u=bN-a,\ v=bt-a,\ r=v/b\). The pairwise coprime \(u,b\) are supported on primes of \(N\). The integer-mean exclusion rules out \(b=1\), so at a prime power \(N\) one needs \(u=1\) and \(r=1+1/b\). At the prime \(p\) of \(N\) the valuation equation gives \(N^N/t\le b\le N^N\), using \(E v_p(t-X)\le\log_p t\). For each prime \(q\) of \(t\), the forced exponent of \(v\) is \(\lceil N v_q(t)/(1+1/b)\rceil=Nv_q(t)\) if \(b>Nv_q(t)-1\). The lower bound on \(b\) implies this for \(N\ge4\), since \(N^N/(N+1)> N\log_2(N+1)\). For \(N=2\) it gives \(b\ge2\), and for \(N=3\) the valuation bound gives \(b\ge9\). Both suffice. Thus \(v=t^N\), contrary to \(v=b+1\le N^N+1<t^N\). Size 1 has only binary feasible data and no allowed \(b>1\) in the valuation test.

**Exact finite search.** The necessary prime filters left zero proposed means for adjacent pairs with lower size 1 through 500, with arbitrary sample length. This extends the previous scan through 100. Existence of a nonconstant positive-mean tie at a composite lower size with at least two prime factors remains open. ### bbinom held alpha

**Proved full unbounded decision for integer hold.** Hold \(a=A\in\mathbb N_{>0}\) and write \(b\) for beta. Zero data give \(N=0,\ b>0\), and positive constants have an empty set. Mixed binary data attain saturation only at \(N=1,\ b=a(1-\mu)/\mu\). For any other histogram and integer \(N\ge M\),
\[
p_0=\frac{(b)_A}{(b+N)_A},\qquad
\frac{p_x}{p_0}=\frac{(N)_{\underline x}(a)_x}{x!(b+N-x)_x},
\]
where \((y)_j\) is rising. The formula defines a rational function for real \(N\ge M,\ b>0\). Its supremum in \(b\) is finite for each such \(N\). As \(b\to\infty\), all positive-count masses vanish. At \(b\to0\), the only possible denominator zero comes from \(N=x>0\) and cancels against \(p_0\). Use real-closed-field quantifier elimination to define the graph of this supremum \(H(N)\) by upper bound and arbitrarily close approach. Semialgebraic decomposition partitions the real size axis into finitely many algebraic endpoints and intervals of strict increase, strict decrease or constancy. On each bounded interval only its extreme integer in the indicated direction can win, or every integer if constant. Include each integer endpoint. On a decreasing unbounded interval use its first integer. On an increasing one only its limit enters the supremum comparison. A constant unbounded interval contributes the complete integer ray. The integer tail limit is the fitted fixed-shape NB height, algebraic for integer \(A\). At every winning integer recover the unique conditional \(b\) by polynomial root isolation. This procedure decides even an identically flat tail or equality to the face. It makes no assumption of unimodality over sizes. [Conditional score and closure](2026-11-27-uniq-atlas-math-report.md#bbinom-held-alpha)

**Proved second-order test for general holds.** At \(\mu>0\) and \(V=\mu+\mu^2/a\), put
\[
B= -\frac{2a^3 f_3-2a^3\mu^3+3a^3\mu^2+4a^2\mu f_3-4a^2\mu^4+
3a^2\mu^2-9a\mu^4+2a\mu^3-5\mu^4}{12a(a+\mu)^2}.
\]
The optimized average log likelihood differs from its NB face by \(B/N^2+O(N^{-3})\). To derive it put \(c=(a+b)/N\) with limiting optimum \(c_0=a/\mu\). The expansion of \(\log\Gamma(z)-\log\Gamma(z-a)\) through \(z^{-2}\) has coefficients \(a,-a(a+1)/2,-a(a+1)(2a+1)/12\) multiplying \(\log z,z^{-1},z^{-2}\). Apply it at \(cN\) and \((c+1)N\) in the log zero mass. Add the log count-ratio corrections from the finite factors \((1-j/N)/(1-(a+j+1)/((c+1)N))\), \(0\le j<x\). If these two resulting coefficients are \(A_1(c),A_2(c)\), profiling adds \(A_1'(c_0)^2/(2I)\) to \(A_2(c_0)\), where \(I=\mu/[c_0(c_0+1)]\) is the face information. The expansion is uniform with derivatives in a compact neighborhood of the optimum by the gamma-ratio expansion and finite log factors. Face uniqueness and the closure bounds confine competing optima to this neighborhood, where the score equation gives the \(O(1/N)\) optimizer displacement. The symbolic replay gives the displayed coefficient. At \(B>0\) there is a strict witness. At \(B<0\) all sufficiently large sizes lose, and uniform remainder bounds plus the face deficit away from \(c_0\) give a cutoff. The examples \((0,3),(0,4),(0,5)\) at holds \(3,2,5/3\) respectively have coefficients \(-1/12,0,1/6\). Equidispersion forces a rational hold, so a finite prefix in these branches can be compared algebraically with its face.

At any algebraic hold, finite-size height comparisons are algebraic. For noninteger holds, finite tail comparison still uses the strict-witness or signed collar bounds. An adjacent pair or a nonadjacent pair is tested by the same height equality and global exclusion, and no nonconstant cross-size co-winner example or universal exclusion is known. The zero quadratic case needs another tail discriminator outside the integer-hold algorithm. [Symbolic and numerical replay](2026-11-27-uniq-discrete-math-data/check.py)

### bbinom held size

**Proved exact full set.** Infeasibility is \(M>N\). At \(N=0\) all positive shapes maximize compatible data. At \(N=1\) mixed data give the empirical-proportion ray and constants give only an excluded endpoint. At \(N\ge2\) endpoint-only samples give an excluded saturated law. With an interior count the attained branch is \(V>\mu(1-\mu/N)\). There, use
\[
p_x(a,b)=\binom Nx\frac{(a)_x(b)_{N-x}}{(a+b)_N}.
\]
The graph of the argmax is the formula \(a,b>0\) and \(L(a,b)\ge L(a',b')\) for all \(a',b'>0\). Clear positive denominators and use real-closed-field quantifier elimination. In an identifiable attained case the stationary candidates form a finite algebraic set. At each positive dispersion the unique mean is an analytic algebraic branch, and its concentration score cannot vanish identically because the attained branch has strict score signs at its ends. A zero-dimensional semialgebraic solution set can be isolated and the joint products compared exactly. This decides cardinality and the entire set at every fixed size. Uniqueness follows for \(2\le N\le8\), and for larger sizes in the endpoint-rich histogram class of the curvature proof. General singleton status beyond those classes remains open. **Proved exact sufficient escape test.** All zeros give an empty set. For \(\mu>0\), look for a supporting line to the log objective in mass space at the Poisson face. Define \(D(t)=e^{\mu-t}P(t)\), where \(P(t)=\sum_x w_x(t/\mu)^x\) and \(w_x\) are histogram proportions. If \(D(t)\le1\) for all \(t\ge0\), the attained set is empty. Every BNB is a nondegenerate Poisson mixture. Integrate \(D\) and apply \(\log z\le z-1\) to its sample mass ratios. The inequality is strict since \(D\) is nonconstant and the mixing law has positive density. To decide the sufficient condition exactly, test the endpoint zero and every nonnegative real root of \(P'-P\). At a candidate \(t\ne\mu\) with \(P(t)>0\), equality would contradict Hermite–Lindemann, since \(t,\mu,P(t)\) are algebraic. Interval refinement of the exponential therefore terminates for these candidates. At \(t=\mu\) equality is exact. This strengthens the sign-change test by checking all critical values. For any histogram on two adjacent counts \(P(t)=t^j(A+Bt)\) with \(A,B>0\), so \(\log D\) is strictly concave on \(t>0\), and \(t=\mu\) certifies the condition. The same holds on positive constants. ### bnbinom held alpha

**Proved unique diagonal fit for binary data with a positive count.** Fix \(a>0\) and let \(\lambda=p_1/p_0=rb/(a+r+b)\). Euler's gamma product gives, with \(s=r+b\),
\[
-\log p_0=\sum_{j\ge0}\log\!\left(1+\frac{\lambda(a+s)}{(a+j)(a+s+j)}\right).
\]
For fixed \(\lambda>0\), every summand with \(j>0\) increases strictly in \(s\). The binary objective \(p_0^n\lambda^{n\mu}\) therefore chooses the smallest feasible \(s\), which is the unique solution of \(s^2/4=\lambda(a+s)\), so \(r=b=t\). There \(\lambda=t^2/(a+2t)\) increases strictly. For \(c=a+j\), differentiate the corresponding summand of \(-\log p_0\) with respect to \(\log\lambda\) to get
\[
\frac{t}{c+t}\frac{t}{c+2t}\frac{a+2t}{a+t}.
\]
All three positive factors increase strictly. The derivative series is locally uniformly convergent by the \(O(j^{-2})\) terms. Consequently the binary log likelihood score in \(\log\lambda\) decreases strictly and has exactly one zero by the positive-superlevel compactness theorem for held alpha. This also covers all ones. All-zero data have no finite maximum. The unique binary root is specified by a strictly monotone special-function equation, which permits certified bracketing and determines cardinality. A general algebraic representation of that root is not supplied. [Euler product and compactness](2026-11-25-bnbinom-math-report.md#what-can-be-proved-about-canonical-uniqueness) · [Held-alpha compactness](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/bnbinom/README.md#scalar-certificates)

**Proved tied labels on a two-observation histogram.** At \(a=2\) and sample \((0,200)\), Lean excludes all diagonal shapes by comparison with \((r,b)=(1,100)\) and proves existence of a distinct swapped pair at the maximum. The golden diagonal exclusion is a separate example. These proofs leave the number of winning orbits on their respective histograms open. On arbitrary positive-count data there is a compact canonical search and the orbit rule above. No rational-function reduction follows merely from integer \(a\). In particular the factor \(\Gamma(a+r)\Gamma(a+b)/[\Gamma(a)\Gamma(a+r+b)]\) involves both free shapes even at that hold. ### bnbinom held beta

\[
p_0=\frac{(a)_{B_0}}{(a+r)_{B_0}},\qquad
p_x=p_0\frac{(r)_x(B_0)_x}{(a+r+B_0)_x x!}.
\]
All sample objectives are rational in the free variables. Use the universally quantified maximizing inequality over the positive quadrant and clear positive denominators, as for held-size beta-binomial. Quantifier elimination returns the entire semialgebraic set and tests its emptiness and cardinality, without needing a strict gap to the face. It can also compare the fitted NB height, algebraic for this hold. The prior analytic scalar profile and compactness criteria prove finiteness of the relevant stationary set under strict finite improvement or strict relative underdispersion. They still leave exact equality between general noninteger held-shape heights unresolved. The empty set on all zeros is direct. [Fixed-beta mixture closure and compact profile](2026-11-27-uniq-atlas-math-report.md#bnbinom-held-beta)

**Proved second-order comparison.** In the same notation as the held-alpha beta-binomial section, now use \(a_0=b\) for the fixed NB size and evaluate its coefficient \(B\) with \(a=a_0\). Suppose \(V=\mu+\mu^2/a_0\). In the mixture chart \(T=r+\alpha,\ c=r/T\), put \(c_0=\mu/(\mu+a_0)\). The optimized average log likelihood has inward coefficient
\[
\ell_T-\ell_{\rm NB}=\frac{(a_0+\mu)^2 B}{\mu^2 T^2}+O(T^{-3}).
\]
Indeed \(p_0=\Gamma((1-c)T+a_0)\Gamma(T)/[\Gamma((1-c)T)\Gamma(T+a_0)]\), and \(p_x/p_0=(cT)_x(a_0)_x/[(T+a_0)_x x!]\). Expand each log gamma ratio through \(T^{-2}\) and each finite-factor log through its square term. If the two coefficients of the resulting expansion are \(C_1,C_2\), the profile adds \(C_1'(c_0)^2/[2(a_0/(1-c_0)^2+\mu/c_0^2)]\) to \(C_2(c_0)\). ### cmb held all free

**Proved exact finite profile characterization on the supported domain.** Use \(M\le N\le 10^7\). All zeros give \(N=0\) with any \(p,\nu\), and \(N>0,p=0\) with any \(\nu\). Positive constants give \(N=M,p=1\) with any \(\nu\). Mixed binary data give \(N=1,p=\mu\) with the full dispersion line. A mixed two-point sample on \(\{0,M\}\) with \(M\ge2\), or on two positive adjacent counts, has no finite winner by the excluded saturated face. Otherwise the empirical sufficient statistic is interior for every feasible size. At each size match \(E[X]=\mu\) and \(E[\log\binom NX]=E_{\rm emp}[\log\binom NX]\). Strict covariance gives a single pair at each size. The full set is exactly the graph of all sizes at the highest of these values. Adjacent and separated candidates use equality of the same profile heights plus dominance over the remaining sizes. General exact arithmetic for the values of the free-dispersion roots is open. [Convex hull, caps and uncapped faces](2026-11-25-cmb-math-report.md#free-size)

**Proved saturation checks.** If the histogram has positive frequencies \(n_j\) at every \(0\le j\le M\), \(M\ge2\), put \(r=(n_M/n_0)^{1/M}\). It saturates at \(M\) exactly when \((n_j/n_0)/r^j=\binom Mj^\nu\) for every interior \(j\), for a single \(\nu\). Its unique maximizing probability is \(r/(1+r)\). Any size \(N>M\) puts mass outside the observed support, and the minimal exponential family at \(M\) identifies both parameters. At \(M=2\) every full-support histogram satisfies the test with \(\nu=\log[(n_1/n_0)/r]/\log2\). At \(M=3\) the rational test is \((n_2/n_1)^3=n_3/n_0\), with \(r=n_2/n_1\) and \(\nu=\log[(n_1/n_0)/r]/\log3\). At any \(M\), decide the existence of a rational dispersion by factoring \(R_j=(n_j/n_0)^M/(n_M/n_0)^j\) and comparing all of its prime exponents to those of \(\binom Mj\). They must be proportional with the same ratio \(M\nu\), including zero exponents. This detects all rational-dispersion saturations, with exactly specified coefficients even when \(r\) is irrational. The unrestricted-size case beyond such pointwise saturation needs an effective CMP-face comparison.

### dgamma held all free

**Proved finite candidate set.** For a constant or a histogram supported in two adjacent cells, the Lean attainment criterion gives no finite winner. For range at least two, the unique profiled rate \(r(a)\) is analytic in shape \(a>0\). Its profile score has strictly positive and negative tails respectively at small and large shape, by the existing tail bounds. On the compact interval between these tails the analytic score is nonzero as a function and has finitely many distinct zeros. Take all zeros with greatest profile height and restore \(r(a)\). This characterizes ties as equal highest critical values. A terminating exact comparison for that list, including degenerate zeros, remains open. [Lean attainment](../../lean/UnivariateML/Family/AllFreeDGamma.lean#L829) · [Score tails and analytic nuisance root](2026-11-25-dgamma-math-report.md)

**Proved uniform integer histogram class.** On two cells \(0,K\) let \(w\) be the observed zero fraction. For each fixed \(0<w<1\), sufficiently large \(K\) gives a unique maximum. In particular \(1/4\le w\le3/4,\ K\ge2^{28}\) suffices. In the existing trace test take \(\bar k=(1-w)K,\ m_*=w+(1-w)K\). Then \(d_*=\log m_*-(1-w)\log K\ge w\log K+\log(1-w)>3\) on that explicit rectangle, so the shape cutoff \(A=1/d_*<1/3\). The trace denominator is \(G(A)>25/32\), its zero-cell contribution in the numerator is at most \(3/4\), and \(B_K\le5/K\) from the rate bracket. Thus the trace bound is at most \((3/4+25/(36K^2))/(25/32)+1/(3K)<1\). For each fixed \(w\), the bound tends to \(w\) as \(K\to\infty\), giving the other assertion. This establishes an exact singleton by a sole bracketed shape score root and its conditional rate. [Trace criterion](2026-11-25-dgamma2-math-report.md#a-sample-only-curvature-check)

**Proved arithmetic obstruction.** On positive-count data a stationary rate at a positive integer shape \(a\) cannot be algebraic. Write \(A_k(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}(kr)^j/j!\). The cell probability is \(e^{-kr}(A_k-e^{-r}A_{k+1})\). At fixed algebraic \(r>0\) its sample rate score is rational in \(Y=e^{-r}\) over the algebraic numbers. This rational function is nonzero because its value at \(Y=0\) is \(E_{\rm emp}[-k+A'_k/A_k]<0\). The term is zero at \(k=0\) and negative for every positive \(k\). A score zero would make \(Y\) algebraic, contrary to Hermite–Lindemann. The obstruction is on integer-shape slices and gives no general height-equality test.

### hyper held all free

**Proved exact unbounded procedure.** For constant \(c>0\), the complete set consists of \(k=c,m=N\) or \(m=c,k=N\) with \(N\ge c\). At \(c=0\) it consists of \(k=0\) or \(m=0\) with feasible \(N,k,m\). For mixed binary data the empirical proportion \(p=a/d\) in lowest terms is attained exactly at \((N,k,m)=(jd,1,ja)\) and its design swap for positive integers \(j\). To see this, saturation requires support exactly \(\{0,1\}\), hence smaller design count one, and its Bernoulli ratio must equal \(p\). This gives nonadjacent population ties. For example \((0,1)\) attains on every even population with smaller count one and larger count half the population. More generally a sample on exactly \(c,c+1\), \(c>0\), can saturate only with smaller design count \(c+1\), larger count \(N-1\), and lower count frequency share \((c+1)/N\), from the two support endpoints. If the share gives such an integer \(N\ge c+2\), return precisely that design and its swap. Otherwise use the general procedure below. For nonconstant samples write \(s=\min(k,m)\) and \(Q=L_{\rm Pois}(\mu)>0\). If \(V<\mu\), compute the highest binomial height \(F>Q\) by the [binomial rule](#binom-held-all-free). The uniform large-\(s\), large-\(N\) dossier envelope drops strictly below \(F\) at computable cutoffs \(S,N_0\). Compare the finite grid \(N<N_0,\ s\ge S\) and the finitely many held-small-count profiles for \(M\le s<S\). At each fixed \(s\), the rational residue-class calculation determines its supremum and its complete finite-parameter set, even if the result includes lattice rays tied with a binomial face. Include the symmetric designs without double-counting the seam. The global supremum is the largest slice or grid height, with \(F\) included as a reachable face. Take all attained points and rays at that height. This decides the entire set even when there is no strict witness above \(F\). For \(V\ge\mu\), every fixed-size binomial face is strictly below \(Q\). The following uniform expansion bounds the whole jointly growing corner below \(Q\), including at exact equidispersion. Let \(u=1/k,\ v=1/m,\ h=u+v,\ \lambda=km/N\). Then on a compact positive \(\lambda\) interval, for small \(h\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\ell-\ell_{\rm Pois}(\lambda)
  &=-\tfrac h2\{(\lambda-\mu)^2+V-\mu\}+J_\lambda(u,v)+O(h^3),\\
J_\lambda(u,v)
  &=\tfrac{\lambda^2uv}{2}-\lambda^3\bigl((u^2+v^2)/3+uv/2\bigr)
   -(u^2+v^2)(f_3/6+f_2/4)\\
  &\quad-\lambda uv(f_2/2+\mu)+\mu\lambda^2h^2/2 .
\end{aligned}
\]
For the zero mass sum \(\log(1-w_j)\) over \(0\le j<k\) with \(w_j=\lambda u/(1-j/N)\). Expand in \(w_j\) through degree three, and expand the denominators \(1/(1-j/N)^i\) through degree \(3-i\) for \(i=1,2,3\). The exact sums of \(1,j,j^2\) give the displayed zero-mass quadratic \(\lambda^2uv/2-\lambda^3[(u^2+v^2)/3+uv/2]\). The count-ratio factors relative to \(\lambda^x/x!\) are \((1-ju)(1-jv)/(1-\lambda h+(j+1)\lambda uv)\), \(0\le j<x\). Their log expansions through degree two give the remaining terms. To make the remainder effective take \(\lambda\le U\), \(Uh\le1/8\), and shrink \(h\) so all count-ratio factors have log arguments within \(1/2\) of one. Then \(j/N\le Uv\) in the zero sum, \(w_j\le2Uu\), and the log remainder there is at most \(2\sum_j w_j^4\le32U^4u^3\). The rational denominator Taylor remainders have bounds by constants times \(\sum_{i=1}^3 U^i u^{i-1}(Uv)^{4-i}\) by the geometric series and its first two derivatives on \([0,1/8]\). Finite count logs have cubic errors bounded by the same geometric log remainder on \([-1/2,1/2]\). All retained sums and neglected polynomial terms can therefore be bounded with rational constants depending only on \(U,M\).

For \(V>\mu\) the first-order coefficient is uniformly strictly negative. At equality \(f_2=\mu^2\), and size-biased Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(f_3\ge\mu^3-\mu^2\). Hence
\[
J_\mu=(u^2+v^2)\{(\mu^3-f_3)/6-\mu^2/4\}-\mu^2uv/2
   \le -\mu^2 h^2/12 .
\]
A small fixed rational neighborhood of \(\mu\) preserves a strict negative quadratic bound uniformly in \(u/h\in[0,1]\). Outside it, the Poisson height has a strict deficit on the compact rate interval. This proves strict tail loss there too, by the uniform remainder. To bound the rate initially, choose any positive rational lower bound for \(Q\). A positive observed count and Markov's inequality exclude sufficiently small \(\lambda\). For \(\lambda>M\), \(\operatorname{Var}(X)\le\lambda\) and Chebyshev give \(p_x\le\lambda/(\lambda-M)^2\) at every observed cell, excluding sufficiently large \(\lambda\). This makes the full growing-corner cutoff effective. For the finitely many \(s<S\), use their strict binomial-face deficits from \(Q\) with the fixed-size population bounds. The resulting finite grid contains every candidate at or above \(Q\). Rational grid heights differ from \(Q=e^{-n\mu}\) times a positive rational, so interval comparison with \(Q\) terminates. If the grid best beats \(Q\), return every rational co-winner. Otherwise the finite set is empty.

This decides arbitrary adjacent or separated proposals by comparison to the computed set. It allows infinite ties on lattice rays and uses finite exact rational comparisons or root bounds with proven strict termination. R_m=\prod_i\frac{(m+1)(N+x_i-m-k)}{(m+1-x_i)(N-m)}.

### skellam held all free

**Proved finite candidate characterization.** Reflect if needed to make the sample mean \(d\ge0\). On the profile the rates are \((t+d,t)\). For \(t>0\) put \(z=2\sqrt{t(t+d)}, H=\sqrt{z^2+d^2}\); solve \(\sum_k w_k zI_k'(z)/I_k(z)=H\), where \(w_k\) are magnitude frequencies. Include \(t=0\) when the Poisson face is compatible. **Proved exact class of one-sided singletons.** For nonnegative counts and \(d>0\), let \(g_x(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^x/x!\). Suppose \(D(\lambda)=\sum_x w_x g_x(\lambda)/g_x(d)\le1\) on \([0,\infty)\). This condition is decidable by the [polynomial critical-value test](#bnbinom-held-all-free), and holds on any histogram on adjacent nonnegative counts by log concavity of \(\lambda^j(A+B\lambda)e^{-\lambda}\) at its stationary point \(d\). Given a negative-side Poisson draw equal to \(j\), the conditional masses at the observed cells are \(g_{x+j}(\lambda)\). Since \(g_{x+1}(\lambda)=\int_0^\lambda e^{-(\lambda-y)}g_x(y)\,dy\), the supporting sum for every shift \(j\ge1\) is obtained by repeated convolution with the nonnegative exponential kernel, whose integral on \([0,\lambda]\) is strictly less than one. ## Open

- **Binomial and beta-binomial size ties.** The binomial arbitrary-length valuation system has no survivor on the checked range or at prime-power lower sizes. A composite-size solution of the valuation and frequency constraints, or a proof excluding it, is missing. In the beta-binomial free-size masks, pairwise algebraic comparison does not answer whether any nonconstant histogram has equal globally winning size profiles. The cited free-shape examples establish separate local peaks. The all-free NB equality needs an unconditional arithmetic separation or an equality proof, and the Poisson case with both prior signed coefficients zero needs a stronger uniform bound. For held noninteger alpha, a zero quadratic coefficient needs a further uniform collar or another cutoff. For fixed size above eight, negative curvature at all remaining stationary configurations or an exact multimodal example is missing.
- **Beta-negative-binomial canonical cardinality.** The binary held-alpha proof uses the likelihood's dependence on only \(p_0,p_1\). Further count ratios vary along its fixed-\(\lambda\) paths, so their products need a new ordering argument. A full proof of one winning canonical orbit, or an exact sample with equal top heights in two orbits, remains open. At noninteger held size or beta, neither the signed collar nor the unique conditional scalar root gives an exact tie decision for the stationary heights. At a vanishing signed coefficient the noninteger tail may also need a higher-order exclusion.
- **Conway–Maxwell–binomial.** The real held-dispersion crossing leaves the indices of other co-winners and rational representability unresolved. General all-free profiles beyond the saturation classes involve powers of several independent binomial bases. Positivity of the covariance at each design gives no between-design height ordering or exact comparison. The unrestricted model additionally needs an effective CMP comparison through equality.
- **Discretized gamma and Skellam.** A global proof for dgamma needs curvature or another root-count bound on the remaining histogram classes, or an exact histogram with separated equal winners. For Skellam, the separated real-weight coexistence proofs do not decide whether the coexistence weights can be rational. A general exact height-equality test at isolated special-function roots, or an arithmetic exclusion of equality, remains missing for both families.
