# Attainment in the fifteen one-sided atlas regimes

TASK-424 · Base revision 61bfcadf

Use likelihood products for counts and average log likelihoods for continuous data unless specified otherwise. Samples are nonempty and in the stated data domain. For laws on the positive half-line, comparisons in \(y=\log x\) omit the same Jacobian \(-\bar y\) at every finite point and face. Write \(\mu,V\) for empirical mean and variance of count data, with divisor \(n\).

**Comparison rule.** Let \(B\) be the largest reachable escaping likelihood, with the data and holds fixed. If \(B\) is finite, an admissible finite value equal to \(B\) suffices for attainment: either it is global or there is a strict witness. A maximizing sequence above a strict witness cannot escape. A subsequence therefore attains the maximum by continuity or upper semicontinuity on the finite domain. Conversely every finite maximum reaches \(B\). When the finite supremum equals \(B\), the question is whether an actual finite candidate has that value. A sequence of finite values increasing to \(B\) does not provide such a candidate. At smooth interior points the search may be restricted to stationary candidates, with included restrictions and support boundaries handled separately. This is the local compactness form of the [closure lemma](../ml-behaviour.tex#lem:closure). Exact comparison in real arithmetic and a terminating exact equality algorithm are different deliverables.

### bbinom held all free

**Proved.** On all zeros, only size zero with arbitrary positive shapes attains one. A positive constant \(M\) escapes to \(\delta_M\) at size \(M\). Mixed data with maximum one attain only at size one with shapes proportional to \((\mu,1-\mu)\). For mixed data supported on \(\{0,M\}\) with \(M\ge2\), only the excluded endpoint mixture at \(M\) can saturate the empirical masses. These conclusions use the likelihood bound by the saturated empirical mass product, with equality only at the empirical pmf. [Elementary cases and closure](2026-11-25-bbinomfree-math-report.md#fixed-size-ordering-and-the-active-face)

For the remaining samples put \(M=\max x_i\). For each \(N\ge M\), compute the supremum \(H_N\) and its attainment flag using the held-size theorem. If \(V\le\mu(1-\mu/N)\), the height is the fitted binomial, excluded at finite shapes. For the strict reverse inequality the positive-shape height is attained, is algebraic, and can be computed by polynomial elimination of the rational mass objective. The following rule uses only the attained heights.

- At \(V<\mu\), use the largest unbounded-size binomial profile height \(B\), and compare only the attained heights for \(M\le N<\lceil\mu^2/(\mu-V)\rceil\). Attainment holds iff at least one is \(\ge B\). The binomial profile has a finite peak and strictly exceeds Poisson.
- At \(V=\mu\), all finite sizes attain their shape optimum. Attainment holds iff some \(H_N\) **strictly** exceeds the Poisson height at \(\mu\). Equality is impossible since the latter height is a positive rational times \(e^{-n\mu}\), which is transcendental.
- At \(V>\mu\), all finite sizes attain their shape optimum. Attainment holds iff some \(H_N\) reaches the fitted finite-size NB height. If one equals it and none beats it, that size attains the maximum.

The bounded-size closure is exhaustive because a subsequence fixes \(N\), and beta mixing with mean \(p\) and concentration \(s\) has the binomial limit at \(s=\infty\), the two-endpoint mixture at \(s=0\), and a point mass at either endpoint of \(p\). For \(N\to\infty\), the count-mass subsequence theorem gives NB, Poisson, or a possibly defective multiple of \(\delta_0\). Only the first two can have positive likelihood when a positive count was sampled. All binomial, NB and Poisson candidates in the rule are reachable. The comparison rule thus proves the iff, including ties between an excluded finite-size binomial and an attained positive-shape fit. If no finite height qualifies, the winning ends are precisely the listed faces at the largest height. **Effective scope.** At \(V>\mu\) the nonzero signed NB coefficient \(T\) in the prior proof either supplies a witness or reduces the comparison to a finite prefix. A witness in any branch gives an effective block cutoff by the rational ratio envelope. At equidispersion the signed quadratic and quartic tests give witnesses or cutoffs wherever the first tested nonzero coefficient is found. The prior proof includes an integer histogram with both coefficients zero, so a general cutoff cannot be inferred from these orders. At fitted NB equality the exact criterion includes ties, while a comparison between a finite algebraic height and the optimized NB height may not terminate numerically. The criterion on the doubly vanishing Poisson branch remains quantified over sizes. Full-support saturation and \((0,1,2)\) give exact attainment cases. The underdispersed sample \((1,2)\) gives exact binomial escape. Separated local peaks do not prove nonunique global fits. ### betapr held shape1

**Proved.** Hold \(p>0\). For every \(q>0\) the scale profile is unique by the strictly increasing equation \(E[b/(b+x)]=q/(p+q)\), with its two endpoint signs. Denote the profiled log likelihood by \(P(q)\) and the gamma log likelihood with shape \(p\) and scale \(\bar x/p\) by \(G\). An MLE exists iff some finite stationary value of \(P\) reaches \(G\). The only competitive escape is that matched gamma. The prior closure and threshold proof gives this criterion, includes actual finite equality at the interior threshold, and proves strict gamma dominance for \(p[(x_{\max}-x_{\min})/x_{\min}]^2\le4\). A strict finite witness follows if \(p\,\mathrm{Var}(x/\bar x)>1\), or if equality holds and the quadratic inward coefficient is positive. Constants obey the strict gamma test. The closure proof also excludes \(q\downarrow0\) and any escape of \(b\) at finite positive \(q\). [Threshold, signs and equality](2026-11-25-betapr-math-report.md#one-shape-held)

**Proved finite scalar list, including vanishing leading coefficients.** The envelope derivative is
\[
 P'(q)=\psi(q+p)-\psi(q)-E\log(1+x/b(q)).
\]
Near zero the scale equation gives \(b=q\,v(q)\) where \(v\) is real analytic with \(v(0)=1/(p E[1/x])\). Thus \(P'(q)=1/q+O(1+|\log q|)>0\). At infinity put \(h=1/q,\ B(h)=hb\). The scale equation is equivalently \(E[x/(B+hx)]=p/(1+ph)\). Its unique root has \(B(0)=\bar x/p\) and a nonzero derivative of the equation with respect to \(B\), so the last term of the score is analytic in \(h\) at zero.

For integer \(p\), the digamma difference is \(\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}(q+j)^{-1}\), analytic in \(h\) at zero. The difference of the two analytic terms cannot vanish identically there: analytic continuation along \(q>0\) would contradict the strict sign near zero. For noninteger \(p\), the gamma recurrence and the digamma integral give
\[
 \psi(q+p)-\psi(q)=\int_0^\infty e^{-qt}\frac{1-e^{-pt}}{1-e^{-t}}\,dt.
\]
Expand the kernel at zero with coefficients \(c_j\). Integrating a finite Taylor polynomial gives the expansion \(\sum_{j=0}^J j!c_j h^{j+1}+O(h^{J+2})\), by bounding the Taylor remainder on a short fixed interval and the exponentially small tail. The kernel has genuine poles at \(\pm2\pi i\) for noninteger \(p\). Its Taylor radius is \(2\pi\), so \(\limsup |c_j|^{1/j}=1/(2\pi)\); multiplication by \(j!\) gives zero radius. These coefficients therefore cannot all cancel those of the analytic data term. The first nonzero score coefficient gives a strict exterior sign in either case. Analyticity in \(q>0\) now gives a finite stationary list, contained in a compact interval. On algebraic data and holds the search for the first nonzero coefficient terminates with exact algebraic coefficient tests, and finite Taylor bounds yield a cutoff. This is an exclusion of the entire tail from the stationary list, including when the displayed low-order signs vanish. It does not supply a general exact comparison oracle for the finite stationary heights.

For exact input where finite equality occurs, take \(x=(1/8,15/8)\) at \(p=p_c(x)\) of the prior threshold proof. The previously proved positive second coefficient at \(p=64/49\) forces \(p_c<64/49\), and the negative first coefficient at \(p_c\) confines the limiting strict witnesses to an interior compact ratio interval. Their limit attains the gamma height. [Exact crossing](2026-11-25-betapr-math-report.md#vanishing-leading-coefficient)

### bnbinom held all free

**Proved.** Use the canonical \(r=\mathrm{size}\le b=\beta\) chart and restore the swap at each winner. All zeros give the excluded zero atom and no finite maximizer. For \(\mu>0\), only the NB and Poisson faces have positive likelihood on escaping sequences. The joint count-mass proof covers simultaneous escapes even when fixed-count mass is lost. The larger face \(F\) is fitted NB for \(V>\mu\), and Poisson at \(\mu\) otherwise. The all-free criterion is that a finite stationary value reaches \(F\), with the score in the full labeled chart used at the canonical seam as well. The proof of the finite comparison and of the exteriors already appears in the [compact canonical comparison](2026-11-25-bnbinom-math-report.md#faces-and-the-finite-versus-face-test) and its cited [joint closure](2026-10-29-bnbinom.md#proved-proper-hellinger-closure).

Specifically the fitted-NB inward coefficient is nonzero by its size score at the rational moment candidate. A positive sign gives finite attainment, and a negative sign supplies a uniform strictly losing NB collar. At \(V<\mu\) the Poisson first-order bound excludes the jointly growing corner. At \(V=\mu\), the second-order bound is at most \(-\mu^2(1/r+1/b)^2/12\) at the fitted rate, with a uniform cubic remainder near it and a strict Poisson deficit away from it. The bounded-rate restriction and small-shape exclusions in the cited proof leave a compact comparison whenever the face does not already have a strict inward witness. If no finite candidate reaches \(F\), the optimized face is the unattained supremum. If an actual maximal finite value equals \(F\), the face and the finite fit both reach the envelope maximum.

The supporting-polynomial escape test from the prior uniqueness round is a separate input-side sufficient condition: \(\exp(\mu-t)\sum_x w_x(t/\mu)^x\le1\) for all \(t\ge0\), where \(w_x\) are empirical weights. A nondegenerate Poisson mixture then loses strictly by the log tangent inequality. In particular data on two adjacent counts, or a positive constant, select Poisson. ### gb2 held all free

**Proved.** On nonconstant data the only positive escaping envelopes in the log observation coordinate are normal, the two orientations of log transformed gamma, and the two-sided exponential family including its one-sided limits. The latter correspond to double Pareto, power and Pareto on \(x\). At a one-sided support contact the envelope uses the supported-side endpoint density. Let \(B\) be the largest of their heights. A finite GB2 MLE exists iff a finite stationary value is at least \(B\). Constants have unbounded scale concentration. The proof below exhausts joint paths, including paths on which the translating log scale and the log mode are far apart.

In public power/scale/beta notation \((a,b,p,q)\), set \(A=ap,\ D=aq,\ m=\log b+a^{-1}\log(p/q)\), the log-density mode. Write the density as \(H e^{-U(d)}\) for \(d=y-m\), \(U(0)=0\), with normalization \(H^{-1}=\int e^{-U(d)}\,dd\). Direct differentiation gives
\[
 U'(d)=\frac{AD(e^{ad}-1)}{D+A e^{ad}}
       = C\,\frac{(e^{ad}-1)/a}{1-r+r e^{ad}},\quad
 C=\frac{aAD}{A+D},\qquad r=\frac{A}{A+D}.
\]
The potentials are convex. On any positive sample superlevel \(\prod_i f(y_i)\ge l>0\), the peaks \(H\) stay bounded above and away from zero, and the modes stay bounded. Indeed \(H\ge l^{1/n}\) and \(f(y_i)\ge l/H^{n-1}\). At least one observation is at distance at least half the positive sample span from \(m\). The integral over that segment is bounded below by its length times the logarithmic mean of \(H\) and \(l/H^{n-1}\), by log concavity. The logarithmic mean grows like \(H/(n\log H)\) if \(H\to\infty\), contradicting normalization. With \(H\) bounded, the sample density lower bound and the same integration give a bound on every sample-to-mode distance. For a single observed level and a held mode different from it the same argument applies with the fixed nonzero distance. We use that case below.

After taking subsequences \(m\) converges and \(a\) has a limit in \([0,\infty]\). If \(a\) is bounded, on every bounded \(d\)-interval the curvature \(U''(d)=C e^{ad}/(1-r+r e^{ad})^2\) is comparable to \(C\) with constants uniform in \(r\). If \(C\to0\), Fatou's lemma gives \(\int e^{-U}\to\infty\). If \(C\to\infty\), the curvature bound on \([-1,1]\) gives \(U\to\infty\) off zero and diverging outward slopes at the endpoints; convexity controls the tails and the integral tends to zero. Both contradict the peak bounds. Thus \(C\) tends to a positive finite value along a further subsequence. Positive lower outward slopes at \(\pm1\) give an integrable dominating exponential for normalization. The limiting cases are:

- \(a\to0\): \(U'(d)\to C d\), giving the normal.
- \(a\to a_*>0,\ r\to0\): \(U'(d)\to(C/a_*)(e^{a_*d}-1)\), giving \(U(d)=(C/a_*^2)(e^{a_*d}-1-a_*d)\), a log transformed gamma. At \(r\to1\) use its reflection.
- \(a\to a_*>0,\ r\to r_*\in(0,1)\): all original parameters converge to finite admissible values.

If \(a\to\infty\), use \(U(d)\le A(-d)_++D(d)_+\) to get \(H\le(1/A+1/D)^{-1}\). Both rates are therefore bounded away from zero. On the right, \(1/U'(d)=(1/D+e^{-ad}/A)/(1-e^{-ad})\) for \(d>0\), with the reflected identity on the left. For a finite rate limit the integrated potential tends to that rate times distance, uniformly on bounded intervals on that side by dominated convergence of the slopes. For an infinite rate limit the potential tends to infinity at every fixed nonzero distance on its side, by integrating the slope from half that distance. The slopes on both sides at unit distance again give an integrable dominating exponential. Two infinite rates would force the normalizing integral to zero. Two finite rates yield the asymmetric Laplace density on the whole line. One infinite rate yields the oriented exponential on the other side; only at the contact can the sample trace fall anywhere from zero to \(H\). Even for tied contacts, the upper value \(H\) is reached simultaneously by setting \(m\) equal to the contact throughout. A wrong-side noncontact gives zero. Each limiting case is reachable by prescribing \(m,a,C,r\) in the bounded-power charts or \(m,a,A,D\) in the large-power chart. These paths also converge as laws by density convergence away from the contact and normalization. This establishes the exhaustive positive envelope and the comparison, including equality. For constants one can fix the two beta shapes, center at the observed level and let \(a\to\infty\); the peak grows linearly in \(a\). Any sequence with diverging density at the common observation has \(H\to\infty\), mode approaching that observation by the same chord-integral bound, and concentration there. For the last assertion, on either side of the mode the integral bound gives \(f(m+\delta)\to0\) for fixed nonzero \(\delta\) when \(H\to\infty\), and the outward log-density secants then bound both tail integrals by vanishing exponentials.

For computing \(B\), the normal uses the mean and RMS about it. Each gamma orientation uses its prior one-dimensional stationary and endpoint comparison. The prior power bounds also give quantitative exclusions above a strict witness. For the two-sided exponential let \(U_l(m)=E(m-y)_+\) and \(U_r(m)=E(y-m)_+\). The optimized height in average log units is
\[
 -1-2\log\!\left(\sqrt{U_l(m)}+\sqrt{U_r(m)}\right).
\]
Indeed maximize \(\log(AD/(A+D))-AU_l-DU_r\) first in the common rate multiplier and then in \(A/(A+D)\), or solve the two rate scores. If either deviation vanishes the supremum uses an infinite rate on that side. Between sample levels the sum of square roots is concave because both deviations are affine, so a minimum is at a sample level. Outside the sample hull the corresponding nonzero deviation only increases. This evaluates all three exponential-family faces with a finite check of the data levels. The prior golden has a strict witness conditional on its archived exterior comparisons. Beyond constants, exact sufficient escape regions and a general effective global equality oracle remain open. ### gengamma held all free

**Proved.** On nonconstant positive data the only positive escaping envelopes are power and Pareto. Their best average log values in the log observations are
\[
 B_+=-1-\log(y_{\max}-\bar y),\qquad
 B_-=-1-\log(\bar y-y_{\min}).
\]
Compare the finite signed stationary lists and the fitted lognormal seam with \(\max(B_+,B_-)\). At least one finite value at or above the bound is necessary and sufficient. Constants have unbounded likelihood by scale collapse. The seam is attained at \(Q=0\).

For explicit exhaustion in the joint parameters, the positive orientation in log units has the potential \(U(d)=k(e^{td}-1-td)\), \(d=y-m\), where \(m=\mu,\ k=Q^{-2},\ t=|Q|/\sigma>0\). The negative orientation reflects \(d\). Use the peak and mode bounds just proved for log-concave densities. As \(t\) stays bounded, the curvature at the mode \(kt^2\) stays bounded above and away from zero, by the same local curvature and tail arguments using \(U''(d)=kt^2 e^{td}\). At \(t\to0\) the limit is normal, \(Q\to0\) and \(\sigma\to 1/\sqrt{\lim kt^2}\). At positive finite \(t\) the limit is an interior point. At \(t\to\infty\), put \(A=kt\). The linear bound \(U(d)\le A|d|\) on \(d<0\) gives \(H\le A\). An unbounded \(A\) would force \(U(d)\to\infty\) on both sides and make the normalizing integral tend to zero, by the outward slope bounds. With \(A\) tending to a positive finite value, the potential tends to \(A|d|\) on \(d<0\) and to infinity on \(d>0\), and the integral tends to \(1/A\). This is the left-sided exponential at \(m\), or the right-sided exponential after reflection. The upper trace at a contact is \(H\) in the limit, with all contacts reached by \(m=y_{\max}\) or \(m=y_{\min}\) throughout. Any noncontact on the wrong side gives zero. Maximizing the cutoff likelihood over its feasible endpoints and rates gives the displayed values. For repeated constants, a diverging peak at the observations concentrates there by the log-concave chord bound.

The finite signed lists are the unique conditional gamma shape and location profiles in the two orientations from the [signed profile](2026-11-10-gg-theory-report.md#free-fit-signed-profile-and-certificate). The [transformed-gamma root proof](2026-11-25-trgamma-math-report.md#signed-exteriors-and-finite-root-count) gives strict score signs in both exterior collars, including the zero-skew seam, so each nonzero signed list is finite. Equally replicated two-level data lose strictly to the two faces by [the Lean balanced-pair theorem](2026-11-26-gengamma-twopoint-report.md#proof-and-implementation). If the fitted normal reaches \(B_\pm\) on both sides, attainment follows even without checking its inward score. This condition is \(\min(y_{\max}-\bar y,\bar y-y_{\min})\ge s\sqrt{2\pi/e}\), where \(s\) is the empirical log standard deviation. Universal uniqueness is refuted by the dyadic replicated sample \((1/4,(1/2)^{[10000]},2^{[10000]},4)\), with distinct reflected winners. ### gpd held mu+xi

**Proved.** Require all excesses \(y_i=x_i-\mu\ge0\), shape \(\xi\ge-1\), and let \(R=\max y_i,\ k=\#\{i:y_i=0\}\). A finite MLE exists iff \(R>0\) and either \(\xi\le0\) or \(\xi k<n-k\). It is unique. Negative excesses are infeasible. If \(R=0\), the product is \(\sigma^{-n}\). At \(\xi=-1\), the closed upper contact \(\sigma=R\) gives the smallest feasible scale and the unique uniform maximum. For \(\xi>-1\), the log-scale score on positive-density scales is
\[
 (1+\xi)\sum_i\frac{y_i}{\sigma+\xi y_i}-n.
\]
It decreases strictly when \(R>0\) and tends to \(-n\) at infinity. The lower limit is \(+\infty\) for \(-1<\xi\le0\) (at \(-\xi R\) or zero), and \((n-k)/\xi-k\) for \(\xi>0\). For positive shape, the small-scale likelihood is asymptotic to \(\sigma^{(n-k)/\xi-k}\prod_{y_i>0}(\xi y_i)^{-1-1/\xi}\). A negative exponent gives divergence. At equality the finite positive limit is strictly unattained, since the score is negative for every positive scale. At the negative-shape nonuniform upper contact density tends to zero, and at infinite scale the likelihood vanishes for all held shapes. These exhaust the one-dimensional ends. This extends the strictly positive-excess theorem to threshold contacts. [Prior classification](2026-11-27-uniq-shape-math-report.md#gpd-held-muxi)

### huber held all free

**Proved.** Constants give unbounded collapse. On nonconstant data a finite shape attains iff at least one stationary conditional profile value reaches both normal and Laplace heights. The normal face uses the sample mean and RMS residual. The Laplace face uses the median set and mean absolute residual about a median. Both are excluded as limiting shapes. The [prior Huber face and profile proof](2026-11-27-lsheld-atlas-math-report.md#huber) gives joint compactness for \(c=\epsilon\) bounded away from zero, and for \(c\downarrow0\) in the rescaled scale \(b=s/c\). In the latter chart the loss differs from \(|x-\mu|/b\) by at most \(c^2/2\) uniformly in \(x\), and \(cZ(c)\to2\). In the former, the linear lower loss bound and a positive absolute deviation confine \(s,\mu\) uniformly. At \(c\to\infty\) the bounded standardized residuals fall in the quadratic core. Thus the only positive escaping traces are precisely the two location-scale faces, including moving nuisance paths. The prior stationary-list argument applies to the full profile, including the kink locations. A winning finite value at face equality attains. The shape score excludes a flat location interval at a finite joint winner. Distinct-shape equality remains open. [Flat-location proof](2026-11-26-huber-flat-set-report.md#maximizer-sets-and-numerical-decisions)

**Additional sufficient condition.** If the median interval has positive length, choose a median strictly inside it and set \(s=cD\), where \(D=E|x_i-\mu|\). For sufficiently small \(c\) every observation lies in the linear tail. The change in average log likelihood from fitted Laplace is \(c^2/2-\log(cZ(c)/2)=c^4/6+O(c^6)>0\), using the elementary expansion of \(Z\). Thus a positive median gap and \(s_N/D\ge\sqrt{2e/\pi}\) guarantee attainment, where \(s_N\) is the RMS about the sample mean. The ratio condition is exactly that fitted Laplace reaches fitted normal. For example, \((-100,-1,-1,1,1,100)\) satisfies both strict comparisons, since \(D=34\) and \(s_N^2=3334\). For nonattainment, equally replicated distinct pairs have the strict normal dominance of the [exact two-point proof](2026-10-29-huber-free-epsilon-report.md#mathematics-proved-from-the-stated-density). Replication raises the likelihood product to a positive integer power and preserves the comparison.

### hyper held all free

**Proved by the prior exact procedure.** For a positive constant \(c\), the maximizing set is \(k=c,m=N\) or \(m=c,k=N\) with \(N\ge c\). For zero data it is \(k=0\) or \(m=0\), by the hypergeometric support endpoints. For nonconstant samples the only positive escaping laws are the binomial with fixed smaller design \(s=\min(k,m)\) and the Poisson with both design counts growing. To see exhaustion, pass to a subsequence with \(N\to\infty\). A bounded \(s\) gives a fixed \(s\) and the binomial by falling factorials. For \(s\to\infty\), if \(km/N\) tends to zero or infinity, Markov or the variance bound and Chebyshev make the sample likelihood vanish. A positive finite rate gives the Poisson by the zero-cell and fixed count-ratio expansion. On constants, winning escapes are the deterministic binomial traces at the corresponding endpoint or the rate-zero degenerate Poisson for zero data. An interior-proportion binomial or a positive-rate Poisson cannot saturate a constant. [Expansion, cutoffs and full sets](2026-11-27-uniq-discrete-math-report.md#hyper-held-all-free)

At \(V<\mu\), \(F\), the highest binomial profile height, strictly beats Poisson. The uniform bound in the jointly growing corner cuts off large \(s,N\) below \(F\). Compare the finite grid left over at large \(s\) and the finitely many fixed-\(s\) profiles, using the held-design rule below. Attainment holds iff at least one finite point or ray occurs at the highest height, with \(F\) included. A finite match to \(F\) is eligible. At \(V\ge\mu\), all fixed binomial sizes lose strictly to Poisson. At \(V>\mu\) the joint first-order coefficient excludes large \(s\); at equality the uniform quadratic is at most \(-\mu^2(1/k+1/m)^2/12\) at the fitted rate.
