### Beta-binomial defective mass

**A genuinely defective zero-cell limit cannot tie a retained singleton.** At held beta one, size $N\to\infty$ and alpha $1/\log N$, the zero mass is
$$
p_0=\prod_{j=1}^N(1+\alpha/j)^{-1}.
$$
The logarithm is $-\alpha\sum_{j=1}^N1/j+O(\alpha^2)$ because the reciprocal squares are summable. Thus $p_0\to e^{-1}$. For each fixed positive count, the ratio to the zero mass vanishes by the initial ratio and bounded subsequent ratios. On a right ray starting at zero the limiting vector therefore has likelihood $e^{-n}$ for (n) zero observations, whereas size zero gives one. Taking alpha $1/(\log N)^2$ gives a **proper** zero atom and ties the attained singleton. The corrected all-free and held-beta rows retain the subsequential mass $q\in[0,1]$ and permit equality precisely at $q=1$. For positive observations this branch has zero sample likelihood. On a bounded window use the finite ratio normalization.
