## Mathematics established

* Log-series: `expm1(u)/u = mean(x)` has a unique positive solution when the mean exceeds one; its derivative is positive. All ones still follows the declared excluded-boundary policy.
* Gamma: `log(k)-digamma(k)=log(mean(x))-mean(log(x))`. Relative deviations avoid cancellation in the Jensen gap; inverse-gamma/loggamma/Nakagami likelihoods follow by their exact Jacobians. Constant data give an unbounded likelihood, not an attained point mass.
* Normal and inverse Gaussian: scaled quadratic reductions prevent overflow. For IG, `lambda = mu / mean((x/mu-1)^2/(x/mu))`; the fitted quadratic contribution is exactly n.
* Balanced two-point Cauchy: `(mu-a)(b-mu)=scale^2`, `a<mu<b`, describes a continuum of maxima; the midpoint is one. Pairwise density product is bounded by `1/(pi^2*(b-a)^2)`.
* Binomial expansion: sum `log(1-j/size)` through the cubic term when `max(x)/size < 1e-5`; omitted absolute error is O(x*(x/size)^4), below ~1e-11 per observation at the supported count ceiling.
* Lomax equality is not an escape criterion by itself. Numerical profile/oracle evidence: `(1,1,40,60)` improves on the exponential by ~0.49222; `(1,1,1,1,20,20,20,36)` by ~0.02592; `(1,1,4,12)` chooses the exponential. The bounded grid/refinement is numerical evidence, not a theorem covering arbitrary multimodal profiles. `(1,1,40,59)` demonstrates the old strict guard can also discard an interior maximum below equality.

* Gompertz: `S(b)/b = sum(x_i*integral_0^1 exp(b*x_i*t)dt)`. Its profiled log likelihood is linear minus the log of this moment-generating function, hence concave. The right derivative at zero is `n*(mean(x)-mean(x^2)/(2*mean(x)))`; equality therefore selects exponential.
* Finite Zipf: log likelihood is concave in shape, so a nonpositive score at zero selects the declared attained uniform face. Exact sums through 1024 terms plus log-coordinate Gauss panels and Euler–Maclaurin endpoint terms remove O(N) storage; direct-sum regressions cover shapes 0.1, 1, 2.5, and 8.
* Inverse paralogistic at `(1e-300,1,1e300)`: a slow oracle in `(log(shape),shape*log(reciprocal scale))` places the optimum's scale beyond double representation. An explicit precision error is the correct numerical outcome, rather than a returned -Inf likelihood.

* Large-sample BFGS: dividing the objective by n preserves the exact maximizer but removes sample-size-dependent line-search/gradient scaling. Total likelihoods are restored before face comparisons. Replication regressions cover std, sstd, GED, SGED and paralogis; the inverse paralogistic fitter inherits the last fix. At one million points, GED's old shape 3.6754 improves to 2.9992 and gains about 3596 log-likelihood units. Paralogis improves from shape 1.5 to 0.9994 and gains about 368799 likelihood units. Student-t's false df=2 escapes are replaced by finite fits near the generating df=3.

## Decisions / limitations

* Existing `power` epsilon intentionally changes the endpoint at tiny scales; it is not a scale-equivariant estimator. Changing that public convention requires a separate decision, and its golden is untouched.
* Extreme parameters can have an attained mathematical MLE outside double representation (e.g. Nakagami scale is mean(x^2)). Such cases should fail numerically rather than claim mathematical nonexistence.
* Reference-density computations can themselves lose precision for nearly degenerate fits. A generic oracle gap alone is not accepted as a bug without checking the objective and parameter domain.

* GPD's declared threshold convention is strict (`x > mu`); observations exactly at a held threshold correctly fail under that current contract.
* Beta samples separated by only a few ulps still raise an explicit precision error even though the mathematical shapes are finite. Reciprocal-based fitters can similarly fail on denormals; the report does not reinterpret these numerical limitations as mathematical nonexistence.
* Hnorm retains the existing nextafter endpoint convention required by its reference density; re-profiling makes its likelihood consistent with the returned parameters. Power retains the explicit `epsilon=0` endpoint-face convention used by existing tests, while its positive default is an approximation to the open-endpoint supremum.
* No new reference-package runtime workaround was introduced. The pre-existing sads quantile workaround is unchanged.

* Reviewer follow-up: protected behaviour notes may still repeat the disproved Lomax moment guard; these protected files were deliberately left untouched.
