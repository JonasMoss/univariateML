# Verified family aliases — 2026-10-31

Audit of all 98 canonical dossiers at source revision `8473d9eec5de74f5b1e4c2e1af3f372717c94302`, branch `codex/aliases`. Result: 114 aliases in 63 families; 35 families have no verified whole-family alternative in the checked references. An empty result is a conservative naming decision, not a claim that no alternative name exists anywhere.

Only the `aliases` front-matter fields and this report are authored changes. No fitters, parameter domains, generated d/p/q/r sources, goldens, or other metadata were changed. All linked sources below were checked during this audit on 2026-10-31; package manuals were read as naming/definition references, not installed.

## Inclusion rule and scope

A name must denote the same probability laws on the same observation space, with the same free parameter space after a bijective change of coefficients. Changing the random variable, freeing a threshold, restricting a shape or degrees of freedom, or adding a mixture component fails this test. A source that provides a location keyword is interpreted at the dossier’s stated threshold; optional software location does not silently add a coefficient. Fitter-only restrictions do not redefine the underlying family. Boundary point masses are interpreted through the dossier’s declared closures; source software need not implement those endpoints.

Accepted spelling variants and established abbreviations help search. Capitalization follows proper names; ordinary descriptors are lower case. Hyphens within spelling variants are retained; joined proper names use en dashes. Typographic duplicates of the canonical name are removed (including double-hyphen/en-dash equivalents). The accent-only variant Fréchet is likewise not entered as another name.

Equivalences below are analytic identifications of the displayed densities/supports, not numerical findings or claims about MLE existence. No numerical search was used. The report distinguishes a checked naming statement from a structural relationship used to reject a candidate.

## Ambiguous names and evidence qualifications

- **Pareto II:** `pareto2` already owns the unqualified canonical name and has a free lower endpoint. `lomax` receives only explicitly zero-threshold forms, supported by SciPy’s second-kind terminology. Its scale is `1/lambda`, shape `kappa`; these cannot create a nonzero endpoint. No bare Lomax alias is attached to the larger translated family.
- **Fréchet:** `frechet` has free location, scale and shape; inverse Weibull has fixed endpoint zero. SciPy `invweibull` supports both through `loc`, so its unqualified type II extreme-value name is assigned to `frechet`, and the two-parameter qualifier to `invweibull`.
- **Burr:** SciPy `burr` means type III/Dagum (`invburr` here), while `burr12` means type XII/Singh–Maddala (`burr` here). The Dagum type I qualifier is checked against the [Dagum definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagum_distribution). Neither family is replaced with its translated Pareto version.
- **Generalized Pareto / Pearson VI:** actuar `GeneralizedPareto` is a beta-prime law, unlike extreme-value GPD. Its `TransformedBeta` note also calls GB2 “Pearson Type VI”, although the ordinary Pearson VI density has no independent power shape. Pearson type VI is assigned to beta prime under the positive-support, zero-location convention explicitly used by Laurent–Legrand appendix A. It is omitted from GB2, whose independent power shape makes it larger. A Pearson VI definition with a freely shifted endpoint is also larger than beta prime and is excluded by this decision. See [actuar GeneralizedPareto](https://stat.ethz.ch/CRAN/web/packages/actuar/refman/actuar.html#GeneralizedPareto) and the linked transformed-beta reference.
- **Generalized beta/gamma:** GB1 here means the bounded positive-power convention in Kleiber–Kotz section 6.5 and actuar. Some definitions admit negative power and an unbounded branch; that larger signed-power family is excluded. For gamma, [VGAM Stacy documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/VGAM/help/gengamma.stacy.html) supports the retained Stacy name. [SciPy gengamma](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.gengamma.html) permits both signs of its power: `c=shape2` gives `trgamma`, `c=-shape2` gives `invtrgamma`, at zero location.
- **Skew normal/t:** the full two-piece normal has finite moments for every skew coefficient and matches `snorm` after moment reparametrization. Its Fernández–Steel label is also supported by [fGarch snorm](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/fGarch/html/dist-snorm.html). Azzalini `sn` has a different density. `std` and `sstd` require degrees of freedom above two; unrestricted Student/two-piece t names would add laws.
- **Zero alteration:** for each of the five zero-modified laws, the dossier is exactly the hurdle formula with zero probability `p0` and the positive conditional base mass. This algebraic identification uses the general brms formula; [the ZMPS paper](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00949655.2017.1289529) independently documents hurdle forms for binomial, geometric and negative-binomial bases. Zero-inflation constrains the zero mass relative to its base and is not the whole hurdle family.
- **Counts and shifts:** geometric at zero and geometric on positive integers have different observation supports. [Wolfram WaringYuleDistribution](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/WaringYuleDistribution.html) likewise uses zero-based counts: its one-parameter law requires adding one to match the dossier’s positive-integer Yule–Simon. The accepted Yule terminology is for the positive-count convention documented in the Yule–Simon literature; the Wolfram function itself is not entered as an alias. [Wolfram beta negative binomial](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/ref/BetaNegativeBinomialDistribution.html) supports inverse Markov–Pólya; shifted beta-Pascal is omitted.
- **Zipf:** finite `zipf` includes exponents down to zero. SciPy’s finite Zipfian mass has exactly the finite harmonic normalizer. Wolfram’s finite Estoup example is restricted to exponent above one, so Estoup is not admitted on that evidence. The infinite law requires exponent above one and is assigned to `zeta`; no finite/infinite alias conflation is made.
- **Gumbel:** maxima and minima are reflections, not coefficient changes with positive scale. SciPy `gumbel_r` matches this dossier; [Wolfram’s distribution table](https://reference.wolfram.com/language/tutorial/NumericalOperationsOnData.html?view=all) names that law ExtremeValueDistribution, whereas GumbelDistribution is the minimum law. “Maximum extreme value” is qualified accordingly.
- **Discretized versus lattice Gaussian:** the cell-integral normal and gamma laws match extraDistr. [SciPy loggamma](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.loggamma.html) does not match this repository’s actuar-style log-gamma, and its dgamma/dweibull identifiers denote continuous symmetric laws rather than discretized positive laws.

## Additional older names and rejected broad labels

Kleiber–Kotz identifies generalized F with GB2 (introduction and section 6.1), beta-P with Singh–Maddala (section 6.2), beta-K and three-parameter kappa with Dagum I (section 6.3), Vinci with inverse gamma (section 5.4), and sech square with logistic (section 7.3). These match the dossier densities after scale changes. Generalized log-logistic is used for both Burr and inverse Burr and for Champernowne laws; it is omitted as an ambiguous unqualified search name. Generalized Laplace is likewise used for several distinct extensions, so it is not added to GED. The four-parameter generalized F convention with a freely signed power produces the same positive-support GB2 laws after interchanging beta shapes when the power changes sign; it does not add a threshold. The ordinary two-shape F fixes the scale and is smaller than scaled beta prime.
