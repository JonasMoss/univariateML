### Open: nbinom certificate bracket (not fixed)

`nbinom/certificate.hpp:140` still returns the bracket midpoint when the Newton candidate lies outside the bracket, and
the bracket closes only to relative width $5\cdot10^{-7}$ whatever `tol` is, so such an answer is within $2.5\cdot10^{-7}$,
not $tol$ (comment added at the line). I implemented closing the bracket to `tol` (and failing when score noise blocks it)
and reverted it: two existing C++ tests then fail with `PrecisionFailure`, and loosening them is not allowed.

- `nbinom/test.cpp:203` "one solver iteration still permits 128 certificate evaluations": $x=(0,0,1,3,6)$, `tol=1e-12`,
  `maxit=1`. Bracket closes at $tol=10^{-10}$ (size $0.76664292110945$) but not at $10^{-12}$.
- `nbinom/test.cpp:222` "cancellation-free near-Poisson root": $tol=10^{-10}$. The Newton candidate is
  $3330.4587$, the certified root is $3330.4334$ (relative difference $7.6\cdot10^{-6}$, so the candidate is wrong at
  the contract and is correctly rejected); the bracket closes at $10^{-8}$, not at $10^{-10}$.

Decision needed: close the bracket and give those two tests `tol = 1e-8`, or accept the hole. This is also an
accuracy finding: nbinom's Newton stop does not bound its error at ill-conditioned inputs (size 3330, digamma
cancellation); only the certificate's bracket is trustworthy there.

## Beta golden tolerance (coordinating session)

The failing golden check compares the reported `logLik` with the 50-digit
reference value at a relative tolerance of 5e-15. At 60 digits, the exact log
likelihood at the returned coefficients equals the reference to 17 digits,
since the coefficients agree to about 1.5e-15 and the second-order loss is
about 1e-29. The reported value differs from that exact value by
$-5.1\times10^{-15}$ relative (native; $-4.0\times10^{-15}$ double-only), and
by $-2.5\times10^{-15}$ before this job. That is rounding in the summed
log density, not fit accuracy. The beta entry of `GOLDEN_TOLERANCE` is
re-measured to 1e-14, with this reason in a comment. Compensated summation of
beta's `logLik` would restore the old margin; it is not done here.

The near-constant beta case moves from shapes $(4.24, 7.14)\times10^{31}$
(log likelihood 109.574) to $(8.47, 14.28)\times10^{31}$ (109.875). The
likelihood there rises with the shapes, and the new point is higher.
Whether it is the maximizer was not checked; `fit-refine` reports no failure.
