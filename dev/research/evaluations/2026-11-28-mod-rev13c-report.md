## Proof errors and scope corrections

### The all-held failure lists import impossible endpoint cases

The 15 all-held failure rows carry over zero or infinite inside density as possible failure ends. All five applicable families declare open support ($(0,\infty)$) and have strictly positive finite densities at every compatible observation and admissible supplied vector. A cut at zero only clips the normalizer. An observation at zero is incompatible even if an evaluator supplies an endpoint extension. For example, holding transformed-gamma scale, shape and power at one gives an exponential density with right limit one at zero, but $(0,1)$ remains incompatible on the nominal window $[0,1]$. For compatible data with positive tight mass the all-held problem always attains. For a compatible null tight event a permitted expansion instead gives divergence. The replacement claims retain the exact grade with these alternatives explicit. ### Fixed shapes and free scale can still give joint divergence

The nine beta-prime, Burr and inverse Burr failure rows with both shapes held say there is no divergence branch. The qualification to positive tight mass is essential. For the exact sample $(1)$ with both cuts estimated and any admissible fixed coefficients, $[1,1+\epsilon]$ gives conditional density asymptotic to $1/\epsilon$. The same mechanism applies in a one-estimated design when the fixed cut equals the constant observation. The replacement claims record the distinction explicitly. On positive tight mass, holding both beta shapes excludes the zero-shape and gamma faces. The two scale ends are the only possible finite nonattainment competitors, and each must be integrable on the realized transformed window. ### The retained-window face tests

For each of the 141 family, hold and design combinations, the density at each positive sample cut is finite and positive under every admissible parent coefficient vector. The normalizer therefore decreases strictly at the same vector when either estimated cut is tightened. Multiplicity at an extreme does not change the tight event and contributes its full multiplicity to the sample product. Supremizing the pointwise identity and using feasibility of the tight window gives the joint result, even if the coefficients escape. At a finite positive supremum, any maximizing loose-window sequence has tight-window likelihood tending to the same height and retained-mass ratio tending to one. Its conditional law is within one minus that ratio in total variation of the tight conditional law. This justifies transporting competitive finite limits, including ties. If a finite candidate equals the largest reachable excluded height, it either wins or a strictly higher finite competitor confines a maximizing sequence to the feasible region.

For a compatible constant $c>0$, tight mass is zero precisely when the nominal cuts coincide. In a one-estimated design this requires the fixed cut at $c$, and the estimated cut can always open a positive-width interval into the parent support. When both cuts are estimated either side can open. The retained density at $c$ is positive and finite before conditioning, so the likelihood diverges as the opening shrinks and the retained law converges to the point mass. The positive-nominal-width null slice in the half-normal correction requires a parent support contact and cannot occur on compatible data here. None of the six has a moving support endpoint or a window-specific parent extension of the kind used by zeta or the positive-window negative-binomial alias. ### Exact scale continuum when both beta shapes equal one

In beta prime hold both shapes at one. In Burr and inverse Burr hold shape1 at one and any positive power. In the corresponding fixed coordinate $u=x$, $x^d$, or $x^{-d}$, use the two observations $(1,2)$ in physical coordinates. Let $u_-<u_+$ be the transformed extremes. Both estimated cuts give the transformed interval $[u_-,u_+]$. The one-estimated designs give the same window when the other cut is fixed at its corresponding physical extreme. Every feasible parent scale gives a kernel $(1+tu)^{-2}$ with $t>0$, and
$$
\int_{u_-}^{u_+}(1+tu)^{-2}\,du
=\frac{u_+-u_-}{(1+tu_-)(1+tu_+)}.
$$
The endpoint kernel product divided by this integral squared equals $(u_+-u_-)^{-2}$ for every $t>0$. The Jacobian factor is independent of scale. Thus every finite positive scale at the tight cuts is a global maximizer, and strict tightening excludes any other cut tuple. Both scale-end limits tie the finite fits, so equality in the attainment comparison matters even for this two-point sample. This refutes general uniqueness for these three held-shape masks in all three designs. It does not classify their winning sets on arbitrary samples. No uniqueness row was proposed for these keys.
