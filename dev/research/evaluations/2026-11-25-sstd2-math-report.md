## Coordinates and competitive regions

Use the right half face and reflect for the left. Let \(w\) be the narrow left fraction, \(z=1-2w\), \(S\) total kernel width and \(c\) the join. The left and right widths are \(wS,(1-w)S\). Write the density as \(K(q)e^{-\phi_q(r)}/S\), with \(r\) the nonnegative side-standardized distance. The half-kernel absolute first and second moments are \(k,v\). Thus
\[
 c=\mu-kzS,\qquad \sigma^2=S^2 W^2/4,\qquad
 W^2=v+(3v-4k^2)z^2.
\]
Substitute only the equations for held moments. At finite Student shape \(q=\nu\), use denominator widths and \(\phi_q(r)=(q+1)\log(1+r^2)/2\). For SGED \(q=p\) and \(\phi_p(r)=r^p\); free \(p\) ranges over \([1,\infty)\). Write \(L=\min x_i\), and assume a nonconstant sample. Apply the earlier collapse and large-shape bounds separately where width can tend to zero or where SGED shape diverges. [Student closure and critical collapse](2026-11-25-sstdsged-math-report.md#1-student-critical-multiplicity) · [SGED upper tail](2026-11-08-sged-free-report.md#uniform-large-shape-tail)

**Proved.** On a compact shape interval with \(S\) bounded away from zero and infinity, take a feasible log-likelihood lower bound \(I\). A bound \(B\) on the total log height implies that each penalty in the region \(\ell\ge I\) is at most \(B-I\). Invert the lowest penalty over the shape interval to obtain a finite ratio bound \(R\). At the normal end this works uniformly, since the Student penalty increases with ratio and converges to the Gaussian penalty at each fixed ratio. In particular \(c\le L+wSR\) at a competitive right half face. A strict unsupported endpoint can be excluded using this inequality before evaluating score bounds. This argument restricts the region on which a signed comparison is needed.

## 1. Uniform comparisons at the Student normal end

**Proved.** Write \(h=1/\nu\), now use ordinary widths \(T=S/\sqrt{\nu}\), and put \(g=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\). The moment factor for total ordinary width is \(k(h)=E|T_\nu|=2g_\nu(0)/(1-h)\), so \(k(0)=2g\), \(k'(0)=3k(0)/4\). With \(v=(1-2h)^{-1}\) and \(V=W^2/4\),
\[
 V'(0)=\frac{1+3z^2}{2}-\frac32 k(0)^2z^2.
\]
Indeed, the mean of the two-piece law is \(c+E|T_\nu|(b^2-a^2)/(a+b)=c+kzT\). The log contribution differentiated at \(h=0\) in any smooth comparison coordinates is
\[
 \frac{r^4-2r^2-1}{4}
 +(r^2-1)\,\partial_h\log T + \frac{r_{\rm signed}\,\partial_h c}{w_e T}.
\]
Here \(w_e\) is the side fraction and \(r_{\rm signed}=(x-c)/(w_e T)\). The first term follows by differentiating \(\log K=\log(2g_\nu(0))\) and the kernel. A convenient integral for the derivative bounds is \(\phi_h(r)=\frac{1+h}{2}r^2\int_0^1(1+uhr^2)^{-1}\,du\). The gamma correction and its differentiated remainders can be bounded by integrating the partial fractions in TASK-157. Use \(\partial_h\log T=-V_h/(2V)\) when sd is held. [Student moment and normalizer remainders](2026-11-08-sstd-moment-report.md#evaluation-and-error-bounds)

**Proved, uniform normal test for free join or strict support.** Hold \(w\) fixed. If mean is free, fix \(c\) along the comparison, holding \(T\) fixed if sd is free and varying it by the variance equation otherwise. If mean alone is held, fix the join by keeping \(k(h)T\) constant, even at \(z=0\). In all of these cases a competitive observation's ratio at intermediate \(h\) differs by a bounded scale factor, uniformly in \(w\). In particular the normal derivative contains no \(1/w\) motion term. If both moments are held, use their equations. On a skew interval away from the half faces the ratios and both one-sided join derivatives are bounded, with the log-likelihood derivative continuous at joins. Near a strict supported half-normal endpoint every observation is on the broad side. Near a strict unsupported endpoint the competitive region is empty. Thus the comparison \(\ell(h)\le\ell(0)+C h\) is uniform in these regions, with \(C\) obtained from the integral bounds. On a tube with a strictly negative derivative enclosure, integrate it to get strict \(\ell(h)<\ell(0)\). A positive derivative at a regular normal optimizer gives a finite witness above its value. A positive derivative on the supported half-normal face also gives a witness if that face is reachable, by approaching the improved half-Student value with finite skew. Away from all contender tubes use a separated upper value enclosure. These are sample-dependent tests. With both moments \(0,1\) and \(\xi=1\) held, the two samples \((-1,1)\) and \((-3,3)\) give per-observation derivatives \(-1/2\) and \(15/2\), respectively, from \((x^4-6x^2+3)/4\). The first excludes a punctured collar, and the second defeats the normal candidate.

**Proved, both held at exact normal half contact.** Suppose \(L=c(0,0)=\mu-k(0)T_0\), \(T_0=\sigma/\sqrt{1-k(0)^2}\). Define \(J=c_w(0,0)=T_0 k(0)/[2(1-k(0)^2)]>0\) and \(H=c_h(0,0)=J/2>0\). The latter follows by differentiating \(T=\sigma/\sqrt{V}\) and using \(k'(0)\) above. For \(h,w>0\) small all copies of \(L\) are on the narrow side, with ratio \((c-L)/(wT)\). On rays \(h/w\to\lambda<\infty\) their per-copy penalty tends to \((J+\lambda H)^2/(2T_0^2)\). When \(h/w\to\infty\) it diverges, by monotonicity in ratio and the normal limit at every fixed ratio. The largest reachable corner value is therefore the broad closed half-normal value minus the number of contacts times \(J^2/(2T_0^2)\), sharp along \(h=o(w)\). It also equals the one-sided normal-profile endpoint value. An exact example is \((0,1)\), held \(\mu=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) and \(\sigma=\sqrt{1-2/\pi}\), for which \(T_0=1\).

For the signed comparison, take a competitive point and compare at fixed \(w\) back to \(h=0\). Continuity gives \(c_h\) bounded below by a positive constant and \(c-L\ge j w\) for some \(j>0\). The ratio bound at the competitive end also bounds ratios along the segment, because \(c\) increases with \(h\) and \(T\) stays comparable. On this segment each contact has \(r\) bounded below by a positive constant, and \(r_h=c_h/(wT)-rT_h/T\). Consequently \(-\partial_h\phi_h(r)\le -A/w+B_1\) for explicit \(A>0,B_1<\infty\); the explicit shape derivative at bounded ratio is bounded. The broad contributions and height have bounded derivatives as well. A small skew collar therefore gives strict \(\ell(h,w)<\ell(0,w)\) for all competitive points. Comparing the normal skew profile itself to its endpoint remains a distinct test. The removable ratio \((c(0,w)-c(0,0))/(wT(0,w))\) supplies that endpoint's one-sided derivative. This exact-contact argument uses \(H>0\) and applies to the attainable shape side \(h\ge0\).

**Proved, obstruction to uniform raw convergence.** On the sample \((-1,1)\) with free moments, take \(c=0,T=1,w=\sqrt h\). The narrow Student log density minus its Gaussian counterpart grows as \((1-\log 2)/(2h)\). These points leave every fixed competitive region. A Taylor expansion applied to both sides without checking standardized residuals gives neither a uniform error nor a sign test on the raw parameter box.

## 2. Signed finite-shape half collars

In this section the shape interval is compact, with finite \(p\ge1\) for free SGED shape and a positive width interval. Held \(p<1\) is also allowed in the half comparisons below. All right-face statements have reflected versions.

**Proved, free mean.** For a right half face use \(c=L\), since lowering the join below \(L\) only increases broad penalties at fixed width. In the interior compare to \(c=L\) at fixed broad width \(b\) when sd is free, or fixed \(S\) when sd is held (then \(S\) is determined by \(q,w\)). If \(\delta=(c-L)_+>0\), the possible improvement on observations other than \(L\) is bounded by \(D_0\delta\). Those observations are separated from \(L\), even for a subunit held shape. At least one copy of \(L\) costs at least \(K_0(\delta/(wS))^P\) for \(0\le\delta/(wS)\le R\), with \(K_0>0\), \(P=\max(2,p_{\max})\) for GED and \(P=2\) for Student. The bound follows from positivity of the kernel on bounded ratios and the power bound at zero. Maximizing
\[
 D_0 w S_{\max} u-K_0 u^P,\qquad u\ge0,
\]
gives at most \(\frac{P-1}{P}D_0 wS_{\max}(D_0 wS_{\max}/(PK_0))^{1/(P-1)}\). This is uniform \(o(w)\) with an explicit constant. A join below \(L\) is dominated by \(L\) in the same coordinates. If sd is free, keeping \(b\) fixed gives exactly \(n\log(1-w)\) relative to the half face before adding the \(o(w)\) term. This proves a strict collar exclusion, uniformly in shape and broad width on the compact block. The collar proof also works in ordinary Student coordinates including \(h=0\), using the kernel's continuous quadratic lower bound. If sd is held, add the broad-profile change calculated at \(c=L\) with \(S=2\sigma/W\). Its signed linear term is given below with join velocity zero. If negative throughout the possible face optimizers, the collar is strictly excluded. If positive at a face optimizer, \(c=L\) itself gives a better feasible point.

**Proved, held mean and free sd.** Use the join as a scale coordinate near the right face, where \(z>0\) and \(c<\mu\). At a supported join fix \(c\), so \(S=(\mu-c)/(kz)\) and \(S_w/S=2\) at the face. If the join overruns \(L\) in a neighborhood of a contact, compare first to \(c=L\) at the same \(w,q\), changing \(S\) by the mean equation. The scale, height and separated broad contributions then have bounded derivatives in \(c\), as \(\mu-L>0\) at a positive-width contact. The same \(o(w)\) maximization applies. Hence the support-constrained half profile with \(c\le L\) gives a signed comparison whenever its relevant broad derivative is separated. At a fixed, supported join the derivative is \(-2n+\sum_i r_i\phi'_q(r_i)\); it applies at equality by keeping the join exactly at \(L\). Free shape can be enclosed along with the supported join. This chart includes a contact with df two when scale can be free.

**Proved, both moments held.** Set \(S=2\sigma/W\), and denote the right endpoint by \(c_0(q)=\mu-k\sigma/\sqrt{v-k^2}\). On strict support the derivative of log likelihood in \(w\) at zero is
\[
 A=-n\alpha+\sum_i\phi'_q(r_i)\,[J/S_0+r_i(\alpha-1)],
 \quad
 \alpha=2(3v-4k^2)/W(1)^2,\quad J=4k\sigma v/W(1)^3.
\]
Here \(S_0=2\sigma/W(1)\). This follows from \(S_w/S=\alpha\), \(c_w=J\) and the broad width derivative \(\alpha-1\). The same expression with \(J=0\) gives the free-mean, held-sd comparison at \(c=L\). A strict sign persists locally by continuity of the separated terms. At a **held-shape** exact contact, each copy of \(L\) costs \(\phi_q(J/S_0)\); differentiate the punctured profile relative to *that* face value using the integral difference quotient of \(c-c_0\). It extends smoothly in \(w\) there. This incorporates the earlier fixed-shape contact result into a signed test without dividing two rounded differences.

Neither direction of the strict-support comparison can be assumed. **Proved counterexamples.** For SGED with \(p=1,\mu=\sigma=1\) held, \(S_0=1\), \(c_0=0\) and the first derivative vanishes on every strictly supported sample. Expand \(S=1+w+w^2/2+O(w^3)\), \(c=w+3w^2/2+O(w^3)\) and \((1-w)S=1-w^2/2+O(w^3)\). The second-order coefficient in log likelihood is \(3n/2-\sum x_i/2\). Thus \((1,2)\) beats the right face locally, whereas \((4,5)\) excludes the punctured neighborhood. This exclusion leaves the opposite half and distant finite points for the global comparison.

### Moving exact half contacts

**Proved.** At every finite shape of either family \(c_0'(q)<0\). In Student denominator coordinates \(k^2/v=(q-2)[\Gamma((q-1)/2)/(\sqrt\pi\Gamma(q/2))]^2\). Its log derivative is \(1/(q-2)-[\psi(q/2)-\psi((q-1)/2)]>0\). Indeed the difference in brackets is the integral of \(e^{-(q-1)u/2}/(1+e^{-u/2})\), strictly less than \(2/(q-1)\) and, more sharply, less than \(1/(q-2)\) using \(1+e^{-u/2}\ge2e^{-u/4}\). For GED \(k^2/v=3e^{-\Delta}/4\) with \(t=1/p\) and \(\Delta=\sum_{j\ge1}\log(1+t^2/((j+t)(j+3t)))\). The derivative of every summand is positive in \(t>0\), so \(k^2/v\) increases in \(p\). The same monotonicity shows that \(c(q,w)\) decreases in \(q\) for fixed \(0\le w<1/2\), since its moment displacement depends increasingly on \(k/\sqrt v\). It increases in \(w\) at fixed \(q\).

If \(c_0(q_*)=L\), the implicit equation \(c(q(w),w)=L\) gives \(q(w)=q_*+Jw/(-c_0')+O(w^2)\). This is admissible, including at the lower SGED endpoint \(p=1\). The sharp reachable value at the moving contact is the closed broad value. Among paths \(q-q_*=\eta w+o(w)\) the contact ratio when unsupported tends to \((J+c_0'\eta)_+/S_0\), with zero limiting cost if the datum ends up on the broad side. The held-shape penalty would therefore understate the free-shape supremum. For Student an exact example at \(\nu=3\) is \((0,1)\) with \(\mu=2/\pi,\ \sigma=\sqrt{1-4/\pi^2}\). For GED at \(p=1\) the same sample has the contact at \(\mu=\sigma=1\). These are contact classifications, independent of which face wins globally.

For a **signed** comparison, use the supported half shape \(s\) as reference. Solve \(c(q,w)=c_0(s)\) with \(s\ge q_*\), and put \(F(s)=\ell(s,0)\) on that face. The derivative of the log likelihood at \(w=0\), with \(s\) fixed, is denoted by \(C(s)\). It can be computed directly using \(q_w=J/(-c_0')\) and keeping \(c\) fixed. Equivalently,
\[
 C(q_*)=A_{\rm ext}(q_*)-\frac{J}{c_0'(q_*)}F'_+(q_*).
\]
Here the broad-side extension in \(A_{\rm ext}\) uses \(\phi'_1(0)=1\) for Laplace, and \(F'_+\) differentiates toward supported shape. The contact contributions in the two terms cancel, since the matched join stays fixed. Direct use of the fixed-join derivative avoids that cancellation in arithmetic. The derivative is continuous up to the contact across supported reference shapes. For GED the only endpoint terms needed there are bounded multiples of \(r^p\) and \(r^p\log r\), extended by zero. For Student all broad derivatives are continuous at zero. Uniform derivative integration thus gives \(\ell(q,w)=F(s)+w(C(s)+o(1))\) for matched joins, with a remainder bounded by the variation of the derivative over the chart.

For \(c>L\) use the clipped reference \(s=q_*\) and first compare to the finite point with \(c=L\) at the same \(w\). The inverse slope of \(q\mapsto c(q,w)\) is bounded nearby. The peak and the noncontact contributions can increase by at most a constant times \(c-L\). The contact penalty offsets that gain up to the explicit \(o(w)\) bound from the free-mean argument. Consequently a negative enclosure for \(C\) excludes the punctured neighborhood relative to the supported face profile, on **both** sides of the contact curve. A positive \(C(q_*)\) defeats a candidate at \(q_*\) via the exact matching path. A strict local sign alone leaves all other contenders for the global cover. If shape is held, use the held-shape comparison instead.

**Proved, opposite moving-contact signs on rational data.** For SGED at the \(p=1\) contact with \(\mu=\sigma=1\), \(c_0'=-1/2,\ J=1,\ q_w=2\). These follow from the recurrence for \(\psi\) in the moment equations. Along the matched join, \(S=1/(kz)\). At \(p=1\) the shape derivative of \(\log k\) is \(-2+\gamma\), and that of \(-\log\Gamma(1+1/p)\) is \(1-\gamma\). The peak derivative along the path is therefore \(-4\) per copy. For a positive observation \(y\), its penalty derivative is \(y(2\log y-5+2\gamma)\), from \(r=ykz/(1-w)\). Thus \(C=-4n+\sum_{y>0}y(5-2\gamma-2\log y)\). For \((0,1)\), \(C=-3-2\gamma<0\). Continuity gives a strict relative exclusion on a tube about that contact. For one zero and five copies of \(5/2\), \(C>1\) using \(\gamma<0.578,\ \log(5/2)<0.917\). A finite skew and \(p>1\) beat the contact value. Shape one remains an attained fitted shape for finite skew, as prescribed by the domain.

## 3. Covers and the SGED near-uniform width

**Proved, cover requirements.** For both held moments use a two-dimensional shape/skew cover after the Student lower-shape/collapse and the SGED large-shape exclusions. In each right-half box the minimum and maximum joins are the opposite corners \((q_{\max},w_{\min})\) and \((q_{\min},w_{\max})\). Use these before multiplying independent moment intervals or dropping penalties. Profile the supported half face as a one-dimensional shape problem. Its support constraint is a monotone inequality on shape, so either bracket the contact root or exclude that side. Use the signed collar on contender tubes and absolute value bounds away from those tubes. At smooth interior boxes bound the score and Hessian and isolate stationary regions. Split at joins or enclose both sides. In SGED at \(p=1\) include the attained edge and the one-sided shape score. Validity of these covers follows from the pointwise inequalities above. Completeness of a finite run requires that all boxes close within the budget.

With both moments free, use the existing Student side-scale inversions for fixed join and df, wherever both sides are nonempty and the strict multiplicity test holds. Cover the remaining join/shape pair, propagate intervals through the scale solves, and include join knots and half collars. A half-Student with free scale and endpoint at an extreme has a unique positive conditional scale root when its strict multiplicity test holds. In denominator widths the scalar equation is \(\sum_i y_i^2/(b^2+y_i^2)=n/(q+1)\); it decreases strictly in \(b>0\) on nonconstant distances. The critical cases require the previous collapse comparison. For a free-scale half-GED the width satisfies \(b^p=p\sum_i y_i^p/n\). With mean alone held keep the supported join as a nuisance coordinate even on the half face. With sd alone held the face join is the sample extreme. **Proved, explicit SGED near-width cutoff with sd held and mean free.** Write \(R_x=\max x-\min x\), \(R_0=2\sqrt3\,\sigma\), \(t=1/p\), \(\eta=\log(R_x/R_0)\), \(B=-n\log R_0\), and \(m_0\) for the smaller extreme count. Using TASK-264's extreme-penalty and gamma estimates, the upper bound uniform in skew is
\[
 \ell \le B+n C_0t^2-m_0\exp(\eta/t-\gamma),
 \qquad C_0=(15/4)\zeta(2).
\]
Keep \(\eta/t\) in the exponent. For \(\eta>0\) and feasible \(I\), take \(n C_0 t^2\le1\) and choose \(\eta/t\) strictly above \(\max\{1,\gamma+\log((1+\max(0,B-I))/m_0)\}\). This excludes the whole smaller-\(t\) tail. For \(\eta=0\) use the sharper signed exact-contact bound of TASK-264.

For \(0<\epsilon=-\eta\le1/4\), use the midpoint join and unit skew at \(t_w=\epsilon/(8+4|\log\epsilon|)\). The peak excess per observation is at least \(3\zeta(2)t_w^2/[2(1+3t_w)^2]>t_w^2\), by subtracting the lower quadratic curvature of \(\log\Gamma(1+u)\) on \([0,3t_w]\). Each penalty is at most \(\exp(-\epsilon/t_w+C_0t_w)\), since \(\log(R_0/S)\le-\gamma t+(7/2)\zeta(2)t^2\). Integrate the upper bound \(g'(u)\le-\gamma+u\zeta(2)\) from \(t\) to \(3t\) for the symmetric term, and add the skew bound \(3\zeta(2)t^2/2\) from TASK-264. Each penalty is therefore less than \(t_w^2/2\): \(C_0 t_w<1\) and \(\epsilon(8+4|\log\epsilon|)\le8\) give an upper bound \(64 e^{-7}t_w^2\). This feasible point beats \(B\) by more than \(nt_w^2/2\). Now drop penalties from the tail upper bound and use \(t<t_w/\sqrt{2 C_0}\) to exclude the whole smaller-\(t\) tail relative to the witness. This construction applies when skew is free or held at one. For held asymmetric skew the same construction uses the join \(L+wR_x\) and both extreme ratios \(R_x/S\), so the identical bounds apply. Positive dyadic data use the sign of \(R_x^2-12\sigma^2\) on the exact represented values, with an exact fallback if needed. The likelihood gain in the narrow inside regime is genuinely small. The bounds give cutoffs in real arithmetic, with precision and representability checked separately. ## What this changes for the fitter

- Correct the Student ordinary-width moment derivative. Enclose normal derivatives in the hold-preserving coordinates above after screening ratios. For both held moments use the strict-support predicate \(L\gtrless c(0,0)\) and the contact formula at equality. A proved winning normal profile returns **SnormBoundary** or the appropriate **RightHalfNormalBoundary** or **LeftHalfNormalBoundary** with the reachable likelihood. Use a finite or other face winner if a positive derivative refutes that normal candidate and the global comparison selects the alternative.
- For finite-shape half candidates compare the actual one-sided value at held-shape contact. With shape free bracket \(c_0(q)=L\), include the reachable closed value, and use the matched-join derivative and clipped bound for signed exclusion. For free moments the explicit negative half collar can be removed from the finite cover once its face envelope is covered. With held moments check the indicated sign or higher-order enclosure. Return the corresponding **HalfT** or **HalfGed** boundary status in its orientation only when the face wins and finite attainment has been excluded. Preserve a winning df-two or collapse boundary under its existing criterion.
- For SGED held width use exact width order, the strict-order cutoffs and the earlier exact-contact penalty. Use **UniformBoundary** only when its reachable path wins. A sign test or witness that eliminates one candidate supplies no certification of the other candidates.
- Use at most \(128\min(\mathrm{iterlim},100000)\) certificate evaluations and \(\min(\mathrm{iterlim},100000)\) solver iterations. Recover each surviving candidate's enclosure in the public coefficients. Separate global comparison from local accuracy, finite attainment and uniqueness. Return a proved finite maximizer even if an excluded face ties it. Preserve a proved complete finite maximizer set or tied winning limit witnesses. If the global cover or a sign enclosure fails to close, keep the successful, accuracy-checked search result flagged uncertified unless a separated comparison refutes it. Search failure follows the existing score and failure rules. ## Open

- **Zero signs and unseparated values.** The Laplace examples show why a first-order test cannot close every collar. A general normal or matched half-contact comparison at simultaneous zero derivatives needs an effective higher-order enclosure through the optimizer tube or a structural equality test. Arbitrary finite/face ties across free shape remain undecided. Near contacts whose support order cannot be resolved by the available precision need a sharper enclosure of the contact root or an exact predicate.
- **Full-domain costs.** The compact sign and value bounds specify when to close each cell. The box counts cover two separated rectangles. General practical operation needs measured whole-domain root isolation and coefficient recovery across the four hold masks, especially the one-moment Student covers and constrained half-shape profiles near zero signs. This includes the upper and lower shape intersections and the distinct collapse comparison, where a positive-width compact proof alone cannot apply.
- **SGED width representation.** The strict-inside witness still calls for shape of order \(|\log|\eta||/|\eta|\). Effective alternatives for comparing in the tiny-gain regime, recovering public coefficients or proving the relevant winner within binary64 and the default budget remain open.
