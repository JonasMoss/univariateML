## Scope and proof tools

All results concern finite positive-likelihood maximizers, with the parameter restrictions of the cited attainment analyses. Generic means Lebesgue almost everywhere on the stated sample chart with **fixed holds**. In the ordinary distinct-data chart, \(m=n\). On a fixed ordered stratum \(z_1<\cdots<z_m\) with positive counts \(k_i\), \(m\) is the number of freely varying levels, apart from explicitly fixed contacts, and the derivative is \(k_i g_\theta(z_i)\), with \(g_\theta=\partial_z\log f_\theta\). At moving contacts use the derivative of the composed height. Claims of generic separation conditional on attainment impose no restriction on where attainment itself holds. The exact examples are real samples. A repeated-data stratum can have positive measure in its own chart while being null in the full sample space. Reflection ties, exact-real crossings and continuous sets have the distinct equality mechanisms described in the tie report. ### Analytic incidence bound

**Proved.** Suppose two prospective winners have analytic spatial scores on the observation interval, and their score functions are distinct for every competing parameter pair under consideration. If the pair has at most \(d\) free real coordinates, then the samples at which all \(m>d\) level derivatives match form a null set. Indeed, for a common score zero of multiplicity \(r\), the \((r-1)\)-st spatial derivative of the score difference has a simple zero there. The implicit function theorem expresses this level locally as a smooth function of the pair parameters. To justify the countable cover, partition the incidence by the vector of zero multiplicities. In each such stratum the implicit graphs cover neighborhoods *relative to that stratum*. Second countability gives a countable subcover for each vector. The projection to sample space therefore lies in countably many smooth images of dimension at most \(d\). This proof requires analyticity in the observation and smoothness of the spatial derivatives in the parameters. It needs no global bound on the number of score intersections. A fixed face, seam or floor has its own smaller parameter dimension.

For a composition with \(r\) exceptional levels (e.g. moving endpoints, critical atoms), hold those levels fixed first, check the other gradients, and apply Fubini on the ordered chart. If \(d\) is a bound on the pair dimension with those levels fixed, \(m-r>d\) suffices when all levels are otherwise free. A held endpoint observation contributes no free coordinate, so count only free noncontacts. This argument permits a candidate chart over fixed contact levels whose supported noncontacts vary in an open neighborhood. Across a whole ordered chamber the same chart can instead express the parameters as functions of the moving contact levels. At Tukey lambda contacts, matching endpoint formulae give smooth composed heights. For other moving faces the actual optimizing charts and their heights are given below.

For an ordinary positive smooth family, the compact-comparison theorem makes the different-gradient ties null. The union of this exceptional set and the analytic incidence projection does **not** generally equal the tie locus. More precisely, let \(E\) be the nondifferentiability test in TASK-412, using the indicated candidate charts and requiring their common height to be the full supremum. It is exactly the set of global ties with different chart gradients. Any remaining tie must lie in the equal-gradient incidence and requires two distinct feasible candidates at the global height. Where pointwise separation has been proved, \(E\) alone is exact. At regular crossings, unequal gradients give a locally smooth equality hypersurface for the two optimized heights. A global switch across it follows when only those two branches can win locally. Degenerate branches and coincidences of three or more heights are covered by the envelope test without a claim that the full tie set is a smooth manifold. Where a finite candidate inventory is already available, its exact equal-largest-height rule gives the actual maximizers at crossings. [Exact envelope test](2026-11-27-uniq-generic-math-report.md#the-theorem)

### Student scores and faces

**Proved.** Write \(g(z)=-p(z-b)/[(z-b)^2+a^2]\), \(p=\nu+1,\ a>0\), with \(a=\mathrm{sd}\sqrt{\nu-2}\) for std or \(a=\sigma\sqrt{\nu}\) for lst. Two distinct triples match at at most three distinct levels by cross multiplication. Identity forces a common center (the score's unique zero), a common \(p\) (the coefficient at infinity) and a common \(a\). With \(p\) held the cubic coefficient vanishes, leaving at most two levels. With \(b\) held, two distinct triples agree at at most one positive absolute residual magnitude, in addition to a zero residual. All these charts are positive and analytic, including at a fitted finite lower shape floor. For comparisons with an optimized normal, use the affine score with its nuisance parameters as chart coordinates. It gives a cubic against Student, and at fixed center gives at most one nonzero magnitude of equality. The positive-scale \(t_2\) face uses the same rational Student score with \(p=3\).

For an eligible critical atom \(b_0\), the optimized log collapse value is a constant minus \(p_0\sum_i k_i\log|z_i-b_0|\) over the noncontacts. On the ordered level chart it is analytic. Its noncontact score \(-p_0/(z-b_0)\) has at most two intersections with a finite Student or a normal. If the atom is the held center there is at most one nonzero absolute magnitude of intersection. For distinct collapse atoms, equality of their noncontact scores gives at most one level, and gives none when the exponents agree. In the Student masks considered here all eligible competing atoms on a finite-supremum stratum have the same exponent. This also separates normal, lower Student and collapse faces whenever they compete at the level counts stated below, except that a critical positive-scale lower Student profile may itself have only an unattained collapse value. The exact face inventory and actual-hold Gaussian and \(t_2\) optimizations come from the Student atlas and its review. ### Azzalini scores and faces

**Proved.** Finite sn and st scores are analytic on the real line, including at zero slant and the finite df floor. For st, let \(w\) denote scale. The two leading coefficients of the density tails, after removing \(|z|^{-\nu-1}\) in the *observation* \(z\), are
\[
 2t_\nu(0)\nu^{(\nu+1)/2}w^\nu
 T_{\nu+1}(\pm\alpha\sqrt{\nu+1}).
\]
The tail exponent identifies df, the sum identifies scale and the ratio identifies slant. Distinct translates of the remaining integrable positive density cannot agree (that would make it periodic). For sn, the coefficients of \(-z^2/2\) in the two log-density tails are \(1/w^2\) on the favorable side and \((1+\alpha^2)/w^2\) on the other, with equality at zero slant. This follows directly from the Gaussian tail asymptotic \(\log\Phi(-u)=-u^2/2-O(\log u)\) as \(u\to+\infty\), where the correction is \(O(\log u)\) in magnitude. Their ordering, smaller coefficient and ratio identify sign, scale and absolute slant. Translation then identifies location. Equality of scores on any interval implies equality of normalized densities by positivity, analyticity and normalization. Therefore two distinct finite tuples have nonidentical scores, even at the singular-information sn symmetry point.

The st infinite-df face \(N\) has the same-hold sn likelihood and a distinct score from every finite st density by the tails. The sn and st oriented half faces have a Student or Gaussian spatial score in the support interior. A free face endpoint is its corresponding sample extreme, by radial monotonicity, and uses the full density. A held endpoint uses the finite-slant endpoint factor, as opposed to the moving-contact factor. At fixed face df the scale optimum, when free and attained, is unique by the monotone Student scale score (or the Gaussian RMS formula). Score identity of a half face with a finite candidate requires zero finite slant and identical base parameters; equality of heights is then impossible as soon as there is a strict noncontact, which gains a factor of two in the half face. The same argument compares \(N\) with a half-normal corner. Opposite half faces, when both positive, have opposite noncontact score signs at interior levels. For st collapse, the critical log trace has the same spatial term as Student collapse, with constant depending on counts by side, df and the slant/displacement optimization. At fixed counts and ordering these constants are independent of distances. Its rational score cannot agree identically with a full-support analytic score. The st face inventory is \(N\) when df is free, oriented half-Student when slant is free, half-normal corners when both are free, and critical collapse when scale is free and a critical atom is compatible with the location hold. For sn only oriented half-normal faces can compete on nonconstant data in these masks. Zero-product ends lose to any positive finite fit. [Joint st envelopes](2026-11-27-st-atlas-math-report.md#joint-envelope-and-attainment-comparison) · [sn face comparison](2026-11-25-sn-math-report.md#strict-tail-exclusion-and-exact-sample-criterion)

In the mask sections, “\(m\ge j\)” for Azzalini finite winners follows from the incidence bound with \(d\) equal to twice the number of free coordinates. These bounds can include face comparisons at the **same counts**, provided the indicated number of levels are free. A same-hold full-support face drops at least one pair coordinate. An oriented half face drops at least the slant coordinate and the moving threshold when free, leaving at most one exceptional level. The two opposite faces lose at least two pair coordinates between them, or separate by sign directly. A collapse face has no free parameter once the atom level is fixed, and at most one exceptional level. These counts allow the incidence lemma for finite/face and face/face ties, apart from identical-score configurations already excluded by strict heights or zero-product faces. They do not assert that a particular face or finite candidate wins.

### Repair of the skew-t scale crossing

**Proved.** Hold \(\xi=0\) and \(\alpha=A=2^{20}\). Put \(p=100,\ n=201,\ R=2^{50000}\). The sample has \(p\) copies each of \(1,D\), and one of \(R\), for \(D\in[D_-,D_+]=[2^{10}A,2^{150}A]\). At df one, let \(S_x=1+A/\sqrt{1+A^2+(w/x)^2}\). Multiplying the true likelihood by \(\pi^n D^{2p}R^2/2^{p+1}\) gives
\[
 F(w,D)=w\left(\frac{w^2}{w^2+1}\right)^p
 \left(\frac{D^2}{w^2+D^2}\right)^p S_1^p
 \left(\frac{S_D}{2}\right)^p\frac{R^2}{w^2+R^2}\frac{S_R}{2}.
\]
The single power of \(w\) follows by cancelling the scale powers of the first two groups. Both compactified scale endpoints have value zero uniformly on the band. Write \(P_-,P_+\) for the continuous maxima over \(0\le w\le8A\) and \(8A\le w\le\infty\), respectively.

At \(w=A/8\), \(S_1>19/10\), and each of the other five dimensionless factors exceeds \(3/4\), by \((1-u)^p\ge 1-pu\) and \((1+y)^{-1/2}\ge1-y/2\). Thus \(P_->E=(A/512)(19/10)^p>1\) throughout. At \(w=8A\), \(F\le 8A(9/8)^p<E\). For \(w\ge8A\), \(S_1\le9/8\) and \(w(1+(w/D)^2)^{-p}\le D\). Thus \(P_+(D_-)\le D_-(9/8)^p<E\). At \(D_+\), take \(w=D_+/16\). Each of the same five factors exceeds \(1/2\), \(S_1>1\), and \(F>w/32>8A\,2^p\ge P_-(D_+)\). The rational comparisons are in the checker. Define the exact sample by the least \(D_*\in[D_-,D_+]\) with \(P_+(D_*)\ge P_-(D_*)\). Continuity and the strict signs give an interior equality. The strict gap at \(8A\) forces a distinct finite maximizing scale on each side.

The same sample gives the same scale set with df free on \([1,\infty)\). Let \(W=2^{1000}\). On \(w\ge W\), the uniform Student envelope gives \(R^2 f_R\le2w\), and the other factors are at most \(2/w\). On \(w\le W,\nu\ge2\), use \(t_\nu(z)\le6(1+z^2)^{-3/2}\), giving \(R^2 f_R\le12W^2/R\). The other factors are at most one by \(f_x\le2w/(w^2+x^2)\le1/x\). In both cases the normalized objective is below one by the checked rational bounds, so loses to \(E\). This also excludes the skew-normal escape by continuity. On \(w\le W,\,1\le\nu\le2\), the base log-density df derivative is at most \(5/4-\tfrac12\log(1+z^2/2)\), using the Student normalizer derivative bound \(1/(4\nu^2)\). At a fixed CDF argument the log-CDF df derivative for df \(u\in[2,3]\) is at most two, obtained by integrating \(\partial_u t_u\le2t_u\), which follows from the same normalizer bound and the differentiated kernel. Differentiation under the integral is justified on this compact df interval by an integrable polynomial-tail bound with a logarithmic factor. The argument \(h=\alpha z\sqrt{(\nu+1)/(\nu+z^2)}>0\) has \(\partial_\nu\log h\le1\) and \(h\,t_{\nu+1}(h)/T_{\nu+1}(h)\le1\) by decreasing density on the positive half-line. Hence the log skew multiplier contributes at most three. The total df score is at most \(5n-\tfrac12\log(1+R^2/(2W^2))<0\) since \(R^2>2W^2\,2^{20n}\) and \(\log 2>1/2\). All winners are at df one. This proof gives at least two co-winners in each mask, with additional winning scales left open. ### Fixed-kernel Student and Cauchy location rule

**Proved.** For given \(a>0\), take the real roots \(u\) of
\[
 \sum_i k_i (u-z_i)\prod_{j\ne i}[(u-z_j)^2+a^2]=0
\]
and retain all roots at the smallest value of \(P(u)=\prod_i[(u-z_i)^2+a^2]^{k_i}\). This polynomial is of degree \(2m-1\) for the score, with leading coefficient \(n>0\), and \(P\to\infty\) at both ends. Equivalently the exceptional set is the existence of two distinct real roots with equal \(P\) and no root with smaller \(P\). This is an exact algebraic predicate and description of all ties for arbitrary real input and kernel width, with real algebraic root and equality algorithms for real algebraic inputs. It includes any accidental ties, and there are no intervals. At one level that level wins. For balanced levels with midpoint \(b\) and half-gap \(h\), the winners are \(b\) if \(h\le a\), and \(b\pm\sqrt{h^2-a^2}\) otherwise. With unequal counts the heavier side wins uniquely: reflection excludes the light half, and the score ratio on the heavy half can be smaller than one only below the smaller balanced stationary root if \(h>a\). To see strict crossing directly, put the heavy point at zero and the light point at \(D\). The ratio \(r(u)=u[(D-u)^2+a^2]/[(D-u)(u^2+a^2)]\) satisfies \(r(u)-1=(2u-D)(a^2-u(D-u))/[(D-u)(u^2+a^2)]\). On \(0<u<D/2\) whenever \(r<1\), \(u(D-u)<a^2\). There
\[
 (\log r)'=\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{D-u}
 -\frac{2u}{u^2+a^2}-\frac{2(D-u)}{(D-u)^2+a^2}>0
\]
since \(a^2>u(D-u)\) gives \(u^2+a^2>uD\) and \((D-u)^2+a^2>(D-u)D\), while \(1/u+1/(D-u)\ge4/D\). The target light/heavy count ratio belongs to \((0,1)\); the ratio runs from zero to one on this initial interval. The midpoint cannot win because its score is nonzero. - A generic result by itself supplies no binary64 cardinality certificate. If the value and coefficients are certified but cardinality is unresolved, retain \(\texttt{unique=NA}\). Use a pointwise singleton proof, a verified exclusion of every other winning candidate, or a certified full set when those predicates close.

The real-valued example exceeds the binary64 exponent range; no production output or default fit changes follow from its construction.

## Open

- **Student and Azzalini.** The Student score comparisons leave possible equal gradients below the stated level or magnitude count. Below the Azzalini incidence thresholds, compare stationary branches using nuisance score constraints or sharper spatial intersection counts. In particular the repaired st crossing leaves additional winning scales, its arithmetic type and finite near-crossing behavior open. Reflection establishes partners, with no general count of additional orbits. The st held-location mixed-sign problem still needs a two-level scale/slant comparison at general df.
- **Slash, half-t and Bhattacharjee.** The slash all-free witnesses establish separate local peaks and a finite/collapse equality. An exact all-free finite/finite equality or a small-level uniqueness proof is missing. For half-t and Bhattacharjee, the stationary comparisons are finite, but when height enclosures overlap there is no general exact equality predicate. Population-identifiable kernels and quadratic moment tests alone cannot order all those peaks.
- **EMG and folded normal.** Below the stated EMG incidence bounds, no general profile curvature or stationary-height ordering is available outside the specified classes. For foldnorm the exact set is found by the seam and finite-root comparison. A direct algebraic predicate for equal values at two positive roots is missing, and the Schanuel-dependent arithmetic result only covers ties with the seam.
- **Huber and Tukey lambda.** The Huber held-scale result identifies the generic cutoff class and the entire location interval within it. General two-level cutoff comparisons and explicit exceptional cutoff-equality equations remain open. For Tukey lambda the nonuniform critical components at low level counts, the full contact competition at arbitrary irrational held shapes, and exact equality across free-shape branches still need pointwise height comparisons. At fixed shape the polynomial lift resolves the algebraic-input case in principle.
- **Exact loci.** Where gradients separate, \(E\) identifies the tie set using the global winning height. Where only the incidence bound applies, any additional same-gradient ties must also satisfy finite stationarity or the relevant active-contact equations and equality of the full height. Removing the global-optimization condition from these descriptions, or proving it always reduces to symmetry or one simple crossing, remains open. No such reduction is assumed here.
