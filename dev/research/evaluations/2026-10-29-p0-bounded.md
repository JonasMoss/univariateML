# P0 M1: bounded families (2026-10-29)

Scope: finite, nonempty samples. For beta, Kumaraswamy and logit-normal,
assume every observation is strictly in (0,1) and held shape/scale values
are positive and finite. For endpoint families assume finite, valid held
coefficients. “Exists” means an attained finite, positive likelihood, not an
arbitrary maximizer of an identically zero likelihood. Feasibility failures
must stop with a diagnostic. Class labels below condition on the stated
sample and hold constraints; “data-dependent” includes degenerate samples.
The full-held case is outside M1; it should only evaluate compatibility.

`master` means the installed `/tmp/umvl-p0lib`. “No” means the wrapper does
not implement that hold, including silent partial matching. For triang, “Yes” means implemented and agreeing on the checked cases;
“partial” identifies an observed numerical failure despite an existing MLE.

## Beta

| Held | Class | Conditions, limit or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | data-dependent | Exists for nonconstant x; constant x: unbounded interior point mass, both shapes → ∞ at fixed ratio | Yes; stops on constant |
| shape1 | always | Any x in (0,1), including constant | No |
| shape2 | always | Any x in (0,1), including constant | No |

**Proof.** The log likelihood is strictly concave in the two shapes. A
one-shape slice tends to −∞ at 0 and at ∞: the normalizer grows only as a
power of the free shape, while the data term is a strictly negative linear
term. For both free, small-shape faces give −∞; a large total concentration
has exponential decay unless every observation equals its limiting mean,
by strict Jensen. For constant x, shapes `(tx,t(1-x))` give log L =
(n/2) log t + O(1). The bivariate shape-to-zero face gives a mixture of
point masses at 0 and 1; any fixed positive mixture weights give zero
interior likelihood. Shapes with one → 0 or one → ∞ and the other held have
endpoint point-mass limits; the same linear decay excludes escapes for
interior data. A U-shaped finite beta (both shapes < 1) is allowed and is
not a divergent likelihood at interior observations.

## Kumaraswamy

| Held | Class | Conditions, limit or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | data-dependent | Exists for nonconstant x; constant x: unbounded interior point-mass collapse | Yes for ordinary data; constant data produce a Boost exception, not an informative no-MLE diagnosis |
| a | always | Any x in (0,1), including constant | No: `a` partially matches `a0`, a starting value, and is silently not held |
| b | always | Any x in (0,1), including constant | No: unused argument |

**Proof.** For fixed a, `b = -n / sum(log(1-x^a))` is unique and positive.
For fixed b, the likelihood in a goes to zero as a → 0 or ∞: the small-a
power is `a^(nb)`; at ∞ the negative `(a-1) sum(-log x)` dominates
`n log a`. Thus a maximum is attained (uniqueness is not needed). For both
free, the profile in `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`, “Closed apart from point
masses”, goes to −∞ at both ends for nonconstant data. For constant x,
`a=t`, `b=-1/log(1-x^t)` gives `log L = n log t + O(1)` and a point mass
at x. With a → 0 and b → 0 at order `1/|log a|`, the weak limit is a
mixture of the endpoint point masses; with only a → 0 at held b, or b → 0
at held a, it is δ0. With a → ∞ at fixed b, or b → ∞ at fixed a, it is
δ1 or δ0 respectively. The U-shaped finite region remains admissible.

## Logit-normal

| Held | Class | Conditions, limit or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | data-dependent | Exists iff logits nonconstant; constant x: scale collapse to point mass | Yes; stops on constant |
| mu | data-dependent | Exists iff at least one logit differs from held mu; otherwise σ → 0, point mass at logistic(mu) | No |
| sigma | always | Any x in (0,1), including constant; mean of logits | No |

**Proof.** Transform `y=logit(x)`; the fixed Jacobian does not affect
maximization. Profiling gives `mu=mean(y)` and `sigma²=mean((y-mu)²)`;
with mu held replace it by the held value. For fixed sigma the quadratic
in mu has a unique maximizer. As mu → ±∞ at finite sigma the distribution
converges to δ1 or δ0. As sigma → ∞ with mu/sigma finite it converges to
an endpoint mixture; with mu fixed the weights are 1/2, 1/2. The interior
likelihood tends to zero. Finite logit-normal densities can be U-shaped;
that is not a divergence in the parameter likelihood for interior data.

## Uniform

Let `m=min(x), M=max(x)`. Endpoint equality with an observation is permitted
by `stats::dunif`. A held lower endpoint greater than m, or a held upper
endpoint less than M, is infeasible and must stop explicitly.

| Held | Class | Conditions, limit or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | data-dependent | m < M: endpoints m, M; m = M: width collapse, unbounded | Yes; stops on constant |
| min | data-dependent | Held min ≤ m and held min < M: max = M; if all data equal held min: collapse; held min > m infeasible | No |
| max | data-dependent | Held max ≥ M and held max > m: min = m; if all data equal held max: collapse; held max < M infeasible | No |
| min,max | evaluation only | All held: positive likelihood iff min ≤ m ≤ M ≤ max and min < max; otherwise stop | No |

**Proof.** The likelihood is `(max-min)^(-n)` when the sample is inside
the interval, so minimizing its positive width yields the table. When a
constant sample can be enclosed in intervals shrinking to it, it tends to
infinity. A held endpoint strictly outside the sample prevents collapse.

## Triangular

Write m and M for the sample extremes. Require `a < b`, `a ≤ c ≤ b`,
`a ≤ m`, and `b ≥ M` whenever the relevant quantities are held. In addition,
if a held a equals m, positive likelihood requires c=a; if a held b equals
M, it requires c=b. Thus both held endpoints observed are incompatible
for a nonconstant sample. A held endpoint strictly inside the sample range
must stop as infeasible. C denotes the constant-sample collapse condition:
all x equal z and every held coefficient (among a,b,c) equals z. Then the
free endpoints can shrink to z and log L → ∞. Otherwise, subject to the
positive-likelihood compatibility conditions, an attained MLE exists.
A held mode may be outside [m,M]: it remains feasible if the endpoints can
contain it. C can occur only when fewer than both endpoints are held, since
held `a=b` is invalid.

| Held | Class | Conditions, limit or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | data-dependent | C: collapse; otherwise attained | Yes |
| a | data-dependent | C or endpoint infeasibility; otherwise attained | Yes |
| b | data-dependent | C or endpoint infeasibility; otherwise attained | Yes |
| c | data-dependent | C: collapse; otherwise attained | Partial: solver can fail on a constant sample with mode outside the data |
| a,b | always | Compatible holds and positive likelihood; otherwise infeasible | Yes |
| a,c | data-dependent | C or incompatibility; otherwise attained | Yes |
| b,c | data-dependent | C or incompatibility; otherwise attained | Partial: solver can fail on a constant sample with mode below the data |
| a,b,c | evaluation only | All held: evaluate if parameters and data have positive likelihood; otherwise stop | Yes, checked |

**Argument (proved, conditional on positive likelihood).** Density is at
most `2/(b-a)`. For nonconstant data, width is bounded below by M−m; for a
constant sample with any held value different from z, valid widths are
bounded below by its distance from z. Hence width collapse is excluded.
As width → ∞ likelihood → 0. On bounded parameter sets, the density is
upper semicontinuous at admissible endpoint modes under its one-sided convention,
including `a=c` and `b=c`; non-mode observed endpoints have zero density.
A positive candidate therefore has a maximizing convergent subsequence.
In the C case use `a=z−ε,b=z+ε,c=z`, respecting any held values, or the
corresponding one-sided interval when one endpoint is held at z. The
likelihood is proportional to ε^(-n). This proof concerns existence, not
uniqueness or numerical convergence. Triangular fitted endpoints are not
generally both sample extremes: a non-mode observed endpoint has zero
density. For example, with x = (0,.25,1) and held a=0, the mode is 0 and
the fitted b exceeds 1. The fitter README describes the same compatibility
and collapse cases.

**Checked exception.** For five observations at .4 and held c = −1, the
MLE is a = −1, b = 1.8, c = −1: the one-sided density at .4 is 1/2.8.
With b=2 also held, the MLE is a=c=−1. The installed fitter reports endpoint
iteration failure in both cases; the independent oracle agrees with these
closed-form maxima. Six of 832 triangular edge probes fail this way (the
six include duplicate parameter combinations); all remaining status
classifications agree. The failure persists at iteration limits 100, 1,000 and 10,000 and
tolerances 1e−12, 1e−9 and 1e−6 (`triang-iteration.csv`).

## Kinds, restrictions and reference domains

The design's `endpoint` kinds for uniform min/max and triangular a/b are
appropriate; triangular c and all beta, Kumaraswamy and logit-normal
coefficients are `regular`. No R5 family restriction is listed for these
families; agree: none has no MLE for almost every ordinary sample. Constant
sample collapse is data-dependent and should stop, not cause a shape
restriction. No collapse exponent, spike, branch or endpoint restriction
is proposed. Endpoint collapse here is a width-collapse of constant data,
not the GEV/GPD R5 endpoint divergence.

R7 discrepancy: `stats::dbeta` explicitly supports zero and infinite shape
limits, with point masses and the `(0,0)` endpoint mixture; README domains
exclude zero and infinity. These are degenerate laws and give zero density
at ordinary interior observations (except both infinite at x=0.5); their
parameter fitting semantics and likelihood dominating measure need an
explicit design decision before admitting them as held values. The current
positive, finite M1 analysis does not silently extend to these values.
At held shape 0 the only laws obtainable by varying the other shape are
endpoint point masses or their mixture, so an interior sample has zero
probability. At a held infinite shape, a finite other shape gives an endpoint
point mass, and both infinite give a point mass at 1/2. Consequently no
positive likelihood exists for ordinary interior samples except the latter
atom case. R's `dbeta` reports `Inf` at the atom and `0` off it; combining
these density values across mixed samples is not a coherent likelihood
without a convention for a common dominating measure. Thus the tables above
are complete on the ordinary positive-finite sample/parameter domain, but
the R7 extension to degenerate beta coefficients requires a design decision;
it cannot safely be classified by silently treating those values as ordinary
continuous densities. `?stats::Beta` explicitly documents the point masses.
For interior samples, the additional R7 hold cases are:

| Held degenerate beta shape | Proper laws available as the other shape varies | Interior-sample result |
|---|---|---|
| shape1 = 0 | δ0, plus the δ0/δ1 mixture at shape2=0 | zero probability for every sample; stop |
| shape2 = 0 | δ1, plus the δ0/δ1 mixture at shape1=0 | zero probability for every sample; stop |
| shape1 = Inf | δ1; δ1/2 if shape2=Inf | zero probability unless every observation is 1/2, when δ1/2 is attained |
| shape2 = Inf | δ0; δ1/2 if shape1=Inf | zero probability unless every observation is 1/2, when δ1/2 is attained |

When neither shape is held, the accepted `(Inf,Inf)` atom coexists with
continuous shapes. The corresponding mixed discrete/continuous likelihood
must be defined explicitly before comparing their values; the finite-shape
classification in the beta table is not a claim about that unresolved model.

`stats::dunif` and `extraDistr::dtriang` also return zero density on finite
x for tested infinite endpoints, although their README endpoints are finite.
Such limits are not proper uniform or triangular probability laws, so R7
should distinguish accepted numerical density arguments from proper reference
models. Equal uniform bounds are rejected; triangular endpoint modes are
accepted. In tested cases `extraDistr::dkumar` rejects zero/nonfinite shapes
and the package's own `dlogitnorm` rejects zero/nonfinite sigma and nonfinite
mu. Actual sample observations 0 or 1 are excluded by the three bounded
fitters: the beta and Kumaraswamy reference densities can be infinite at a
boundary under U-shaped parameters, so evaluating such boundary observations
would require a separately specified likelihood convention.

## Proposed README YAML

Absence of `kind` means regular; absence of `fit_domain` means no R5 bound.
`holds` captures structural implementation gaps, not sample-dependent stops.

Each block is for a separate README; no `fit_domain` is proposed for any
coefficient because there is no R5 restriction.

```yaml
# beta
holds:
  - {held: [shape1], status: not-implemented, reason: one-shape fit absent}
  - {held: [shape2], status: not-implemented, reason: one-shape fit absent}
```

```yaml
# kumar
holds:
  - {held: [a], status: not-implemented, reason: a partially matches the starting argument a0 and is not held}
  - {held: [b], status: not-implemented, reason: one-shape fit absent}
```

```yaml
# logitnorm
holds:
  - {held: [mu], status: not-implemented, reason: held-location fit absent}
  - {held: [sigma], status: not-implemented, reason: held-scale fit absent}
```

```yaml
# unif
params:
  - {name: min, kind: endpoint}
  - {name: max, kind: endpoint}
holds:
  - {held: [min], status: not-implemented, reason: held-endpoint fit absent}
  - {held: [max], status: not-implemented, reason: held-endpoint fit absent}
```

```yaml
# triang
params:
  - {name: a, kind: endpoint}
  - {name: b, kind: endpoint}
# holds: []
# all five: fit_domain absent; no R5 restriction
```

## Numerical evidence and limits

All computations below ran on simbox using the shared slot wrapper, seed
791029, and the installed master package. `oracle.R` evaluates `stats::dbeta`,
`extraDistr::dkumar`, `stats::dunif`, and `extraDistr::dtriang`; logit-normal
uses the README formula directly. It uses multistart `optim` in log-parameter
coordinates; uniform uses exact order statistics, independently checked by
`uniform-search.R` with transformed-coordinate `optim`. That search was
within 2.79e−8 log-likelihood units of the exact boundary optimum.

The sweep includes `simulate_default` at n=2,3,5,20,100,1000; nonconstant
ties, constant samples, near-0 and near-1 point-mass approximations,
U-shaped data, and a uniform sample (the beta/Kumaraswamy shape-1 member). Exact δ0, δ1,
and endpoint-mixture samples were also passed to every bounded-family hold
subset (`limit-samples.csv`): the package rejects 0 and 1 at input. Their
interior approximations are covered by the numerical oracle. Both reference
beta and Kumaraswamy densities return Inf at 0 and 1 for shapes (0.5,0.5).
All 19 nontrivial hold subsets were searched. The result files identify
unsupported holds rather than treating a partially matched argument as a hold.
The 97 finite, genuinely implemented comparisons had maximum absolute
log-likelihood differences: beta 3.59e−10 (9), Kumaraswamy 8.98e−11 (9),
logit-normal 3.05e−11 (9), uniform 0 (8), triangular 1.94e−12 (62). All are
within 1e−6*n. `corners.R` adds 32 boundary and constant cases, including
all-held evaluation, incompatible endpoints, held modes outside the data,
and the two solver failures above; all successful finite cases agree within
1e−6*n. `edges.R` checks 912 endpoint/hold combinations; `audit.R` records
the six failed triangular edge rows and no unexplained status mismatches.

`paths.R` and `boundaries.R` record explicit divergent collapse paths and
parameter-boundary likelihoods for held and free cases. For n=5, the beta,
Kumaraswamy, logit-normal, uniform and triangular free-collapse log
likelihoods at path parameter 10 versus 500 are respectively 4.60→14.51,
11.09→35.65, 14.05→33.61, 8.05→27.61 and 11.51→31.07. Held-mode,
held-endpoint and held-logit-location collapse paths also increase.

The package's own `dlogitnorm` log density was checked against the README
formula on 101 points, maximum difference 3.34e−15. The triangular oracle
exhausts candidate data modes up to 100 distinct values; for larger samples
it searches 41 spread-out candidates and neighborhoods of its three best.
It is a lower bound, not a proof of global optimality. The independent
existence argument above does not rely on this numerical search. Exact
boundary beta laws and improper infinite endpoints require the R7 decision
above, rather than an extrapolation of these ordinary-domain results.
