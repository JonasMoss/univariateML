# P0 skew-real analysis — preliminary, incomplete

This is a partial result, not an M1 classification of every subset. In
particular, finite multistart searches cannot certify global optimality or
exclude a limit. `oracle.R` is an exploratory likelihood optimizer, not a
certificate; see its limitations below. Held-value validity and finite,
nonempty data are assumed throughout.

## Truncated normal: a design contradiction (proved)

`extraDistr::dtnorm` gives density zero at either finite support endpoint.
For any feasible finite parameters with a free lower bound, the bound must
satisfy `a < min(x)`. Raising `a` while staying below `min(x)` strictly
decreases the normalizing probability and increases every observation's
density. Thus there is no maximizer. The analogous argument applies to a
free upper bound. This holds regardless of which other coefficients are
held, even if the parent normal is fixed. At the sample extreme the density
is zero for at least one observation. For constant samples, the supremum
can also be infinite. If the fit density were explicitly changed to include
the endpoints, the optimal bounds would be the sample extremes; that is a
change in the density's pointwise likelihood, and R7 currently precludes it.

Here `H` is the held set; a finite held bound must strictly contain all
observations. `M` = mean, `S` = sd, `A` = a, `B` = b. Master always holds
bounds, interpreting `NULL` as the default infinity, not an estimated bound.

| H | Classification | Condition or mechanism | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any H missing A or B, including empty H and all such one-, two- and three-element subsets | never | Open endpoint: move free bound toward the appropriate extreme; supremum is not attained (possibly unbounded for constant data). | no: bound `NULL` means infinite, not estimation |
| A,B | data-dependent | Nonconstant: parent normal exists iff empirical variance is strictly less than the variance of the mean-matched exponential tilt on the held support; entire real line always exists. Otherwise exponential, reflected exponential or truncated exponential face; constant data collapse. | yes, numerical face tolerance 1e-8 n |
| A,B,M | data-dependent | Positive empirical mean squared distance `q` from M. With finite A,B, exists iff `q` is strictly less than the uniform expectation of `(X-M)^2`; otherwise uniform face. Infinite bound: exists whenever q > 0; q=0 collapses if M is inside support. | yes, numerical face tolerance 1e-8 n |
| A,B,S | always | Fixed positive S; for a feasible sample the mean lies strictly between the support bounds, and the strictly concave location likelihood attains its maximum. | yes |

For the A,B row, on `[a,∞)` the comparison variance is `(xbar-a)^2`;
reflect for `(-∞,b]`. On finite bounds it is the variance of the unique
exponential tilt with mean `xbar`; on the full line there is no exponential
face. This is the strict-concavity and moment-space argument already given in
`tnorm/README.md`. For A,B,M, the uniform expectation is
`((b-M)^3-(a-M)^3)/(3*(b-a))`. With infinite support the upper
natural-parameter endpoint is not integrable.

Proposed metadata: `a` and `b` are design coefficients under the stated
policy, but their `NULL` behavior needs a decision; each exact held subset
missing either bound would need a `no-mle` entry if the current support is
preserved. There is no R5 shape restriction here. A normal face can still
win after changing endpoint semantics, so estimating the extrema alone does
not ensure existence.

```yaml
# Per parameter
kind: design  # a and b
# Representative entry; expand to each exact held subset missing a or b.
holds:
  - {held: [mean, sd, b], status: no-mle, reason: "a approaches min(x) from below; dtnorm is zero at a"}
```

## Azzalini skew-normal: all hold subsets

Write L=`xi`, S=`omega`, A=`alpha`. The classifications assume n >= 2;
constant samples and exact equalities are specified where relevant. When a
finite-alpha fit and a half-normal face tie, existence requires an attained
finite-alpha global maximizer; comparing numerical values alone cannot settle
a tie. Master uses a tolerance and a finite-grid shape search.

| Held set | Classification and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | data-dependent: finite maximizer if one beats or ties both translated half-normal face suprema; otherwise right or reflected translated half-normal. Constant data: unbounded scale collapse. | yes; numerical global search, face tie tolerance |
| L | data-dependent: for nonconstant sample, MLE iff observations occur strictly on both sides of L; otherwise shape goes to the half-normal on the occupied side, with `log(2)` penalty per observation at L. All observations at L: unbounded scale collapse. | yes; face tie tolerance |
| S | data-dependent: compare finite shape candidates with both half-normal faces constrained to have S as scale; free location at the corresponding sample extreme. | yes; numerical global search, face tie tolerance |
| A | always for nonconstant data; constant data collapse at xi=x, omega→0. | yes |
| L,S | data-dependent: finite shape MLE iff sample has points strictly on both sides of L, or all observations equal L (any shape); otherwise one-sided half-normal limit with endpoint penalties. | yes; constant sample handling is numerical |
| L,A | always if at least one observation differs from L; otherwise scale collapse. | yes |
| S,A | always: location likelihood is continuous, strictly concave and tends to -infinity at either end. | yes |

For held L, write `d=x-L`, `eta=1/omega`, `beta=alpha*eta`.
The likelihood separates into a normal scale term and `sum(log Phi(beta*d))`.
The latter is strictly concave; its derivative crosses zero exactly when
there are strictly positive and negative d's. If all d=0 it is constant;
otherwise one-sided data drive beta to an infinite sign. A fixed alpha gives
a strictly concave likelihood in inverse scale and scaled location; the
nonconstant-data scale and location coercivity ensures attainment. With L
free and A free, the boundary comparison still depends on a global search;
the package does not certify it.

## Established facts for the other families

These facts do not classify all their hold subsets.

| Family | Established result | Master interface |
|---|---|---|
| sn | With finite held alpha, its location/inverse-scale likelihood is concave; free alpha can escape to either translated half-normal. Held xi requires all observations on the relevant side for a half-normal escape; equality requires special treatment. | All three coefficients holdable; numerical shape search and explicit face comparison. |
| snorm | For the free fit of nonconstant data, the exact Q-profile in `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`, §snorm, compares interior modes to the two translated half-normal endpoints. | Only free fit implemented. |
| sstd | Free fit has snorm, translated half-t, and two-piece t with df 2 faces. If a tie has multiplicity k > 2(n-k), free-df likelihood is unbounded; equality is not settled by this leading exponent. | Only free fit implemented; multistart and face comparison, not a global certificate. |
| sged | The full free fit without a lower shape bound has an unbounded spike: fix xi=1 and put the mode at an observation, profile scale and send nu to zero. The package restricts nu >= 1, with uniform and translated half-GED faces. | Only free fit implemented. |
| emg | Free fit has the normal and translated exponential faces; fixed endpoint at the minimum changes the pointwise exponential limit by a factor 1/2 at each tied minimum. | All three coefficients holdable; multistart and face comparison, not a global certificate. |

The `sn` statements follow the concave profile and documented comparison in
its README; the `snorm` result follows its displayed Q formula. For the sstd
tie, a t density at a repeated mode scales as `s^-1`, while each of the
other observations contributes `s^nu`: likelihood is proportional to
`s^(nu*(n-k)-k)`. Choosing nu close enough to 2 proves divergence when
`k>2(n-k)`. This exponent alone does not decide equality.

For sged, with symmetric exponential-power shape p, scale profiled and k
observations at the mode, the profiled log likelihood has leading term
`-n*log((n-k)/n)/p`, tending to +infinity for a nonconstant sample. This
proves the spike for the all-free subset and for xi held at 1; it does not
prove that `nu >= 1` is necessary for every other held subset. Tumlinson
(2015), *Statistics & Probability Letters* 107, 111–114,
DOI 10.1016/j.spl.2015.08.009, is the cited published result in the
repository; the above argument is independent of that citation.

The sged README domain `[1, inf)` differs from the reference density's
`nu > 0` (its Details explicitly say so). Under R7 the coefficient domain
should be `(0, inf)` and the fit restriction separately expressed as below;
whether that restriction can be relaxed for some holds remains open.

```yaml
# Proposed sged nu, conditional scope not yet established
fit_domain:
  restriction:
    mechanism: spike
    while_free: []
    reason: "nu below 1 admits a nu-to-zero spike in the fully free model; necessity under holds is unresolved"
```

All coefficients of sn, snorm, sstd, sged and emg are regular under the
design; this is consistent with their support and role. No other R5
restriction is listed for these families. No other R7 mismatch has been
verified.

## Evidence gaps

The requested complete subset-by-subset classification and conditions for
translated half-family escapes under holds remain unfinished. `oracle.R`
uses the independent formulas in `density.R`; it samples the interior only
and does not prove that an escape wins. The shape transform approaches but does not include the
attained sged `nu=1` face. Interpret its output accordingly. `paths.R`
implements the GED spike algebra and probes the reference endpoint directly.

`paths.R` ran in 0.35 s. For `x=(0,1,2,4,5)` and held normal
parameters and upper bound, log likelihood rose from -9.84131 at lower-bound
gap 1 to -9.31034 at gap 0.001, then was `-Inf` at gap 0. The GED path
rose from -7.41106 at p=0.1 to 197.21918 at p=0.005. The installed

checks of the above arguments, not a substitute for the missing sweep. The
shared CPU-slot wrapper had no available slot during this session, so the
longer oracle sweep was subsequently run on simbox; its results are summarized below.

## Translated half-family faces under holds

The following exact geometric constraints apply to snorm, sstd and sged
for every hold subset, but they do not by themselves say which face wins.
Use the variance-one symmetric base density at shape nu, and let
`m=E|Z|` (for normal, `m=sqrt(2/pi)`; for GED,
`m=Gamma(2/nu)/sqrt(Gamma(1/nu)Gamma(3/nu))`; for the
variance-one t, `m=Gamma((nu-1)/2)/Gamma(nu/2)*sqrt((nu-2)/pi)`).
If the finite limiting half-family has its endpoint `c` and parent scale
`t>0`, the limit has mean `c + sign*m*t` and standard deviation
`t*sqrt(1-m^2)`, where sign is +1 for a right half-family and -1 for a
reflected left half-family. It is feasible only if every observation lies
on the supported side of c. Thus a held mean forces
`c=mean-sign*m*t`; a held sd forces `t=sd/sqrt(1-m^2)`; holding both
fixes c and t for each nu. Held nu fixes the half-family's shape. A finite
held xi excludes a pure xi-to-infinity or xi-to-zero half-family escape.
With neither mean nor sd held, profiling moves c to the appropriate sample
extreme. If either is held, doing so need not be feasible or optimal. For
sstd, limits involving nu approaching 2 or infinity simultaneously with xi
need separate joint-limit analysis; the preceding finite-nu formulas alone
do not settle those corners. For sn, held xi fixes the endpoint and held
omega fixes the parent scale, while a free xi moves to the relevant extreme.
A held xi exactly at an observation creates a log(2) penalty per such
observation in the direct alpha-to-infinity limit; a free xi can approach
from outside more slowly than 1/|alpha| and avoids that penalty.

## Further restriction observation

The stated sged spike demonstration requires shape nu to be *free*: if nu
is held at a fixed value below 1, nu cannot tend to zero. This shows that
the given spike proof does not justify rejecting every fixed subunit nu.
It does not establish existence for every such held model, because other
boundary paths must also be excluded. In particular, the current proposed
`while_free: []` needs review, and should not be treated as proved minimal.

The SSTD high-tie divergence can persist with sd fixed. At xi=1 and mean
held at the tied value, take nu=2+epsilon; with k observations at the mean
and n-k away, the likelihood is asymptotic to a positive constant times
`epsilon^((2*(n-k)-k)/2)`. For `(0,0,0,0,1)` it diverges as
`epsilon^-1`. The numerical path in `paths.R` rises from -1.27687 at
`epsilon=.1` to 14.9549 at `epsilon=1e-8`. This is a degenerate-tie issue,
not a proof that a new R5 restriction is needed for almost every sample.

## Fernandez–Steel skew-normal: hold subsets

Here M=`mean`, S=`sd`, X=`xi`. Assume nonconstant finite data unless
otherwise stated. The right/left half-normal faces are constrained as in
"Translated half-family faces under holds" below. `Interior wins` means an
attained finite-parameter global maximizer has likelihood at least as high
as both face suprema. For these conditional problems the face comparison is
an implicit criterion, not a closed-form decision procedure.

| Held | Classification | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | data-dependent: interior wins or half-normal face; exact free-fit Q profile is in `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`. | yes; compares exact Q-profile |
| M | data-dependent: interior wins or constrained half-normal face. | no |
| S | data-dependent: interior wins or constrained half-normal face. | no |
| M,S | data-dependent: interior wins or constrained half-normal face; when both faces put some observation outside support, finite xi attains the maximum. | no |
| X | always for nonconstant data; otherwise location/scale collapse. | no |
| M,X | always if the sample is not identically equal to M; otherwise scale collapse. | no |
| S,X | always: location density has Gaussian tails and a continuous positive maximum. | no |

With X held, the model has fixed left/right scale ratio. Its log density is
a concave function of residual divided by scale; reparametrizing by inverse
scale and scaled location yields a strictly concave, coercive objective on
nonconstant data. With X free, limiting X=0 or infinity produces the
half-normal faces. The comparison criterion is an analysis of possible
closure faces, not a numerical certification of a particular sample.

## Skew-t and skew-GED: unclassified subsets

For each of sstd and sged, the empty set and most proper nonempty subsets of
`{mean, sd, nu, xi}` remain without a complete existence criterion here.
Master accepts only the empty set. The specific full-free facts, SSTD tie
path and SGED spike proof above apply; the half-family constraints below
apply to every subset. Numerical comparisons alone do not establish the
other classifications, so those unresolved cases are not labelled always/data-dependent/never.

Some three-coefficient held cases can nevertheless be settled directly:

| Family | Held | Classification | Argument; master |
|---|---|---|---|
| sged | sd,nu,xi | always | A fixed-shape, fixed-scale two-piece GED is continuous and vanishes in either location tail; master no. |
| sged | mean,nu,xi | always unless all observations equal held mean | With fixed nu,xi the scale likelihood vanishes at zero for any nonconstant sample and at infinity; constant data at the held mean collapse; master no. |
| sstd | sd,nu,xi | always | A fixed-shape, fixed-scale two-piece t is continuous and vanishes in either location tail; master no. |
| sstd | mean,nu,xi | data-dependent | At scale zero, k copies of the held mean contribute scale^-k and others scale^(nu*(n-k)); the likelihood is unbounded if k>nu*(n-k), is coercive and attains a maximum if k<nu*(n-k), and equality needs further analysis; master no. |

## Numerical evidence from simbox

`density.R` independently evaluates the five own-density README formulas,
using R's log normal and t functions. On 100 fixed-seed normal points per
family at the simulation parameters, its greatest absolute discrepancy with
the installed density was 3.56e-15; see `density-check.csv`. This is a
limited check, not validation across the full parameter space.

`oracle.R` ran 1,020 subset/sample combinations: 20 sample types per
family, including n=20,100,1000 defaults, n=2,3,5, rounded ties, and draws
from normal, skew-normal, exponential, reflected exponential, half-normal,
reflected half-normal, half-t, reflected half-t, half-GED, reflected
half-GED, uniform, t2 and skew t2 limits. See `oracle-results.csv`.
Each interior search uses three BFGS starts; when the package returns a
fit, its coefficients are also used as a fourth start, with the objective
still evaluated from the independent formula. This makes the comparison a
useful check of the reported likelihood and local improvement, but it is
not an independent global search or an independent proof of agreement.
The sged Laplace boundary is approximated from above. On 200 returned fits,
all absolute differences were below `1e-6*n`; the largest normalized
difference was 1.55e-10. Error statuses and boundary cases are recorded in
the CSV, not certified by the interior-only search. The transformed search
has finite guards and can miss a divergent or finite face.

`tnorm-oracle.R` uses `extraDistr::dtnorm` and tested 135 subset/sample
combinations; all 27 returned master fits (the three implemented
parent-parameter hold subsets on nine samples) matched the optimizer within
`1e-6*n`, maximum absolute difference 6.26e-7. To avoid the documented
reference tail cancellation, the numerical search is bounded away from
extreme parent parameters. Free-bound numerical maxima are only near the
open endpoint, not attained. `tnorm-faces.R` checks a finite-bound uniform
face and samples from uniform and one-sided exponential limits; its results
are in `tnorm-faces.csv`. The reference optimizer has not independently
verified the tnorm face likelihoods.

Still missing: a certified or independently established global boundary
comparison for the unresolved holds, paths for *every* potentially
unbounded subset, complete R7 reference-domain verification, and the full
set of data-dependent classifications. The scripts and results should not be
read as establishing those claims.
