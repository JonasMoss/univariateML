## Almost-everywhere uniqueness

The generic comparison theorem proves equality of sample derivatives at global winners and takes a separate almost-everywhere separation premise to conclude equality of winning classes. Attainment is separate. On a fixed-count chart the derivative at a free level is its count times the spatial score; checking a repeated-level subspace requires applying the generic theorem to the weighted chart in its own Lebesgue measure. A null set in unrestricted sample coordinates can contain an entire repeated-level stratum. [Generic Lean theorem](../../lean/UnivariateML/Generic/UniqueAE.lean) · [Written incidence and contact argument](2026-11-27-uniq-locscale-math-report.md#analytic-incidence-bound)

- **Student.** The fixed-df, fixed-scale application proves exactly one finite location for Lebesgue-almost every ordinary sample with at least three observations, with denominator scale `sd * sqrt(nu - 2)` for `std` and `sigma * sqrt(df)` for `lst`. The proof uses three distinct coordinate values; a weighted adaptation on each three-or-more-level stratum is available mathematically via the same quadratic intersection bound. The location-scale module proves the standardized one- and two-level cases and the ordinary finite polynomial comparison in separate statements. The full atlas rows still need those parts joined across the parameter charts. With only standardized df held, the exact all-sample cardinality is already proved directly, independently of the generic theorem. Other Student holds have the relevant rational score inputs where stated, including two unequal nonzero absolute magnitudes at a common centre and four distinct observations on the moving-centre finite comparison. The weighted applications, face charts when required, and any exact counterexamples or general stationary lists remain separate.
- **Skew-t.** Finite-score identities give parameter identification in every reviewed mask. The indicated level thresholds are nine for all free, seven for `alpha`, `nu`, and `omega`, five for `alpha+nu`, and three, five, five and seven freely varying noncontacts for `alpha+nu+xi`, `alpha+xi`, `nu+xi`, and `xi`, respectively. These are the written dimension bounds, not conclusions of the Lean score identity. Lean still needs the analytic incidence projection, smooth dependence of spatial-score derivatives on the jointly moving parameters, and the weighted charts and restricted contact arguments. Finite versus collapse and skew-normal score separation and the strict half-Student height comparison supply inputs to those arguments. None of these skew-t a.e. claims is closed by importing the generic file alone.
- **Slash.** The two-parameter finite score identification is an input to the five-level incidence argument and the held-scale three-level argument. The collapse pole exclusion supplies the distinct finite/collapse score premise away from the atom. The weighted applications and the incidence-to-null-set proof remain; the exact reflected held-scale example and the all-free local competition are already covered. No joined slash a.e. uniqueness claim follows from the files alone.
