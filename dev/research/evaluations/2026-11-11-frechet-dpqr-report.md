# Fréchet d/p/q/r near the Gumbel limit — TASK-187

Fréchet and inverse Weibull now avoid shape-amplified cancellation in density and probability evaluation, and Fréchet sampling uses the centered quantile. On the hardest stored GEV-held2 seam case, the summed log-density error against an exact-input 100-digit reference fell from **1.04725e-5 to 1.83291e-15**. Every required validation run finished successfully. The requested strict uniform relative-error bound remains **open for canceled near-zero quantiles with a nonzero center**, with a concrete counterexample below.

## Formulation and error argument

When `sigma/2 <= -mu <= 2*sigma`, Sterbenz's lemma makes `b = mu + sigma` exact. For observations near this center, Fréchet evaluates the log ratio as `log1p((x-b)/sigma)`. If `x-b` is of order `sigma/lambda`, its absolute rounding error is of order `epsilon/lambda`; multiplication by shape therefore produces an error of order epsilon, rather than lambda times the error of subtracting separate logarithms. This is a shape-independent local stability argument, conditional on ordinary floating-point range and elementary-function accuracy.

Outside that centered regime, an error-free distance residual retains the information lost when forming `x-mu`. Near equal distance and scale, the residual enters the `log1p` numerator. Elsewhere a normal quotient is logged directly; logarithmic differences preserve range when the quotient overflows or underflows. Exact halving handles overflow of either the distance or the virtual subtraction used to recover its residual. Density and CDF share this log ratio; the CDF uses the existing log-hazard/complement helpers.

Fréchet quantiles use the exact center plus an `expm1` displacement, combined with `fma`. Split probability-center constants preserve tiny nonzero quantiles around `F = exp(-1)` on raw-probability and upper-log-probability inputs. An underflowed exponent is rescued before scaling. Outside the centered branch, fused affine evaluation and quarter-exponent products retain finite answers when an intermediate multiplier is outside range. Both samplers consume one uniform and call their quantile.

Inverse Weibull needed the analogous correction: its former reciprocal-scale logarithm plus `log(x)` suffers the same shape amplification. The baseline comparison found relative density errors around **5.0e6** at shape `1e15`. It now uses a centered log ratio when observation and scale are within a factor of two, with quotient/range handling elsewhere. This decision is supported by the independent measurements, not by agreement with an upstream implementation.

The family-specific arguments are recorded in the [Fréchet dossier](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/frechet/README.md#details) and [inverse Weibull dossier](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/invweibull/README.md#details).

## Open: strict relative accuracy at canceled near-zero quantiles

The unconditional few-epsilon relative-error target is not closed. This exact binary64 call is a reproducible normal-output counterexample:

```cpp
qfrechet(-7.3890561137087625, 1e9, -999999998., 1e9, true, true)
```

The computed result is `-1.2456318291555971e-16`; the 100-digit reference is `-1.9991476033510408592152e-18`. Absolute error is approximately `1.23e-16`, but relative error is **61.308**. The nonzero center is `2`; subtracting a displacement close to `-2` exposes errors already present in the log-hazard/division/exponential chain. Fused final addition cannot recover those lost bits. The special zero-center probability-rounding case is fixed and has tight regressions, but it does not settle this general cancellation.

Closing this case requires a compensated or higher-precision transformation through the entire canceled quantile chain and an accompanying error argument. A uniform relative contract also needs explicit treatment of exact zeros and subnormal outputs. The current implementation establishes the stated shape-independent local stability and the measured improvements above; it does not establish the stronger global relative bound. The exact probe and both baseline/current values remain in the comparison script and JSON, and the dossier explicitly states this limitation.
