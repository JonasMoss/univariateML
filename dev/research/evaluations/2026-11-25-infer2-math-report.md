# Global localization for nonregular likelihood with fitted nuisance

TASK-283 · 2026-11-25 · source revision d33239bd

**Proved.** The global GEV and known-threshold GPD MLEs on the fitted shape domains are consistent and attain the mixed rates for fixed \(-1\le \xi\le-1/2\). At \(-1<\xi<-1/2\) a moving endpoint has a Poisson LR, while shape has a Gaussian profile LR if a nuisance parameter can vary the endpoint at fixed shape. At \(\xi=-1/2\) both profile LRs are chi-square, even with different coefficient rates. This remains true in the bounded \((A-2)\log n\) layer, where \(A=-1/\xi\), with a transition-dependent Gaussian information. At \(\xi=-1\), a fit with only shape free has a different law from a fit with a free endpoint at fixed shape. The all-free triangular MLE has two critical endpoints and an ordinary mode at an interior mode. At a boundary mode the coincident endpoint and mode have a joint Poisson limit at rate \(n\), with an independent critical Gaussian opposite endpoint.

**Proved finite-sample alternative.** Invariant rank tests and order-statistic pivots give intervals with unknown shape or mode fraction across all these regimes. The intervals can be conservative, wide, or unbounded. The Python study evaluates actual interval coverage as well as simultaneous coverage.

## Scope and sources

Data are iid from the specified continuous family. Holds are independent of the sample. Use the dossier's closed density at the reverse-exponential contact and at a triangular boundary mode. GEV free shape ranges over \([-1,1]\); GPD free shape ranges over \([-1,\infty)\) with known threshold. All nonempty free-coordinate subsets on these domains are allowed in the argument. The LR nulls considered are a genuinely fixed endpoint or shape, and a fixed endpoint or mode for triangular. Log likelihoods \(\ell_n\) are sums. Rates multiply estimation error. "Global MLE" refers to the mathematical maximum. A production estimate can use the results when its total likelihood gap meets the condition below.

## Global consistency and attainment

**Proved.** In each case the likelihood above the true value minus \(O_p(1)\) lies inside a deterministic compact set with probability tending to one. At a fixed law this exclusion and consistency also hold almost surely for the exact MLE. The same exclusion applies to a restricted fit containing the truth.

* **Triangular.** The density is bounded above by \(2/(b-a)\). Any feasible width exceeds the sample range, which is bounded below by a positive constant eventually. A large width cannot match the true mean log likelihood, which converges to a finite value. A bounded width and support of the sample also bound \(a,b,c\). The contact convention gives upper semicontinuity at \(a=c\) and \(b=c\).
* **GEV.** The [dossier's uniform bound](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gev/README.md#sharp-density-bound-and-the-held-minimum-half-tie) gives \(f_{\mu,\sigma,\xi}(x)\le \min(\sigma^{-1},\sigma/(x-\mu)^2)\). In fact \(h_\xi(z)=y^{1+\xi}e^{-y}\le1\) for \(y=(1+\xi z)^{-1/\xi}\), with the exponential limit at zero. The identity \(z=(y^{-\xi}-1)/\xi\) and \(|\sinh(\xi v)/\xi|\le|\sinh(v)|\) give \(z^2h_\xi(z)\le(1-y)^2 e^{-y}\le1\). Pick \(d>0\) so a length-\(2d\) interval has true probability less than \(1/4\). A uniform empirical CDF bound then puts at least \(2/3\) of the observations at distance greater than \(d\) from every candidate \(\mu\), eventually. For \(\sigma<\min(1,d^2)\), the mean log likelihood is at most \(\frac13\log\sigma + \frac23|\log d^2|\). The bound \(1/\sigma\) excludes large scales. With scale bounded on both sides, use a fixed bounded interval with positive true mass. Its observations have uniformly vanishing candidate densities as \(|\mu|\to\infty\), and the other densities have a constant upper bound. This excludes large \(|\mu|\). At the negative endpoint the density tends to zero for \(\xi>-1\); at \(-1\) contact has the closed upper semicontinuous value. The positive-shape lower endpoint has density vanishing there.
* **GPD.** Put \(Y=X-\mu\). Every fitted density is nonincreasing on positive excesses, so \(f(y)\le1/y\). For \(\xi>0\), \(yf(y)=(t/\xi)(1+t)^{-1-1/\xi}\le1/\xi\) with \(t=\xi y/\sigma\). Thus \(\xi\to\infty\) costs a diverging log likelihood on any fixed positive-mass interval away from zero, while the empirical average of \(|\log Y|\) stays bounded. Large \(\sigma\) is excluded by \(f\le1/\sigma\). For \(-1\le\xi\le K\) with fixed \(K>0\), monotonicity gives \(yf(y)\le2P_{\sigma,\xi}(Y>y/2)\le2(1+Ky/(2\sigma))^{-1/K}\). The survival bound follows by coupling the quantiles \(\sigma\int_0^{-\log(1-p)}e^{\xi s}\,ds\). Consequently small \(\sigma\) costs a diverging likelihood on a fixed positive-mass interval with the same integrable upper envelope elsewhere. The closed uniform endpoint again gives upper semicontinuity.

The compact likelihood attains a maximum, since likelihood at the true parameter is positive almost surely. For an almost sure finite-sample attainment statement it suffices that triangular data are nonconstant, GPD excesses are strictly positive, and GEV data have at least three distinct observations and no ties. The same bounds give data-dependent compact sets then. In the GEV small-scale argument choose \(d\) below half the minimum spacing, which places at most one observation in any length-\(2d\) interval. For consistency on a compact set, at each false \(\theta\) the expected log likelihood is strictly smaller by identification and the log inequality. GPD and GEV are identified by their quantile functions, and the triangular support and mode identify its law. Upper semicontinuity lets the local suprema of the log density decrease to the log density. Their upper parts on the compact set are uniformly bounded. Truncate below at a large negative constant, shrink the neighborhood to get a strict expected gap, and apply the LLN to a finite cover of the closed set outside a neighborhood of truth. The truncated likelihood is an upper bound for the original. The true log likelihood has finite expectation and variance. This yields consistency, also for any fit with \(o_p(n)\) total gap. For a sequence of true extreme-value laws tending to a critical law, the compact exclusion holds with fixed constants, \(\ell_n(\theta_n)/n\) tends in probability to the limiting true mean, and the finite cover uses bounded upper truncations and convergence in total variation of the true laws. These follow from the quantile coupling, or from pointwise convergence of the densities and a uniform second moment of their own log densities.

There is no unrestricted low-shape MLE when a free endpoint can contact the sample maximum with \(A<1\). For example use \(x=(1/4,1/2,3/4)\) and take \(e=3/4+\delta\), \(\delta\downarrow0\). Take GPD \(\mu=0,\sigma=2e,\xi=-2\), or GEV \(b=1,\mu=e-1,\sigma=2,\xi=-2\). The maximum density factor diverges as \(\delta^{-1/2}\), with other factors finite and positive. The same sample witnesses GPD threshold collapse with both scale and shape free even at \(\xi=3\). Put \(\mu=1/4-\sigma^2\) and let \(\sigma\downarrow0\). The first factor is asymptotic to \(\sigma^{-1}\) and the other two are constant multiples of \(\sigma^{1/3}\). These paths are proved by direct substitution. With GEV shape held below \(-1\), a location hold can prevent contact on samples with \(M\le\mu\), an event of probability \(e^{-n}\) under the held-shape law. The dossier and prior report describe the exceptional finite-sample fit.

## Uniform control outside bounded local sets

**Proved.** Write \(H(\theta,\theta_0)=\|\sqrt{f_\theta}-\sqrt{f_{\theta_0}}\|_2\), and \(\rho(t)=t\sqrt{\log(1/t)}\) for small \(t>0\), with \(\rho(0)=0\). The following local lower bounds hold up to positive constants. \(\Delta\) denotes displacement from the true parameter, and \(u\) denotes coordinates along the exact fixed-endpoint surface for the extreme-value families.

| True regime | Lower bound for \(H\) |
|---|---|
| GEV or GPD, \(1\le A<2\) | \(|\Delta e|^{A/2}+\|\Delta u\|\) |
| GEV or GPD, \(A=2\) | \(\rho(|\Delta e|)+\|\Delta u\|\) |
| GEV or GPD, fixed \(A>2\) near 2 | \(|\Delta e|+\|\Delta u\|\) |
| Triangular, \(a<c<b\) | \(\rho(|\Delta a|)+\rho(|\Delta b|)+|\Delta c|\) |
| Triangular, \(a=c<b\) | \((|\Delta a|+|\Delta c|)^{1/2}+\rho(|\Delta b|)\) |

For the extreme-value families use coordinates \((e,A)\) for GPD and \((e,b,A)\) for GEV, where \(e=\mu-\sigma/\xi\), \(b=-\sigma/\xi\) for GEV, and \(w=e-\mu\) for GPD. In a chart with holds, \(u\) uses the free coordinates left after solving for one endpoint-moving coordinate. All such charts are smooth near the true negative shape because each original free coordinate has a nonzero endpoint derivative. Bounds in the ambient chart restrict to these charts.

Here are the lower-bound details. On fixed interior subintervals the derivative of the root density has a positive definite Gram matrix in the extreme-value charts. For GPD the log-density derivatives are spanned by \(1/(e-x)\), a constant, and \(\log(e-x)\), with nonzero independent endpoint and shape combinations. At \(A=1\) the endpoint derivative is a nonzero constant. For GEV the \(b\)-score is affine in \((e-x)^A\). The \(A\)-score has a nonzero logarithmic term with coefficient proportional to \(1-((e-x)/b)^A\). The \(e\)-score has an inverse-distance term for \(A>1\), or a nonzero constant at \(A=1\). Analytic continuation on the support, or the distinct asymptotics of these functions at zero, gives linear independence. For triangular with interior mode, the left and right inverse-distance terms identify the two endpoint directions, and the remaining mode score is nonzero. The interior Gram bound first gives Euclidean control. Freezing the other coordinates costs at most a constant times their displacements for extreme-value fixed-edge shape and scale, and for the triangular mode at fixed endpoints. Their root-density derivatives there have square-integrable envelopes.

For a single \(1\le A<2\) edge shift of magnitude \(d\), the interval between the supports carries squared root mass at least \(C d^A\). For \(A=2\), integrate the squared difference of the shifted \(\sqrt{t}\) terms over inward distances \(K d<t<t_0\), with fixed large \(K\) and small \(t_0\). The integrand is bounded below by a constant times \(d^2/t\). Smooth normalization and tail factors contribute a root error \(O(d)\). For triangular interior edges apply the same estimate on disjoint collars with the mode frozen. For simultaneous changes of the two triangular edges, first ignore the other edge's normalization change on each collar. Restoring it costs \(O(|\Delta a|+|\Delta b|)\) in root norm. The Euclidean control absorbs all these costs, yielding the table.

At a left boundary mode put \(t=\max(|a'-a|,c'-a')\). On a scaled left collar the root density, after dividing by its edge constant, is a shifted step or the square root of a ramp of width \((c'-a')/t\). The true root is a step. The normalized shift and ramp width live on a compact set with maximum absolute coordinate 1, and the two functions disagree on a set of positive length for every point of that set. The squared distance on a fixed collar containing all their breakpoints has a positive infimum by continuity in \(L^2\), including at zero ramp width. The right-endpoint displacement only perturbs the multiplier by \(o(1)\) there. Thus \(H\ge C\sqrt{t}\). On a right collar the single-edge critical variation costs \(\rho(|b'-b|)\) and the two left changes cost \(O(t)\) in root norm. This proves the last lower bound. Reflection covers a right boundary mode.

### Bracket widths and likelihood exclusion

Let \(\mathcal B_r\) be the densities in a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood with \(H\le r\). In all rows of the table the root densities have \(L^2(dx)\) bracketing entropy at width \(r\eta\) bounded by \(C\log(C/\eta)\) for \(0<\eta<1\). Here a bracket consists of pointwise lower and upper root densities, and the width is the norm of their difference.

For an extreme-value edge use a box of endpoint width \(d\) and tangent widths \(s\). On the collar within \(2d\) of the cell's edge, the squared envelope oscillation in the edge shift is \(O(d^{A_l})\), where \(A_l\) is the smallest allowable exponent in \(\mathcal B_r\). Off the collar it is bounded by the squared derivative times \(d^2\). Inward distances from \(2d\) to 1 use the root derivative bound \(C t^{(A_l-3)/2}\); above 1, GPD has bounded distances and GEV has a polynomial-times-exponentially-decaying envelope. At fixed edge, the tangent derivatives have an \(L^2\) envelope by the same formulas with \(t^{(A_l-1)/2}(1+|\log t|)\) near zero. Taking infima and suprema over the box therefore gives squared width at most
\[
 C\left(s^2 + d^{A_l} + d^2\int_d^1 t^{A_l-3}\,dt+d^2\right).
\]
For fixed \(A<2\), take \(s\) proportional to \(r\eta\) and \(d\) proportional to \(r^{2/A}\eta^k\), with a fixed sufficiently large \(k\). Since \(A_l\ge\max(1,A-Cr)\), the factor \(r^{-O(r)}\) is bounded. For \(A=2\), use \(d\) proportional to \(r\eta^k/\sqrt{\log(1/r)}\) and the bound \(\int_d^1t^{A_l-3}dt\le d^{-Cr}\log(1/d)\). The factor \(\log(1/\eta)\) and the loss \(\eta^{-kCr}\) are absorbed by taking \(k\) large and the neighborhood small. For \(A>2\) fixed the off-collar integral is bounded, so ordinary linear mesh suffices. In each case the lower Hellinger bounds put the coordinates into a box whose lengths divided by the respective mesh sizes are polynomial in \(1/\eta\). Smooth holds can be enforced by intersecting the ambient boxes with the held surface.

For triangular, write the density as \(2/(b-a)\) times the minimum of its capped left and right ramps, zero outside the two edges. The left ramp is the distribution function of \(a+(c-a)U\) for uniform \(U\), with a step at zero width. The right ramp is the reflected version. Pointwise minima preserve an oscillation bound by the sum of the individual oscillations. For interior ramps the square-root collar bound above applies with \(A_l=2\), and varying the mode at fixed endpoints costs \(O(s)\) in root norm since \(c-a\) and \(b-c\) stay bounded below. At the left wedge, varying \(a,c\) by at most \(d\) gives a bracket via componentwise monotonicity of the ramp distribution function. The integral of the difference between its bounding ramps is the difference of their means, at most \(2d\). Squaring root differences only reduces this bound. Use mesh proportional to \(r^2\eta^2\) for both left coordinates and \(r\eta^k/\sqrt{\log(1/r)}\) at the right edge. The smooth width multiplier has a squared width bound of order the squared coordinate mesh. This proves the entropy bound, including brackets that meet the zero-width ramp.

Apply the likelihood ratio inequality of Wong and Shen, Theorem 1, on each dyadic annulus \(r\le H\le2r\) within the fixed neighborhood. Its bracketing integral, from a constant multiple of \(r^2\) to a constant multiple of \(r\), is at most \(C r\) by the estimate above. Hence its condition, an upper bound by a sufficiently small constant times \(\sqrt{n}r^2\), holds for \(r\ge M/\sqrt n\) for sufficiently large fixed \(M\). On that annulus the probability of any \(\ell_n(\theta)-\ell_n(\theta_0)>-c n r^2\) is bounded by \(C e^{-c' n r^2}\). Summing on \(r=2^jM/\sqrt n\) gives a bound tending to zero as \(M\to\infty\), in addition to the \(o(1)\) error from global consistency. In particular every fit with total gap \(O_p(1)\) is tight in the mixed-rate coordinates, since the true point supplies an incumbent in both the full and null domains. This argument excludes distant global maxima, including candidates on other shape branches of the fitted domain. [Wong and Shen (1995)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176324524)

## Extreme values

**Proved.** Write \(f(e-d)\sim c_0 d^{A-1}\), with \(c_0=A/w^A\) for GPD and \(A/b^A\) for GEV. For local displacements denote the scaled endpoint shift by \(h\), and the root-\(n\) tangent displacement by \(v\). Write the Gaussian tangent likelihood as \(G(v)=v^\top Z-v^\top I v/2\), with \(Z\sim N(0,I)\). \(I\) is the Fisher information of scores on the exact held, fixed-endpoint surface. It is positive definite on the tangent coordinates. In GPD with both coefficients free the \(A\)-score at fixed \(e\) is \(1/A+\log((e-X)/w)\) with information \(1/A^2\).

### Subcritical \(1<A<2\)

At \(n^{1/A}\), let \(\{Y_j\}\) be ordered Poisson distances with intensity \(c_0 y^{A-1}\,dy\). Set
\[
 V=\int_0^\infty y^{-1}(d\Pi-c_0 y^{A-1}dy),\qquad
 L_A(h)=(A-1)\left[hV+\sum_j\{\log(1+h/Y_j)-h/Y_j\}\right]
\]
for \(h>-Y_1\), and \(L_A(h)=-\infty\) otherwise. Interpret the integral by first moment compensation at zero and mean-square compensation at infinity. The joint log likelihood converges locally to \(L_A(h)+G(v)\) with independent factors. The Poisson formula is the integrated score in the [held-shape GPD proof](2026-11-25-infer-math-report.md#extreme-values). In GEV the same singular terms are accompanied by the variation of \(-((e-X)/b)^A\). Its second endpoint derivative is bounded by a constant times \(d^{A-2}\) near zero and a polynomial in the exponentially damped tail. Away from the first pole the total second-order term is \(O_p(n^{1-2/A})\). The mean of its first-order term together with the normalization cancels the expectation of the singular score, since the endpoint density vanishes. This calculation applies to the GPD normalization as well.

For uniformity on bounded local sets separated from the pole, split the normalized sample point process at \(B\). The score-compensation tail variance and the mean absolute second-order log tail are bounded by \(C B^{A-2}\) uniformly on each such set. In moving the tangents by \(O(n^{-1/2})\), the change in the coefficient of the singular log sum is \(O(n^{-1/2})\); that sum is \(O_p(n^{1-1/A}+1)\) uniformly there by splitting off finitely many extremes and using \(E(1/d)<\infty\). Smooth mixed terms are \(O_p(n^{1/2-1/A})\). At a sequence approaching the pole from within support, the closest observation contributes a divergent negative log; removing that observation leaves the other terms bounded above on bounded local sets by the same split. Infeasible sequences have negative infinite log likelihood. Independence follows by cutting tangent scores away from zero distance, applying the Gaussian CLT jointly with the rare-point counts, then removing the score cut using its square-integrable envelope. On the fixed endpoint surface, the tangent expansion is uniform by the integrable derivatives of the log density in shape and scale. This also holds for held charts with their smooth substitutions.

The limiting score decreases strictly on \((-Y_1,\infty)\) and diverges positively at the pole. At large positive \(h\) its mean is \(-C h^{A-1}\) and its variance \(O(h^{A-2})\), by integrating the change in score against the intensity and substituting \(y=ht\). Monotonicity gives an eventually negative score almost surely, so \(L_A\) has a unique maximizer \(H_A\). The localization just proved makes compact argmax transfer valid. Thus the endpoint estimate has scaled limit \(H_A\), independent of \(I^{-1}Z\) for fitted tangents. The profile endpoint LR is \(2L_A(H_A)\), with \(L_A(0)=0\). For shape with another coefficient free, use \(A\) as one tangent coordinate and vary the other coefficient to obtain \(e\). The null deletes that Gaussian direction and leaves the Poisson fit unrestricted, so shape LR is \(\chi^2_1\). If shape is the sole free coefficient, the shape and endpoint nulls coincide and both LRs have the Poisson law. For GPD with fitted scale the shape variance at rate \(\sqrt n\) is \(\xi^2\) by transforming \(A\).

### Critical and the transition

For \(A=2\) the local likelihood is \(hZ_e-h^2 I_e/2+G(v)\) at endpoint rate \(\sqrt{n\log n}\), with \(I_e=c_0/2\), \(Z_e\sim N(0,I_e)\) independent of \(Z\). The [critical score argument](2026-11-25-infer-math-report.md#extreme-values) extends to both families since the singular log is \((A-1)\log(e-X)\) and the remaining endpoint terms have ordinary integrable variance and curvature near \(A=2\). Cross terms with tangent scores vanish on the mixed scale since the unnormalized cross moments are finite. The support margin is positive throughout a bounded local set with probability tending to one. Thus the global estimates have independent limits \(Z_e/I_e\) and \(I^{-1}Z\). Each free scalar endpoint or shape null has \(\chi^2_1\) profile LR by maximizing the quadratic on its exact held chart. With fixed \(A>2\) near 2, the same localization with linear Hellinger bounds, followed by the regular DQM expansion, gives ordinary root-\(n\) Gaussian fits and \(\chi^2_1\) for those scalar LRs. The bounded root-\(n\) neighborhoods lose support with probability \(O(n^{1-A/2})\), and the inverse-square-distance Hessian has finite mean there by truncation. [Bücher and Segers (2017)](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.05702)

**Proved bounded transition layer.** Suppose \((A_n-2)\log n\to\tau\in\mathbb R\) and the true remaining parameters tend to finite interior scale and location values, with scale bounded away from zero. Holds are imposed at the true values of each design. Keep \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) as the endpoint multiplier and use the limiting \(c_0\) at \(A=2\). The information becomes
\[
 I_e(\tau)=c_0\int_0^{1/2}e^{-\tau t}\,dt
 =c_0\,\frac{1-e^{-\tau/2}}{\tau},
 \qquad I_e(0)=c_0/2.
\]
For the global step, use the same shell proof for \(r\ge n^{-1/2}\). To bound the endpoint coordinate, when \(|\Delta e|\ge r/\sqrt{\log(1/r)}\), the lower-bound integral over \([K|\Delta e|,t_0]\) has \(d^{A_n-2}\) bounded above and below by constants. Thus a radius-\(r\) ball lies in the same critical box. In the upper bracket use \(A_l\ge 2-Cr-C'/\log n\). For the stated meshes the extra factor \(r^{-O(1/\log n)}\) is bounded and the loss in the power of \(\eta\) is absorbed by the fixed mesh exponent. Constants are uniform for bounded \((A_n-2)\log n\). The likelihood inequality and consistency therefore localize at the transition rate.

For the local expansion set \(d_n=(n\log n)^{-1/2}\). The probability of any observation within \(K d_n\) of the endpoint is \(O_K(1/\log n)\) for fixed \(K\). The truncated centered endpoint score divided by \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) has variance \(I_e(\tau)+o(1)\). Indeed its leading variance is \(c_0(A_n-1)^2\int_{d_n}^{t_0} x^{A_n-3}dx/\log n\), and \(t=-\log x/\log n\) gives the displayed integral. The removed score's centering has normalized size \(O(1/\log n)\). The truncated contribution to the variance from scores above any fixed fraction of \(1/d_n\) is \(O(1/\log n)\), which proves Lindeberg. The normalized singular Hessian uses the coefficient \(A_n-1\) in place of its square. Its variance is \(O(1/\log n)\) by the integral of \(x^{A_n-5}\) truncated at \(d_n\). On a bounded local set, cut below a sufficiently large multiple of \(d_n\); the smooth remainder and the Taylor remainder from the singular log are \(o_p(1)\), the latter bounded in mean by \(C n d_n^3\int_{d_n}^{t_0}x^{A_n-4}dx=O(1/\log n)\). The cross moments and tangent expansion are uniformly controlled as at \(A=2\). This proves a Gaussian limit throughout the bounded layer and gives the same scalar \(\chi^2_1\) LR conclusions. Endpoint information cannot in general be replaced by \(c_0/2\) for normalized estimator inference in this layer.

### Positive endpoint density at \(A=1\)

If \(e\) can vary at fixed shape, the limit at rate \(n\) is \(L_1(h)=-c_0h\) for \(h\ge-Y_1\), negative infinity otherwise, with \(Y_1\sim\operatorname{Exp}(c_0)\). The Gaussian tangent block is independent, and with shape free the block is restricted to \(v_A\ge0\). With shape held drop that coordinate. To transfer the maximization at the discontinuity, separate the smooth endpoint term and the term \((A'-1)\sum_i\log(1+h/(nd_i))\), \(d_i=e-X_i\), after expanding the tangent likelihood at fixed endpoint. Above the first contact the smooth derivative divided by \(n\) tends uniformly to \(-c_0\) on these local sets by the LLN and its locally integrable envelope. For bounded \(h\ge0\) the singular sum is at most \(C n^{-1}\sum 1/d_i=O_p(\log n)\). This last bound follows by truncating at a fixed small multiple of \(1/n\) with arbitrarily small failure probability and using the truncated first moment. For \(h<0\) the sum is nonpositive on the feasible side and \(O_p(\log n)\) on compact sets separated from the first contact. Because \(A'-1\ge0\) and is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) locally, the term vanishes off contact and gives the required upper bound at contact. Every finite limiting value there is approached from above the contact, including in the full or shape-null model. For the endpoint null \(h=0\), contact has probability zero. This proves the limiting suprema and the unique scaled endpoint estimate \(-Y_1\). Shape LR with free nuisance is the Gaussian one-inequality LR \((N_+)^2\) after projecting out the other tangents, for \(N\sim N(0,1)\). The endpoint LR is \(2c_0Y_1\), because the same tangent cone appears in both suprema.

If shape alone is free, \(e=\mu+\sigma A\) so the actual held chart imposes \(h\ge0\) and has no Gaussian tangent. Both endpoint and shape LR converge to zero. For GPD the null MLE is exactly \(\xi=-1\) by the prior uniform-density bound with known scale. For GEV the probability of this boundary fit tends to one. To see the stronger GEV statement, localization first puts the unrestricted maximum within \(A'-1\le C/n\) with arbitrarily high probability. Along that segment the log likelihood difference from \(A=1\) is \((A'-1)(-n\sigma c_0+o_p(n))\) uniformly for positive \(A'-1\). The tangent term at fixed endpoint has an \(O_p(\sqrt n)\) first derivative at truth and a locally integrable second derivative. The smooth endpoint drift has derivative \(-n c_0+o_p(n)\), and the remaining singular term has size at most \((A'-1)O_p(\log n)\). The sign excludes every interior point of the localized segment.

## Triangular

**Proved.** For \(a<c<b\) put \(w=b-a\). The global estimate has independent Gaussian limits at rates \((\sqrt{n\log n},\sqrt{n\log n},\sqrt n)\) in \((a,b,c)\), with information \(1/[w(c-a)]\), \(1/[w(b-c)]\), and \(1/[(c-a)(b-c)]\). Drop held coordinates, and use \(\chi^2_1\) for a free scalar endpoint or mode LR. The [prior experiment](2026-11-25-infer-math-report.md#triangular) gives the three blocks. For compact uniformity, the two endpoint Taylor expansions use disjoint collars and the critical truncated-score bounds. In the mode expansion freeze the branch at the true mode first. The observations whose branch changes under a displacement \(t=O(n^{-1/2})\) add a log-ratio correction of size \(O(|t|)\) on an interval of width \(|t|\). Its mean is \(-n I_c t^2+o(1)\), since the first-order log-ratio slopes at the switch sum to \(1/(c-a)+1/(b-c)\) and the density at the switch is \(2/w\). This subtracts from the frozen positive quadratic \(n I_c t^2/2\). The centered correction is \(o_p(1)\) uniformly. For example on a fixed mesh in the scaled interval the variance of each count of switching observations is \(O(\sqrt n)\), and monotonicity extends the normalized counts to uniform convergence by refining the mesh. The correction is an integral of those counts against a bounded local slope. Bounded mixed shifts in the endpoints change the slope by \(o(1)\). The critical endpoint scores have finite cross moments with each other and with the mode score. Their scaled cross terms vanish. This also explains why a branchwise observed Hessian evaluated between knots gives the wrong mode curvature.

For \(c=a\), write \(h=n(a'-a)\), \(k=n(c'-a)\), so \(h\le k\), and let \(\{y_j\}\) be ordered Poisson arrivals of intensity \(2/w\). The joint left likelihood from the [prior wedge experiment](2026-11-25-infer-math-report.md#triangular) in these coordinates is
\[
 W(h,k)=\frac{h+k}{w}+\sum_{y_j<k}\log\!\frac{y_j-h}{k-h},\qquad h\le y_1.
\]
Set it to negative infinity on support violation, including \(h=y_1<k\). The sum is zero on the step \(h=k\le y_1\), including the permitted closed contact. The right endpoint has an independent Gaussian block with information \(1/w^2\) at rate \(\sqrt{n\log n}\).

For the compact transfer, take the all-right density formula as a reference. Each observation to the left of \(k\) incurs the ramp log term above plus an \(o_p(1)\) factor from the ratio of the unscaled right distances. There are \(O_p(1)\) such observations on each bounded local set. The normalization of the right formula gives \((h+k)/w\) and its right shift gives the critical Gaussian expansion. The right block and the left Poisson process are independent in the limit by splitting the score and rare point counts at disjoint collars. Point convergence gives uniform left log terms off the poles. At \(h=y_1<k\) the first term of the ramp goes to negative infinity. At \(h=k=y_1\) each ramp factor is at most one, giving the upper bound, and the step reaches that bound (or can approach from \(h=k<y_1\)). The held lines \(h=0\), \(k=0\), and their intersection have recovery sequences by the same reasoning, because \(y_1>0\) almost surely.

The annulus likelihood bound implies a coercive upper comparison for \(W\), and hence attainment. In detail, carry the bound through the compact convergence and recovery on any finite union of local shells, then enlarge the finite union. On a compact shell with coordinate radius \(R=\sqrt{|h|+|k|}+\lvert z_b\rvert\), where \(z_b\) is the normalized right shift, the Hellinger lower bound gives \(\sqrt n H\ge C R\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Outside radius \(M\) the summable annulus bound thus controls the probability of any likelihood above zero by a quantity tending to zero as \(M\to\infty\). One can use rational recovery points and continuity off the support boundary, with a separate step recovery at contact. This suffices for finite suprema and their compact transfer, also in the restricted models.

The left maximizer is unique almost surely for all left hold masks. With \(k=0\) it is \((0,0)\): feasibility forces \(h\le0\), no point enters the sum, and \(h/w\) increases with \(h\). The actual fit sets \(a'=c\) at such a genuine hold, because there are almost surely no data on its left and increasing \(a'\) up to \(c\) improves the normalization for every feasible \(b'\). If \(k\) is free, the likelihood is convex in \(k\) between consecutive knots for fixed \(h\), with positive linear slope if the count in the sum is zero. Thus maxima lie on a knot \(k=y_j\) or the step. With both left coordinates free the best step and first knot have \((h,k)=(y_1,y_1)\). At knot \(j>1\), the objective is strictly concave as a function of \(h<y_1\), since each second derivative is \(-1/(y_i-h)^2+1/(y_j-h)^2<0\). It tends to negative infinity at either end and gives one root. Conditional on earlier arrivals the resulting height is a nonconstant analytic function of \(y_j>y_{j-1}\). At any fixed feasible \(h\) it tends to infinity as that one knot is moved to infinity while keeping its index and earlier points fixed. The strictly concave root is analytic by the implicit function theorem. Hence ties with an earlier knot have probability zero. On \(h=0\) use the same knot and nonconstant-height argument without a root. The true step is strictly improved there by the first positive arrival. The Gaussian right estimate is unique as well.

The scaled left estimates converge to this maximizer. The LR for the coincident endpoint or mode is twice the difference of wedge suprema on the actual held domains, adding the null line \(h=0\) or \(k=0\) respectively. The opposite endpoint's Gaussian block cancels in those LRs, and its own LR is \(\chi^2_1\) when it is free. When both left coordinates are free and the mode is tested, the null wedge supremum is zero and the step gives at least \(2y_1/w>0\) in the full wedge. Thus the mode LR has no half-chi-square atom. When the coincident endpoint is known, \(h=0\) reduces to the prior one-coordinate Poisson mode law. Reflect the entire argument at \(c=b\).

### Maximization and numerical error

For the preceding transfers the compact local upper bound on any convergent feasible parameter sequence and recovery of each finite limiting value give convergence of compact suprema. For continuous quadratics, the compact uniform expansions give both properties directly. For Poisson objectives the pole/contact arguments give them with the stated support convention. The likelihood annulus bound and compact consistency let the local compact sets grow after the limits in \(n\), both for the unrestricted fit and for every listed null. Uniqueness of the limiting argmax yields convergence of any exact maximizer and of any fit within \(o_p(1)\) total log likelihood. It allows finite-sample ties. For example the [triangular dossier](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/triang/README.md) exhibits two maximizers on the exact sample \((0,1)\).

A mathematical gap guarantee is possible without making an assumption about score convergence. On the data-dependent compact set, use a cover of mesh tending to zero and bound a cell's likelihood by the product of the supremum of each observation density on that cell, respecting the held surface. The largest cell bound decreases to the global maximum on nested covers by upper semicontinuity and compactness. A feasible dense sequence gives matching lower bounds in these models. Where the fitted density is discontinuous at contact, include the face itself or approach from within support. At the extreme-value uniform or reverse-exponential contact one can open the support slightly, with the exponent changing by \(O(d)\) over displacement \(d\) if shape must move. Then \(d\log d\to0\). At a triangular step contact move the coincident endpoint and mode together just outside the sample, or include the step face. When either coordinate of that step is genuinely held or the null fixes the endpoint, equality of an observation to the fixed value is a null event. The same construction works for the null cover. This is an exact-arithmetic termination argument for any positive total gap target, given evaluable cell upper bounds and an enclosing compact set. It gives no complexity claim. Choosing a vanishing target suffices mathematically. A production gap bound requires actual computed enclosures, including rounding allowances at the stated precision.

Pointwise convergent upper bounds for these covers can use the density formulas directly. For triangular, take the minimum of the upper bounds for the two monotone ramps and multiply by the upper width factor. For a closed extreme-value contact, bound a power of an inward distance on \([0,d_+]\), using exponent zero for its upper bound if the exponent range includes zero. A strictly positive lower exponent then makes the bound vanish as \(d_+\downarrow0\). At the positive-shape GEV lower support, use \(y^2e^{-y}\) for sufficiently large \(y=(1+\xi z)^{-1/\xi}\). Around shape zero at fixed finite standardized observations use the continuous extension of \(\log(1+\xi z)/\xi\). Away from support contacts and the shape-zero seam, ordinary continuous box bounds suffice. These bounds are for the mathematical cover. Their floating-point evaluation and cell costs are specific to an implementation.

## Finite-sample intervals

**Proved.** The following procedure eliminates location and scale from a shape or mode-fraction test by invariance. It then uses exact extreme pivots to project intervals for the physical endpoints. It has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) jointly, including at \(\xi=-1\), \(\xi=-1/2\), and either triangular boundary. It remains valid along the transition sequences without a preliminary shape classification. Values \(\mu\) held for GPD enter as a known threshold. For other holds one may intersect the joint candidate set with the genuine hold surface before projecting, or use the same conservative outer intervals in the held submodel.

Choose distinct interior order indices \(i<j<k\), for example \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor,\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lfloor 3n/4\rfloor\) with \(n\ge8\). For GPD use \((X_{(k)}-\mu)/(X_{(i)}-\mu)\). For GEV and triangular use the right spacing over the left spacing, \((X_{(k)}-X_{(j)})/(X_{(j)}-X_{(i)})\), with negative sign for triangular. Require positive denominator. In a correctly specified continuous sample this holds almost surely. For each candidate shape generate independent replicated uniform order statistics and apply the standardized quantile function of that candidate. Use one common set of replicates across candidates. Test by the two inclusive tails of the observed invariant's rank in this reference sample and invert. With \(B\) reference draws, rejecting at most \(\lfloor\alpha_{\rm sh}(B+1)/2\rfloor\) positions at each end has rejection probability at most \(\alpha_{\rm sh}\) by exchangeability at the true candidate. The coverage guarantee averages over the independent reference draws. Inclusive ties retain coverage. An exact analytic reference law could replace the draws.

The reference quantile bounds are nondecreasing functions of the tested candidate for each coupling. For GPD the standardized quantile is \(\int_0^s e^{\xi t}dt\) with \(s=-\log(1-p)\). Its higher-to-lower ratio is one plus the integral on the upper subinterval divided by the lower integral. Increasing \(\xi\) increases that ratio because \(e^{\Delta \xi t}\) is increasing. For GEV use the same integral representation with \(s=-\log(-\log p)\); the two spacings integrate \(e^{\xi t}\) on adjacent ordered intervals. For triangular write the mode fraction \(p=(c-a)/w\) and quantile at probability \(u\) as \(Q_p(u)=\sqrt{pu}\) below \(p\) and \(1-\sqrt{(1-p)(1-u)}\) above. For \(p_2>p_1\), the ratio \(Q_{p_2}'/Q_{p_1}'\) is constant below \(p_1\), proportional to \(\sqrt{(1-u)/u}\) between \(p_1,p_2\), and constant above \(p_2\), with continuous joins. It is nonincreasing, including for \(p_1=0\) or \(p_2=1\) by continuity. Thus the positive spacing ratio decreases, and its negative increases. Monotonicity permits certified outward grid inversion. Enlarge the lower bound to the last grid point whose upper reference cutoff is strictly below the observation, and reduce the upper bound to the first whose lower cutoff is strictly above it. When no such grid point exists use the domain bound. The interval includes every exactly accepted candidate, including off-grid values and untested tails. Use conservative comparison allowances in floating point. Shape domains are \([-1,\infty)\), \([-1,1]\), and \([0,1]\), respectively. A narrower prespecified true-shape domain may be imposed independently of the fit.

Use the remaining error budget \(\alpha_{\rm ext}=\alpha-\alpha_{\rm sh}\) for the extreme pivots. The union bound needs no independence between the statistics. The GPD and GEV pivots below are the held-shape pivots from the [prior inference report](2026-11-25-infer-math-report.md#extreme-values), here inverted jointly with the shape acceptance set.

- **GPD.** For each negative candidate \(\xi\), the CDF of \(V=(M-\mu)/(e-\mu)\) is \([1-(1-v)^A]^n\). Invert its equal-tailed \(1-\alpha_{\rm ext}\) band and project over the shape interval. The endpoint bounds increase as \(\xi\) increases toward zero, since \(v=1-(1-u)^{-\xi}\) decreases at fixed probability \(u\) on the uniform-max scale. Evaluate the lower endpoint bound at the most negative accepted shape bound and the upper at the least negative, with the limit \(+\infty\) at zero.
- **GEV.** With \(D=M-X_{(n-1)}>0\), \(T=(e-M)/D\) has CDF \(n/[n+(n-1)((1+1/t)^A-1)]\). Its \(p\)-quantile is \(1/\operatorname{expm1}[-\xi\log(1+n(1-p)/((n-1)p))]\). Invert the equal-tailed \(1-\alpha_{\rm ext}\) band via \(e=M+DT\). Both bounds likewise increase as \(\xi\) increases toward zero. A finite endpoint is defined on the negative-shape domain. If the shape interval reaches nonnegative values, an unbounded upper projection or an included extended endpoint \(+\infty\) must be reported as such. An interval entirely above zero has no finite endpoint candidates.
- **Triangular.** Use uniform extreme bands \(u_L\in[\ell_L,r_L]\) and \(u_U\in[\ell_U,r_U]\) at marginal error \(\alpha_{\rm ext}/2\) each, for the minimum and maximum of \(n\) uniforms. Their CDFs are \(1-(1-u)^n\) and \(u^n\). For each accepted \(p\) and pair \(u_L<u_U\) in the bands, put \(q_L=Q_p(u_L),q_U=Q_p(u_U)\), \(l=X_{(1)},m=X_{(n)}\), and map to
  \[
  w=\frac{m-l}{q_U-q_L},\quad a=l-wq_L,\quad b=m+w(1-q_U),\quad c=l+w(p-q_L).
  \]
  This candidate set includes the true triple on joint acceptance. One inexpensive outer projection uses intervals for \(q_L,q_U\) from monotonicity of \(Q_p(u)\) in both arguments, bounds \(w\) by interval division, and propagates these intervals through the three formulas. If the lower denominator bound is nonpositive use \(+\infty\) as the width upper bound. Any positive unknown width and the genuine holds may be enforced before a sharper projection. With both endpoints held, the prior exact binomial mode inversion is generally simpler.

**Calibrated LR option, proved.** One may instead invert a full-null Monte Carlo test using the profile LR at the candidate estimand. Generate from each full null, retaining the genuine holds during both fits. Accept a target if any compatible full-null candidate is accepted. The exchangeable inclusive upper-tail rank provides finite-sample coverage before projection. A computed upper bound on this nuisance supremum is conservative. A finite grid evaluated without error bounds on the unsampled candidates does not establish coverage. For bracketed LR computations, an observed LR lower bound and replicate LR upper bounds give a conservative rank by counting every possibly exceeding replicate. If profile calibration uses the asymptotic law instead, it is pointwise at continuity cutoffs (and for the proved bounded critical layer). In particular use the appropriate Poisson supremum or Gaussian quadratic for the actual null. At a concentration atom use finite-null calibration or a separately justified conservative rule. The finite interval simulations below use the invariant procedure, which can be evaluated without global constrained fits.

## What this changes for the fitter

- The mathematical full and restricted fits localize for the stated iid domains, including the actual holds. Existence still follows the sample conditions. In particular retain the GPD threshold-tie and free-threshold collapse rules, the GEV shape bound, and triangular width/support checks. A reverse-exponential, uniform, or triangular step maximum is an attained fit with an active constraint when the constraint was estimated.
- Record the exact endpoint chart, independent holds and active bounds. If shape alone is free at the \(-1\) boundary, the endpoint displacement is one-sided and there is no Gaussian tangent. If a nuisance remains free at fixed shape, the endpoint LR has the exponential reference and the shape LR has the Gaussian cone reference. For triangular boundary-mode inference profile both coincident coordinates when both are free. Avoid inferring exact holds from coefficients or from the reported restriction.
- Expose a total log-likelihood gap bound for each of the unrestricted and null fits if their LR is offered. If incumbents are \(L_1,L_0\) and proved upper bounds are \(U_1,U_0\), the LR is bracketed by \(\max(0,2(L_1-U_0))\) and \(2(U_1-L_0)\). Compare feasible candidates and support contacts in the actual held chart. The asymptotic transfer needs \(U_j-L_j=o_p(1)\) for both fits, in summed log-likelihood units. The [GEV](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gev/README.md), [GPD](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gpd/README.md), and [triangular](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/triang/README.md) dossiers describe tolerances for coefficient enclosures, pruning and candidate comparisons. A fixed positive margin on the average log likelihood alone can allow a gap growing as \(n\). A production certificate needs the accumulated numerical allowance for the requested data size.
- If the fit search succeeds and a certificate is unresolved on ordinary data, retain its estimate with the uncertified status under the existing fallback rule. An LR that needs the unavailable gap can be refused independently. The invariant intervals use data and genuine design holds and can bypass the optimizer when their sample conditions hold. Return explicit unbounded endpoints and preserve set topology when inverting a projected LR. Neither ordinary endpoint Wald nor endpoint percentile is a substitute for this dispatch.
