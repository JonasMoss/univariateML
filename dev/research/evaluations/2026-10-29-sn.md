# Azzalini skew-normal: fitting, singularity and infinite estimates

2026-10-29. Direct coefficients are location $\xi$, scale $\omega>0$,
and shape $\alpha$. This is not the Fernández–Steel `snorm` family.

## Proved here

Writing $\eta=1/\omega$, $h=\xi/\omega$ and $z_i=\eta x_i-h$ gives

$$\ell=n\log\eta+n\log2-\frac n2\log(2\pi)
 -\frac12\sum_i z_i^2+\sum_i\log\Phi(\alpha z_i).$$

For fixed finite shape this is strictly concave on $\eta>0$:
$n\log\eta$ is strictly concave in $\eta$, the Gaussian term is strictly
concave in $h$, and $\log\Phi$ is concave. The quadratic form of the
negative Hessian is bounded below by
$n(d\eta)^2/\eta^2+\sum_i(x_i d\eta-dh)^2>0$ for nonzero directions.
On nonconstant data the objective goes to minus infinity at the relevant
boundaries, so its unique stationary point is its maximum. Holding location
imposes the affine constraint $h=\xi\eta$; holding scale fixes $\eta$.
These constraints preserve concavity and give the exact inner routes for
all fixed subsets. Numerically these routes solve the score, rather than
using a separate unconstrained location/log-scale optimizer.

There is a stronger exact route when location is fixed and shape is free.
Put $d_i=x_i-\xi$ and $\beta=\alpha\eta$. The likelihood separates:

$$\ell=n\log\eta-\frac{\eta^2}2\sum_i d_i^2
 +\sum_i\log\Phi(\beta d_i)+n\log2-\frac n2\log(2\pi).$$

Thus $\widehat\omega^2=n^{-1}\sum_i d_i^2$ when scale is free,
and $\beta$ solves the strictly decreasing score
$\sum_i d_i\phi(\beta d_i)/\Phi(\beta d_i)=0$ by bracketed
bisection. With observations on both sides this root is unique; with only
one nonzero sign the corresponding infinite-shape face wins. This covers
both fixed-xi subsets without a shape grid.

At shape zero, the unrestricted normal fit has $\sum_i z_i=0$, hence
$\ell_\alpha=\sqrt{2/\pi}\sum_i z_i=0$. The envelope theorem therefore
makes zero stationary in the profiled likelihood for every nonconstant sample.
For one observation at symmetry,
$s_\alpha=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,z=\omega\sqrt{2/\pi}\,s_\xi$,
which proves singular direct-parameter Fisher information. A shape score
of zero at symmetry is not a global optimality condition.

For $\alpha\to+\infty$ the density tends to a right half-normal on
$x>\xi$; for the opposite sign it tends to a left half-normal.
With free location the right face has $\xi=\min x_i$ and
$\omega^2=n^{-1}\sum_i(x_i-\xi)^2$, and the left face uses the maximum.
For a fixed scale, retain that scale and optimize the endpoint in the same way.
The full endpoint likelihood is a supremum along a sequence approaching
from outside the sample while $|\alpha|$ increases faster than the inverse
location gap. This avoids the density-at-endpoint discontinuity.
With fixed location a face is feasible exactly when all observations are
on its allowed side. Each observation exactly at that location contributes
$\log\Phi(0)=-\log2$, even in the limit, so subtract that penalty from the
half-normal face likelihood. If both location and scale are fixed and all
observations equal location, shape is unidentified and zero is returned.
Constant data with a free scale and a free location (or location fixed at
the observations) have an unbounded scale-collapse likelihood and are rejected.
With fixed location away from the observations, or a fixed positive scale,
the corresponding concave constrained routes remain available.
With location strictly inside the sample range, both signs of standardized
observations occur and the log likelihood tends to minus infinity at either
infinite shape. With location on or outside the range, every nonzero
shape-score summand has one sign, so free shape escapes in that direction.
Fixed finite shape precludes shape escape. Thus every fixed subset either
uses the concave inner problem alone or the same one-dimensional shape
profile with only its feasible, constrained half-normal faces.

Let $b=\sqrt{2/\pi}$ and $\delta=\alpha/\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}$.
The centred parameters are $\mu=\xi+b\omega\delta$,
$\sigma^2=\omega^2(1-b^2\delta^2)$ and
$\gamma_1=(4-\pi)(b\delta)^3/[2(1-b^2\delta^2)^{3/2}]$.
At symmetry the centred density derivative with respect to $\gamma_1$
is $\phi(y)H_3(y)/6$, with $H_3(y)=y^3-3y$; this follows by
expanding the mean/variance-standardised density in $\delta$ through order
three and using $\gamma_1=O(\delta^3)$.
Consequently the centred scores are $y/\sigma$,
$(y^2-1)/\sigma$, and $H_3(y)/6$, with Fisher information
$\operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma^2,2/\sigma^2,1/6)$ at zero skewness.
Thus mean/SD/skewness coordinates remove the first-order information
singularity. The transformation is not a smooth invertible coordinate
change at symmetry: skewness is cubic in shape. This first-order information statement does not assert regularity of all
higher derivatives at zero skewness. Reparameterisation does
not create a finite direct shape estimate when the likelihood escapes.

## Numerical implementation and assumptions

The fitter standardises the data, solves the concave inner score by damped
Newton, and searches both signs of shape. It uses a central delta grid
(spacing 0.02) and logarithmic alpha wings through $2^{32}$, then refines
every sampled local maximum in `asinh(alpha)` coordinates. Both analytic
faces are compared; a face wins within $10^{-8}n$. This finite grid search
is numerical evidence, not a proof that an arbitrarily narrow unsampled
maximum cannot exist. There is no claimed exhaustive root-isolation theorem.

The density uses a continued fraction for thin normal log tails. The CDF
uses Owen's T in ordinary regions and a scaled integral of the density in
cancellation regions. Quadrature uses 80 panels of width 0.5 and 8-point
Gauss–Legendre rules, after scaling by the endpoint log-density slope.
Its finite truncation and accuracy are checked numerically, not certified
by a uniform error bound. Quantiles bisect an expanding bracket on the
requested log tail; random generation uses two independent R normals.

## Literature and scope

[Azzalini (1985), *A class of distributions which includes the normal ones*,
Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 12, 171–178](https://www.azzalini.it/SN/abstracts.html)
introduced the family and centred parameterisation. The author's publication
list verifies this bibliographic identity; the 1985 full text was not retrieved.

[Azzalini and Capitanio (1999), *Statistical applications of the multivariate
skew-normal distribution*, JRSS B 61, 579–602](https://www.azzalini.it/SN/paper98e.pdf),
sections 6.1–6.2 of the expanded author version, explicitly discuss the
stationary inflection at symmetry, singular information and centred parameters.
The centred-coordinate calculation above independently explains the remedy.

[Pewsey (2000), *Problems of inference for Azzalini's skew-normal distribution*,
Journal of Applied Statistics 27, 859–870](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664760050120542)
studies inference using centred parameters and tests against the folded-normal
limit. Its identity is corroborated by the
[author's publication list](https://sites.google.com/site/arthurpewsey/research);
the publicly indexed abstract was read, not the complete publisher article.

[Sartori (2006), *Bias prevention of maximum likelihood estimates for scalar
skew normal and skew t distributions*, JSPI 136, 4259–4275](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jspi.2005.08.043)
uses a modified shape score and establishes finite solutions in the scalar
shape-only setting. With nuisance location and scale, a two-step construction
is used. The publisher abstract and the later authors' detailed account below
were consulted; this note does not claim to re-prove Sartori's theorem.

[Azzalini and Arellano-Valle (2013), *Maximum penalized likelihood estimation
for skew-normal and skew-t distributions*, JSPI 143, 419–433](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jspi.2012.06.022)
propose maximizing $\ell-Q$ to prevent diverging shape. Their
[author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.2376) gives the univariate penalty
$Q(\alpha)=c_1\log(1+c_2\alpha^2)$, with positive constants;
one proposed choice is approximately $(c_1,c_2)=(0.875913,0.856250)$.
It diverges at infinite shape while remaining independent of sample size,
so it prevents the escape with a small asymptotic likelihood perturbation.
This is an alternative estimator, not ordinary ML, and is not implemented here.
The retrieved arXiv PDF is a later author revision (dated 2024), not the
publisher's frozen 2013 typesetting.

## Literature comparison: explained by a bimodal profile (reviewer, 2026-10-29)

An independent R implementation (`2026-10-29-sn-literature-check.R`: profile
by Nelder–Mead at fixed shape on a grid to $|\alpha| = 10^5$, exact half-normal
suprema, seed 7, 100 samples per cell) classifies 0.31 ($\alpha = 5$) and
0.64 ($\alpha = 10$) of the $n = 50$ samples as having an infinite shape
estimate, in line with the table above. On the same samples, a local
optimiser over $(\xi,\log\omega,\alpha)$ from a moment-type start, counted as
divergent when $|\hat\alpha| > 100$ (the published criterion), gives 0.09
and 0.39, close to the published 0.139 and 0.345.

The reason is the shape of the profile. For many samples whose supremum is
the half-normal face, the profile has a finite local maximum at moderate
shape, then a dip, then climbs back to the face from below at rate about
$1/\alpha$. Sample 3 of the $\alpha = 5$ stream (confirmed with `mlsn(x, alpha = a)`):

| $\alpha$ | 5 | 30 | 100 | $10^3$ | $10^4$ | $10^6$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| profile minus face | −0.077 | −0.839 | −0.579 | −0.109 | −0.014 | −0.0002 |

A local optimiser stops at the finite local maximum, and even a global search
capped at $|\alpha|\le 1000$ prefers it, since the profile at $\alpha = 1000$ is
still below it. Divergence criteria of the form $|\hat\alpha| > c$ therefore
undercount infinite estimates. (This is the reviewer's explanation, checked on
these samples; the authors' exact code was not available.)

## Original note on the comparison

Table 1 in the Azzalini–Arellano-Valle author version reports infinite-estimate
frequencies 0.139 at shape 5, sample size 50, and 0.345 at shape 10, size 50,
using magnitude above 100 as a numerical divergence criterion. The present
analytic-face study gives 0.34 and 0.65, respectively. The discrepancy is
larger than ordinary Monte Carlo variation would suggest. The independent
R-RNG check and SciPy multistart check support the present implementation,
but do not explain the difference. Different numerical fitting/classification
procedures are a possible explanation, not an established attribution.
Reproducing the authors' exact simulation and optimization code remains open;
these counts must not be presented as a replication of that table.

C++17 with optimization level O2. Every long build, test and computation
ran under the shared CPU-slot wrapper with one thread and nice priority.

The two rounding regressions are recreated from seed 20361529, samples 13
and 88 (zero-based), size 500 and shape 10. Their final regular direct scores
are each below 1e-3 in absolute value. The status correction accepts a last
Newton gain below 64 machine epsilons times (1 + absolute log likelihood)
when the scaled coordinate step is below 1e-7; it does not alter the
likelihood objective, iterates, or profile comparison.

Unrestricted bootstrap resamples can correctly stop at an infinite estimate;
this is the stated D1 behavior, not a sampler or routing failure. Bootstrap
with fixed shape is explicitly tested. Global-search completeness and a
uniform quadrature error bound remain assumptions rather than proved claims.
