# Inference with moving support, singular information and fitted boundaries

TASK-265 · 2026-11-25 · source revision 7ed10c94

Exact endpoint intervals are available for uniform, Pareto and power, and for negative-shape GPD and GEV with shape genuinely held. Triangular mode has an exact binomial inversion when both endpoints are held, including at a boundary mode. These procedures can be implemented without a bootstrap. The current percentile method has **zero coverage** for uniform, Pareto and power endpoints. A critical endpoint can have a chi-square LR at a non-root-n rate, while a boundary mode can have a Poisson-process LR.

## Scope and evidence

The results assume correctly specified iid, uncensored, untruncated observations. Held means known independently of the sample. Likelihoods are sums. Rates multiply estimation error. **Proved** identifies an exact argument here or a linked prior proof. A proved **local experiment** specifies a likelihood limit on bounded local parameter sets. An MLE or LR limit from it also needs localization of the unrestricted and relevant restricted optima, uniqueness for a point-estimator limit, and an optimization error of \(o_p(1)\) in total log likelihood. **Numerical** describes the stated grids and procedures. **Conjectured** identifies transfers still needing one of those arguments.

## Exact endpoints

Write \(l=\min X_i,\ u=\max X_i,\ R=u-l,\ \varepsilon=1-\mathrm{level}\).

**Uniform, proved.** The MLE is \((l,u)\). Integrating the joint extreme density \(n(n-1)(y-x)^{n-2}/(b-a)^n\) at fixed range gives \(V=R/(b-a)\sim\operatorname{Beta}(n-1,2)\) for \(n\ge2\). Given \(V=v\), \((l-a)/(b-a)\) is uniform on \((0,1-v)\). Hence
\[
 P((l-a)/R>t)=\int_0^{1/(1+t)}n(n-1)v^{n-2}(1-(1+t)v)\,dv=(1+t)^{1-n}.
\]
Reflect for the upper gap. The lower one-sided interval is \([l-R(\varepsilon^{-1/(n-1)}-1),l]\). For simultaneous inference require \(a\le l,\ b\ge u,\ b-a\le R/q_\varepsilon\), with \(q_\varepsilon\) the lower beta quantile. Coverage is exactly \(1-\varepsilon\). The set also inverts the joint LR, which on its support is \(2n\log((b-a)/R)\). With \(a\) known, \((u-a)/(b-a)\) has CDF \(v^n\), giving \([u,a+(u-a)\varepsilon^{-1/n}]\). Reflect for known \(b\). The joint limit of \(n(l-a,b-u)/(b-a)\) is independent unit exponentials, from the joint survival \((1-(s+t)/n)^n\).

**Pareto and power, proved.** Put \(Y=\log X,\ \theta=\log b,\ r=a\) for Pareto lower endpoint \(b\) and shape \(a\). For power put \(Y=-\log X,\ \theta=-\log\alpha,\ r=\beta\). Then \(Y-\theta\sim\operatorname{Exp}(r)\). With \(D=Y_{(1)}-\theta,\ S=\sum(Y_i-Y_{(1)})\), the minimum has rate \(nr\). Conditional on its index and value, the other gaps are independent exponentials of rate \(r\) by memorylessness. Thus \(D,S\) are independent, \(2rS\sim\chi^2_{2(n-1)}\), and
\[
 T=(n-1)nD/S\sim F_{2,2(n-1)},\qquad P(T>t)=(1+t/(n-1))^{-(n-1)}.
\]
The survival follows by averaging the exponential survival over the gamma \(S\). With quantiles \(q_L,q_U\), invert to \([Y_{(1)}-Sq_U/(n(n-1)),Y_{(1)}-Sq_L/(n(n-1))]\), then exponentiate for Pareto or exponentiate the negatives and reverse for power. The one-sided version uses \(q_L=0\). The endpoint profile LR on its support is \(2n\log(1+nD/S)\), by profiling \(r=n/(S+nD)\), with exact law \(2n\operatorname{Exp}(1)/(n-1)\). With shape held, \(nrD\) is the pivot. With endpoint held, replace \(S\) by \(\sum(Y_i-\theta)\) and \(n-1\) by \(n\) in the shape pivot. The MLE \((Y_{(1)},n/S)\) gives an exponential limit at rate \(n\) for log endpoint, independently of the Gaussian limit with variance \(r^2\) at rate \(\sqrt n\) for shape by the gamma CLT. On the original endpoint scale multiply by \(b\) or by \(-\alpha\), respectively. Free shape requires \(n\ge2,\ S>0\), and held shape permits \(n=1\).

**Percentile failure, proved.** Fitted-support parametric draws and empirical draws have maxima at most \(u\) and minima at least \(l\) in these three models. Refitted endpoints retain those inequalities. Consequently either percentile endpoint interval misses its true endpoint almost surely. An exact sample witnessing both failures is \((1/4,3/4)\) in uniform \([0,1]\). Every refitted endpoint is inside \([1/4,3/4]\) regardless of replicate count or quantile interpolation.

## Triangular

**Interior mode.** For \(w=b-a,\ a<c<b\), the edge constants are \(2/[w(c-a)]\) and \(2/[w(b-c)]\). The critical endpoint experiment gives independent Gaussian endpoint blocks at \(\sqrt{n\log n}\), with information half those constants. The exact pilot gives regular mode information \(1/[(c-a)(b-c)]\) at \(\sqrt n\). The cross terms vanish because its bounded score has finite cross moments with critical edge scores. With one endpoint held drop its row. These local experiments are proved. Transfer to the all-free MLE is conjectured pending global mixed-rate localization and a local optimization-error bound. The known-endpoints interior mode limit is proved in the exact pilot. Its ordinary pointwise Hessian misses the mode kink.

**Exact mode inversion with endpoints held, proved.** The area to the left of the mode gives \(P_c(X\le c)=(c-a)/w\), also at \(a,b\). Accept a candidate \(c\) if both inclusive tails at \(K_c=\#\{X_i\le c\}\) under \(\operatorname{Bin}(n,(c-a)/w)\) exceed \(\varepsilon/2\). An inclusive discrete tail is super-uniform, by ordering outcomes by the tail value, and the union bound gives coverage at least \(1-\varepsilon\). Invert on each observation interval by monotonicity in the candidate success probability. Preserve all accepted components. The pilot already supplies a disconnected triangular LR example.

**Boundary mode with endpoints held, proved.** Reflect if the true mode is \(b\). Normalize endpoints to 0 and 1 and take \(t=nc\) at the null \(c=0\). The null density is \(2(1-x)\). The points \(nx_i\) tend to a rate-2 Poisson process \(\Pi\). Above \(t\) the bulk ratio is \((1-t/n)^{-1}\) per observation, and below \(t\) the finite point factors tend to \(y/t\). Hence the compact local log likelihood tends to
\[
 L(t)=t+\sum_{y\in\Pi,\ y<t}\log(y/t),\quad L(0)=0.
\]
Entry of a new point is continuous. For localization the sample derivative has sign \(nc-K_c\). For \(c\le\delta\) small, \(F_0(c)=2c-c^2\ge1.5c\). A grid of ratio 1.25 from \(M/n\), monotonicity of counts, and binomial Chernoff bounds give \(K_c>nc\) throughout \([M/n,\delta]\) except with probability bounded by \(\sum_{j\ge0}\exp(-C M\,1.25^j)\). Away from 0 the uniform LLN follows from the threshold sums of \(\log X,\log(1-X)\) with integrable envelopes. Terms such as \(-(1-K_c/n)\log(1-c)\) near 1 are bounded by the sample tail sum of \(|\log(1-X)|\), which vanishes as the tail shrinks. The population criterion has a strict gap on \([\delta,1]\) by identification. The limiting objective eventually decreases since \(\#(\Pi\cap[0,t])/t\to2\). Its maximum occurs at a Poisson knot by convexity between knots, and the first knot improves \(L(0)\). Equality of distinct knot heights is a nontrivial analytic equation in continuously distributed arrivals and has probability zero. Thus \(n(\hat c-a)/w\Rightarrow\arg\max L\), and the null LR tends to \(2\sup L\), which has no zero atom. The regular half-chi-square law has an atom of one half.

**Boundary mode with free endpoints.** At \(c=a\), write \(h=n(a'-a),\,v=n(c'-a')\ge0\). With \(b\) fixed the new left Poisson intensity is \(2/w\) times zero below \(h\), a ramp \((y-h)/v\) on \((h,h+v)\) and one above \(h+v\), using a step for \(v=0\). The log likelihood includes a sum of log intensity ratios and compensator \((2h+v)/w\), from the bulk density ratio \(1+(2h+v)/(nw)+o(n^{-1})\). With \(b\) free add its independent critical block of information \(1/w^2\) by the edge split. Held \(a\) imposes \(h=0\), and known coincident mode imposes \(h+v=0\). This proves the local wedge experiment. Free-edge MLE localization remains open.

## Extreme values

Write \(A=-1/\xi,\ e=\mu-\sigma/\xi\), GEV \(b=-\sigma/\xi\), and GPD \(w=e-\mu\) with \(\mu\) held. The [moving-feature proof](2026-11-04-infer-moving-report.md#gpd-and-gev) gives the endpoint Poisson experiment at \(n^{1/A}\) for \(1<A<2\), Gaussian information \(c_0/2\) at \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) for \(A=2\), and homogeneous endpoint Poisson at \(n\) for \(A=1\). Tangents keeping \(e\) fixed form an independent root-n Gaussian block. With \(\xi=-1\) free, impose \(A\ge1\) in the true held chart. With scale and shape free, the GPD tangent information in \(A\) is \(1/A^2\) and both \(\sigma,\xi\) inherit root-n variation. With either held there is no tangent. For negative-shape GEV with \(d\) free coefficients there are \(d-1\) tangents. For example with \(\xi\) held and \(\mu,\sigma\) fitted, \(e\) is fast but both \(\mu,\sigma\) inherit root-n variation from \(b\). These are proved local experiments. For an interior critical experiment the localized LR for a linear equality in the mixed chart is chi-square of the codimension. For an endpoint below critical, use the difference of Poisson likelihood suprema, including the Gaussian factor and actual hold constraints if the hypothesis also constrains it. Joint-fit transfers generally need localization.

**GPD held negative shape, proved.** The maximum \(M\) gives \(U=(M-\mu)/w\) with CDF \([1-(1-u)^A]^n\), by raising the GPD CDF to the \(n\)th power. Divide \(M-\mu\) by the two quantiles in reverse order for the interval for \(w\). Transform to \(e\) or \(\sigma=-\xi w\). At \(A=1\) the MLE is \(M\), with LR exactly \(2\operatorname{Exp}(1)\). If \(\sigma\) is held and \(\xi=-1\) is the true free boundary, all observations are below \(\mu+\sigma\) a.s. At every positive excess the density is bounded by \(1/\sigma\) for \(\xi\ge-1\), with equality only at \(-1\) on that support, so \(\hat\xi=-1\) a.s.

For \(A>1\) rescale to \(\mu=0,e=1\). The log likelihood at a candidate \(e'\) is \(-An\log e'+(A-1)\sum\log(e'-X_i)\) plus a constant. Its score times \(e'\) is \((A-1)\sum X_i/(e'-X_i)-n\), strictly decreasing for \(e'>M\), with opposite signs at \(M+\) and \(AM\). At \(A=2\) the true-point score is \(\sum(1/d_i-2)\) with \(P(d_i<x)=x^2\). Truncating \(1/d\) at \(\sqrt{n\log n}\) gives variance \(\log n+o(\log n)\), probability of any truncation \(O(1/\log n)\), and normalized centering error \(O(1/\log n)\). For every fixed positive fraction of the truncation level the variance contributed above it is \(O(1)\), giving Lindeberg. Likewise \(\sum d_i^{-2}/(n\log n)\to1\) by truncation at \(n\log n\) and Chebyshev. The smallest distance dominates bounded shifts at the critical scale in probability, so normalized curvature converges uniformly there to \(-1\). Evaluating the score on either side of its Gaussian root and using monotonicity localizes the maximum. This proves \(\sqrt{n\log n}(\hat e-e)/w\Rightarrow N(0,1)\) and a \(\chi^2_1\) null LR.

For \(1<A<2\) let \(Y_j\) be ordered arrivals of intensity \(Ay^{A-1}\) in the same unit-width chart. For \(z=n^{1/A}(e'-1)>-Y_1\) the normalized score tends to
\[
 (A-1)\left[\int_0^\infty y^{-1}(d\Pi-A y^{A-1}dy)
       +\sum_j ((Y_j+z)^{-1}-Y_j^{-1})\right].
\]
At \(z=0\) sample centering is exact. The scaled tail variance above \(B\) and absolute mean of the difference tail are \(O(B^{A-2})\), by integration of \(y^{A-3}\). Integrability of the compensated integral at zero follows in first moment since \(A>1\). These bounds give compact score convergence away from the first pole; the change of \(-An/e'\) on this scale vanishes. The limit is strictly decreasing, diverges at \(-Y_1\), and for large positive \(z\) has drift of order \(-z^{A-1}\) and variance \(O(z^{A-2})\), by substituting \(y=zv\) in the compensated Poisson integrals. Monotonicity therefore gives a unique finite zero almost surely and the sign bracket localizes the sample root. The limit of \(n^{1/A}(\hat e-e)/w\) is that zero. Integrating the score gives the likelihood process for LR. The exact pivot avoids simulating it.

**GEV held negative shape and fitted scale, proved.** \(Z_i=((e-X_i)/b)^A\) are independent unit exponentials. For \(n\ge2\), write \(R_2=M-M_2>0\) for the top spacing and \(T=(e-M)/R_2\). Then \(Z_{(1)}=E_1\) has rate \(n\) and is independent of \(Z_{(2)}-Z_{(1)}=E_2\) of rate \(n-1\). \(T>t\) iff \(E_2<kE_1\) with \(k=(1+1/t)^A-1\). Integration gives \(P(T>t)=(n-1)k/[n+(n-1)k]\). The interval \(M+R_2[q_L,q_U]\) cancels \(b\), even with both nuisance location and scale expressed in the original coordinates. If a hold makes \(e\) a one-to-one function of the remaining coefficient, transform the interval. At \(\xi=-1\) held with both \(\mu,\sigma\) fitted, reflected-exponential profiling gives \(e=M,\ b=\sum(M-X_i)/n\), by minimizing the fitted average inward distance. Exponential spacings then give independent unit exponential and standard normal limits for \(n(e-\hat e)/b\) and \(\sqrt n(\hat b-b)/b\). The Pareto \(F\) and chi-square spacings arguments also apply to this reflection.

**Below \(\xi=-1\).** When a free endpoint can be moved onto the maximum with other observations strictly in support, \(d^{A-1}\) with \(A<1\) proves likelihood divergence. The fitted free-shape bound excludes this branch. The held-shape GEV exception with \(\mu\) held and scale free requires \(M\le\mu\), with probability \(\exp(-n)\) at the true model. The all-free GPD threshold path is unbounded by the dossier proof. A pivot for a prespecified sampling distribution still defines a data-based set even when the corresponding fit does not exist.

## Power cusps

For GED the cusp location is the mean. For SGED it is the raw mode \(m\); the dossier's mean is \(m+a\,g(\nu,\xi)\) for raw scale \(a\), and its sd is \(a\,s(\nu,\xi)>0\). The [DQM-B chart](2026-11-04-dqm-b-report.md#two-piece-models-snorm-sstd-and-sged) gives the two side scales. Write the density near the mode as \(f_0(1-A_\pm |x-m|^\nu+o(|x-m|^\nu))\). On the supported low-power routes \(\nu\le1/2\) is held.

**Proved local experiments.** For \(0<\nu<1/2\) use \(h=n^{-1/(1+2\nu)}\) and \(B(y)=A_+ y_+^\nu + A_-(-y)_+^\nu\). With \(m\) shifted by \(hu\) the square-root density increment near the cusp is \(-h^\nu\sqrt{f_0}(B(y-u)-B(y))/2\) at \(x=m+hy\). The increment has squared integral asymptotic to \(h^{1+2\nu}K|u|^{1+2\nu}\), \(K=(f_0/4)\int(B(y-1)-B(y))^2dy\). Its tails are integrable by the derivative bound \(O(|y|^{\nu-1})\). Translation and homogeneity give stationary increment covariance by polarization even for unequal side constants. Single-observation log increments are uniformly \(O(h^\nu)\) by the Hölder bound, which gives the triangular-array CLT. The likelihood drift follows from normalizing the root density, and the product Hellinger increment bound \(C|u-v|^{1+2\nu}\) gives compact tightness. The limiting log likelihood is \(2\sqrt K W_{\nu+1/2}(u)-2K|u|^{1+2\nu}\) with unit-variance-at-one two-sided fractional Brownian motion. This extends the prior even-cusp calculation to SGED. For \(\nu=1/2\) integrate the derivative squares on each side down to the critical displacement. The Hellinger coefficient of \(h^2\log(1/|h|)\) is \(f_0(A_+^2+A_-^2)/16\). Thus the critical rate is \(\sqrt{n\log n}\), with information \(f_0(A_+^2+A_-^2)/8\), by the truncated-score argument of the moving-feature report. These coefficients pertain to raw mode displacement.

Tangents fixing the mode exactly are ordinary root-n Gaussian directions. To see the independence, split the tangent central sequence at a fixed small cusp neighborhood. Its inner part has vanishing limiting variance as the neighborhood shrinks by square integrability. Outside, the mode increments are differentiable and their cross moment with the central sequence vanishes at the faster scale. Smooth side-scale and skew perturbations have integrable squared scores by the DQM-B envelopes. Thus the local product limit applies after restricting to the true held surface. Holding GED mean fixes the cusp. Holding SGED mean alone generally does not fix it, since free sd or skew can change \(a\,g\). Use \(dm=d(\mathrm{mean}-a\,g)\) on the free coordinates and reparameterize along the exact level surface where that derivative has rank one. With free raw modes and tangents, named mean has root-n variation whenever its differential on the tangent block is nonzero. At zero differential use the fast contribution and check higher-order terms. Here a squared root-n tangent perturbation is smaller than the mode scale for every fixed \(\nu>0\). At exceptional held points with a vanishing cusp derivative on the whole free subspace, inspect the exact hold equation before assigning the tangent-only model.

The MLE candidate is the argmax of the fractional Brownian likelihood with independent Gaussian tangents, or the Gaussian in the critical chart. The global and production transfers for all supported hold masks remain conjectured. The exact LR simulation below avoids those asymptotic transfers. The iid cusp literature includes [Prakasa Rao (1968)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177698506). The prior moving-feature report checks the scope of its sources.

**Fixed skew and power, exact LR for raw mode.** For GED or for SGED with \(\xi,\nu\) held and raw scale free, maximizing over scale amounts to minimizing the side-weighted sum of powers of residuals. For \(0<\nu<1\) it is concave between knots and diverges at infinity, so the minimum is attained at a sample knot. The statistic for a specified raw mode profiles scale under both hypotheses. Under the null, shift and positive-scale equivariance make its law independent of scale. Compute the Monte Carlo rank under unit scale and invert. This proves coverage by the rank argument below. It calibrates raw mode. For named SGED mean a null must impose its scale-dependent raw location, profile that likelihood and recalibrate the resulting statistic. For free skew use nuisance protection or an independently proved joint limit.

## Tukey lambda

For positive shape let \(a=\sigma/\lambda,\ e_\pm=\mu\pm a\). Restrict the endpoint differential \(de_\pm=d\mu\pm(d\sigma/\lambda-\sigma\,d\lambda/\lambda^2)\) to the true holds. In the all-free case the endpoint rank is two and the sole tangent changes shape at fixed location and radius. With only scale and shape free the rank is one. With only location and either scale or shape free the rank is two. A single free coefficient moves at least one endpoint. Only a mask with both scale and shape free has a nontrivial tangent.

**Proved local experiments**, from the [moving-feature analysis](2026-11-04-infer-moving-report.md#tukey-lambda). At \(\lambda=1/2\) use independent edge Gaussian factors at \(\sqrt{n\log n}\), filtered through the endpoint map. For \(1/2<\lambda<1\) the two edges have power Poisson processes at \(n^\lambda\). In both regimes the exact endpoint-preserving shape tangent is root-n and Gaussian. If both scale and shape are free, \(\sigma=a\lambda\) therefore inherits root-n variability. An endpoint LR uses the difference of calibrated experiment suprema (Gaussian only in the critical case) subject to the actual endpoint velocities and nuisance tangent. An endpoint-preserving regular restriction may have a chi-square LR if the Poisson term cancels under both hypotheses and localization holds.

At \(\lambda=1\) the law is uniform and the edge experiment is homogeneous Poisson at \(n\). With scale and shape free, shape at fixed radius has positive finite tangent information from the score \(-(\log u+\log(1-u)+2)/2\). On the fitted branch it has a one-sided Gaussian tangent. If scale is held and shape free, shape instead moves the radius at rate \(n\) on a one-sided endpoint range. In particular at fixed scale the null radius is the smallest permitted radius on the positive principal branch. If location is also free, the leading endpoint objective can be constant over a range of compatible centres at that radius. The leading process alone cannot select a unique centre. For fixed \(\lambda>1\), the edges have positive density and rate \(n\); for free location alone the homogeneous leading objective is likewise flat on the compatible shifts because the two edge density constants agree. Limit laws of selected point estimators in such sets require the next likelihood term or a tie convention. The general mixed argmax transfers and finite search errors remain open.

**Held uniform members, proved.** The quantile in the radius chart is \(\mu+a[u^\lambda-(1-u)^\lambda]\), and at both 1 and 2 the bracket is \(2u-1\). If held at either value and location and scale are fitted, apply the joint uniform set and transform location to the midpoint and scale to \(\lambda\) times the half-width. This gives simultaneous coverage and marginal projections. With known scale the width is known and \((l-e_-)/(2a)\) has the Beta\((1,n)\) law, from the minimum order statistic; invert and intersect with containment. With known centre, \(\max|X_i-\mu|/a\) has CDF \(v^n\) since absolute deviations are uniform on \((0,a)\). The fixed-shape observations distinguish support, even though interior location scores vanish at these uniform members. At free \(\lambda=1\), the shape upper constraint and the fast support must both enter inference. The second uniform member \(\lambda=2\) is outside the free-shape branch, so comparing the two fitted representations as if locally identifiable is invalid. For example \((\mu,\sigma,\lambda)=(0,1,1)\) and \((0,2,2)\) give identical likelihoods on every sample in \((-1,1)\), including \((-1/2,1/2)\).

## Singular charts

### Azzalini skew-normal

Let \(\delta=\alpha/\sqrt{1+\alpha^2}\), \(b_0=\sqrt{2/\pi}\). The representation
\(X=\xi+\omega(\delta |Z_0|+\sqrt{1-\delta^2}Z_1)\) uses independent unit normals. To verify it, condition a bivariate normal of correlation \(\delta\) on the first variate being positive. The density of the second is \(2\phi(z)\Phi(\alpha z)\) by normal conditioning. Define mean \(m=\xi+\omega b_0\delta\), sd \(s=\omega\sqrt{1-b_0^2\delta^2}\) and signed chart \(\eta=\delta^3\). After standardizing by \(m,s\), the linear and quadratic Taylor coefficients vanish because the changes through order two are mean and variance changes. More explicitly the cumulant generating function after standardization is the sum of a linear term, a quadratic term and the log-mgf of a centered half-normal at argument \(\omega\delta t/s\). The linear term is zero and the quadratic term equals \(t^2/2\); the first remaining term is \(\kappa_3\delta^3 t^3/6\), where \(\kappa_3=(4-\pi)b_0^3/2>0\). Differentiating the density at zero gives normal density times \(\kappa_3 H_3/6\) for the cubic coefficient, since \(H_3=z^3-3z\) has normal exponential transform \(t^3e^{t^2/2}\). Taylor remainders through this order in the square-root density have an \(L^2\) Gaussian-polynomial envelope. This follows by differentiating \(\sqrt{2\phi(z)\Phi(\alpha z)}\) and the affine standardization, using polynomial bounds on derivatives of \(\log\Phi\) near the left tail and Gaussian decay uniform for sufficiently small \(|\delta|\). Hence in the signed chart DQM has orthogonal Hermite scores of orders 1, 2 and 3. Information \(I_\eta=\kappa_3^2/6\) is unchanged by fitted mean and scale.

For a localized MLE the proved Gaussian chart gives a root-n normal \(\hat\eta\); \(n^{1/6}\hat\alpha\) is asymptotically its signed cube root. The raw \(\xi,\omega\) inherit respectively the order-\(\alpha\) and order-\(\alpha^2\) corrections. At true zero, \(n^{1/6}(\hat\xi-\xi)\) has limit \(-\omega b_0\) times that cube root, and \(n^{1/3}(\hat\omega-\omega)\) has limit \(\omega b_0^2/2\) times its square. Root-n inference attaches to the centered coordinates. LR for symmetry uses \(\chi^2_1\) after localization in the signed chart. Project an interval for \(\eta\) back monotonically to \(\alpha\), or use an affine-invariant simulated symmetry null with fitted location and scale. An ordinary first-order delta Wald interval in the raw shape has limiting coverage \(P(|Z|\le z_{1-\varepsilon/2}/3)\) in the localized experiment. This follows because the estimated delta standard error is asymptotically the raw absolute estimate divided by \(3|Z|\). A percentile interval is invariant under a monotone transformation. It *can* inherit validity from a proved regular-chart parametric percentile procedure; asymmetry of the raw rate alone does not prove percentile failure. A centered basic bootstrap in raw shape generally depends on the random fitted local cubic coordinate through the increment \(\sqrt[3]{Z+Z^*}-\sqrt[3]{Z}\).

If \(\xi\) is held, the shape score at zero is a nonzero first Hermite score and independent of the scale score. If shape is held at zero, use the normal held submodel. If \(\omega\) is held with \(\xi,\alpha\) free, absorb the mean shift but the variance changes at order \(\delta^2\). The leading chart \(t=\delta^2\ge0\) has efficient score \(-b_0^2 H_2/2\) at fixed mean and information \(b_0^4/2\), by the same expansion. It yields a cone limit for \(\sqrt n\,\hat t\) and an \(n^{1/4}\) limit for absolute shape if localized. This leading experiment erases the sign. A signed estimator needs comparison of the two higher-order branches. The general identifiable-singularity precedent is Rotnitzky et al.; the simple skew-normal cubic rate and centered chart are also discussed by [Hallin and Ley](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.4177).

### Beta negative binomial

**Proved quotient chart.** Put \(s=(r+\beta)/2,\ t=(r-\beta)^2/4\in[0,s^2)\). The adjacent-mass ratio is
\[
 p_{x+1}/p_x=\frac{(x+s)^2-t}{(x+1)(x+\alpha+2s)}.
\]
The denominator pole cannot cancel a numerator factor for positive \(\alpha,r,\beta\). Equality of the rational functions for every nonnegative integer identifies the denominator parameter, sum and product of the two shapes, hence the unordered pair and \(\alpha\). At \(t=0\) the differential of the log ratio is \(2\,ds/(x+s)-dt/(x+s)^2-(d\alpha+2ds)/(x+\alpha+2s)\). The distinct poles and double pole force the three increments to vanish if this rational differential is identically zero. The quotient law is smooth through zero from the right. For a local extension to small negative \(t\), use the positive numerator and normalize the successive-ratio products; their tail power is \(x^{-1-\alpha}\). This follows by expanding the ratios as \(1-(1+\alpha)/x+O(x^{-2})\) uniformly for compact positive \(s,\alpha\) and small \(|t|\). Summed derivative expansions give at most logarithmic growth and an integrable polynomial tail envelope. Thus DQM and positive definite information hold in the extended chart.

Profile the two regular nuisance coordinates in the information metric. The localized MLE of \(t\) has \(\sqrt n\,\hat t\Rightarrow\max(0,G/I_e)\) with \(G\sim N(0,I_e)\) and \(I_e\) the Schur complement. Its half gap has limit \(n^{1/4}\sqrt{\hat t}\Rightarrow\sqrt{\max(0,G/I_e)}\). Nuisance estimates are the remaining components of the information projection; they need not be independent of the active bound in their original coordinates. The LR for equality is half point mass plus half \(\chi^2_1\) under localization. Recover an unordered confidence set by projecting a joint quotient set through \(s\pm\sqrt t\). Off the diagonal both label permutations describe the same law, so a single labeled Wald interval is not justified. With one shape held, vary the other in its original coordinate. The symmetric first derivative is now a nonzero score and the held parameter fixes the labeling; verify global uniqueness in that submodel. The 80-digit determinant grid checks the quotient rank calculation. It cannot check localization of a numerical fit passing near an escaping face.

### Generalized beta

**Proved ridge and conditional test.** With scale \(b\) held, write \(Y=-\log(X/b)\), \(a=\mathrm{shape3}\), \(q=\mathrm{shape2}\), \(p=\mathrm{shape1}\), \(r=ap\). The density \(a e^{-ry}(1-e^{-ay})^{q-1}/B(r/a,q)\) is exactly \(\operatorname{Exp}(r)\) at \(q=1\), for every \(a>0\). Even a noiseless population law cannot identify the factors. With \(q=1\) held the product estimator is \(n/\sum Y_i\), with gamma CLT variance \(r^2\) at root-n rate and exact chi-square pivot \(2r\sum Y_i\) with \(2n\) df, from exponential addition. Under the composite null \(q=1\) the normalized vector \(Y_i/\sum Y_i\) is \(\operatorname{Dirichlet}(1,\ldots,1)\) independently of its sum. The Jacobian of the sum-and-proportions transform is \((\sum Y_i)^{n-1}\) and the joint exponential density depends only on the sum. Thus simulate exponential proportions and multiply by the observed sum to calibrate a deterministic full-vs-ridge score or LR by exchangeable ranks, with no fitted-\(r\) approximation. Inversion for the full parameter space requires tests at other values of \(q\) as well. With either factor held, the remaining rate and shape have independent score directions in the sense of linear rank. The rate score is \(1/r-y\); the shape score contains the nonaffine function \(\log(1-e^{-ay})\).

**Proved compact local experiment.** At fixed \(a\) the shape score is \(s_a(y)=\log(1-e^{-ay})+\psi(r/a+1)-\psi(1)\). Project away the rate score and denote the result by \(g_a\). On a compact interval \(0<a_-\le a\le a_+<\infty\), all score derivatives in \(a\) and the local chart Taylor remainders have integrable logarithmic envelopes at zero and polynomial envelopes under the exponential tail. For instance the raw log term is \(\log y+O(1)\) near zero, and \(\partial_a\log(1-e^{-ay})=y/(e^{ay}-1)\le1/a_-\). Thus the bounded-index score process is tight by the \(L^2\) Lipschitz bound and the iid CLT, and the quadratic expansions in bounded local \(r,q\) are uniform by the same envelopes. The compact local profiled LR versus the ridge is the supremum of \(G(a)^2/E g_a^2\), with Gaussian covariance \(E[g_a g_{a'}]\), because both signs of local \(q-1\) are allowed. This is the nuisance-only-under-alternative geometry of [Davies (1977)](https://www.stats.org.uk/statistical-inference/Davies1977.pdf). Distinct score directions do not become proportional after projection: taking \(y\to\infty\) in a putative affine relation first removes all linear growth, then the different exponential residual orders rule out proportionality. Calibration by a single fixed index is therefore unjustified. The unrestricted \(a\to0,\infty\) need separate tightness and localization. The exact conditional rank test needs no such theorem if its statistic is defined on all draws.

No general all-free MLE limit for the numerical factors on this ridge is claimed. Even within a compact chart, selecting the maximizing index of a Gaussian process can produce a random order-one displacement in the unidentifiable factors. Conjecturally the regular joint local coefficients are obtained by taking the profiled quadratic maximizer at that index, if the maximizing index is essentially unique and the global fit localizes there. Inference for a product when \(q\) floats requires nuisance protection; the gamma interval is exact when \(q=1\) is imposed by design.

## Boundary and closure

**Regular constrained nuisance, proved given LAN and localization.** If \(C\) is the true tangent cone and \(C_0\) its null subset, complete the square in \(h^\top Z-h^\top I h/2\), with \(Z\sim N(0,I)\). The estimator tends to the \(I\)-metric projection of \(I^{-1}Z\) onto \(C\). The LR tends to the squared \(I\)-distance to \(C_0\) minus that to \(C\). For a single inequality and otherwise unconstrained, regular identified nuisance, project it out with its Schur complement. Testing that inequality at equality then gives \((Z_+)^2\) in a unit normal coordinate, the half-atom/half-chi-square mixture. A different tested contrast or several constraints generally require both cones. This calculation applies to the underlying regular GEV upper shape bound \(1\), the GED/SGED free power lower bound \(1\), and the Tukey lower bound \(-1\) when present, conditional on localized accurate fitting. For symmetric GED the odd location direction is orthogonal to the constrained even scale/shape block. A known held value removes its direction before the calculation. Holding a boundary value may instead leave a moving support, give a degenerate law, or make other coefficients unidentified. Examples are uniform GPD with shape held at \(-1\), binomial-type known size 0, and beta-binomial size 1, where only the Bernoulli probability is identified. An active bound reported after fitting is not a known hold. [Self and Liang (1987)](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/763-2015/week06/papers/self-liang-1987.pdf)

**Named limits.** The [closure proofs](2026-11-04-infer-closure-report.md) give regular half-line LR limits for exponential versus Lomax, Weibull versus Burr, Poisson against each of NB and unknown-size binomial separately, and finite binomial or NB against beta-binomial, with the stated nuisance fitted. The Poisson beta-binomial corner gives \(\tfrac12\chi^2_1+\tfrac12\chi^2_2\) at positive mean. Burr versus Pareto with endpoint fitted has a Poisson-process LR in endpoint and width at rate \(n\), independent of the Gaussian tail-shape factor. With a held endpoint the null LR in that particular alternative is zero a.s. and support violations reject. These are hypothesis-specific proved results; the closures also include unbounded coordinates and model-dependent nuisance identifiable only by their finite combinations. Confidence intervals for diverging original coefficients cannot be attached to a named escape. Inference in a separately posited limiting family uses that model's own holds and target. Choosing it after the same data indicate a limit does not establish the coverage of such an interval for the selected target.

For a proper finite degenerate member, distinguish a deterministic statistic under the boundary null from a confidence interval over nearby models. For example all zero counts give Poisson MLE zero, and \(P_\mu(\sum X_i=0)=e^{-n\mu}\) gives upper bound \(-\log\varepsilon/n\) in the exact count inversion. Plugging in zero and simulating always gives the same all-zero count, which cannot yield that upper bound. For one-point support or a design such as size 0, check whether there are any free identifiable targets before returning a set.

## Resampling and test inversion

**Finite Monte Carlo tests, proved.** Given a full null law, draw \(B\) independent datasets and apply the same deterministic statistic to all of them and the observed data. The inclusive rank p-value \((1+\#\{T_b\ge T_{\rm obs}\})/(B+1)\) is super-uniform by exchangeability, including ties. Use the exact same held parameters, restrictions and observation mechanism. Equivariant and conditional cases above remove nuisance. For composite hypotheses, accept a target value if the supremum of valid p-values over *all* compatible nuisance values exceeds \(\varepsilon\). Rejecting at that supremum implies rejection at the true nuisance when the target is true, proving coverage. A certified upper bound on the supremum is conservative. A finite grid of point evaluations usually gives a lower bound and can undercover. For full confidence sets preserve holes, unbounded components and disconnected components, or return an explicitly conservative hull. This construction works with any predetermined statistic defined on every sample. A genuine LR claim requires correct null and alternative optima, including comparable closure likelihoods. Retrying only until a successful refit creates a different statistic and changes the experiment.

**Empirical \(n\)-out-of-\(n\), proved failure for extremes.** For a unique maximum the empirical bootstrap repeats that maximum with probability \(1-(1-1/n)^n\to1-e^{-1}\). The limiting law of the true normalized endpoint gap is continuous in the uniform example. The atom precludes a consistent distribution estimate. Parametric refitting evades the atom, but its *percentile* interval still fails. A centered or pivot-based parametric inversion can be valid: uniform maximum with lower endpoint held has exact pivot \(\hat b/b\) after translation and the plug-in model reproduces its distribution exactly. The basic interval using an unstudentized parametric difference is only asymptotically equivalent to the exact ratio inversion. For a regular Gaussian half-line, \(\hat h=Z_+\) at the null with efficient variance one. The unrestricted parametric refit in the local experiment is \((Z_++Z^*)_+\), so its conditional distribution depends on the random fitted drift. Even the equal-tailed percentile interval covers zero with probability \(\Phi(z_{.975})=.975\), since the lower endpoint is zero iff \(Z_+\le z_{.975}\); this is not .95. This gives a specific failure, without claiming every null-imposed or transformed bootstrap fails. [Andrews (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00114)

**Subsampling.** Suppose \(a_n(\hat\theta_n-\theta)\) converges at the true law and the limit CDF is continuous at the needed quantiles. For \(m\to\infty,\ m/n\to0,\ a_m/a_n\to0\), the full-sample centering replacement is \(o_p(1)\) on the subsample scale. For the oracle-centered indicator average over subsets, random disjoint blocks of size \(m\) give independent indicators with conditional average over random partitions equal to the subset average. The variance is thus at most \(1/(4\lfloor n/m\rfloor)\) by conditional Jensen, and its expectation tends to the desired CDF. Bracketing a threshold by fixed small shifts justifies replacing the parameter by the full estimate in the centering. Invert quantiles of the centered law on the \(a_n\) scale, with sign chosen for the gap, rather than reporting raw subsample percentiles. For an atom, continuity at the relevant quantile fails and a conservative test or explicit treatment of the atom is required. Each nuisance and target needs its own rate; a fitted shape used in \(n^\kappa\) needs \((\hat\kappa-\kappa)\log n=o_p(1)\). This argument is pointwise and assumes a proved estimator law.

For uniform endpoints the empirical \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) maximum also works pointwise. For a known lower endpoint and unit width, the count exceeding \(1-y/m\) has mean \(ny/m\) and relative fluctuations vanishing for each \(y>0\). Thus the conditional probability of a resampled maximum below this threshold tends to \(e^{-y}\) by the binomial-to-exponential limit of the resampling probability. Replacing the endpoint centre by \(\hat b_n\) changes the rescaled threshold by \(O_p(m/n)\). Scale equivariance covers other widths. This proof does not validate the bootstrap with replacement for all mixed-rate refits, particularly where induced ties change fit existence. The subsampling argument applies after the appropriate family-specific asymptotics and computation are secured. [Bickel and Sakov (2008)](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~bickel/BS2008SS.pdf)

## What this changes for confint/bootstrap

The [current confint and bootstrap implementations](../../../bindings/r/R/code_generics.R) use equal-tailed quantiles of refits, default to parametric draws, and guard on known uniqueness and data provenance. [bootstrapml](../../../bindings/r/R/code_bootstrap.R) replays holds, zero handling and truncation, uses whole observation rows for empirical resampling, and refuses parametric censoring without a mechanism. It does not dispatch by target/regime or calibrate a test under a null.

1. **Dispatch by target, provenance and fit outcome.** Use family ID, recorded holds and restrictions, requested estimand, support, sample checks and certification. Keep original computational failures and escape conditions, including the named limiting model and its certification. Do not infer a known design value from a fitted coefficient or warning. Return held parameters in a separate fixed-value result if explicitly requested, or signal a fixed-target condition. Preserve requested target order, aliases and transformed-target definitions.
2. **Compute exact procedures first for proved scopes.** For uniform endpoints, Pareto and power endpoint/shape, held-negative-shape GPD with known threshold, and estimable GEV endpoint with negative shape held, use the pivots above. GEV needs an explicit endpoint target with both \(\mu,\sigma\) free, since an interval for \(e\) alone cannot be re-labeled as either coefficient. Include reflected-exponential scale at held \(\xi=-1\) with location and scale fitted. For held Tukey uniform shapes use the transformed uniform set under the true holds, even if a location fit is set-valued at fixed width. For triangular mode with endpoints held offer the exact binomial set, and optionally exact Monte Carlo LR inversion. For generalized beta with \(q=1\) held the identifiable product receives the exponential pivot. Exact procedures depending only on data and genuine holds may bypass maximizer uniqueness when the target and the pivot remain well-defined. Require the stated sample and support conditions.
3. **Calibrate tests at the candidate null.** A method such as null-LR must carry its null family, actual hold mask, nuisance strategy, replicate count or analytic reference, optimizer accuracy, and returned set topology. Ship first the affine or conditional cases with a controlled statistic, including fixed-power GED/raw SGED mode and generalized-beta ridge testing. At true regular inequality nulls use the appropriate cone only when the family/hold/target rule and localization have been validated. For centered skew-normal offer symmetry tests and a signed-cubic interval under the proved chart with a validated fit or test inversion. For beta negative binomial offer quotient or unordered targets only after certifying fit transfer, or use finite null simulation with nuisance protection. For free-endpoint triangular, noncritical free-shape extreme values, moving-support Tukey, and unsupported SGED holds, offer a proved finite-null inversion where computable or refuse the interval method with its specific missing calibration. A candidate-space supremum must include held boundaries and relevant faces. Preserve all set components or label an explicitly conservative projection.
4. **Refuse unsupported interval claims with a classed condition.** Use the existing unsupported stop hierarchy with a specific inference subclass, fixed/nonidentified/regime/limit or refit-failure reason, plus family, target, method and holds. Examples are percentile endpoint inference, separately labeled all-free beta negative binomial, generalized-beta factors on the held ridge, and a diverging original coefficient at a named limit. For ambiguous regime membership, calibrate candidate nulls across the transition rather than choosing a critical law because a floating-point estimate happens to lie within an arbitrary neighborhood. If calibration spanning that region is unavailable, report it precisely. Preserve the existing non-unique condition for procedures that require a unique point estimate.
5. **Distinguish exploratory refit distributions from confidence intervals.** Allow explicit raw bootstrap draws with a classed warning for unsupported method/regime pairs, including arbitrary maps whose target cannot be inferred. Label these as draws without a coverage guarantee. Do not return default endpoint percentile limits as confidence intervals. Validate refit outcomes before reduction, retain per-replicate failure or limit reasons, and refuse interval reduction when the advertised procedure lacks a predetermined treatment of these outcomes. No silent dropping, replacement until success, or mixing coefficients across families. Use smaller-sample inference only with its stated rate, centering, inversion and atom treatment. For censored/truncated/modified models the exact iid pivots here require their own observation-law derivation before dispatch.

## What this changes for the fitter

Keep the statistical inference regime separate from existence, uniqueness and numerical certification. A proved fit at a legitimate boundary, or a proved support-determined fit, remains an estimate. Record the exact active constraint, independent held values, endpoint chart and whether the maximizing set is unique. Where certified, provide the set or its proved projection for compatible uniform centres and label-symmetric beta negative binomial. A held generalized-beta ridge can carry its identifiable product and the positive factorization set. For held GPD shape with \(-1<\xi\le-1/2\) and positive excesses, the monotone bracket above is a global scalar certificate; the \(-1\) result is the sample maximum. At held scale and true uniform-boundary support, the per-observation density bound provides a finite-sample certificate for \(\xi=-1\) whenever all excesses lie in \([0,\sigma]\) and at least one is positive. At a named face preserve both the existing reason and evidence distinguishing proved from margin-based comparisons.

For likelihood inference add a separately reported upper bound on the total log-likelihood gap for restricted and unrestricted optima, plus a localization region in the relevant chart. A fixed per-observation comparison allowance yields an \(O(n)\) total allowance and does not establish an \(o_p(1)\) likelihood error. If an ordinary-data certificate fails to close, retain the fitter's existing search result marked uncertified under the fallback rule. This can disqualify likelihood-based inference without changing the fit outcome. An independent exact pivot can still be valid if its input conditions hold. Stop only for proved wrong/nonexistent results or failed searches as in the existing contract.
