Beta-binomial with size and both shapes free: closure and finite attainment

TASK-113, 2026-11-03. Source revision: f14d43321b89e5a685eec2889da9fa42679de958, branch codex/bbinom-closure. Research only: the additions are this report and its small reproducer/data directory. No library, binding, dossier, test, golden, or roadmap changes were made.

Results

The positive-likelihood escape conjecture in the numerical map can be proved. If size tends to infinity and any fixed positive count retains positive probability, a subsequence converges to a proper negative binomial or Poisson distribution. There is no defective limit retaining mass at a positive count. Defective limits do exist, but are exactly subprobabilities concentrated at zero.

Consequently, the full two-shape profile at size NN, not just a profile with one shape held, converges to the optimized NB/Poisson face. This is a qualitative uniform upper-tail result; it does not supply an implementable numerical cutoff.

For a nonconstant sample with an observation strictly between zero and its maximum, a finite positive-shape MLE exists if and only if some finite beta-binomial has likelihood at least as large as the best boundary law. The active boundary is the best free-size binomial for underdispersion, Poisson for equidispersion, and the best NB for overdispersion. Strict improvement is sufficient and avoids equality questions.

At equidispersion there is a useful sufficient condition:

1h∑ixi(xi−1)(xi−2)<x‾3⇒a finite positive-shape MLE exists, \frac1h\sum_i x_i(x_i-1)(x_i-2)<\bar x^3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{a finite positive-shape MLE exists},

provided the sample is not supported only on its two endpoints. It follows from the second-order expansion at Poisson proved below. The threshold is sharp for the sign of the second-order cancelling direction, not a necessary condition for global attainment.

A stronger local/global counterexample than a negative NB slope is available: for (2,2,3,3,4,7,7)(2,2,3,3,4,7,7), Poisson is a strict local maximum in the joint near-Poisson model, but the explicit law BB(7,2,1)BB(7,2,1) beats it. Thus a finite MLE exists despite local stability of the boundary. Its likelihood comparison has an exact rational certificate.

2. Exhaustive closure

2.1 Bounded size

2.3 Proper and defective closures

It follows that the proper weak closure of the whole family consists exactly of:

  • finite-size beta-binomials;
  • finite-size binomials, including their point masses;
  • endpoint mixtures on {0,N}\{0,N\};
  • arbitrary proper NB laws and Poisson laws.

The same proper closure is obtained in total variation or Hellinger distance. Every listed law is approachable by the paths above; the subsequence argument excludes any other proper limit.

In the topology of pointwise convergence allowing subprobabilities, the only additional limits are

qδ0,0≤q<1. q\delta_0,\qquad 0\le q<1. \tag{8}

Indeed, any limit with positive mass at a positive integer must be proper by the theorem. Conversely, for b=1b=1 and a=t/log⁡Na=t/\log N, PN(0)→e−tP_N(0)\to e^{-t} while every fixed positive probability tends to zero. The product in (1) proves this because its log is −aHN+O(a2)-aH_N+O(a^2). Fixed a>0,b=1a>0,b=1 gives the zero subprobability.

This explains precisely why defective escape does not matter when M≥1M\ge1: it gives zero sample likelihood. It can retain positive likelihood for an all-zero sample, for which N=0N=0 already attains likelihood one.

No moment-tightness assumption was used. Such an assumption would be unsafe: a=N−1/2,b=1a=N^{-1/2},b=1 gives weak convergence to δ0\delta_0 but EX∼NE X\sim\sqrt N.

3. What closure proves about attainment

Let

QN=supa,b>0ℓBB(N,a,b),F=supa>0,m>0ℓNB(a,m), Q_N=\sup_{a,b>0}\ell_{BB}(N,a,b),\qquad F=\sup_{a>0,\ m>0}\ell_{NB}(a,m), \tag{9}

where FF includes the Poisson limit. The NB mean profiles at m=μm=\mu. The standard NB result is that its finite-shape maximum is unique when v>μv>\mu, whereas its supremum is Poisson when v≤μv\le\mu; see Brøns and Jensen (1988), especially Section 3.

3.1 Uniform upper-tail convergence of the full profile

Corollary.

limN→∞QN=F. \boxed{\lim_{N\to\infty}Q_N=F.} \tag{10}

For the lower bound, approach each fixed NB law along b=Na/μb=Na/\mu; approach Poisson along a=N,b=N2/μ−Na=N,\ b=N^2/\mu-N. Taking suprema gives liminf⁡QN≥F\liminf Q_N\ge F.

For the upper bound, if QN≥F+ηQ_N\ge F+\eta infinitely often, choose finite shapes with likelihood above F+η/2F+\eta/2. Each observed probability is then bounded below by the positive joint likelihood, since all other factors are at most one. Apply the theorem at m=Mm=M. Its proper subsequential NB/Poisson limit has log likelihood at most FF, a contradiction.

The uniform statement is: for every η>0\eta>0, all shapes at all sufficiently large sizes have ℓ≤F+η\ell\le F+\eta. It does not say that every shape path approaches the optimized face, or give a computable size as a function of η\eta.

3.2 Finite size and finite shapes are different questions

If fixed-size shape boundaries are admitted, a global maximum at a finite size exists if and only if

∃N≥M:QN≥F. \exists N\ge M:\quad Q_N\ge F. \tag{11}

Each QNQ_N is attained in its compact fixed-size closure. If one exceeds FF, (10) excludes the integer tail above some finite cutoff, so finitely many compact profiles suffice. If one equals FF, either it is already a maximum or another profile exceeds FF, reducing to the preceding case. If every QN<FQ_N<F, only the infinite-size face gives the supremum.

For attainment with positive finite shapes, define BB as the supremum over all finite-size binomial laws, including their Poisson limit, and EendE_{\mathrm{end}} as the best endpoint/point likelihood. Let E=max⁡(F,B,Eend)E=\max(F,B,E_{\mathrm{end}}). Then the exact characterization is

A finite positive-shape MLE exists⇔∃(N,a,b) finite with ℓBB(N,a,b)≥E. \boxed{\text{A finite positive-shape MLE exists} \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists (N,a,b)\text{ finite with }\ell_{BB}(N,a,b)\ge E.} \tag{12}

Necessity holds because all faces are approachable by actual beta-binomials. For sufficiency, if the global supremum is EE, the displayed candidate attains it. If it exceeds EE, a maximizing sequence can approach neither an infinite-size limit nor any fixed-size shape boundary. A subsequence stays at a finite size with shapes in a compact positive interval and attains the supremum.

Equality in (11) or (12) is an exact mathematical comparison. A small numerical difference is not an equality certificate.

For nonconstant samples having some 0<xi<M0<x_i<M, Eend=−∞E_{\mathrm{end}}=-\infty. The boundary threshold simplifies:

Sample regime Boundary threshold EE in (12)
v<μv<\mu Best free-size binomial, at a finite integer size
v=μv=\mu Pois(μ)Pois(\mu)
v>μv>\mu Best finite-shape NB(â,μ)NB(\widehat a,\mu)

The binomial ingredient is the positive-mean existence result in DeRiggi (1983): its finite-size maximum exists for v<μv<\mu, while the other cases have Poisson supremum. The binomial dossier records the variance convention and integer-search interpretation. These two classical face results, combined with the closure theorem, prove the table; the table alone does not decide whether the BB interior beats the threshold.

Exceptional samples are explicit:

  • All zeros attain likelihood one at N=0N=0.
  • Constant positive data have only the point-mass supremum; finite positive shapes cannot attain it.
  • Nonconstant data on {0,1}\{0,1\} attain their empirical Bernoulli law at N=1N=1, with concentration unidentified.
  • Nonconstant data on {0,M}\{0,M\}, M≥2M\ge2, have the empirical endpoint-mixture supremum at size MM. It is not attained by positive shapes, since a finite beta-binomial at N≥MN\ge M assigns positive mass to unobserved counts.

4.3 Why NB is a seam, but BNB is not an extra BB limit

The exact ratio to zero can also be written

P(x)P(0)=(−N)x(a)x(1−b−N)xx!. \frac{P(x)}{P(0)} =\frac{(-N)_x(a)_x}{(1-b-N)_x\,x!}.

when r,α,a>0r,\alpha,a>0. In the mean-fixed coordinates above, negative kk corresponds to

This BNB has mean exactly μ\mu and approaches NB(a,μ)NB(a,\mu) as r→∞r\to\infty. The BB and BNB expansions meet there from opposite signs of kk. At the NB fitted mean, the first derivative is (14); the derivative on the BNB side reverses sign when expressed in 1/r1/r. The reproducer checks both sides.

The earlier map used b=Na/μb=Na/\mu, whose finite-size mean differs slightly from μ\mu. Replacing it by the mean-fixed path subtracts aa from bb; its first-order contribution to the summed likelihood is a multiple of the NB mean score, which is zero at μ=x‾\mu=\bar x. Thus the fitted-face derivative is the same in the two coordinates.

Similarly, the ratio μ(1−κx)/[(x+1)(1−κμ)]\mu(1-\kappa x)/[(x+1)(1-\kappa\mu)] describes binomial for positive reciprocal integer κ\kappa, Poisson at zero, and NB of shape −1/κ-1/\kappa for negative κ\kappa.

These statements concern analytic extensions of probability ratios and their asymptotic germs. Positive BB sizes are still integers. Substituting a negative size is not an admissible BB parameter path. A proper finite-parameter BNB has a power tail and is neither NB nor Poisson; the closure theorem excludes it as a BB limit. The seam explains local geometry, not an additional escape family.

Open: computable unrestricted tail cutoff

Equation (10) proves a uniform upper-tail limit, but its compactness argument does not calculate the first excluded size. An implementation still needs a computable upper bound valid over all a,ba,b, tied to a likelihood gap and propagated fixed-size allowances. Equation (5) now excludes small aa uniformly; (6) controls small b/Nb/N on compact shape intervals; (15) controls the joint large-shape corner locally. Combining these into an economical global numerical certificate, with explicit constants and practical floating-point margins, remains open.

The held-alpha correction proportional to a2/Na^2/N cannot simply be optimized over unrestricted aa; the held-alpha report already explains that obstruction. It is now a quantitative-bound problem, rather than an unidentified-limit-family problem.

Open: deciding genuine infinite-size winners and equality

No global certificate is supplied for a nonendpoint sample whose only maximizing size is infinity. The map’s NB candidates remain candidates until every finite profile is excluded. The necessary-and-sufficient comparison (12) and the sufficient conditions (13), (14), and (18) do not turn a negative local slope into that exclusion. Exact ties with a boundary threshold also require a separate argument.

Open: uniqueness and production inference

Neither the closure theorem nor these witnesses prove uniqueness of unrestricted finite MLEs. No estimator asymptotics, calibration, or covariance procedure is established here. Enabling the unsupported fitter would additionally require certified shape/size comparisons and explicit failure outcomes; research-only authorization does not enable it. No change to the settled d/p/q/r or truth machinery is indicated by this study.
