TASK-477 reviewed all 591 assigned TASK-475 proposals. The submission contains **425 accepted, 166 regraded and zero rejected**. The count-law exceptions in the earlier reviews depend on changing the parent domain as the window changes. ## Proof errors and scope corrections

### Divergence can survive upper-cut widening

At a positive finite supremum, the likelihood factorization forces the tight and loose retained masses to have ratio tending to one on any maximizing sequence. The conditional laws then differ by total variation tending to zero. This argument uses the positivity and finiteness of the supremum. The proposed exact GPD and Pareto IV failure lists with upper or both cuts estimated describe the tight problem and miss joint paths with different limiting laws. Their attainment comparisons still hold. [Joint profiling proof](2026-11-28-trunc-est4-math-report.md#joint-profiling-lemma)

**GPD with threshold and scale held.** Take sample `(0,1)`, hold `mu=0, sigma=1`, and fix the lower cut at zero if only the upper cut is estimated. Let the free shape be $t^4$ and the upper cut $e^t$. Then
$$
P([0,e^t])=1-(1+t^4e^t)^{-1/t^4}\sim t^{-3},
\qquad f(0)=1,\qquad f(1)\sim t^{-4}.
$$
The conditional sample likelihood is asymptotic to $t^2$ and diverges. At each fixed positive $x$, the conditional CDF is asymptotic to $\log(1+t^4x)/t$ and tends to zero, so the mass escapes every bounded interval. With the tight upper cut at 1, the conditional CDF tends to one at each fixed $0<x<1$, giving the threshold atom. Every cut on the widening path is finite and feasible. For the lower-estimated design the upper cut stays fixed. With a threshold tie the support starts at the tight lower cut, so lower loosening has no effect. Divergence from support shrinkage at a constant above the threshold requires the effective width to vanish. The all-threshold ray ties the held-scale height along the infinite-shape trace already identified in G2. [G2 fixed-window derivation](2026-11-27-trunc-misc-math-report.md#generalized-pareto)

**Pareto IV with scale and both shapes held.** Take sample `(0,1)`, hold `scale=1, shape1=1, shape2=1/2`, and set the lower cut to zero, held or estimated. Let the threshold approach zero from below. The raw factor at zero grows as a positive constant times the inverse square root of the threshold gap, while the factor at 1 and retention have positive limits. The likelihood diverges both at the tight upper cut 1 and at the looser upper cut 4. The limiting conditional laws put probabilities 1 and $3/4$ on $[0,1]$, respectively, by the survival $1/(1+\sqrt{x})$. A single scalar contact mechanism therefore yields different conditional laws on the joint branch. With the upper cut fixed, lower loosening can retain only a vanishing strip below the occupied threshold in this limit. Shrinking at the fixed upper cut yields a point mass, and the remote-threshold uniform competes on bounded windows. [P4 fixed-window derivation](2026-11-27-trunc-misc-math-report.md#pareto-iii-and-iv)

The source already grades the all-free GPD escape one-sided. Its path with positive shape growing and scale shrinking on the right ray has escaping mass, while the same coefficient path with finite tight upper cut has a threshold atom. The shape2-only Pareto IV mask also has a joint divergence witness with held lower power $p>0$. Put scale $s=e^{-T}$, threshold $m-s$, and tail shape $1/T$. The density at a minimum is asymptotic to $p e^T/(2T)$ and every positive gap $g$ contributes $pe^{-p}/(Tg)$. The raw product diverges and positive-mass conditioning only increases it. This covers the positive-width branch and is a candidate for a separate main attainment row. The source contains only named cases for that mask. ### Zero retained mass and zero occupied density

A null tight slice needs a support-side test and an inside-density test. For held triangular `(a,b,c)=(0,1,1)`, sample `(1)` and fixed upper cut 2, the tight nominal interval `[1,2]` has zero mass. The loose interval `[1-epsilon,2]` gives height $2/(2\varepsilon-\varepsilon^2)$. With the mode at zero, the same loosenings have mass $\varepsilon^2$ and occupied density zero. The held GPD with `(mu,sigma,xi)=(0,1,-1)` gives $1/\varepsilon$ on those loosenings. Changing it to `(0,1/2,-1/2)` gives mass $\varepsilon^2$ and zero density at 1. For the reflected geometry in Pareto IV, hold `min=0, scale=1, shape1=1, shape2=1`, use sample `(0)` and fixed lower cut −1. The tight interval `[-1,0]` is null, and upper expansion to $\varepsilon$ gives height $(1+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$. These examples distinguish nominal width, actual mass and sample density. [TASK-475 endpoint examples](2026-11-28-trunc-est4-math-report.md#corrections-and-scope) · [Independent exact checks](2026-11-28-mod-rev13b-data/validate.py)

On nonconstant data the support interval contains the two distinct sample extrema and therefore retains positive mass at the tight cuts, even with repeated extrema. The occupied endpoint factors still govern whether the height is positive finite, zero or singular. For nonstandard beta the shape at each occupied endpoint must equal one for a positive finite carrier. A sample with both zero and singular factors needs a separate convention if an extended likelihood is to be assigned. For Pareto IV, threshold contact needs `shape2=1` for a positive finite density. In fixed-support beta with one shape free, the corresponding log statistic must also be finite.
