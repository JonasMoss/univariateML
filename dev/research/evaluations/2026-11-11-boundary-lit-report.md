## Two proof details and the nearest-face correction

### Completing the BB full-cone proof

Suppose mean-fixed beta-binomial laws converge in Hellinger to Poisson with positive mean `mu`. Hellinger convergence implies convergence of every atom probability. If the integer sizes have a bounded subsequence, a further subsequence has a fixed finite size; its probabilities beyond that size are zero, contradicting the positive Poisson probabilities there. Thus `N→infinity`, `k→0`.

The exact ratio at zero is

$$
\frac{p(1)}{p(0)}=\frac{\mu}{1+\mu\{u(1-k)-k\}}.
$$

Its limit is `mu`, so `u(1-k)-k→0` and consequently `u→0`. This excludes all alternative parameter routes. In the cancellation regime the expansion in the note yields the half-plane. Outside it, the leading direction is one of the two quadratic directions. Subsequences settle any oscillation of the coefficient ratio. The Charlier identity gives

$$
\|C_2\sqrt g\|^2=2\mu^2,\qquad
\|C_3\sqrt g\|^2=6\mu^3,\qquad
\langle C_2\sqrt g,C_3\sqrt g\rangle=0,
$$

and hence the Gram determinant of `C2 sqrt(g), A2 sqrt(g)` is `4mu^5/3`. There is no further hidden cancellation. These are exact factorial-moment identities, not numerical observations.

### A nearest translated-Pareto member has mixed leakage

Hold the rate `alpha>0` fixed and consider the valid limit family on the log scale

$$
g_{\alpha,s}(y)=\alpha e^{-\alpha(y-s)}\mathbf1_{y\ge s},\qquad s\in\mathbb R.
$$

This is a specified subface of the Pareto limits, with endpoint free. Use the note's dominant-leakage Burr path, with `b=-k log(1/k)/alpha`, and write a candidate face endpoint as `s=b+kz/alpha`. Put `w(z)=1/(1+exp(-z))`. Its exact affinity with this face member is

$$
J_k(z)=k e^{kz/2}\int_{w(z)}^1
 w^{(1-k)/2-1}(1-w)^{k-1}\,dw.
$$

The outside mass is `sigma_k(z)=1-(1+exp(z))^(-k)`. Boundary-layer scaling `v=alpha(y-b)/k`, splitting off the exponential tail, gives uniformly for bounded `z`

$$
\frac{H^2(p_k,g_{\alpha,s})}{k}\longrightarrow
C(z)=\log(1+e^z)+\int_z^\infty
 \left\{\sqrt{w(v)}-1\right\}^2\,dv.
$$

For completeness, the inside integrand arises from
`p_k/g_(alpha,s)=exp(-kz) w(v)^(1+k)`; the tail factor differs from one by order `k` and contributes order `k²` to the squared integral. On a bounded `v` interval dominated convergence applies after division by `k`; for large positive `v`, `1-sqrt(w(v))=O(exp(-v))`, giving an integrable envelope. Leakage gives the first term of `C` exactly to first order.

Differentiating gives `C'(z)=2sqrt(w(z))-1`; its unique minimum is `z*=-log(3)`. This is also the limit of the exact nearest endpoints: differentiating the exact affinity gives

$$
J_k'(z)=kJ_k(z)/2-k\sqrt{e^z(1+e^z)^{-k-1}}.
$$

Strict log concavity of the Burr density implies that the affinity divided by `sqrt(p_k(s))` strictly decreases with `s`: represent that ratio as the integral over `x≥0` of `sqrt(p_k(s+x)/p_k(s)) exp(-alpha x/2) sqrt(alpha)`. Thus there is at most one stationary maximum. The derivative has opposite signs at any two fixed `z` values straddling `z*` for all sufficiently small `k`, by the preceding expansion. The affinity tends to zero at both infinite endpoints. Its unique maximizer therefore converges in `z` to `z*`.

At that nearest member,

$$
\sigma/k\to\log(4/3),\quad A/k\to\log(81/64),\quad
H^2/k\to\log(27/16),\quad
R\to\frac{\log(4/3)}{\log(27/16)}\simeq0.5498004644.
$$

For example, directly evaluating the exact affinity at `z*` gives ratios `0.5499134182`, `0.5498117640`, `0.5498015944` for `k=10^-3,10^-4,10^-5`. Those evaluations illustrate the proved limits; no numerical minimization or unchecked capped estimate was used. The example suffices to reject an unrestricted nearest-family assertion. It does not claim to compute the nearest member of the larger face with **both** endpoint and rate free. With endpoint fixed at zero, the note's matching-rate calculation does show dominant leakage. Those are different designated faces.
