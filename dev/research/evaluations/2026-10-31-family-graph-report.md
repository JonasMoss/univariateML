# Family graph: single-canvas overview

2026-10-31, TASK-124.3. This revision responds to the first atlas review: a long family-by-family list did not show the organisation at a glance. The previous report remains at `build/scratch/graph/report-v1.md`. The atlas and its useful detailed labels are retained below the overview, behind a native disclosure.

## Layout

The overview is a build-time, 1000 × 800 SVG. Every family has one short id label and a dossier link. Primary groups have soft coloured rounded regions, including families with no declared edges. The map displays 60 relation arrows by default; 104 limit paths are present in the SVG but hidden inline until the Limit checkbox is selected. They remain hidden without JavaScript. Limits do not influence the layout.

The layout is a discrete constrained graph optimisation written in `site/src/lib/graph.ts`, with no new dependencies. Thirteen groups initially occupy the first thirteen slots of a fixed 4 × 4 ordering. Each column then receives variable-height regions: minimum height is determined by the number of two-column label rows (24 units per row plus heading/margins), and the remaining height is distributed in proportion to family count plus three. Twenty complete, ordered pair-swap sweeps minimise the sum of Euclidean distances between group centres for the undirected relation edges. Empty slots can swap too; column overflows receive a prohibitive penalty. Strict improvements only are accepted, so ties retain the existing order. Within each region, sorted ids initialise two columns of label slots; twelve ordered pair-swap sweeps minimise the sum of relation-edge lengths between node positions. Same-group attraction is a hard regional constraint rather than a soft force. The final pass rounds coordinates and checks label separation, nudging colliding boxes down one slot or failing the build if the region cannot contain them. The current slot spacing separates the labels by construction; the browser independently checks all 98 label rectangles for pairwise intersections.

This method was chosen over unconstrained force placement or stress majorisation to guarantee separated labels and region membership without a delicate continuous hull/label solver. The cost is rectangular regions, unused cells and a weaker geometric interpretation: distance is a readability aid, not a metric of distributional similarity. No optimiser convergence or global optimality claim is made. Fixed sweep counts, fixed traversal order, integer coordinates and exact string comparison make the result reproducible. Arrow endpoints stop at label-box boundaries. Quadratic curves separate repeated source/target paths; same-column local arrows curve beside labels, and same-column inter-group arrows use the central gutter. Header masks keep group names readable. Relations retain their line patterns and transforms use a second stroke colour; complete coefficient maps, transforms and limit paths remain in SVG titles and the reusable neighbourhood list.

## Interaction and static content

Hover or keyboard focus dims non-neighbours. Clicking or pressing Enter on a map node selects it and opens the existing neighbourhood panel. Each node is an ordinary dossier link without JavaScript, and modified clicks retain normal link navigation. Search focuses the matching map label. Group selection dims other map groups and filters the detailed atlas. Native disclosure elements keep both the atlas and its limit sections accessible without JavaScript. The mobile map scales to the viewport; the atlas provides readable full labels on narrow screens.

The map and atlas are Astro-rendered named slots inside the Svelte controller. SVG strings are no longer serialized in island props. The controller receives only ids, names, groups and edge metadata; the neighbourhood uses that same graph data. No dossiers, fitters, bindings, generated density functions or family pages were changed.

## What the declarations show

The data has 26 special cases, 19 transformations, three truncations and 12 modifications. Modified-count families show repeated construction patterns around binomial, Poisson and negative-binomial parents. Normal transformations connect the normal, bounded and skewed/robust groups; gamma/Weibull and generalised-beta/Pareto relationships connect otherwise separate primary groups. Group membership is therefore an organisational guide, not a connected-component partition.

Beta is an isolated node in these declarations, despite its mathematical importance. Eighteen families have no incoming or outgoing declared family-to-family edge: `beta`, `cmb`, `cmp`, `dlaplace`, `dunif`, `fatigue`, `gpd`, `hyper`, `invgauss`, `nhyper`, `sgomp`, `skellam`, `slash`, `tlambda`, `triang`, `yulesimon`, `zeta`, `zipf`. This indicates metadata coverage rather than mathematical isolation. All remain inside their primary group regions; no edges were invented.

Across all kinds, `ht` has 14 incoming paths, `pois` 12, `unif` and `hnorm` 10 each, and `binom`, `norm`, `snorm` nine each. These are path counts, not unique-neighbour counts. The limit toggle exposes that different hub structure without letting those 104 paths determine the initial overview. Another 160 dossier limit records lack a catalog model and are not fabricated into family nodes.

- Second readability revision, final: `map-final-map-desktop.png`, `map-final-map-mobile.png`, `map-final-limits-desktop.png`, `map-final-limits-ht-desktop.png`, plus full-page `map-final-desktop.png` and `map-final-mobile.png`. Headers mask crossing arrows, count-family arrows use a gutter, and the unhighlighted limits capture explicitly parks the mouse outside the map. Opened and inspected the final full map, mobile map and unhighlighted limits view. The final ht-focused image is also retained as a separate interaction state.

On desktop, all 13 groups and all 98 labels are visible in one approximately 1028 × 823 rendered map. The common reference ids `norm`, `gamma`, `beta`, `binom`, `pois` are bold. The count/modification cluster sits on the left, normal/skewed transformations below it, and the beta/Pareto/gamma connections on the right. Beta visibly lacks arrows because the declarations lack them; it is not falsely turned into a hub. The initial picture is much more useful than the earlier 9,300-pixel atlas. Limits add a faint web across groups and a different concentration around ht, Poisson, uniform and half-normal; focus makes an individual neighbourhood easier to trace.

What still looks wrong: the count-family gutter bundles many arrows, some local arrowheads remain small, long diagonal transformations still cross regions, and rectangular areas retain uneven whitespace. Conditional paths between the same pair remain hard to distinguish even with offsets. The 390px map is an overview of shapes and groups, not a legible reading surface for every id: its type scales with the entire SVG. The native atlas below it is the accessible detailed view. This compromise is explicit rather than claiming that 98 labels are readable simultaneously on a phone. Group colours are similar in places; headings, labelled nodes, line patterns and focus provide redundant cues. The layout is stable for identical input, but adding a node can change the greedy assignment globally. It is not a taxonomy tree, a proof of relationships, or a statistical distance embedding.
