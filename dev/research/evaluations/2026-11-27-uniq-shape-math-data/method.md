## Proofs used across regimes

All measures below are Lebesgue measures on the open chart of ordered distinct levels with a fixed vector of positive integer counts. Write \(m\) for the number of levels and \(n\) for total count. A statement for \(m ≥  j\) in particular applies to almost every ordinary sample of size \(n ≥  j\). The dimension restriction matters at small \(n\). Positive, log, reciprocal, and fixed-power sample charts are related by smooth diffeomorphisms wherever used and preserve null sets. A held threshold is subtracted before taking logs. Additive data-only Jacobians are common to every compared chart and cancel from score differences.

### Comparison and incidence

**Proved.** Use the compact-envelope theorem of [TASK-412](../2026-11-27-uniq-generic-math-report.md#the-theorem). For each positive finite chart the log likelihood and its sample derivative are analytic on the common support. Countably many compact parameter pieces with local sample neighborhoods suffice. At a support contact, use its composed height on the level chart, with the endpoint recalculated as the level moves. A face contributes only if its height is approached by a feasible common parameter path. The theorem applies to face candidates with their nuisance parameters still free, so no regularity assumption on the maximizing nuisance branch is needed. The ordered chart keeps a fixed contact index and multiplicity. A max of two feasible face formulas can be dealt with by treating each formula separately on its feasible chart, including contact where they agree.

Here is a sufficient separation test that needs neither an explicit root bound nor an invertible parameter Hessian. Suppose the noncontact scores are definable in the real exponential field and the difference \(g_\theta-g_\eta\) is analytic and nonidentically zero on the common open support interval for every distinct candidate pair. Include one real coordinate per contact level omitted from the test unless that level is already determined by a candidate parameter. For each fixed pair and contact coordinates, the remaining level choices lie in a product of finite zero sets. Indeed an analytic difference has no interval of zeros and a definable subset of the line is a finite union of points and intervals. The incidence set therefore has dimension at most the dimension of the candidate pair and the extra coordinates. Its projection to \(m\) sample levels is null when \(m\) is larger. All sets in this incidence construction use only the data scores, so gamma and digamma factors in parameter-only normalizers are immaterial. The same proof applies when a fixed observation is excluded as a break in the chart by taking the finitely many resulting pieces. [Wilkie's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-96-00216-5)

The exceptional set has two parts. Remove the projected equal-score incidence set when using a dimension argument. On its complement a multiple finite winner occurs exactly when a compact two-piece comparison envelope reaches the global height and is nondifferentiable. The same condition describes coexistence with a reachable face when the pair separates. See the [exact exceptional-set test](../2026-11-27-uniq-generic-math-report.md#the-theorem). This is a description by comparisons, and outside the proved separation ran ≥ s the equal-score incidence may contain the entire sample chamber. At a nondegenerate two-branch contact with unequal gradients, the analytic height equality is a regular hypersurface. A flat fiber with distinct labels is impossible wherever all finite winners separate and the envelope is differentiable. Thus in each proved null-tie ran ≥  no ridge can occupy a positive-measure set of samples. This conclusion does not extend to smaller level counts by dropping repeated observations.

### Logistic scores

**Proved.** With \(y=\log x\), the GB2 score has the form
\[
g(y)=L-(L+R)q(a(y-b)),\qquad q(v)=\frac{e^v}{1+e^v},\quad L,R,a>0 .
\]
Here \(L=ap, R=aq\) for lower and upper beta shapes \(p,q\), and \(b\) is log scale. Its two limits identify \(L,R\), its centered level identifies \(b\), and its derivative there identifies \(a\). Clearing the two denominators gives a sum of exponentials with rates (0,a,a',a+a'). At most three zeros are possible, and at fixed common power there are at most two. This follows by induction, dividing by the first exponential and applying Rolle to the derivative. Burr sets \(p=1\), paralogistic sets \(p=1,q=a\), beta prime sets \(a=1\), and reciprocal laws reflect the axis and interchange the tail slopes. The transformations preserve entire maximizing sets. In the beta-prime single holds the same quadratic bound applies.

The smooth GB2 face scores in log units are affine for the normal, and constant plus one nonconstant exponential for each gamma orientation. The logistic score has distinct finite limits at both ends, so no identity with these scores is possible. With a single beta shape held, the surviving smooth face is the corresponding oriented gamma with that gamma shape held. Beta prime instead has a gamma and inverse gamma, with the shape fixed on a smooth face only if required by the held coefficient. An exponential cutoff has constant score apart from its moving contact. Thus it separates from a strictly decreasing logistic score at two noncontact levels. A gamma or Weibull face gives at most two intersections when its exponential rate has the same magnitude as the finite logistic power, and at most three otherwise. The count follows by multiplying by the one logistic denominator. For a normal face of GB2 the derivative of the logistic score is a negative single-trough function, so it meets any constant at most twice. Rolle bounds the original finite/normal comparison by three intersections. For a transformed gamma use the cleared exponential sum with at most four rates. For beta prime both gamma faces have the same power magnitude as the finite chart. Two opposite gamma scores coincide at at most two levels, by Rolle. The all-free large-power branch also includes asymmetric Laplace in log units. Its noncontact score is constant on either side of its join. A join inside an observation gap has an ordinary smooth height germ. At a join coinciding with a level, use the moving knot height and omit that level from the comparison. With four levels, at least two noncontacts lie on the same side of the join and separate it from a finite logistic fit. An interval of face modes on a balanced-slope gap is a face-only plateau. Face/face ties with cutoffs can use noncontact incidence; two fitted opposite cutoff means are equal precisely when the log mean is the midpoint of the extremes. This equation is proper on every stratum with an interior level.

For the asymmetric Laplace face at join \(b\), the mean log height in log units is \(\log(LR/(L+R))-L\overline{(b-y)_+}-R\overline{(y-b)_+}\). Compare all its feasible joins and slopes, allowing the two cutoff limits. The precise restrictions and other optimized values are the ones in the [beta-prime proof](../2026-11-25-betapr-math-report.md#compact-scalar-decision-for-all-free-data), [Burr proof](../2026-11-25-burr-math-report.md#inverse-and-shifted-burr), and [GB2 review](../2026-11-27-genbeta-atlas-math-report.md#gb2-exteriors-with-one-beta-shape-held). For GB2 all free the [joint compactification](../2026-11-27-lsheld-atlas-math-report.md#fpareto-held-min) applies after subtracting the held minimum. It has normal, two oriented transformed-gamma, asymmetric Laplace and two cutoff possibilities in log units. The normal is the lognormal in physical units. Enlarging a smooth face to its unconstrained analytic score family is valid for proving separation. The comparison heights must still use the actual face restrictions.

### Lomax scores

**Proved.** In physical excess units the Lomax score is (-(1+k)/(s+x)), with \(s,k>0\). Distinct such functions coincide at at most one point, since cross multiplication gives an affine difference. The exponential score is constant and has at most one intersection. Thus two levels suffice for both finite/finite and finite/face. The [exact scalar proof](../2026-11-25-lomax-uniq-math-report.md) gives pointwise uniqueness on two levels when attained. A fixed positive power transforms Burr at held power into Lomax, including its exponential face. A held threshold with positive excesses and any reciprocal transfer leave both conclusions valid. Constants have the face-only or divergence outcomes of the respective dossiers.

### Powered gamma scores

**Proved.** In the oriented log coordinate the score is \(kp-Ap e^{py}\), with \(A=k\exp(-p\mu)\) for Prentice and \(A=\exp(-p b)\) for Stacy. The signed Prentice parametrization is bijective for \(p ≠ 0\); Stacy uses \(p>0\) after orientation. The difference of distinct scores has at most two zeros by the exponential argument, and an affine normal score versus one exponential plus a constant has at most two by Rolle applied twice. A one-sided exponential face has constant noncontact score and therefore separates at two noncontact levels. These comparisons apply to every hold by restriction. At a fixed endpoint that is also an observed level, do the comparison on the lower-dimensional chart using the remaining distinct levels. A held-endpoint face has analytic height in the strict chart, even if its branch does not exist for other sign arrangements. Excluded tails add no finite parameter points. Normal and cutoff faces can be compared by the same two-noncontact-level argument. The all-free two-level result for Prentice also excludes finite attainment in the two Stacy orientations. ### Other score charts

**Proved.** The following formulas give the score and the identity check needed by the incidence argument.

- Exponentiated Weibull in \(y=\log x\): \(g=b[1-u+(c-1)u/(e^u-1)]\), \(u=e^{b(y-μ)}\). At the right end the negative exponential identifies \(b,μ\); the left limit \(bc\) identifies \(c\). The smooth inverse-Weibull face in log units is a Gumbel score with bounded right limit, and the power face has constant noncontact score. Thus all-free finite/finite and finite/inverse-Weibull ties are null for \(m ≥ 7\) and \(m ≥ 6\), respectively. At held scale \(m ≥ 5\) suffices for finite/finite; the threshold face on its available branch strictly wins or is irrelevant, as specified in the held-scale proof.
- PGW in \(y=\log x\): \(g=b(1-q)+bcq[1-(1+v)^c]\), \(v=e^{b(y-μ)}, q=v/(1+v)\). The left limit is \(b\), the right log-growth slope is \(bc\), and the right leading coefficient determines \(μ\). Against the transformed Gompertz face, the face has double-exponential growth of the negative score at the right, ruling out identity. At held shape the face has one free location in this chart, so \(m ≥ 4\) suffices against it, and \(m ≥ 5\) for finite/finite. Held scale excludes this face, and its threshold trace is strictly winning on the nonattained branch. Finite/finite ties at held scale are null for \(m ≥ 5\).
- Fatigue: \(g=1/(t+β)-3/(2t)-(1/β-β/t^2)/(2α^2)\), \(t=x-μ\). The poles at \(μ\) and \(μ-β\) identify the threshold and scale and the double-pole coefficient gives shape. This proves separation in measure for \(m ≥ 5\) when two coefficients are free and \(m ≥ 3\) when one is free. With shape and threshold held, clearing the pole denominator \(t^2(t+β)(t+β')\) also gives a direct degree-four bound. With scale held and \(3k=n\) at a minimum of count \(k\), the boundary height in squared-likelihood units is \(W_0\propto\prod_{d_i>0}(d_i+β)^2/d_i^3\). Hence the noncontact log score of the profiled face is \(1/(d+β)-3/(2d)\). Its poles differ from those of every strict finite threshold below the sample minimum. Dimension \(2+1=3\) suffices for the incidence since the extra parameter is the contact location itself.
- GEV and Fréchet in physical units: use \(t=x-ℓ>0\) for the Fréchet chart, with score \(-(a+1)/t+a s^a/t^{a+1}\). Its singularity, order and coefficient identify the parameters. The negative-shape GEV chart has \(t=E-x>0\) and score \(-(a-1)/t+a t^{a-1}/s^a\) for \(a>1\). The pole at \(E\), its residue and its power coefficient identify this chart. Different support charts have different singularities at the ends of their common interval. Gumbel is entire on the real line and has score \((e^{-(x-μ)/σ}-1)/σ\), which identifies both parameters. Reverse exponential is the only constant-score chart here. At its closed moving upper endpoint the score above is used only at noncontact levels. With \(a=1\), the best endpoint/scale is explicitly determined by the face formulas in the GEV report; allowing the rate and endpoint in the incidence costs at most two dimensions and the contact level is determined by the endpoint. At fixed public scale or location, there is at most one free reverse-exponential chart coordinate for that comparison, including the support-slack branch. On all-free nonconstant data the critical collapse stratum \(k=n/2\) has score (-2/(x-x_{min})) off the minimum, and the same form holds on the held-location critical stratum with pole at the hold. That trace is not identical to an interior score, by the stronger Fréchet pole or the different singularity/analytic continuation. The Fréchet Gumbel face uses the full Gumbel family with all free and is unavailable with public scale held on \(a ≥ 1\). The critical minimum-half stratum in all-free Fréchet does not attain; the GEV critical branches are as in their exact two-level and hold criteria. The dimension thresholds for generic finite uniqueness are \(m ≥ 7\) all free and \(m ≥ 5\) with one nuisance held.
- Genbeta in \(y=\log x\): \(g=pa-(q-1)a/(e^{a(B-y)}-1)\), \(B=\log b\), \(p=shape1,q=shape2,a=shape3\). If \(q ≠ 1\), the pole at \(B\) has residue \(q-1\), the left limit is \(pa\), and the first decaying correction identifies \(a\). For fixed \(B\), clearing denominators gives at most four distinct exponential rates and at most three score intersections. At \(q=1\) the labels share a rate \(pa\), and the log density is an exponential up to the upper endpoint. The two free-endpoint faces for \(q>1\) are the shifted gamma of fixed shape \(q\) on log distances below its endpoint, with score (ν-(q-1)/(B-y)), and the moving power cutoff with constant noncontact score. The former cannot equal the finite score as a function, by its algebraic left asymptotic. With \(q>1\) held, finite ties are null for \(m ≥ 7\); finite/shifted-gamma ties are null for \(m ≥ 6\); the power cutoff separates at two noncontacts. At fixed \(B\) the gamma face has fixed endpoint, free shape and rate, and the power face has moving endpoint at the sample maximum. The gamma score identity check is unchanged, and \(m ≥ 6\) separates by incidence. The constant finite score at unit shape can agree with the cutoff off its contact, but the fixed endpoint imposes a strictly lower unit-shape height than the moving cutoff for every nonconstant interior sample. The fixed-endpoint face law and actual contact traces are given in the cited atlas report.
- Feller–Pareto with scale \(s\), power \(c\), lower and upper beta shapes \(p,q\), location \(u\): the physical score is ([cp-1-c(p+q)h/(1+h)]/t), \(t=x-u, h=(t/s)^c\). At fixed \(u\) the logistic comparison applies after taking logarithms. With \(s,c,p\) held and \(cp>1\), a pole at \(u\) identifies the threshold, and the tail coefficient identifies \(q\); the incidence bound is \(m ≥ 5\). For \(cp=1\) the likelihood is decreasing in each excess and therefore the closed contact \(u=x_{min}\) is optimal for every \(q\). Then the log likelihood's \(q\)-dependent terms are \(-n\log B(p,q)-q\sum_i\log(1+h_i)\). The beta normalizer has strictly positive second derivative in \(q\) by the variance of (log(1-U)) under a nondegenerate beta law. This gives the unique contact fit on nonconstant samples; constants diverge as \(q → ∞\). For \(cp<1\) the endpoint spike gives an infinite supremum. The same reasoning for Pareto IV has beta lower shape one and upper shape free. Its normalization reduces to \(n\log q\). Fixed scale and power above one confine the threshold search to an interior interval (prior proof), and the score identity and \(m ≥ 5\) incidence bound apply.
- Shifted Gompertz: \(g=-b+bη e^{-bx}(1+(1+η-η e^{-bx})^{-1})\). Its right limit identifies \(b\) and for positive \(η\) the coefficient of \(e^{-bx}\) identifies \(η\) because (η(1+1/(1+η))) increases strictly. For \(η=0\) the score is constant. The positive-shape score is strictly decreasing in \(x\), so a tie against the fitted exponential is null on every stratum with two variable levels. Finite/finite ties between positive shapes are null for \(m ≥ 5\). A fixed zero can be removed from the comparison and the dimension bounds applied to the remaining positive levels. The exact profile comparison also covers zero observations.
