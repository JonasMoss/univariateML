# P0 generalized beta group: partial analysis (2026-10-29)

**Incomplete.** This is not an existence oracle for all hold subsets. `?`
means not proved or checked, not data-dependent. No claim is made that the
listed proper faces exhaust all joint paths. In particular the proposed R5
restrictions have not been shown sufficient. The multistart sweep is saved
and discussed below. Do not use these tables as a conformance specification.

Notation: `H` is the held set, `s` scale, `a`/`b`/`t` the shapes in their
README order, `m`/`M` the lower/upper threshold, and `∅` no holds. The
conditions in the positive-support families assume finite positive samples;
held thresholds must permit the observed sample. “Yes” means the adapter
implements the hold, not that its numerical global search is certified.
Nonconstant means at least two distinct observations. Error exits and silent
ignoring of arguments both count as not implementing a hold.

## invburr

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | Data-dependent; inverse Weibull and power faces; exact global sample condition unresolved. Constant samples: unbounded. | yes; face search numerical |
| s | Data-dependent: all x ≤ s gives power face, with equality penalty below; if any x > s a finite maximum exists. All x = s unbounded. | yes |
| a | Always for nonconstant samples; constant samples unbounded. | no |
| b | Data-dependent: apply the exact Lomax criterion to y=x^(-b); otherwise inverse Weibull with shape b held. | no |
| a+b | Always, including constant data. | no |
| a+s | Always unless every x=s, when unbounded. | no |
| b+s | Always, including constant data. | no |

For burr, the Weibull face needs `a` and `s` free; the Pareto face
needs `a` and `b` free and has endpoint `s`. The reciprocal transports
these to inverse Weibull and power. Holding `s` inside or outside the data
range matters. For nonconstant data all x ≥ s, the held-scale supremum
is the endpoint-s Pareto log likelihood minus `k log 2`, where k counts
x = s. This follows from the held-scale README; the power counterpart is
its exact reciprocal. These are face statements, not a proof that other
faces never win in other hold subsets. See `dev/research/ml-behaviour.tex`,
paragraph “burr with the scale held at 1”.

With b fixed, Y=X^b is Lomax with shape a and scale s^b; the Jacobian is
parameter-independent. The b-fixed Weibull face is its exponential limit.
With a fixed, log X is a location-scale family with exponential tails, so
its likelihood is coercive on nonconstant samples. With s and a held,
the b→∞ log likelihood decreases linearly unless every x=s; with s and b
held the maximizer a=n/sum(log(1+(x/s)^b)) is explicit. These are analytic
arguments; reciprocal transformation proves the inverse cases.

## invparalogis

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | Always for nonconstant samples; constant samples unbounded. | yes, reciprocal implementation |
| s | Always, including constant positive samples. | yes |
| shape | Always for positive samples, by reciprocation. | no |

With shape fixed, write `z=(x/s)^shape`. The scale score is proportional
to `sum((shape+1)z/(1+z)-1)`, strictly decreasing in s and crossing zero.
With scale held the README establishes coercivity in shape. The free case
is a log-axis location-scale problem; with nonconstant observations the
log likelihood tends to minus infinity at the boundaries. The oracle's
seeded `paralogis n2` run agreed within 6.1e-11 in log likelihood for
`H=∅` and `H={s}`. For `H={shape}` the adapter returned a different shape,
with log likelihood 1.0553 above the constrained oracle: `...` can swallow
an unsupported hold, so successful return alone is not evidence of support.

## invtrgamma

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | Data-dependent for nonconstant samples: lognormal and Pareto faces. | yes; numerical face search |
| a, b, a+b | Always for nonconstant samples (the last also for constant samples). | yes |
| s | ?; a fixed-endpoint Pareto face can compete when all x ≥ s. | no |
| a+s | Always unless all x=s, when unbounded. | no |
| b+s | Always, including constant samples. | no |

`dev/research/evaluations/2026-10-29-trgamma-derivation.md` proves the profile
equations and the nondegenerate closure in the free location-scale model.
Lognormal requires a→∞, b→0, s adjusted, so neither shape nor scale can
be held. Power requires a→0, b→∞, a*b finite; s can be held. With s
held the fixed-endpoint power face is relevant only when all observations
lie at or below s; an observation exactly at s needs a separate endpoint
calculation. Holding either shape excludes both faces. Reciprocal mapping
reverses the power support and gives Pareto. The numerical search for the
unheld case does not certify the global sample criterion near the lognormal
face; a negative inward slope is not sufficient by itself.

For held s and b, the a-score is `sum(log((x/s)^b))-n*digamma(a)`;
it decreases from positive to negative infinity. For held s and a, the
b-log likelihood has second derivative `-n/b^2-sum(log(x/s)^2*(x/s)^b)`;
its endpoints are coercive unless all x=s. These proofs include constant
samples in their stated exceptional cases.

## gb2

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| b+s | Always for nonconstant samples; constant samples unbounded. | yes |
| a+b+s, t+b+s | Always, including constant samples. | partial; constant samples rejected |
| a+t | Always for nonconstant samples; constant samples unbounded. | yes |
| a+t+b | Always, including constant samples. | yes |
| a+t+s | Always unless all x=s, when unbounded. | partial; constant samples rejected |
| all other proper subsets | ? in general; nonconstant data can escape to constrained faces depending on H, and constant data may have unbounded likelihood. | yes, subject to numerical unresolved errors |

Here `a=shape1`, `b=shape2`, `t=shape3`. Transformed gamma requires a and s
free, inverse transformed gamma t and s free; lognormal requires a, b, t
free, potentially with s held; the double-Pareto face requires a, b, t
free, with s held or free. Pareto needs a,b free; power needs t,b free;
free s gives movable endpoints. These are candidates, not global conditions.
The held-scale face incurs `t*k*log(2)` for Pareto with t held, and
`a*k*log(2)` for power with a held, at k observations exactly at s (see
`gb2/mle.hpp`). Further mixed paths remain unverified.

Holding b and s turns the data into strictly interior beta observations,
with the beta log likelihood strictly concave in a,t; holding one beta
shape leaves a one-dimensional score with a unique root even for constant
data. Holding both beta shapes gives a log-axis location-scale family with
exponential tails: nonconstant samples give a coercive likelihood.
Holding its power b leaves a unique scale root for any sample. Holding s
instead makes the b-log likelihood strictly concave; it tends to minus
infinity at both ends unless all x=s, when it diverges as n log b.

## genbeta

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| s+t | Always for nonconstant samples; constant samples unbounded. | yes |
| s+t plus a or b | Always, including constant samples. | partial; constant samples rejected |
| s+a+b | Always for data strictly between 0 and s, including constant samples. | yes |
| s+b with b=1 | Always, including constant samples: attained power ridge. | partial; some constant samples rejected |
| s held, remaining shape holds | ?; gamma and power faces may compete when a and t are free. | partial; some constant samples rejected |
| s free, shape2 free or held below 1 | Never: endpoint singularity, even if other shapes held. | no |
| s free, shape2 held = 1 | Always for nonconstant data: power with endpoint at max(x), if any other shape is free; also for constant data if both other shapes are held. Constant data with a free product are unbounded. | no |
| s free, shape2 held > 1 | ?; possible other escaping paths and endpoint attainment need proof. | no |

The upper-endpoint singularity exponent is `shape2-1`. The README's
gamma face needs shape1 and shape3 free; shape2 may be held, in which case
the gamma shape is fixed. The power face also needs shape1 and shape3 free;
holding shape2 fixes its endpoint at s. A free shape2 may move that
endpoint. See the README for its correction to the gamma-face slope.

With s and t held, `(x/s)^t` is a beta observation, giving the beta
existence results above. With s,a,b held, the log likelihood as a function
of t tends to minus infinity both as t→0 (like `n*b*log(t)`) and as t→∞
(like `a*t*sum(log(x/s))`), so a maximum exists even for constant data.

## pareto2

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| m | Data-dependent: shifted Lomax, exact profile criterion in `lomax/README.md`; otherwise shifted exponential. | yes |
| m+shape | Always for data strictly above m; the scale score has one root. | no |
| m+s | Always for data strictly above m; shape has an explicit maximizer. | no |
| m free and shape, s both free | Never: collapse with shape below k/(n-k), s→0. | no |
| m free, s held, shape free | Always for nonconstant samples, using m=min(x); constant samples unbounded. | no |
| m free, shape held, s free | Data-dependent: for k tied minima, a finite maximum exists iff shape > k/(n-k); below it collapse diverges, at equality s→0 is an unattained finite supremum. | no |
| m free, shape and s held | Always, at m=min(x), reference endpoint included. | no |

For fixed shape and scale, the likelihood increases strictly with min, so
the optimum is the sample minimum, which `dpareto2` includes. Let
`E=shape*(n-k)-k`. After setting min to the minimum, the scale log-score
times s is `E-(shape+1)*sum_{y>0} s/(s+y)`, strictly decreasing from E to
`-n`. This proves the stated fixed-shape criterion and the equality case.
The package currently demands strictly interior data at held min.

## nsbeta

| H | Classification / conditions | Master |
|---|---|---|
| m+M | Always for nonconstant data strictly inside bounds; constant data unbounded. | yes |
| m+M plus one shape | Always, including constant data strictly inside the held bounds. | no |
| either bound free and its associated shape free or held below 1 | Never: endpoint singularity, subject to compatible held opposite bound. | no |
| both shapes held ≥ 1, either or both bounds free | Always for nonconstant data and compatible held bound, with reference endpoint equality allowed. | no |
| all other proper subsets | ?; compare gamma, exponential, uniform and other boundary candidates. | no |

With both bounds held and one beta shape held, the remaining shape score
is `n*(digamma(a+b)-digamma(a))+sum(log((x-m)/(M-m)))`, strictly decreasing
from positive to negative and with a unique root; swap endpoints for b.
For both shapes held at least 1 and nonconstant data, the likelihood is
continuous on the feasible endpoint closure, tends to zero as either bound
escapes, and remains finite as a bound meets an extreme. Thus a maximum is
attained, including the shape-1 endpoint case.

## Endpoint calculations and M2

For `m = min(x)-epsilon`, scale `epsilon`, k tied minima and the other
observations separated from the minimum, the likelihood behaves as
`epsilon^(c*(n-k)-k)`. Direct expansion gives:

| Family | collapse c | separate fixed-scale endpoint singularity |
|---|---|---|
| pareto2 | shape | none; density finite at m |
| pareto3 | shape | shape < 1 |
| pareto4 | shape1*shape2 | shape2 < 1 |
| fpareto | shape1*shape2 | shape2*shape3 < 1 |
| nsbeta | not established for all joint endpoint paths | shape1 < 1 at min; shape2 < 1 at max |
| genbeta | not established for all joint paths | shape2 < 1 at upper endpoint |

Thus `c ≥ 1` and k < n/2 rules out the *displayed* collapse path, but
does not by itself restore existence: pareto4 with shape1=2, shape2=0.6
has c=1.2 yet diverges as its fixed scale and min approach the sample
minimum; fpareto has analogous counterexamples. A proper restriction must
also control the singular endpoint exponent. These product restrictions
cannot be encoded as one independent per-parameter `fit_domain` without
extending the schema. Even after both mechanisms are removed, finite
unattained limit faces may remain; existence has not been proved.

Other proper faces remain after the endpoint bounds. Set `min=-R`,
`scale=R+mu`, and Pareto III `shape=R/sigma` for R→∞: the density converges
pointwise to the logistic(mu,sigma) density. The same path is present in
pareto4 with shape1=1, and fpareto with shape1=shape3=1. Every relevant
shape bound is met for large R. For nsbeta, `min=-R`, `max=R`, and
`shape1=shape2=R^2/(2*sigma^2)` gives the normal(0,sigma) face. For genbeta,
`shape1=a`, `shape2=R`, `shape3=1`, `scale=R*theta` gives the gamma(a,theta)
face. These follow by expanding log densities; they show additional
noncompact paths, not by themselves that a given sample has no finite MLE.
`limit-paths.R` and `limit-paths.csv` independently evaluate the convergence
using the reference densities. For the example there, the three logistic
log likelihoods are within 0.00112 of their common limit at R=1000; the
normal and gamma discrepancies are 0.000105 at R=100 and 0.00678 at
R=1000, respectively.

`paths.R` evaluates six explicit singular/collapse paths using the
reference densities, fixed sample 1:5 and epsilon 10^-1 through 10^-8.
All six log likelihoods increase over the final decade; the asymptotic
positive slopes prove divergence. This numerical demonstration is not a
proof of sufficiency of any bound. On Pareto II the shape is 0.2 and
`c(n-k)-k=-0.2`; the bound k<n/2 holds.

## Kinds, domains, and proposed metadata

The design's `threshold` classification describes the current intended
user-required endpoints, but those are genuine support endpoints in the
reference distributions; the `threshold`/`endpoint` distinction is a
policy choice rather than a mathematical distinction. All remaining
coefficients are regular in the design's sense. **R7 discrepancy:**
extraDistr's nsbeta reference accepts shape1=0 or shape2=0 in p/q/r and
returns a point mass at the corresponding bound; its README says both
shapes are strictly positive. `domains.R` reproduces this. Density-based
likelihood at such a degenerate member needs an explicit dominating-measure
decision before extending the fitter. Other coefficient domains need a
full reference audit. In particular,
`actuar::dpareto2(0,min=0,shape=1,scale=1)` and
`extraDistr::dnsbeta(c(0,1),shape1=1,shape2=1,min=0,max=1)` return 1,
whereas the current fitters require strictly interior data. Some reference
density calls return zero for zero genbeta shapes even though actuar
documents strictly positive shapes and its q/r functions return NaN; do not
infer a valid distribution from that density return alone.

Candidate YAML, deliberately limited to established facts; the restriction
entries are necessary bounds, not a claim of sufficiency. In each file
`params` entries below are fragments of the existing parameter definitions.

```yaml
# genbeta
params:
  - name: scale
    kind: threshold
  - name: shape2
    fit_domain:
      restriction: {mechanism: endpoint, while_free: [scale], reason: 'shape2 >= 1 prevents the fixed-shape upper-endpoint singularity'}
holds:
  - {held: [], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free upper endpoint is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}
  - {held: [shape1], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free upper endpoint is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}
  - {held: [shape3], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free upper endpoint is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}
  - {held: [shape1, shape3], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free upper endpoint is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}

# pareto3
params:
  - name: min
    kind: threshold
  - name: shape
    fit_domain:
      restriction: {mechanism: endpoint, while_free: [min], reason: 'shape >= 1 prevents the fixed-scale lower-endpoint singularity'}
holds:
  - {held: [], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free min is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}
  - {held: [scale], status: not-implemented, reason: 'free min is not implemented; unrestricted likelihood is unbounded'}

# pareto2
params:
  - {name: min, kind: threshold}
  - name: shape
    fit_domain:
      restriction: {mechanism: collapse, while_free: [min, scale], reason: 'shape >= 1 rules out the displayed scale-collapse divergence when minimum ties are below half'}
# pareto4, fpareto: also require joint-product constraints; schema unresolved
params:
  - {name: min, kind: threshold}
# nsbeta
params:
  - {name: min, kind: threshold}
  - {name: max, kind: threshold}
  - name: shape1
    fit_domain:
      restriction: {mechanism: endpoint, while_free: [min], reason: 'shape1 >= 1 prevents the lower-endpoint singularity'}
  - name: shape2
    fit_domain:
      restriction: {mechanism: endpoint, while_free: [max], reason: 'shape2 >= 1 prevents the upper-endpoint singularity'}
```

No non-regular kinds are proposed for burr, invburr, paralogis,
invparalogis, trgamma, invtrgamma or gb2. The full `holds` YAML remains
open: under R2 an unrestricted held value can itself cause nonexistence,
but the proposed schema has no condition on held values. The product
restrictions likewise need a schema for joint constraints. Bounds may need
to be adjusted after the unresolved noncompact paths are classified.

## Numerical evidence

The independent `oracle.R` uses the external reference log density, four
BFGS starts in transformed coordinates, and enumerates every proper hold
subset. It uses seed 791029, defaults at n=2, 3, 5, 20, 100, 1000,
paired ties, ties at and above half the sample, constant samples, all
README-named proper limit models, and for relevant families samples above,
below, across and at the held scale. The CSV files contain all 1,961
sample/hold results, including failed searches and master messages. Of 641
returned master fits that respected holds, all match the reference-density
optimizer within `1e-6*n` (maximum absolute difference per observation
`1.4e-7`); 86 returned while failing to honor a held value, and 1,234
stopped. These counts do not prove all stops correct, and four-start BFGS
is neither a global certificate nor a reliable divergence test. Its
transformed endpoint coordinates exclude equality, so it cannot check
attained boundary optima and can report large finite surrogates for
unbounded likelihoods. Some external reference densities lose accuracy
near extreme shapes. Explicit asymptotic paths address divergence separately.

Shao (2004), [*Notes on maximum likelihood estimation for the three-parameter
Burr XII distribution*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-9473(02)00367-5),
pp. 675–687, states that finite full Burr estimates need not exist and
that its profile can have multiple maxima; it does not establish the
new held-subset classifications above. Wang (2005), [*A Note on Estimation
in the Four-Parameter Beta Distribution*](https://doi.org/10.1081/SAC-200068514),
pp. 495–501, describes endpoint nonregularity; its abstract does not supply
a sufficiency result for the restrictions proposed here.
