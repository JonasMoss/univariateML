## Implementation and accuracy

- `mle.hpp:22` decides constant data by equality on the original observations. `mle.hpp:49` forms each held-shape score target from the original distances to the endpoints. For example, if the right distance $R$ is smaller than the left distance $L$, $\log y=-\log(1+R/L)$. Direct subtraction retains $R$, exactly when Sterbenz's lemma applies. Division and `log1p` preserve relative accuracy while $R/L$ is small and resolved. The single-shape score decreases strictly. The code accepts the public shape only when the original-data score separates the signs on either side within the requested tolerance, allowing for rounding and digamma evaluation error. It uses the closed form for a unit hold if it needs to replace the candidate. [Fitter and held-shape check](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/nsbeta/mle.hpp#L22)
- `physical_fit.hpp:21` checks diffuse joint candidates in an ordinary shape rectangle. Each score increases with the other shape by the positivity of trigamma. The two diagonal corners establish inward signs on all four faces. The continuous likelihood reaches a maximum on the closed rectangle, and the face signs put that maximum in its interior. Strict concavity then gives the unique global solution. This route preserves the existing diffuse fits on `c(1e-320, .5)` and `c(1e-300, .5)` in either order. The full binding probe agreed with independent score roots to $2.6\times10^{-15}$ relative error across those cases and precision modes. [Shape rectangle](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/nsbeta/physical_fit.hpp#L21) · [Binding probe results](2026-11-26-nsbeta-transform-data/binding-reference.csv)
- `physical_fit.hpp:38` uses a centered logit and a precision profile when the raw score signs cannot separate the faces. For an anchor $x_0$, its endpoint distances $L_0,R_0$, and $c=R_0/(L_0+R_0)$, the left relative deviation at logit offset $\eta$ is $(x-x_0)(1+c\operatorname{expm1}(-\eta))/L_0+c\operatorname{expm1}(-\eta)$. The complementary expression uses the opposite signed offset and $R_0$. The computation subtracts the original observations before normalizing. Thus the quadratic deviance term can retain dispersion even when the stored affine coordinates coincide. [Centered statistics and profile](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/nsbeta/physical_fit.hpp#L38)
- `physical_fit.hpp:137` checks a box in logit and log precision. At fixed mean, the ray score decreases strictly with precision. At fixed precision, it is concave in the mean because $2\psi'(z)+z\psi''(z)=2\sum_{k\ge0}k/(z+k)^3>0$. Hence the endpoint values bound it below and its center tangent bounds it above. At each logit edge a narrower ray bracket bounds the angular score because $z\psi'(z)$ decreases with $z$. The opposite angular signs enclose the unique profile optimum. Each log shape varies by at most the sum of the logit and log-precision radii. The configured box uses at most $0.375\,\mathrm{tol}$ in log-shape units and leaves room to retain a prior candidate within $0.5\,\mathrm{tol}$ of the checked solution. These enclosure implications hold in exact arithmetic. The computed signs use practical allowances for cancellation, digamma truncation and rounding, checked numerically in both build modes. [Profile enclosure](../../../univariateML-cpp/include/cppunivariate/distributions/nsbeta/physical_fit.hpp#L137)
- `mle.hpp:26` evaluates the affine log density at the public coefficients on the physical observations, including when all coefficients are held. It retains the previous likelihood when it agrees within the accuracy contract and reports the physical evaluation otherwise. The corrected endpoint sample returns a joint `logLik` of `2.7087862640283271` and a `shape2 = 1` fit with `shape1 = 1200959900632132.8`. The singleton outcome is justified by the fixed-endpoint uniqueness proof and the physical coefficient check. ## Open

- Finite endpoints whose difference overflows binary64 are rejected by the common binding's finite-width check. Public support for those inputs requires a binding change and cross-language extreme-width fixtures.
- The floating-point sign margins are practical bounds for the resolved cases. A uniform error bound covering subnormal intermediate scores and caller tolerances below the solver's numerical resolution would require further analysis of the libm and special-function kernels.
