# P0 counts: hold subsets, 2026-10-29

**Scope and evidence.** All listed hold subsets are covered below. Exact
existence criteria expressed as one-dimensional global profile comparisons
are mathematical characterizations, not certified numerical algorithms. The
finite multistart checks are numerical evidence and do not prove globality.

Notation: `∅` means all coefficients free; `A` means an attained MLE on
all *compatible* samples; `D` means data-dependent; `?` means unresolved.
Counts are nonempty nonnegative integers (positive for truncated laws).
A held value is compatible when at least one parameter setting assigns
positive probability to every observation. Incompatible holds (e.g. `p0=0`
with zeros, `p0=1` with positives, or `prob=1` with a count above the
support point) yield likelihood identically zero and are reported separately;
calling every parameter a maximizer of a zero likelihood is not a useful fit.
Master status: Y implemented, N rejected as unused argument, P implemented
but exceptions/errors or numerical limitations mean agreement is incomplete.
“Profile criterion” below is a definition in terms of the *global* likelihood,
not a claimed closed-form test. Ties of a limit with a finite member count as
attainment; the existing 1e-8 n limit tolerance can stop on a finite MLE.

## nbinom

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | positive mean: finite iff population variance > mean; otherwise `mlpois`; all zeros attained at `prob=1` or reference `size=0` | P: zero-size reference domain missing; finite cases checked |
| size | A | for finite positive size, `prob=size/(size+mean)`; size 0 is a point mass at 0 | P: rejects reference size 0 |
| prob | A | 0<prob<1: strictly concave log likelihood in size, unique root for any positive observation; all-zero maximum size 0; at prob=1 only zeros compatible | N |

Aragón, Eberly and Eberly, *Statistics & Probability Letters* 15 (1992),
375–379, [doi:10.1016/0167-7152(92)90157-Z](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-7152(92)90157-Z),
prove existence and uniqueness of the finite two-parameter ML equations iff
population variance exceeds mean. For held prob the second size derivative is
`-sum_i sum_{j=0}^{x_i-1} 1/(size+j)^2`; its score decreases from +infinity
to `n log(prob)<0` if any count is positive. For all zeros the score is
negative, and stats accepts size=0. Thus no infinite-size Poisson escape is
available with prob held strictly below 1.

## zip

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | A | `pi=0` Poisson face if zeros insufficient; all zeros point mass | P: reference lambda=0 gives NaNs, see R7 |
| lambda | A | for held lambda>0, optimize pi by clipping `(z-exp(-lambda))/(1-exp(-lambda))` to [0,1]; lambda=0 is undefined in the reference | N |
| pi | D | if positives and pi<1, a finite lambda maximizes; if all zeros and pi<1, lambda→0 is unattained; pi=1 attains for all-zero data | N |

Here `z` is the empirical zero fraction. The reference `extraDistr::dzip`
returns NaN at lambda=0 even for zero observations. The all-zero fit can use
`pi=1, lambda>0`, but lambda=0 held is not a valid reference evaluation.
For pi held at 1 a positive sample is incompatible. With pi<1 and all
zeros the supremum is the zero point mass (`mlpois`), approached as
lambda→0 but not evaluated by the reference at that endpoint.

## zinb

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | positive sample: compare global finite supremum with `mlzip` face; a strict winning ZIP face escapes, tied finite max attains; all zeros attained | P: numerical tolerance and size=0 |
| size | A | positive finite size: profile probability and inflation; size=0 is zero point mass | P: rejects size=0 |
| prob | A | 0<prob<1 and positives: size cannot escape to infinity at fixed prob; optimize size and pi; all zeros attained at size=0 or pi=1 | N |
| pi | D | if pi<1 compare global finite optimum with ZIP at the *held pi*; pi=1 only zeros compatible | N |
| size, prob | A | optimize pi by clipping; size=0 only compatible with all zeros | N |
| size, pi | A | positive finite size and pi<1: optimize prob; all zeros attained at prob=1 | N |
| prob, pi | A | 0<prob<1, pi<1, positives: size has a finite optimum; all zeros size=0 | N |

The `mlzip` face in a held-pi problem must itself hold pi; current `mlzip`
does not implement that hold. For positives, let Q(r) be the global
maximum over prob and any free pi at held size r>0; let Q∞ be the corresponding globally maximized ZIP face,
with pi held when applicable. An MLE exists iff **some finite r has
Q(r) ≥ Q∞**; otherwise the supremum is Q∞. Compactification proves this
criterion, including equality: a finite point at least as high as the face
forces an attained global maximum. This is an exact one-dimensional profile
condition, but locating all its peaks requires a global search. Free size
at held prob<1 cannot produce a finite-mean Poisson because its mean tends to infinity instead.

## ztnbinom

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | compare global finite likelihood (including size=0 log series) to `mlztpois` face; all ones attained at prob=1 | P: tie tolerance and uncertified global search |
| size | A | finite nonnegative size: unique conditional mean fit; size=0 is logarithmic | Y, numerically agrees |
| prob | A | for held 0<prob<1 positive sample: likelihood vanishes as size→infinity, while size=0 is attained; prob=1 only ones compatible | N |

For positive conditional sample mean m>1, define Q(r) as the positive
log likelihood at its conditional-mean solution in prob, for each r≥0;
r=0 is the logarithmic-series member. Let Q∞ be its `mlztpois` log
likelihood. A finite maximum exists iff **some finite r≥0 has Q(r)≥Q∞**;
otherwise the supremum is Q∞. At m=1, prob=1 attains the point mass for
every finite size. Monotonicity of the conditional mean in prob follows
from the variance of this exponential family; the solution is unique.
The same criterion applies to zmnbinom after dropping its binomial factor.

For modified families, likelihood factors into `p0^n0 (1-p0)^n+`
times the positive conditional likelihood. A held p0 equal to z is the
binomial optimum, **not** a degeneracy. If p0=0 with positive observations
and no zeros, the positive model remains identifiable. If p0=0 with zeros,
or p0=1 with positives, the observed likelihood is identically zero. If
there are no positives and p0>0, base parameters are unidentified.

## lgser

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | all ones: theta→0, point mass at 1 (`mlztpois` at lambda=0), unattained by extraDistr; otherwise unique finite root | Y, agrees |

The distribution is an exponential family with natural parameter log(theta).
Its mean increases continuously from 1 to infinity, so the score has a
unique zero iff the sample mean exceeds 1. `actuar::dlogarithmic` includes
the zero endpoint; `extraDistr::dlgser` returns NaN there. These are different
reference domains despite representing the same interior law.

## pig

| Held | Class | Condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D | positive mean: finite iff population variance > mean; otherwise `mlpois`; all zero attained at actuar shape=0 | P: rejects shape=0 and says all-zero has no MLE |
| mean | D | finite positive held mean: finite shape exists iff some finite shape likelihood is ≥ Poisson at that mean; all-zero attained shape=0. Held mean=Inf is the Poisson–Lévy family and always attains, including shape=0 for all zeros | P: all-zero disagreement; rejects mean=Inf; global search numerical |
| shape | D | finite positive held shape: positive sample has finite mean optimum; all-zero tends to mean=0 (not accepted), hence D for this hold; held shape=0 only zeros compatible and any positive mean maximizes | P: zero shape rejected; positive cases checked with reference caveat |

For a finite positive held shape, the all-zero exception is an
unattained limit. For the mean profile at fixed positive shape, the Lévy face
at mean infinity has a positive inward derivative, and for positive samples
the likelihood tends to zero as mean tends to zero. The two-free dispersion
criterion and the derivative argument are proved in
[prior PIG analysis](2026-10-29-pig/analysis.md). For finite held mean and
positive counts, continuity plus the shape-zero
limit of zero likelihood gives the exact criterion: some finite shape with
likelihood ≥ the Poisson face at that held mean. The held-mean profile can
have multiple peaks; no variance-only test is claimed. For held mean=Inf,
the reference is the proper Poisson–Lévy law: positive-sample likelihood
vanishes as shape approaches either zero or infinity; for all zeros shape=0
is an attained point mass. `actuar` can return grossly incorrect positive
log probabilities near the Poisson face; the multistart oracle using this reference is invalid
there. The three apparent PIG oracle wins in the first sweep are actuar
cancellation errors, confirmed using 120-digit half-integer Bessel values
in `pig-reference-probe.csv`.

## Kinds, restrictions, and reference domains

All coefficients in these 14 families are `regular`; agree with the design.
There are no R5 collapse, spike, branch, or endpoint restrictions listed for
these count families, and none is indicated by the above bounded discrete
likelihoods. Discrete likelihoods cannot diverge above 1. Nonexistence here
is an unattained supremum, not an R5 shape-restriction case.

Direct reference checks: `stats::dnbinom(size=0,prob=.5)` and
`extraDistr::dzinb(size=0,prob=.5)` define a zero point mass, despite their
README `(0,inf)` size; `actuar::dpoisinvgauss(mean=2,shape=0)` does too,
despite its README `(0,inf)` shape. The current fitters reject those holds.
`extraDistr::dzip(lambda=0)` gives NaNs despite README `[0,inf)`;
`actuar::dpoisinvgauss(mean=Inf,shape>0)` defines the proper Poisson–Lévy
law despite the README `(0,inf)` mean domain;
`extraDistr::dlgser(theta=0)` returns NaN, agreeing with its open README.
For actuar zero-truncated/modified NB, size=0 is the *log-series* law, not
the zero point mass. The `size` role is shape, not trials. These are tested
at stated points, not an exhaustive reference-domain audit.

## Evidence and limitations

`full-oracle.R` (seed 791030) evaluates the external reference log densities,
optimizes the free coefficients with three starts in bounded log/logit
coordinates, and explicitly tests each combination of attainable free
endpoints. Every subset is exercised on `simulate_default` at n=20, 100,
1000, tied data, n=2, 3, 5, and appropriate Poisson, zero-truncated Poisson,
ZIP, zero-modified Poisson, log-series, Poisson–Lévy, zero and one point-mass
samples. It also tests endpoint holds and empirical-zero-share holds on
selected samples. Every script runs from the repo root with `Rscript`.

The result `full-oracle.csv` contains 602 cases: 197 returned fits have a
finite oracle value and **all 197 agree within 1e-6 n**. Other rows record
unsupported holds, detected escapes, and incompatible endpoint holds. A
package stop is not a finite fit and cannot be compared by its logLik;
its message and the oracle value are retained in the CSV. The PIG oracle
uses the actuar reference and, to avoid its documented cancellation and
underflow, also the independent Bessel-K log-density formula. The earlier
three apparent PIG oracle wins were false: actuar returned positive log
likelihoods at extreme parameter values; 120-digit Bessel calculations in
`pig-reference-probe.R` and `pig-reference-probe.csv` resolve them. Its
high-precision check uses Python `mpmath`, present on simbox.

`domain.R` and `domain.csv` enumerate each coefficient's endpoint and nearby
invalid values. Infinite arguments that return all-zero or subprobability
vectors do **not** define a probability law; the PIG mean=Inf exception is
a proper law. `paths.R` and `paths.csv` trace the NB, ZINB, truncated and modified NB,
PIG and logarithmic-series faces, as well as held-pi ZIP and held-shape PIG
zero-point limits; these paths illustrate limits, not by themselves global
optimality. `boundaries.R` probes the attained PIG shape-zero point mass. No generic subset in these discrete families has an unbounded
likelihood: all probabilities are at most one.

**Limits of the evidence.** The profile criteria are exact but no proof of
unimodality or certified global-search implementation is claimed for the
ZINB, truncated-NB or held-mean PIG profiles. Three numerical starts on a
finite parameter box cannot certify those profiles or every held value. The
oracle tests selected, not all combinations of held endpoint values, and
R7 endpoint probing is empirical. The PIG reference itself is inaccurate at
extreme parameters, so its Bessel supplement is necessary. No own-dpqr
families occur in this group. Numerical closeness to a limiting face can
trigger the master fitter's 1e-8 n tie rule even when a finite MLE exists.
