# P0 location–scale existence analysis: work in progress

This is **incomplete**: it is not a complete M1 oracle or a basis for schema
migration. Every proper hold subset was swept numerically, but exact conditions,
held-value coverage and boundary validation are not complete. Statements marked proved below
are direct arguments or existing proofs, not conclusions from optimizer output.
`H` lists held coefficients; ∅ means none. Samples are finite, nonempty, and
held scales are positive unless stated otherwise. “Master” concerns the adapter, not a guarantee of convergence or agreement on every sample.

## Normal, logistic, Laplace, Gumbel

### norm

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| norm | ∅ | data-dependent: attained for nonconstant data; constant data collapse at the common value | yes |
| norm | mean | data-dependent: attained iff some observation differs from held mean; otherwise scale collapses | no |
| norm | sd | always: sample mean | no |

### logis

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| logis | ∅ | data-dependent: attained for nonconstant data; constant data collapse | yes |
| logis | location | data-dependent: attained unless all observations equal held location | no |
| logis | scale | always | no |

### laplace

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| laplace | ∅ | data-dependent: attained for nonconstant data; constant data collapse | yes |
| laplace | mu | data-dependent: attained unless all observations equal held mu | no |
| laplace | sigma | always: any sample median | no |

### gumbel

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| gumbel | ∅ | data-dependent: attained for nonconstant data; constant data collapse | yes |
| gumbel | mu | data-dependent: attained unless all observations equal held mu | no |
| gumbel | sigma | always | no |


**Proved:** normal and Laplace follow by profiling residual sums. Logistic and
Gumbel log densities tend to minus infinity at a scale boundary when at least
one residual is nonzero; at fixed scale their location likelihood is continuous
and tends to minus infinity at either location infinity. Joint nonconstant fits are coercive. The corresponding master fits have not yet been compared
against an independent oracle here; “yes” does not assert such agreement.

## Cauchy and slash

### cauchy

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| cauchy | ∅ | data-dependent: every multiplicity < n/2 suffices; > n/2 is unbounded; equality requires comparison, except two equally frequent distinct values attain a nonunique maximum | yes |
| cauchy | location | data-dependent: if k values equal the held location, k > n/2 gives unbounded likelihood, k < n/2 gives attainment; equality requires comparison with collapse | no |
| cauchy | scale | always | no |

### slash

| Family | H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|---|
| slash | ∅ | data-dependent: k > n/2 unbounded; k < n/2 attained; equality compare finite collapse value and global interior profile, as in README | yes |
| slash | mu | data-dependent: k > n/2 unbounded; k < n/2 attained; equality requires a boundary comparison | no |
| slash | sigma | always | yes |


**Proved:** both densities have Cauchy-order tails, so collapse at a value of
multiplicity k is proportional to scale^(n−2k). The slash README gives an exact variational equality criterion and its proof. Cauchy's README states
its exact free-location tie result, including the two-point exception. Fixed scale likelihood is continuous and vanishes at location infinity. At held
location and equality the limiting value need not be attained: for Cauchy, `x = c(0,1)`, held location 0 gives likelihood
`1/(pi^2 * (1 + scale^2))`. The master slash search selects collapse on a numerical tie even when a finite maximizer also attains it.

## Student families, GED, Tukey lambda, Huber, Bhattacharjee

The following tables enumerate every remaining proper hold subset. `D` means
data-dependent, `A` always under the stated sample conditions, and `?` means
the exact condition or a boundary argument remains unresolved. They concern the planned restrictions where stated; “yes” records adapter availability,
not universal agreement with the numerical oracle.

### lst

Let `k` denote the largest tie multiplicity if mu is free, otherwise the multiplicity at held mu, and let `d` be the held or limiting df. A collapse
with `k > d(n−k)` is unbounded; equality needs a global comparison.

| H | Class, condition and limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D: with restricted df>=1 and every k<n/2, compare finite fits to normal; equality/collapse ties need separate comparison | yes |
| mu | D: collapse at held mu as above; finite fit must also beat conditional normal face | no |
| sigma | D: normal face or finite df; for fixed sigma the density vanishes uniformly as df approaches 0 | no |
| df | D: strict collapse inequality gives attainment, reverse is unbounded; equality is a finite-boundary comparison | yes, partial |
| mu,sigma | D: normal face versus finite df; the df=0 boundary has zero likelihood | no |
| mu,df | D: strict collapse inequality gives attainment if k<n, reverse unbounded; equality compare boundary | no |
| sigma,df | A: location maximum attained for any finite nonempty sample | no |

### std

| H | Class, condition and limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D: compare finite fit with normal and ordinary location-scale t2; ties can cause collapse | yes, partial |
| mean | D: conditional normal and t2 faces; scale collapse depends on k at held mean | no |
| sd | D: normal face; nu→2 forces collapse and may diverge when k>2(n−k); equality needs comparison | no |
| nu | D: fixed-df collapse exponent nu(n−k)−k; positive gives attainment, negative unbounded, equality compare boundary | yes, partial |
| mean,sd | D: normal face versus finite nu; nu→2 collapse depends on ties at held mean | no |
| mean,nu | D: fixed-df collapse as above with k at held mean | no |
| sd,nu | A: fixed-scale location fit exists | no |

### lst and std arguments

**Proved:** for fixed degrees of freedom d, ordinary t scale collapse at a location with multiplicity k has likelihood proportional to
`sigma^(d*(n-k)-k)`. If the location is held, only its own multiplicity is relevant; a location outside the sample has k=0. Thus `df >= 1` is a
uniform free-location restriction when scale is also free, and is not needed
for collapse prevention with held location away from every observation. If location is held at an observation, the relevant inequality is
`df*(n-k) >= k`; equality requires an additional global comparison. If scale
is held, the scale-collapse path is unavailable. This does **not** by itself settle other shape boundaries or prove that all remaining fits exist.

**Proved:** `std` uses `nu > 2` as the natural variance-parametrization domain, not an R5 fit restriction. Its ordinary t scale is
`s = sd*sqrt((nu-2)/nu)`. As `nu -> 2+` with free `sd`, setting `sd = s*sqrt(nu/(nu-2))` gives the ordinary location-scale t with two degrees
of freedom, not a finite `std` coefficient vector. If `sd` is held, this path instead forces ordinary scale to zero: it is not the finite-scale t2
face, and ties at the held or free mean can matter. The normal face is reached
as `nu -> infinity`. Neither boundary automatically wins the global profile.
The master adapters support only ∅ and a held `df`/`nu`, respectively; their
numerical searches and tie guards are documented in the corresponding README.

### ged

With shape restricted to nu>=1, define `U` as the appropriate conditional uniform-face supremum and `I` as the greatest finite-shape likelihood; when
the relevant compactness argument applies, existence requires an attained finite fit with `I >= U`. Whether the criterion covers every equality and
held-value case has not been proved here.

| H | Class and condition under the restriction | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D: nonconstant data; compare finite fit with uniform limit | yes, partial |
| mean | D: compare conditional uniform limit; all data equal held mean gives collapse | no |
| sd | D: compare conditional uniform limit; fixed sd excludes scale collapse | no |
| nu | D: fixed shape >=1, nonconstant data attain; constant data collapse | no |
| mean,sd | D: conditional uniform limit versus finite shape; boundary details unresolved | no |
| mean,nu | D: fixed shape >=1, attain unless all observations equal held mean | no |
| sd,nu | A: fixed scale and shape give attained location fit | no |


**Proved:** with location and scale free, unrestricted shape approaching zero
has unbounded likelihood by the profiled argument in `notes/ml-behaviour.tex`, which cites Tumlinson (2015), *Statistics & Probability Letters* 107,
111–114, [doi:10.1016/j.spl.2015.08.009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167715215002886), Theorem 1. Its exact statement here is the
formula `ell/n = -log((n-k)/n)/nu + 0.5*log(nu) + O(1)` for k observations at the fitted location. The README's `nu >= 1` is a fitting restriction;
the reference density permits positive `nu < 1`. The master adapter has no
hold parameters. The restriction is **not** limited to free `sd`: for fixed sd and k
observations equal the location, Stirling's formula applied to the README density gives

`ell(nu) = [n*A - (n-k)*B]/nu + O(log(1/nu))`,

where `A = 3*log(3)/2` and `B = 3*sqrt(3)/e`. Thus for `k/n > 1-A/B = 0.1379185`, the likelihood diverges as nu→0 even with sd held. A
free mean can coincide with one observation, so this occurs on every distinct
sample of sizes 2 through 7. It also applies with mean held at a sufficiently
frequent observation. At equality the next-order terms need analysis. This
corrects a too-narrow `while_free: [mean, sd]` condition from the first pass.
The uniform-face comparison and a weakest restriction for each held subset remain open.

### tlambda

The rows refer to the principal branch [-1,1]. Let k be the multiplicity at held mu, or the largest tie when mu is free. For lambda<0 the collapse
exponent is `(n−k)/abs(lambda)−k`. At lambda=1 the uniform member is attained; feasibility of held positive-lambda support is required.

| H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | A for every tie strictly below n/2 (proof in README); other ties require boundary comparison | yes, partial |
| mu | D: k<n/2 gives attainment, k>n/2 unbounded; k=n/2 compare collapse with interior | no |
| sigma | A: fixed positive scale; shape belongs to the compact principal branch | no |
| lambda | D: negative exponent unbounded; positive excludes collapse; equality compare boundary; support must contain sample | yes, partial |
| mu,sigma | A: shape belongs to the compact principal branch; lambda=0 has full support | no |
| mu,lambda | D: collapse at held mu governed by exponent; equality requires comparison | no |
| sigma,lambda | A if some location gives positive likelihood; for 0<lambda<1 this requires range < 2*sigma/lambda, and for lambda=1 range <= 2*sigma | no |


**Proved:** for negative lambda, the tail exponent is `c = -1/lambda`; the collapse likelihood is `sigma^(c*(n-k)-k)`. With mu held, use only the
multiplicity at that held value; in particular, no observation at mu means this collapse is suppressed. Thus the lower bound `lambda >= -1` need not
apply merely because shape is free; the scale-collapse path also requires
free scale, and ties at held mu need their own inequality and equality check. This does not rule out other boundary mechanisms. With sigma held, boundedness of the density, compact shape [-1,1], and
vanishing likelihood as location escapes imply an attained maximum; at the
lambda=1 endpoint, the uniform density bounds every one-sided limiting value.
With mu and sigma held, compactness and the same upper semicontinuity apply.
With mu held and sigma free, k<n/2 uniformly suppresses the collapse, by the
README's bound; k>n/2 diverges at lambda=-1, and equality needs comparison.

The branch restriction
`lambda <= 1` avoids the discontinuous U-shaped branch and the unrestricted
large-lambda spike described in the README; whether it is required for every
held subset is not established here. Both branch endpoints are attained on the
chosen branch. The master accepts ∅ or lambda held (also lambda=2, a uniform alias), and rejects held mu or sigma. Its tie guard is sufficient, not
necessary. The owned density accepts all finite lambda.

### huber

Write `N` and `L` for the normal and Laplace face likelihoods with the same
held coefficients, and `I` for the global finite-epsilon maximum. When shape is free, a finite maximizer must attain at least the eligible face value;
strictly larger is sufficient. Exact equality and global numerical search require care. `L` is eligible only with sigma free.

| H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D: constant sample unbounded; otherwise compare I with N and L; two distinct observations select N | yes, partial |
| mu | D: collapse if all x=mu; otherwise compare I with conditional N and L | yes, partial |
| sigma | D: compare I with conditional N; L unavailable | yes, partial |
| epsilon | D: attained for nonconstant samples; constant sample collapses | yes, partial |
| mu,sigma | D: compare I with conditional N; L unavailable | yes, partial |
| mu,epsilon | D: attained unless all x=mu, when scale collapses | yes, partial |
| sigma,epsilon | A: location fit exists | yes |


**Proved:** free epsilon has a normal face as epsilon tends to infinity. With sigma free it also has a Laplace face as epsilon tends to zero with
`sigma/epsilon` fixed; held sigma excludes this Laplace face. Fixed epsilon
with nonconstant data has an attained fit (README); for constant data a free
scale collapses only when location can coincide with the data. Two distinct observations and all coefficients free have no MLE: the normal face wins
(strict proof in `2026-10-29-huber-free-epsilon-report.md`). The master accepts all seven proper hold subsets. A boundary comparison is numerical;
a face winning within tolerance does not prove nonattainment in exact arithmetic.

### bhatt

In the R7 domain `a>=0`, the normal face is an attained model member. `U` denotes the conditional sigma→0 uniform-face likelihood, and `I` the
best attained likelihood (including `a=0`). A uniform face tied by an attained candidate does not preclude an MLE.

| H | Class and condition | Master |
|---|---|---|
| ∅ | D: constant samples unbounded; otherwise compare attained I with U | yes, disagrees when normal wins |
| mu | D: all observations at held mu unbounded; otherwise compare I and conditional U; full boundary proof open | no |
| sigma | A: with sigma>0 held, location and a>=0 have an attained maximum | yes, disagrees when a=0 wins |
| a | D for a>0: range<=2a gives uniform escape, range>2a gives attainment; a=0 is normal | yes, rejects a=0 |
| mu,sigma | A: a>=0 has an attained maximum | no |
| mu,a | D for a>0: max distance from held mu <=a gives uniform escape, >a gives attainment; a=0 is normal unless all x=mu | no |
| sigma,a | A: location fit exists | yes, rejects a=0 |


**Proved:** the reference `extraDistr::dbhatt` accepts `a = 0` and gives the
normal density, although the README declares `(0, inf)` and the fitter treats
normal as an escape. Under R7 this is an attained natural boundary and the
normal face should be returned, subject to verifying the full reference domain.
The sigma-to-zero uniform face remains unattained when sigma is positive. With a held positive and mu free, range < 2a gives a uniform boundary; range
= 2a gives the endpoint-probability penalty described in the README; range
> 2a gives an attained finite-scale maximum. The adapter supports ∅, sigma,
a, and {sigma,a}; it does not accept held mu. Other subsets and effects of accepting a=0 are not classified here.

## Parameter kinds, domains and proposed metadata

All coefficients of these twelve families are `regular` in the design; no
reason has been established here to assign a non-regular kind. R7 differences
confirmed directly: lst `df` accepts positive values below 1; ged density accepts positive `nu` below 1; tlambda density accepts finite values outside
[-1,1]; bhatt reference accepts `a=0`. For std, `nu>2` is natural. `stats::dnorm` also accepts `sd=0`, returning `Inf` at the mean and zero
elsewhere, while the README says `sd>0`. Whether R7 regards this degenerate law as an attained member needs a measure/likelihood policy; the normal
table above assumes the positive-scale Lebesgue-density model. The full reference-domain audit is unfinished.

schema must support conditional held values, not just free-coefficient lists):

```yaml

  fit_domain:
    lambda:
      - {restriction: {mechanism: collapse, while_free: [mu, sigma], reason: "lambda >= -1; held mu needs its own multiplicity test"}}
```

The lower bounds above are sufficient policies, not proofs of weakest bounds for every subset; lambda needs two restrictions on one parameter,
which the proposed singular schema may not express. `std` nu > 2 is a natural domain, not `fit_domain`.

## Numerical probes completed

`2026-10-29-p0-locscale/paths.R` evaluates reference densities on explicit paths. On `x = c(0,1,2,4,7)`, decreasing scale from 0.1 to 1e-6 raises
log likelihood from −17.66 to −10.75 for lst with df=0.1, and from −23.96 to −13.91 for tlambda with lambda=−8. The unrestricted GED profile rises
from −7.87 at nu=0.1 to 196.80 at nu=0.005. These checks illustrate, rather
than replace, the proofs above. For std the t2-face log likelihood converges from −15.71397 at nu=2.1 to −15.60857 as nu approaches 2, matching the
ordinary t2 value −15.60857. The script also checks the domain examples.

### Independent sweep and master comparison

`oracle.R` uses multistart transformed-coordinate Nelder–Mead searches on stats/extraDistr log densities; `std` and `ged` use their README formulas,
and `tlambda` uses an independent vectorized quantile inversion. It saves samples and per-hold results in `samples-*.rds` and `oracle-*.csv`. For each
family it checks all proper hold subsets on seeded `simulate_default` samples at n=20, 100, 1000; n=2, 3, 5; a tied sample; normal, Laplace, Cauchy,
ordinary t2, logistic and uniform samples; and a sample entirely above the held location. Held coefficients use the README simulation defaults. These
finite searches are evidence, not proofs that all maxima or all limit faces
were found. Held values other than the defaults are not exhaustively checked.

Among package fits that return a finite answer, oracle likelihoods matched to `1e-6*n` in all cases except two Bhattacharjee cases. In those cases the
external density loses precision or returns nonfinite values in the tail; `bhatt-tail.R` independently evaluates the same convolution using stable
log normal-CDF differences, and both differences are below `3e-11`. Package errors and unsupported subsets are recorded verbatim in the CSVs; an error
was not automatically treated as a proved escape. The package's fixed-shape
and tie guards, numeric face tolerances, and unresolved boundary cases mean this sweep does not establish agreement for every possible sample.

`density-check.R` compares the owned density wrappers with independent README expressions, including quantile-generated Tukey points. The maximum
absolute log-density differences were `4e-14` (std), `5.5e-11` (ged), and `2.3e-14` (tlambda) on the checked grids. This is a numerical check, not a
global identity proof.

### M4: Huber free epsilon

`huber-m4.R` ran 100 independently seeded replicates per cell on simbox. The normal and Laplace faces were fitted independently; the interior was
optimized directly on `extraDistr::dhuber` with five starting shapes. Ten n=1000 cases initially lacked enough accuracy near the Laplace face;
`huber-refine.R` independently profiles location and scale on a shape grid and refines its local maxima. In all ten, its finite likelihood agrees with
the package within `5e-11`, and exceeds the Laplace face by more than `1e-8*n`. After this refinement the numerical classifications agree in all
1,200 replicates. Near ties are tolerance-dependent, and neither numerical search certifies global optimality.

| Sample | n | finite | normal escape | Laplace escape |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| normal | 20 | 40 | 60 | 0 |
| normal | 100 | 28 | 72 | 0 |
| normal | 1000 | 36 | 64 | 0 |
| Laplace | 20 | 76 | 23 | 1 |
| Laplace | 100 | 96 | 1 | 3 |
| Laplace | 1000 | 90 | 0 | 10 |
| t3 | 20 | 68 | 32 | 0 |
| t3 | 100 | 97 | 0 | 3 |
| t3 | 1000 | 97 | 0 | 3 |
| Cauchy | 20 | 96 | 1 | 3 |
| Cauchy | 100 | 90 | 0 | 10 |
| Cauchy | 1000 | 38 | 0 | 62 |

Free epsilon makes the default call stop in 60–72% of the simulated normal samples and 62% of the n=1000 Cauchy samples. That is a strong usability
argument against making it the unconditional default under the current D1 policy. The design's R3 forbids a held default, so changing this requires a
policy decision: keep R3 and accept frequent escapes, require an explicit choice, or change the escape/default rule. The study does not decide which.
