## 1. Binomial free size: no magnitude cap

### What was wrong

`binom_mle`'s free-size search bracketed the DeRiggi (1983) unimodal integer
profile and bisected it, but the bracket was a `long long` hard-capped at
`1,000,000`: any sample whose true free-size MLE exceeded that size failed
outright, regardless of how much of the iteration budget (`maxit`) was left.

A second, more serious problem surfaced once the cap was lifted: the
"increment" (forward difference of the profile log-likelihood) was computed
as `-m*log1p(1/z) + (z+1-m)*log1p(-m/(z+1)) - (z-m)*log1p(-m/z)`, scaled by
`n`. Each of the three terms is `O(z)` while their sum — the increment
itself — is `O(1/z^2)`; in `long double` this cancellation already loses the
correct sign by `z ~ 2-3e7` for realistic data. Checked against an `mpmath`
oracle at 60 decimal digits (`/tmp/binom_inc_mpf.py` during this session;
reproduced inline below): for a sample with 1 zero and 10,000 copies of
10,000, the true crossing is at size 33,335,000, but the old formula's long
double evaluation found a spurious sign change at 28,096,865 — 15% low, not
a rounding error at the last digit.

### The fix

Two changes to `cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/binom/mle.hpp`:

- **Stable variance.** `var_pop` is now the two-pass
  `mean((x-x̄)^2)`, not `sum(x^2)/n - x̄^2`: the naive one-pass form cancels
  catastrophically exactly when the dispersion gap `x̄ - var_pop` is small —
  which is exactly the regime that produces a large free-size MLE. Getting
  this sign right is what the `Underdispersed` → "use `mlpois`" escape
  depends on.
- **Cancellation-free increment.** The forward difference is now computed
  via the exact identity `g(y+1) - g(y) = log(y+1) + y*log1p(1/y)` for
  `g(w) = w*log(w)` (applied to `y = z - x̄` and `y = z`), which removes the
  first level of cancellation, and `phi(w) := w*log1p(1/w) - 1 = Σ_{j≥1}
  (-1)^j / ((j+1) w^j)` — a directly convergent series, not a subtraction of
  near-equal `O(1)` numbers — which removes the second. Verified against the
  `mpmath` oracle at every scale tested (up to size `1e15`); the search's
  bracket-and-bisect sign test now reproduces the oracle's exact integer
  crossing for the 33,335,000 example above.
- **The bracket itself** is now `long double`, grown by doubling with no
  magnitude cap (only `maxit` bounds it, and growth needs just
  `O(log2(size))` iterations — `~35` even for size `~5e9`); `long double`
  represents every integer up to `2^64` exactly and degrades gracefully, not
  silently (unlike doubling a capped `long long`, which would eventually
  overflow — undefined behaviour — had the cap simply been raised instead of
  removed).
- **`log_likelihood`**'s "regular" branch (used whenever `max(x)/size ≥
  1e-5`, i.e. outside the existing Poisson-scale branch for astronomically
  large sizes) now also uses `long double`, the stable `p = x̄/size` /
  `log1p(-p)` entropy form instead of the old
  `(size-x̄)*log(size-x̄) - size*log(size)` cancellation, and
  `std::lgamma` on `long double` arguments for the combinatorial term. This
  is accurate to the long-double ULP for `size/max(x)` up to `~1e5` (the
  Poisson branch's own domain starts there), verified against `mpmath`.

### Why the stopping rule is a certificate, not a heuristic

DeRiggi (1983) proves the profile log-likelihood in integer size, after
profiling `prob` at its conditional MLE `p = mean(x)/size`, is unimodal
whenever a finite MLE exists (`x̄ > var_pop`; the DeRiggi criterion, already
checked before this search starts). Unimodal means the forward difference
changes sign **at most once**, from positive to non-positive. Bracketing that
one sign change (grow until the difference is non-positive, then bisect) is
therefore exact, not an approximate search — provided the difference's sign
is itself computed correctly, which is what the cancellation-free rewrite
above establishes. There is consequently no sample for which this search
needs — or should have — a fixed magnitude limit; the budget is iterations,
exactly as `roadmap/2026-10-29-parameter-rules.md`'s R6 asks.

### Tests

`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/binom/test.cpp` (3 new
cases, appended) and `test.R` (1 new `test_that`, appended):

- Two samples engineered to have `x̄` just below `var_pop` (one 0, `v` copies
  of `v`, for `v = 1000, 5000`), giving true optimal sizes 333,500 and
  8,334,166 — both far past the old 1e6 cap, the second by 8×.
- A third sample (`v = 1e6`) with a true optimal size near `3.4e11`: checks
  the search terminates (doesn't hang) and beats the old 1e6-cap answer by
  over 5,988,000 in log-likelihood.
- A budget-vs-cap distinction test: `maxit = 2` fails cleanly
  (`IterationFailure`), the same sample succeeds with `maxit = 1000`.
- `test.R` reimplements the cancellation-free profile **independently**, in
  R double precision (not reusing any C++), including its own
  bracket-and-bisect search, and checks it against the fitter's answer. Near
  such a large optimum the profile is so flat (the increment itself is
  `O(1/size^2)`, its own rate of change `O(1/size^3)`) that this is a
  mathematically inherent limit, not a bug: for `v=1000` the two
  independent searches (C++ `long double`, R `double`) land on the exact
  same integer; for `v=5000` they differ by 181 out of 8,334,166 (0.002%,
  log-likelihoods agreeing to `7.7e-5` out of `-30887`). The test therefore
  checks the *region* and the attained likelihood tightly, and separately
  checks unambiguous, widely-separated alternative sizes (0.01×–100× the
  found size, and the old 1e6 cap) by large, cleanly resolvable margins.

No golden changed: `binom/golden.json`'s free-size fit lands at size 13,
nowhere near where the old cap or the old cancellation bug could matter.

## 2. zib / ztbinom / zmbinom free-size grids

### What was provable vs. what stays numerical

`notes/evaluations/2026-10-29-p0-binomial.md` already gives the right
existence criterion for these families: a finite free-size MLE exists iff
some integer size's profile likelihood (after exactly profiling the
remaining free coefficients — prob, and pi/p0 where free) reaches or beats
the limiting Poisson/ZIP/zero-modified-Poisson face. That comparison is
already implemented exactly (closed-form prob and pi, root-isolated by a
quadratic in the shared turning-point equation) and is a real certificate
for *that* question — it correctly detects the infinite-size escape.

What is **not** proved, in the P0 note or in the literature consulted, is
unimodality of the integer profile once truncation (ztbinom) or inflation
(zib) is in play. For plain `mlbinom`, DeRiggi's proof goes through the
untruncated binomial's exact combinatorics; profiling `prob` at each
candidate size by conditional-mean matching for the *truncated* law (what
`zero_detail::bin_prob` does) does not obviously inherit that argument — an
envelope-theorem expression for the profile's derivative can be written down
(digamma differences plus a `log(1-p)/(1-(1-p)^size)` correction term,
worked through in this session but not included here because it was not
carried to a proof), but whether it changes sign only once is open. The
existing code already acknowledged this with a comment ("global discovery is
checked against independent multistart density fits rather than proved");
this session did not close that gap.

### What changed

All three free-size exponential-grid-plus-golden-section searches (ztbinom's
one; zib's two, for held-`pi` and fully-free) had the same hardcoded
`for (int t = 0; t <= 24; ++t)` — a magnitude cap at `max(x) + e^24 ≈
max(x) + 2.6e10` — with the post-loop check `best.size >=
floor(max(x)+e^24)` flagging failure if the grid's last point won, i.e. an
implicit "did we hit the wall" test tied to that same fixed magnitude.

Replaced with: the grid always explores at least `t = 0..24` (a strict
superset of the old search — no existing case regresses, checked by running
every existing test unchanged), then keeps growing — governed only by
`maxit` (the iteration budget) and by 10 consecutive non-improving cells
(`patience`), neither a magnitude. The post-loop failure check now fires
when the budget (`maxit`) is exhausted while *still* improving, not when a
specific size is reached — "ran out of budget" rather than "too big",
exactly the distinction item 1 draws for `mlbinom`. This is a genuine
improvement (no sample-independent cap survives), but it is **not** a
certified search: a pathological sample could in principle have its true
integer maximum past where 10 consecutive exponential steps stop improving,
undetected. No such counterexample was found in the oracle testing below,
but none was proved not to exist either.

### Oracle tests added

`test.R` (1 new `test_that` each in zib, ztbinom, zmbinom) and `test.cpp`
(1 new `TEST_CASE` each in zib, ztbinom) cover the four cases requested —
`n=20`, `n=100`, a tied sample, and a near-Poisson sample (generated from a
large size, small prob) — cross-checked against the external reference
density (`extraDistr::dzib`, `actuar::dztbinom`/`dzmbinom`) via a brute-force
grid over candidate sizes with the remaining coefficients re-optimized
(1-D `optimize` for ztbinom/zmbinom's prob, 2-D `optim` for zib's prob and
pi) at each one. `zmbinom`'s free-size search delegates entirely to
`ztbinom_mle` on the positive subset (confirmed by reading
`zmbinom/mle.hpp`), so its test exercises that delegation rather than
separate logic.

READMEs (`zib`, `ztbinom`, `zmbinom`) gained a paragraph stating plainly that
the free-size-and-more search is numerical, not certified, naming the gap
(no known unimodality proof for the truncated/inflated profile) and pointing
at this report.

No goldens changed: all three use a free-size fit that lands at size 8 (well
within the old `t ≤ 24` exploration depth), confirmed by inspection before
touching any code.

## 3. GEV / Fréchet: the k = n/2 tie with free location

### The gap

With location and scale both free (and shape estimated too), a minimum
repeated `k` times out of `n` makes the shape collapse to `xi → 1`
(equivalently Fréchet's `lambda → 1`) a real concern. The existing code
(from TASK-85) correctly rejected `k > n/2` outright — the collapse there
genuinely diverges to infinity, "never" in the P0 extreme note's language —
but it rejected the boundary case `k = n/2` (possible only for even `n`) the
same way, as `InvalidData`, without ever comparing it against anything.

The held-mu analogue (only scale free) already did this correctly, via a
`collapse_face` flag added by TASK-85: at `k = n/2` the `xi → 1` face has a
finite but *unattained* supremum, `A_mu = -k - 2*Σ_{x_i>mu} log(x_i - mu)`,
and the code compares the best attained interior fit against it. The
all-free path had no analogous comparison.

### The derivation

`notes/evaluations/2026-10-29-p0-extreme.md` already states the free-location
analogue, `A_free = n*log(2) - n - 2*Σ_{x_i>m} log(x_i - m)` (`m = min(x)`),
without a full derivation. This session verified it numerically: a direct
2-D Nelder-Mead optimization of `gev_loglik` over `(mu, sigma)` at fixed
`xi = .9, .99, .999, ..., .999999`, for `x = (0, 0, 1, 3)` (`n=4, k=2=n/2`),
converges monotonically to `A_free = -3.424636` as `xi → 1` (gap `0.59` at
`xi=.9` down to `2.9e-5` at `xi=.999999`), and separately to `A_mu =
-4.197225` when `mu` is held at `m=0` — confirming both the formula and that
`A_free > A_mu`, as expected (more free parameters raise the attainable
supremum along the same degenerate path).

### The fix

`gev_mle_impl` (and Fréchet's structurally identical `frechet_gev_mle`,
which operates on the same GEV log-likelihood internally): the early
rejection now only fires for strict majority (`2k > n`); at exact equality
the ordinary shape-profile search runs, but with its scan's upper end nudged
to `1 - 1e-6` rather than `1` itself (mirroring the file's own existing
`-1+1e-6` nudge off the opposite boundary) — evaluating the search's
`gev_profile`/`gev_held_profile` Newton solve for `(mu,sigma)` *at* `xi=1`
with `k=n/2` has no root to find (the supremum is only approached, never
attained, exactly the held-xi row's "finite unattained collapse face"), so
it must not be asked to. After the search, the best attained candidate's
log-likelihood is compared to `A_free`; the interior fit is returned when
strictly higher, otherwise the fit stops with the existing `NoMLE` status
(same "scale collapse" message GEV's held-mu path already used — made more
generic since it no longer mentions "at held mu" specifically). Fréchet
gained a new `FrechetStatus::NoMLE` (it had no equivalent before) wired
through `frechet_mle_impl` and the adapter.

`k > n/2` strict majority is untouched; held-xi (any fixed value, k=n/2) is
untouched — holding xi removes the "elsewhere" the search could find a
better point, so it correctly stays "never" there, per the P0 table.

### An empirical finding: the interior candidate never won

The first version of the GEV/Fréchet tests below used a handcrafted `n=6`
sample and asserted the fit must succeed and beat `A_free`. It failed: the
sample was accidentally `k=2, n=6` (not `k=3=n/2`), and for that genuine
`k<n/2` case the fitter correctly returned an ordinary, unrelated `Ok` fit
whose log-likelihood had no reason to exceed `A_free` at all — a bug in the
test data, not in `mle.hpp`. Fixing the tie count exposed a real question:
once restricted to genuine `k=n/2` samples, does the interior search ever
actually win?

Across every handcrafted example tried for this report (arithmetic and
geometric progressions, extreme scale separations, near-clustered upper
points, `n` from 4 to 10) and 5,000 further random GEV samples (four
families: log-normal, exponential, near-clustered, both-tailed heavy — see
`/tmp/gev_face_probe*.cpp`, not committed) plus 3,000 random Fréchet samples,
**the interior candidate never once beat `A_free`**: every `k=n/2` sample
gave `NoMLE`, with the best interior point found converging to `A_free` as
`xi → 1` to within the search's own approach tolerance (gaps of order
`1e-5`–`1e-4`, consistent with evaluating right at the `1-1e-6` scan
boundary and nowhere else). This is consistent with, though short of a
proof of, `A_free` being the unconditional global supremum of the GEV
log-likelihood whenever the minimum's tie fraction is exactly `1/2` —
plausibly because that fraction is the exact threshold past which the
ordinary (`mu` or `sigma` held) collapse already diverges to infinity, so
the borderline case may generically dominate every other configuration. No
proof was attempted; the finding is reported as strong numerical evidence
only, and the implementation does not assume it — the comparison against
`A_free` genuinely runs and could in principle return `Ok` on a sample not
yet tried.

### Tests

New `TEST_CASE`s in `gev/test.cpp` and `frechet/test.cpp`: for several
genuine `k=n/2` samples, the test accepts either outcome the code can
produce — succeeding with a log-likelihood strictly above `A_free` (checked
with the same central-difference score gate CLAUDE.md asks for, not just a
logLik comparison, a genuine interior stationary point) or failing with
`NoMLE` — rather than asserting the one actually observed, per the finding
above. A strict-majority sample is
checked to still diverge outright (`InvalidData`, unaffected). READMEs for
both families gained a paragraph describing the new data-dependent behaviour
and a `limits:` entry.

No goldens changed: both `gev/golden.json` and `frechet/golden.json` have
`k=1` at `n=500`, nowhere near the `n/2` boundary.

  double-precision R-side log-likelihoods, which cannot resolve a true
  margin of `~1.5e-4` at that scale. Fixed by comparing against multiples

  regime, with margins from `~4` to `~0.004` — still far above double
  precision's noise floor there), not by loosening the tolerance or the
  fitter. No golden changed.

## Open work

- No unimodality proof (or disproof) for the truncated/inflated binomial
  profile (zib, ztbinom, zmbinom); the envelope-theorem derivative sketched
  above is a plausible starting point for a future attempt.
- The `patience=10` / `grid_floor=24` constants in the zib/ztbinom grid are
  a reasonable, tested choice, not a proof that 10 non-improving exponential
  steps is always enough; a pathological counterexample is conceivable but
  none was found.
- `binom_mle`'s free-size `double` return type cannot represent sizes beyond
  `2^53` exactly; for the search internals this is handled in `long double`,
  but the public `size` field loses exactness beyond that point. Not
  encountered by any realistic sample in testing (even the deliberately
  adversarial `v=1e6` case's true size, `~3.4e11`, is well within `2^53
  ≈ 9e15`).
