## Mathematics: proved identities versus numerical checks

For y_i=x_i-mu >= 0, each half density decreases in y_i. Thus increasing
mu up to min(x) increases likelihood: a free threshold is exactly min(x).
This proves the location result, including ties, for all finite nu/sigma.

Half-Cauchy with free scale has log-scale score
`S = -n + 2 sum_{y_i>0} y_i^2/(sigma^2+y_i^2)`.
It decreases strictly from n-2k to -n when there is a positive distance.
Therefore a unique positive-scale MLE exists iff 2k<n. For 2k=n the
scale-zero supremum is finite and unattained; for 2k>n it is unbounded.
A held positive scale removes that collapse, including constant samples.
These are analytic conclusions, checked independently with reference-density
optimization, rather than inferred from optimizer success.

For half-t at fixed nu, the log-scale score is
`-n + (nu+1) sum y_i^2/(nu*sigma^2+y_i^2)`; the collapse exponent is
`c=nu`, since `L ~ C sigma^{nu(n-k)-k}` at scale zero.
A scale root exists iff nu(n-k)>k. Equality gives a finite boundary limit;
the reverse inequality is unbounded. With free endpoint/free nu/free scale,
unrestricted nu can always enter the unbounded region. Restricting nu>=1
excludes it for 2k<n; 2k>n remains unbounded. At 2k=n compare the explicit
nu=1, scale-zero face `n log(2/pi)-2 sum_{y_i>0} log(y_i)` with interior
fits and the half-normal face. The latter uses the RMS scale (or held scale)
and is reported as an escape, never as a capped large-nu fit.

The identities, monotone scale equation and boundary limits above are
proved. Finding the global maximum over nu is numerical: the implementation
refines every sampled local maximum on its log-nu grid and compares the
exact limiting faces. Multistart reference-density optimization checks the
selected examples; it does not certify absence of arbitrarily narrow peaks.
