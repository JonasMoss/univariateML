## 1. General theory for sstd and sged

### 1.1 The Fernandez-Steel affine equivalence (used throughout)

For fixed `(nu, xi)`, write the two-piece density in mode/scale form
`g(x; c, a, b, nu) = (2/(a+b)) f_nu((x-c)/a)` for `x < c` and
`(2/(a+b)) f_nu((x-c)/b)` for `x >= c`, with `f_nu` the symmetric
unit-variance base (Student-t with df `nu`, or the generalized-error
density with shape `nu`), `a = s/xi`, `b = s*xi`. The code's
`sstd_sigma`/`sged_sigma` (`special/skewed_densities.hpp:42-63`) give the
affine map between `(c, s)` and `(mean, sd)`:

$$
\text{mean} = c + s\,\Delta(\nu,\xi), \qquad \text{sd} = s\,\Sigma(\nu,\xi),
\qquad \Sigma = \sigma_u,\ \ s\,\Delta/\Sigma = s\,\mu_u/\sigma_u,
$$

with $m_1(\nu) = E|Z|$ for the unit-variance base, $\mu_u = m_1(\xi - 1/\xi)$,
$\sigma_u^2 = (1-m_1^2)(\xi^2+\xi^{-2}) + 2m_1^2 - 1$. This map is linear and
invertible in $(c,s)\leftrightarrow(\text{mean},\text{sd})$ for every fixed
$(\nu,\xi)$ with $s>0$. **Consequence (used everywhere below): holding any
subset of `{mean, sd}` is literally holding the corresponding subset of
`{c, s}` (mode, scale) of the same two-piece density; nothing is lost by
reasoning in whichever coordinate is convenient.**

**Lemma 1 (boundedness of $\sigma_u$ for fixed `xi`).** $m_1(\nu)\in(0,1)$
for every proper $\nu$ (Cauchy-Schwarz: $E|Z|\le\sqrt{E Z^2}=1$, strict since
$Z$ is nondegenerate). For *fixed* `xi`, $\sigma_u^2=(1-m_1^2)(\xi^2+\xi^{-2}-2)+1\ge 1$
is therefore bounded on the whole `nu`-domain (checked numerically: sstd's
$m_1\to 0$ as $\nu\to2^+$, $\to\sqrt{2/\pi}$ as $\nu\to\infty$; sged's
$m_1\to\sqrt{1/2}$ at $\nu=1$, $\to\sqrt{3}/2$ as $\nu\to\infty$ — all in
$(0,1)$). **Proved.**

**Lemma 2 (unboundedness of $\sigma_u$ for fixed `nu`, varying `xi`).**
$\sigma_u^2\sim(1-m_1^2)\xi^2\to\infty$ as $\xi\to\infty$ (nu fixed), with
$a=s/\xi\to 0$ and $b=s\xi\to \text{sd}/\sqrt{1-m_1^2}=:t$ finite. This is
the *half-family limit*, not a density blow-up: $f(\text{mode})\to 2f_\nu(0)/b$,
finite. **Proved** (and it is exactly the already-stated "Translated
half-family faces under holds" formula in `2026-10-29-p0-skewreal.md`, with
$t=\text{sd}/\sqrt{1-m^2}$, $c=\text{mean}-\text{sign}\cdot m\cdot t$ when both
`mean` and `sd` are held).

**Lemma 3 (tie-collapse exponent, general `nu`).** If the mode is placed
exactly at a value with multiplicity $k$ in the data ($k\ge1$), $n-k$ points
elsewhere, and the two-piece scale $s\to0$ along any path with `xi` bounded
away from `0,inf`, then for sstd
$$
\log L(s) = \big(\nu(n-k)-k\big)\log s + O(1),\qquad s\to0^+,
$$
so the likelihood is **unbounded iff $k>\nu(n-k)$**, has an uncertified
boundary at equality, and $\to-\infty$ otherwise. Proof: the base t-density
has $f_\nu(0)$ finite (contributes $-k\log s$) and tail
$f_\nu(z)\sim C|z|^{-(\nu+1)}$, so a point at fixed distance contributes
$\sim s^{\nu}$ (contributes $+\nu(n-k)\log s$ per non-tied point); `xi`
affects only the $O(1)$ constant (worked out in full in the old
`notes/evaluations/2026-10-29-p0-skewreal.md`, "SSTD tie path"). **Proved.**
This is *exactly* the mechanism already in `sstd_mle_impl`'s own
`collapse_multiplicity` check and in the implemented `{mean,nu,xi}` hold
(`mle.hpp:668-677`); Lemma 3 shows it survives unchanged when `nu` is held at
any fixed value, mean free or held (the only requirement is that the mode can
be *placed* at the tie — automatic if `mean` is free; if `mean` is held, the
tie must be *at the held value*, since Lemma 1 forces the mode to converge to
`mean` as $s\to0$ regardless of `xi`).

**Lemma 3' (the worst case is `nu -> 2+`, giving `k > 2(n-k)`).** If `nu` is
also free, the optimizer can push $\nu\to2^+$ simultaneously with the tie
collapse; the exponent's infimum over $\nu>2$ is the limit $2(n-k)-k$, so
unboundedness is reachable **iff $k>2(n-k)$** (a strictly weaker, more easily
met condition than any fixed `nu>2`). Numerically confirmed on
`x=c(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,1,2)` ($k=7,n=9$: $7>2\cdot2$): the log-likelihood rises
monotonically along the path mode@0, `sd` shrinking ($-6.87\to-3.33\to0.38\to
3.85$) for `H={sd}` (nu free, `sd` held fixed — Lemma 3 at the held `nu=3`:
$7>3\cdot2=6$, already unbounded) **and** along the matching path for
`H={nu}` (sd shrinking, `nu` held at 3: same $7>6$) **and** even faster for
`H={xi}` (sd and nu both shrinking toward 2: $-4.12\to6.50\to17.0\to27.4$).
On the "safe" tie `x=c(0,0,1,2,...,7)` ($k=2,n=9$: $2<2\cdot7$), all the same
paths *decrease* ($28.5\to-42.8\to-53.5\to-80.5$ for `H={sd}`, etc.) — see
`skewreal-results.csv`, rows `id in {sstd}, sample in {heavy,safe}`.

**Lemma 4 (sd -> infinity is a *different*, finite boundary: the `nu->2`
two-piece-t2 face).** When **`sd` is free**, pushing $\nu\to2^+$ together
with $\text{sd}\to\infty$ (not $\to0$!) sends *every* standardized residual
$z_i\to\mu_u(2^+,\xi)=0$ simultaneously (since $m_1(2^+)=0$ regardless of
`xi`), which looks like a "k=n" collapse but the mode-height blow-up
($g_\nu(0)\sim C/\sqrt{\nu-2}$) is now paid for by the vanishing normalizing
factor $1/\text{sd}$: the limit is **finite**, equal to the ordinary MLE of
the (infinite-variance) two-piece-t-with-2-df on the *actual* data — the
already-documented `DfTwoBoundary` face. Numerically confirmed: on a
*generic, untied* n=20 sstd sample, profiling `sd` at each `nu` gives a
monotone, convergent sequence $\nu=3\to\mathrm{nll}=35.46$,
$\nu=2.01\to31.96$, $\nu=2.0000001\to31.939816$ — converging, not diverging
(script output above). **This face requires `sd` free; it is unreachable
when `sd` is held** (Lemma 1: $\sigma_u$ stays bounded for fixed `xi` as
`nu` varies, so `s=sd/sigma_u` cannot diverge when `sd` is pinned).

**Lemma 5 (sged has no polynomial-tie collapse, but a genuine `nu->0`
spike that Lemma 1 does *not* block).** For sged, away-points decay like
$\exp(-C/s^{\nu})\to0$ super-exponentially as $s\to0$ for any *fixed*
$\nu>0$, which beats any polynomial tie blow-up: **held at any fixed `nu`,
a sged tie never collapses the likelihood except the fully-constant sample**
(this is Lemma 1 + the standard GED tail argument, re-derived independently
here; no change from the plain-GED theory already in the repo).
**However**, when `nu` is *free* and tends to `0`, the *unit-variance* GED's
own normalizer $\lambda(\nu)=\sqrt{2^{-2/\nu}\Gamma(1/\nu)/\Gamma(3/\nu)}\to0$
superexponentially — *independent of `sd`* — so the mode height blows up even
with `sd`, `mean` (and `xi`) **held**. This is the mechanism behind the
already-documented "GED with `sd` held still spikes" finding
(`2026-10-29-p0-summary.md`, A3); **numerically reconfirmed here** for sged
directly (not just plain GED): with `mean=0, sd=3, xi=1` all held and an 18%
tie at 0 (`k=2,n=11`), log-lik rises from $-45.6$ (`nu=2`) to $+102.7$
(`nu=0.01`); with only 9% (`k=1,n=11`) it falls monotonically ($-43.6\to
-159.7$) — confirming the roadmap's $\approx13.79\%$-type threshold exists
and is `sd`-independent. **But the threshold is strongly `xi`-dependent**:
with the *same* tie fractions (even up to 44%) and `xi=3` held instead of 1,
log-lik falls monotonically in every configuration tried (`skewreal-results.csv`,
`id=sged_spike`, plus the follow-up xi=3 checks above) — the critical tie
fraction grows sharply as `xi` moves away from 1 (mechanism not fully
derived: the mode under `xi != 1` is a nu-dependent point
$\text{mean}-\text{sd}\cdot\mu_u/\sigma_u$ that also converges to the tie as
$\nu\to0$, so the *geometry* is the same, but the blow-up and decay rates
evidently combine differently as `xi` departs from 1). **Checked
numerically; exact threshold formula open.** Per rule A3 the existing
`nu >= 1` restriction carries **no exemption** for any held subset
(`sged/README.md`'s `exempt_when_held: []`); this finding is consistent with
(does not contradict) that policy and the restriction is sufficient to block
the spike outright (nu=1, Laplace, is an ordinary attained boundary member).

**Lemma 6 (half-family xi-escape, every hold subset).** Whenever `xi` is
free, $\xi\to\infty$ (or $0$) is reachable at *any* fixed or free `nu`,`sd`
(Lemma 2), giving a finite competing candidate: a translated half-t/half-GED
with scale $t=\text{sd}/\sqrt{1-m_1(\nu)^2}$ (forced if `sd` held, profiled
if free) and endpoint $c=\text{mean}-\text{sign}\cdot m_1\cdot t$ (forced if
`mean` held, else the free mode moves to the sample extreme automatically).
**Feasibility** (the candidate is a real competitor, not $-\infty$) requires
every observation to lie on the supported side of $c$. Already established
in `2026-10-29-p0-skewreal.md`; reused here unchanged. Numerically confirmed
(Experiment 3, `H={mean,sd,nu}`, i.e. `xi` free alone): a straddling sample
keeps the interior maximum (best at finite `xi`, no boundary flag); a
one-sided sample's log-likelihood keeps climbing as `xi` is pushed to 1000
($-126.8\to-28.5\to-26.2$, still rising) — matches "escape iff one-sided."

### 1.2 Reading the 12 subsets off Lemmas 1-6

| mechanism | needs free | blocked by held |
|---|---|---|
| sstd tie-collapse (Lemma 3/3') | `mean` or a tie at held mean; `sd` free (scale->0) | `sd` held (Lemma 1, fixed `xi`) |
| sstd `DfTwoBoundary` (Lemma 4) | `nu` **and** `sd` free | `sd` held |
| `SnormBoundary`/`UniformBoundary` (nu->inf) | `nu` free | — (compatible with `sd`,`mean` held) |
| half-family corner (xi->0/inf) | `xi` free | — (compatible with `sd`,`mean`,`nu` held; feasibility gated by held `mean`) |
| sged `nu->0` spike | `nu` free | nothing (A3, no exemption); numerically much harder to trigger for `xi` far from 1 |

Every one of the 24 subsets keeps at least one mechanism live (the richest,
`{nu,xi}` free with `mean,sd` held, keeps none of the "always-blocked"
mechanisms free, narrowed to the Snorm/Uniform/half-family triad). **None of
the 24 is `always` or `never` unconditionally** — each has a generic regime
(interior attains) and a non-generic regime (collapse or escape), which is
exactly the task's `data-dependent` bucket. This is itself a finding: the
Fernandez-Steel closure is rich enough that no proper subset of
`{mean,sd,nu,xi}` collapses the problem to a textbook one.

## 3. sged: classification of the 12 subsets

polynomial tie-collapse exists for any fixed `nu`** (Lemma 5, first half);
the only unbounded mechanism is the `nu->0` spike, live **whenever `nu` is

## 4. Implementable algorithms (sstd/sged)

For every `data-dependent` subset above, the implementation pattern is the
same four-step recipe already used by `sstd_mle_impl`/`sged_mle_free`:

1. **Interior search**: multistart BFGS on the free coordinates (reuse
   `SstdCost`/`SgedCost`/`finite_diff_cost`), holding the rest; starts
   seeded from quantile-based heuristics as in the existing free fit.
2. **Enumerate the reachable faces** per the table above (never more than
   3: e.g. `{Snorm, DfTwo}` for `{mean,xi}`-held sstd; just `{Uniform}` for
   `{sd,xi}`-held sged).
3. **Evaluate each face** at the subset's held values:
   - `nu->inf` (Snorm/Uniform): call the already-implemented `snorm_mle`
     (or the uniform MLE) with whichever of mean/sd are additionally held
     (snorm's own `{mean,sd}`-held row is *already proved* `always`/collapse
     in the existing table, so this call is always well-founded).
   - `xi->0/inf` (half-family): if `mean`,`sd` both free — reuse
     `sstd_detail::best_half_t` / the existing half-GED profile loop
     unchanged (mode and scale free). If one or both are held, the new
     piece: the forced-$(c,t)$ 0-dimensional case (`{mean,sd,·}` held, only
     need to check feasibility and evaluate once) or the 1-parameter
     "forced $c(t)$, `sd` free" case (`{mean,·}` or `{sd,·}` held) — a
     1-d scan over `t>0` of $c(t)=\text{mean}-\text{sign}\cdot m\cdot t$,
     evaluating the one-sided log-likelihood of the data at scale $t$,
     endpoint $c(t)$; unimodal in practice (not proved), scan+refine as the
     existing `half_t_profile` does.
   - `nu->2` (sstd `DfTwoBoundary`): only reachable when `sd` is free;
     reuse `sstd_detail::TwoPieceTCost` with `mean` substituted if held.
4. **Tie / collapse check** (Lemma 3/3'/5): compute $k_{\max}$ (free mean)
   or $k$ at the held mean; compare against the threshold using the
   held `nu` if fixed, else `2` (sstd) / rely on the `nu>=1` floor (sged);
   return `Unbounded`/`ScaleBoundary` before running steps 1-3 if triggered
   (as the existing code already does for the implemented subsets).
5. Compare interior vs faces with the existing `1e-8*n` tie tolerance;
   attained face wins ties (as `sstd_mle_impl` already does).

## 5. Proposed `holds:`/`limits:` YAML (text only, not applied)

## 6. bbinom, cmb, tbinom: the 11 free-size subsets

`notes/evaluations/2026-10-29-p0-binomial.md` already gives detailed (mostly
conjectured) existence criteria for exactly these subsets; this section adds
direct numerical corroboration (`binomial_checks.R`: profile-over-size up to
`N=4000` (bbinom), `1500` (cmb), `20000` (tbinom), compared against the
analytically computed limiting faces) and sharpens one subset to `always`.
A real bug was caught and fixed during this work: an unconstrained
`optim` search over bbinom's shapes can run `alpha,beta` to ~$10^{30}$–$10^{116}$,
where `extraDistr::dbbinom` itself underflows and spuriously reports a
log-likelihood of exactly 0 (consistent with the already-known
`KNOWN_ISSUES.md` accuracy limit around $10^{10}$); the fix bounds the
search to $\log(\text{shape})\in[-16,16]$.

### bbinom (held `{}`, `{alpha}`, `{beta}`; `size` free in all three)

| held | classification | condition / limit |
|---|---|---|
| `{}` | data-dependent | finite MLE iff some finite `(alpha,beta,size)` beats $B=\max$(best binomial-over-size face, best NB-over-r face incl. its Poisson limit); else escapes to that face |
| `{alpha}` | data-dependent | same, with $B=$ NB(size=`alpha_held`,·) maximum (closed form: $p=\text{alpha}/(\text{alpha}+\bar x)$) |
| `{beta}` | data-dependent | mirror of `{alpha}` |

**Numerical confirmation.** On an actual negative-binomial(size=2,prob=0.4)
sample (`negbinom_limit`, n=100), the profile for `{}` and `{alpha}` is
*still rising* at the grid cap `N=3726` (`grid_best_N == grid_last_N`,
inconclusive by construction — consistent with a genuine escape, matching
theory for data that truly looks NB). On every other tested sample (default
bbinom draws at n=20/100/1000, a binomial-looking draw, a tied n=7 sample,
a small n=5 sample) the profile peaks at a modest finite size (7-15) and
is **catastrophically worse** at `N=3998` (gaps of tens to thousands of
log-lik units) — `interior_wins` for all of these, confirming "always" is
the generic outcome and the escape is genuinely data-dependent, not a
blanket non-existence. Proof status: the comparison criterion itself is
**conjectured** (p0-binomial.md already flags: "requires a proof that the
listed faces exhaust all size -> infinity paths"; not closed here).

**Algorithm**: reuse the existing fixed-size `bbinom_mle(x, N, alpha, beta)`
kernel (already certified at each fixed `N`) inside an adaptive geometric
grid + bisection refinement over `N` (exactly what `binomial_checks.R`
does), compare the grid supremum against the closed-form `B`; a provably
sufficient stopping bound on the grid (rather than a fixed cap) is the open
implementation gap.

### cmb (held `{}`, `{prob}`, `{nu}`, `{prob,nu}`; `size` free in all four)

| held | classification | condition / limit |
|---|---|---|
| `{}` | data-dependent | compare interior vs the best empirical two-point law (point mass / adjacent / endpoint, per the already-stated CMB rule) |
| `{prob}` | data-dependent | same, with the two-point weights forced by the held `prob` under $\nu\to\pm\infty$ (not the unconstrained empirical split used in the `{}` row — the numerical face values reported here use the empirical split as a conservative stand-in; see caveat below) |
| `{nu}` | data-dependent | same shape, nu-specific scope |
| `{prob,nu}` | **always** | **proved** (see below) |

**`{prob,nu}` is always.** With *both* shape-like coordinates pinned, the
only remaining freedom is `size`. At fixed canonical `prob` the per-observation
mass of any bounded count decays geometrically in `size` (same mechanism as
the already-proved `binom`-with-`prob`-held fact: "the integer likelihood
tends to zero as size increases" — the `nu`-power-of-binomial-coefficient
factor only reweights polynomially, it cannot change the exponential decay
rate), so the likelihood is coercive as `size`$\to\infty$ and attains a
maximum at the smallest feasible `size` upward. **Numerically confirmed**:
every tested sample (`default_20/100`, `tied`, `adjacent`, `endpoint`) peaks
at a small size (2-18) and falls off a cliff at `N=1391` (gaps from $-0.6$
to $-45{,}000$) — no exceptions in 9 samples tested. This is the cleanest of
the 11 discrete subsets.

**Numerical results for the other three**, held empty/`prob`/`nu`: generic
cmb-shaped and even deliberately-adjacent/endpoint-concentrated samples (`n=50`,
built to sit on `{4,5}` or `{0,10}`) all show `interior_wins` by wide margins
up to `N=1391` — the size does **not** want to grow for these samples (an
adjacent/endpoint-style two-point concentration is explained by an *extreme
but finite* `nu` at a *modest* size, not by `size->infinity`; the `size->
infinity` question and the `nu->+-infinity`-at-fixed-size question are
different axes, and only the former is in scope here). One genuinely
**unresolved** case: the `default_100` sample (n=100, actually generated from
a genuine `cmb(10,0.3,1.5)`) shows the grid *still rising* at the `N=1391` cap
for held `{}`, `{prob}`, and `{nu}` — unlike every other sample. This may be
a real slow escape, or an artifact of the bounded 3-start inner `(prob,nu)`
optimizer losing accuracy as `cmb_ll`'s $O(N)$ sum grows; **not resolved
here**, flagged as an open question.

**Caveat on the `{prob}`/`{nu}` face formula**: the `two_point_face()` used
above is the *unconstrained* empirical two-point entropy, valid for the
`{}` row; for `{prob}`/`{nu}` held the true limiting weights are fixed by
the held value under the $\nu\to\pm\infty$ limit (as stated in
p0-binomial.md's CMB paragraph) and were not separately re-derived here —
the `interior_minus_face` numbers for those two rows should be read as
indicative, not a certified comparison.

### tbinom (held `{}`, `{a}`, `{b}`, `{a,b}`; `prob` and `size` free in all four)

| held | classification | condition / limit |
|---|---|---|
| `{}` | data-dependent | constant samples escape to the truncated-Poisson limit unless the constant sits at an attained point-mass edge; nonconstant: compare against the truncated-Poisson face (the "T rule" of p0-binomial.md) |
| `{a}` | data-dependent | same, with the lower truncation forced |
| `{b}` | data-dependent | same, with the upper truncation forced |
| `{a,b}` | data-dependent | same, both bounds forced |

**Numerical confirmation.** The regular samples (n=20/100/1000, drawn from
an actual tbinom) show a clean, well-separated finite optimum (`size`
8-13) far below the `N=18587` cap, with the likelihood falling steadily
(gaps of 0.7-45 log-lik units) — `always`-like behaviour for *typical*
data. The degenerate/thin samples are exactly where the escape threatens:
`small_5` (n=5) and the exact-tie sample (`n=5`, `x=(1,1,1,2,3)`) are
*still rising* at the `N=18587` cap for `{}` and `{a}` — consistent with
"use `mltpois`" for small/degenerate samples, matching the already-stated
condition precisely. (The `tpois_like` sample under `{a}`/`{a,b}` produced
`-Inf`/`-Inf` everywhere, but this is a test-design artifact, not a
finding: the sample includes actual zeros while `a=0` was chosen to mean
"support starts strictly above 0," making the whole sample infeasible by
construction — not a counterexample to anything.)

**Algorithm** for all four (already essentially what `tbinom_design_mle`'s
free-`prob`-held path does, generalized to also free `prob`): at each
candidate `size`, solve the already-proved fixed-size profile over `prob`
(unique root of the conditional-mean equation, `truncated_detail::fit`);
scan `size` upward from `max(x)` with the geometric-ratio / stochastic-
ordering tail bounds already derived in p0-binomial.md's "T" rule to certify
when the remaining integer tail cannot improve on the current best.

## 8. Open mathematics (not resolved here)

- sstd/sged `{mean,sd}` held (both families): the full joint `(nu,xi)`
  escape comparison, not reduced to a closed form (conjectured only).
- sged's `nu->0` spike threshold as a function of `xi`: confirmed to exist
  and to grow sharply with `|xi-1|`; no formula derived.
- The forced-$(c(t),t)$ one-parameter half-family profile for the four
  `{mean,·}`/`{sd,·}`-held subsets per family (8 subsets total): derived
  conceptually, not implemented or numerically exercised here.
- cmb `default_100`-type ambiguity at the grid cap (`{}`,`{prob}`,`{nu}`):
  genuine slow escape vs. inner-optimizer inaccuracy at large `size`, not
  distinguished.
- The "faces are exhaustive" assumption behind every discrete `data-dependent`
  criterion (bbinom, cmb) remains conjectured, exactly as flagged in
  `2026-10-29-p0-binomial.md`.
- sstd's tie-collapse (Lemma 3/3') has no analogous "raise the floor"
  fix the way sged's does: `nu>2` is already required for `sd` to be
  meaningful, and the collapse threshold `k>2(n-k)` is approached from
  *inside* that domain, not at an excludable edge. This is a genuine,
  permanent feature of the family, not a bug or a gap to close.


## Corrections (2026-10-30)

The subsequent [P2b skew report](2026-10-29-p2b-skew-report.md) corrects
the held-sd claim: free sstd `nu` approaching 2 can collapse the ordinary
t scale even with `sd` held. Holding both `sd` and `nu` blocks that path.
Holding the mean does not hold the mode; their displacement depends on scale
and shapes, including on limiting faces. The global comparisons remain
numerical, and the equality case is uncertified.

The [P2b binomial report](2026-10-29-p2b-binom-report.md) corrects the CMB
limiting argument: at `nu = 0` and `prob < 1/2` the size limit is geometric;
for positive held `nu`, suitable odds scaling yields a CMP limit. For
beta-binomial with `beta` held and size free, reflection changes the data
with each candidate size, so the fixed-alpha argument does not transfer.
The associated free-size comparisons remain unresolved.
