## The rule

A golden-regression comparison cannot be more precise than the fitter's own
declared convergence tolerance. Once a multi-D optimiser (BFGS with a
finite-difference gradient, or a bisection-based quantile root-finder) is
stopped by a gradient-, step-, or ULP-level criterion, its output is only
guaranteed to that criterion's resolution. A different machine (compiler,
libm, Boost.Math minor version) can take a different trajectory —
different floating-point summation order inside a finite-difference
gradient, a different number of BFGS line-search or bisection steps — and
land anywhere inside that resolution. The drift concentrates in whichever
coordinate is least curved (Student-t `nu`, with its near-flat profile
likelihood, is the textbook case). Comparing goldens any tighter than the
declared tolerance chases that platform noise, not a real regression.
Closed-form fits and well-conditioned 1-D Newton solves on an explicit
score equation are exempt and keep the existing tight floor (`kTol = 1e-10`
in C++, `1e-12` in R): there is no multi-D optimiser whose trajectory can
diverge, so no multi-run drift is introduced by platform.

Concretely:

- **C++** (`cppunivariate/tests/test_support.hpp`): `kFdTol` used to be an
  independently-chosen `1e-6`. It is now `kFdTol = kFitTol` (the shared
  `gradtol` default, `1.220703e-4`, for every fitter that passes `kFitTol`
  as its own `tol`). Families with a different declared default (`burr`,
  `invburr`: `1e-8`) are not routed through `kFdTol` — their golden check
  uses that literal value directly, next to the `tol` argument passed to
  the same fit two lines above it.
- **R** (`univariateML/tests/testthat/test_golden_regression.R`): the old
  `FD_BFGS_IDS` vector (one flat `1e-6` for five ids) is replaced by
  `GOLDEN_TOLERANCE`, a named vector mapping each affected id to *its own*
  declared default tolerance. Everything not in the table keeps the
  `1e-12` floor.
- **Frozen quantile references** (`reference.json`, fGarch): the quantile
  functions for `std`/`sstd` (`beta_logit_q`) and `ged`/`sged`
  (`gamma_logq`) invert by 120-iteration bisection to the ULP of a
  transformed log-coordinate, through a chain of series/continued-fraction
  special-function evaluations (read-only: `cppunivariate/include/
  cppunivariate/distributions/gb2/beta_tails.hpp`,
  `cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/ged/tails.hpp`). Only
  the `q*` cases of these four `reference.json` files get a looser
  tolerance; `d*`/`p*` cases (direct formula evaluation, no root-finder)
  are untouched.

### 2. sstd frozen quantile (item 2)

`qsstd` at `nu=100, xi=4` (and, by the same mechanism, every other `q*`
case in `std`/`ged`/`sged`/`sstd`'s `reference.json`): tolerance
`9.9999999999999998e-13` → `1e-09`. `qsstd` delegates to `qstd`
(`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/std/dpqr.hpp`, read
only), whose quantile (`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/
lst/tails.hpp::quantile`, read only) inverts the Student-t CDF via
`missing_detail::beta_logit_q`
(`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/gb2/beta_tails.hpp`,
read only): a 120-iteration bisection that converges to the ULP of a
transformed log-odds coordinate `t`, built from a chain of
series/continued-fraction evaluations of the incomplete beta function.
`qged`/`qsged` hit the structurally identical `gamma_logq`
(`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/ged/tails.hpp`, read
only). Two consequences:

- The bisection's own stopping rule (`mid==lo || mid==hi`) is exact to the
  ULP of `t`, but the nonlinear back-transform to `x` (`exp(...)`)
  amplifies that, and the chained special-function evaluations
  (`ibeta`/`ibetac`-style continued fractions, `log1p`/`expm1`) each carry
  a few ULP of their own that can differ across Boost.Math minor versions
  and libm implementations (glibc 2.39 vs 2.43) — this is exactly the
  mechanism the task names ("a quantile root-finder tolerance").
- `d*`/`p*` cases call the corresponding CDF directly (no root-finder, one
  evaluation of the same continued fraction) and were not reported
  failing, so they were left at `1e-12`.

`1e-9` keeps three orders of magnitude of margin above the observed
exactly-at-threshold `1e-12` failure while staying far tighter than any
plausible algorithmic regression (a wrong tail branch, a sign error, a
wrong parameter mapping move a quantile by orders of magnitude more).
`qsnorm` was left untouched: its quantile
(`cppunivariate::special::fs_normal_q`) uses Boost's closed-form
`quantile(complement(normal_distribution, p))` for the probabilities used
in the reference grid, falling back to a 1100-iteration bisection
(`fs_log_quantile`) only in the deep tail that the grid never reaches — no
root-finder risk at these parameter points, so `snorm/reference.json` and
the `snorm` branch of `fgarch_cases()`'s tolerance were not touched in the
JSON (the generator script's uniform `q`-tolerance will apply to it too on
a future regeneration, which is harmless since `1e-9` is still far tighter
than `snorm`'s actual precision there).

`univariateML/scripts/reference_values.R`'s `fgarch_cases()` was updated
in parallel so `just reference-values` (which needs `fGarch`, not run
here) reproduces the same `1e-9`/`1e-12` split instead of reverting to a
flat `1e-12`.

### 3. Other fixtures at a threshold found (item 3)

Found and fixed (same mechanism as the two reported bugs, just not yet
observed failing on this specific machine pair):

- **`lst` golden** (C++ + R): identical finite-difference-BFGS fitter and
  default `gradtol` as `std`/`ged`, but never wired into `kFdTol`/
  `FD_BFGS_IDS`. Fixed above.
- **`invparalogis` golden** (C++ + R): `paralogis_mle` on `1/x`, same
  risk as `paralogis` (which *was* in the old list). Fixed above.
- **`burr`/`invburr` golden** (C++ + R): the most extreme case found — the
  test already passes `tol=1e-8` to the fit two lines above the check,
  then compared the result at `1e-12`, four orders of magnitude tighter
  than the convergence it asked for. Every other check in the same file
  (`burr: limits and invalid inputs`, `burr n20 actuar multistart oracle
  fixtures`, the reciprocal/scaled-fit checks) already uses `1e-4`–`1e-8`,
  so the golden check was the outlier. Fixed above.
- **`bnbinom` golden** (R only): reported failure; root cause is the same
  pattern — `bnbinom`'s own hand-rolled finite-difference-gradient BFGS
  (`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/bnbinom/mle.hpp`,
  not routed through `core/bfgs.hpp`, so not caught by the
  `finite_diff_cost`/`bfgs_minimize` grep used to find the rest of this
  family) has its own default `gradtol=1e-8`, but the blanket R default
  was `1e-12`. The C++ side already used `2e-6`/`1e-10` and was fine.
  Fixed above.

Investigated and found *not* fragile (left unchanged, for the record):

- **`nhyper`'s `m`** (`cppunivariate/include/cppunivariate/distributions/
  nhyper/mle.hpp`): an integer-valued MLE found by a discrete,
  log-concave search, compared with exact `==` in `nhyper/test.cpp:28`.
  Could look like a "status near a decision boundary" risk (adjacent
  integers tying), but the comment at `nhyper_mle`'s peak search
  ("Combine before taking logs to retain adjacent-integer signs") shows
  the original implementer already designed the tie comparison to avoid
  exactly this floating-point sign-flip failure mode; `NhyperResult::tie`
  exists precisely to surface a genuine tie as a distinct status rather
  than silently picking a side.
- **`gpd`'s `mu`** (`gpd/test.cpp:11`, exact `==`): `mu` defaults to the
  fixed threshold `0`, never refit by `gpd_mle(x)` with no further
  arguments — an echo of a constant, not a converged quantity.
- Every other unconditional golden `CHECK(r.<param>==g.estimates.at(...))`
  found by grep (`bhatt`, `cauchy`, `geom`, `gumbel`, `laplace`, `logis`,
  `norm`, `pig`, `slash`, `tlambda`, `zip*`, `zt*`, `zm*`, `nbinom`,
  `zinb`, …) is inside an `if (mask & N)` guard: it checks that a *held*
  coefficient is returned unchanged, not that a refit one converged to a
  specific bit pattern.
- `gb2` (`gradtol=1e-7` default; golden check `1e-5` for shapes/scale,
  `1e-9` for logLik), `genbeta` (`1e-7` default; `1e-5`/`1e-8`), `betapr`
  (`1e-6` default; `1e-6`/`1e-10`), `huber` (`1e-10` default; `1e-10`×3):
  all already compare coefficients no tighter than their own default, and
  the logLik checks that are nominally tighter than the coefficient
  `gradtol` are expected to be tighter — near an MLE, `logLik` is locally
  quadratic in the parameters, so a `gradtol`-sized parameter error
  produces an `O(gradtol²)` logLik error. This also explains why the
  `std`/`ged`/`sstd`/`sged`/`paralogis`/`lst`/`invparalogis` fix reuses one
  `kFdTol` for both logLik and coefficients without separately tightening
  logLik: that pre-existing single-macro structure (`C_APPROX_FD`/
  `D_APPROX_FD`) was not introduced by this change, just re-pointed to a
  justified value.

Not fixed, flagged for awareness only: the `fgarch_cases()` tolerance
split now also applies to `snorm`'s quantile on regeneration (see item 2);
harmless, not acted on beyond the generator-script change.
