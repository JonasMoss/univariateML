# P0 M1: discrete families (TASK-79)

2026-10-29; numerical continuation 2026-10-29. Analysis with unresolved global-certification questions identified below. Samples are nonempty and integer-valued on the model support; support-incompatible holds have zero likelihood and are excluded from existence claims. `H` lists held coefficients; omitted coefficients are free. A, D mean always and data-dependent. There are no proved R5 “never” cases here. Attainment includes nonunique maxima and reference boundary members. Master status is for the combination, not a guarantee of global numerical convergence. `partial` records specific known mismatches. All-held evaluations are outside the requested subsets, but matter for boundary semantics.

## cmp

The reference is the package's own `dcmp`, which accepts `lambda=0` and `nu=0` (the latter requires `lambda<1`). Write `T=mean(log(X!))` and `m=mean(X)`; at the geometric member with `lambda=m/(1+m)`, put `G=E_geom(log(X!))`.

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: all-zero data attain `lambda=0`; range 0 at positive k or range 1 escapes to the corresponding point mass / adjacent two-point law. For range >=2, `T>=G` selects attained `nu=0`; otherwise a finite interior MLE exists. | partial: rejects attained zero and geometric faces |
| nu | A for positive nu; also for nu=0: `lambda=0` for all zero, otherwise solve mean=m. | partial: rejects lambda=0 |
| lambda | D: for held lambda>0, all observations in {0,1} escape to Bernoulli with probability lambda/(1+lambda); otherwise finite, including `nu=0` when lambda<1 and the one-sided score is nonpositive. Held lambda=0 and all-zero data: every admissible nu is an MLE. | partial: geometric boundary and lambda=0 rejected |

**Argument (proved, subject to the stated domain):** exponential-family concavity in `(log lambda,-nu)`, and strict convexity of log normalizer. The closed convex support of `(k,log(k!))` has vertices at integers and edges joining adjacent integers. Range >=2 is in its relative interior; boundary restriction nu>=0 selects the geometric face when its inward score `G-T` is nonpositive. With lambda held, likelihood is strictly concave in nu, its infinity face has support {0,1}; at nu=0 the one-sided score is `E log(X!)-T`. At lambda=0 positive counts have probability zero. The finite normalizer requires lambda<1 at nu=0. The current README's open domains and `limits:` are inconsistent with the actual reference; this changes existence, not just notation.

## zeta

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: finite iff any x>1; all ones approach point mass at 1 as s increases. | yes, agrees |

**Argument (proved):** the Yule–Simon log-shape score decreases from n to n−sum(x); the zeta score is n times model minus empirical mean of log X, decreasing from infinity to zero. Their README Details give these exact statements. Near the lower shape boundary mass escapes to infinity and likelihood tends to zero.

## zipf

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | A: N=max(x); s=0 if empirical mean log X >= uniform mean log(1:N), otherwise unique positive root; if N=1 all s are equivalent. | yes, agrees |
| N | D: N>=max(x) required. If N=1 all s fit; if N>1 and all x=1, point mass at 1 is approached as s→infinity; otherwise finite s, with the attained s=0 boundary when the score is nonpositive. | no |
| s | A: N=max(x) for every finite s>=0, including all ones. | no |

**Argument (proved):** increasing N strictly increases the normalizer for finite s and never helps support; for fixed N the shape score is model mean log X minus sample mean log X, with derivative minus its variance. The chosen endpoint need not be unique for N=1 in s, and the geometric limit of N is irrelevant because N is minimized.

## tpois

Let L=max(0,floor(a)+1), U=floor(b), with U possibly infinite. Held bounds must cover all observations and yield a nonempty support. `lambda=0` is a reference member only when zero belongs to the conditioning event. A free lower bound may be any a in [min(x)−1,min(x)); a free upper bound may be any b in [max(x),max(x)+1). Thus a itself is not the sample minimum under the strict convention.

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | A: narrow to [min x,max x]. If nonconstant, solve conditional mean=sample mean; if constant, singleton support makes every positive lambda a maximizer. | no: both bounds required |
| lambda | A for held lambda>0, using sample-extreme effective bounds. Held lambda=0 permits only zero samples and bounds containing zero. | no |
| a | D: set U=max x. For nonconstant data mean is interior and finite lambda exists. For constant k, singleton support if L=k gives any positive lambda; otherwise upper point mass k as lambda→infinity is unattained. | no |
| b | D: set L=min x. For nonconstant data finite lambda exists. For constant k, singleton support if U=k; otherwise lower point mass k is attained at lambda=0 only for k=0, and is unattained for k>0. | no |
| lambda,a | A with held lambda>0 and compatible a; choose U=max x. At lambda=0 only a zero sample with zero included is feasible. | no |
| lambda,b | A with held lambda>0 and compatible b; choose L=min x. At lambda=0 only a zero sample with zero included is feasible. | no |
| a,b | D: for L<U, finite positive lambda iff L<mean(x)<U; all at L have an attained lambda=0 only when L=0, otherwise approach the lower point mass; all at finite U approach the upper point mass. On singleton support every positive lambda attains likelihood 1. | partial: singleton rejected as unidentified |

**Argument (proved):** deleting support points outside the sample weakly decreases the normalizer and increases each observed probability. For fixed support the derivative of its conditional mean with respect to log lambda is its variance. Singleton supports are unidentifiable but nonetheless have MLEs. Conditional probabilities at lambda=0 with L>0 are undefined in `extraDistr::dtpois`, despite a well-defined limit. Infinite or nonintegral bounds can encode the same integer support.

## skellam

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none; mu1; mu2 | A for samples compatible with held zero rates; zero-rate faces and the point mass at zero are attained. | yes; finite numerical search can fail or choose a face within 1e-8 n |

**Argument (proved existence):** each mass is bounded by the maximal Poisson mass of either factor, which vanishes when that rate diverges. Continuity on the closed quadrant gives attainment. A held zero positive-count rate requires x<=0; the converse rate requires x>=0. README Details provides the full boundary proof; it explicitly does not prove the finite grid finds every global maximum.

## dlaplace

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: finite iff sample nonconstant; constant data approach point mass at the observation as scale→0. | yes for integer location, agrees |
| location | D: finite iff at least one observation differs from held location; otherwise point mass there as scale→0. | yes for integer location, agrees |
| scale | A: location is an integer sample median, possibly nonunique. | yes for integer location, agrees |

**Argument (proved for integer centres):** the likelihood depends on location through the sum of absolute deviations, minimized by a median. With mean absolute deviation d>0, the unique scale root is d/(sqrt(1+d²)+1). But `extraDistr::ddlaplace` accepts *real* location and evaluates the displayed expression at integer x; the README integer restriction is narrower than the reference. Its off-integer values on integer x do not sum to one, so R7 needs an explicit model decision; with a real held centre unequal to any observation the same positive-deviation scale root exists. Real free medians form an interval.

## dnorm

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: finite iff range>=2; constant gives point mass and adjacent cells give shifted `mlbinom(size=1)`. | yes, agrees |
| mean | D: finite iff some observed cell closure [x,x+1] excludes held mean; otherwise sd→0 and point mass or fixed half/half on adjacent cells. | yes, agrees |
| sd | A: unique finite mean for any sample. | yes, agrees |

**Argument (proved):** README Existence and identification gives compact-level-set and grouped-normal concavity arguments, citing Burridge (1981), JRSS B 43, 41–45, doi:10.1111/j.2517-6161.1981.tb01146.x. At a held integer mean the half/half limit is not generally the fitted Bernoulli; it has fixed probabilities.

## dgamma

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: finite iff range>=2; constant gives point mass, adjacent gives shifted `mlbinom(size=1)`. | yes; numerical search not certified global |
| shape | D: finite iff any x>0; all zeros give point mass at zero as rate→infinity. | yes, agrees |
| rate | D: finite iff any x>0; all zeros give point mass at zero as shape→0. | yes; numerical search not certified global |

**Argument (proved existence):** the README bounds each positive-cell probability uniformly by `shape^shape exp(-shape) log(1+1/k)/Gamma(shape)` as shape→0; at large shape, concentration around the latent mean permits at most two adjacent cells. This establishes compactness for range>=2; the held cases follow from their one-dimensional limits. Gamma uniqueness is not established. Rate is the reference coefficient here; the design's C4 scale convention needs a specific decision for this discretized gamma (its README reference offers `rate`, not a separate scale argument).

## bnbinom

Let r=size, a=alpha, b=beta. A *boundary comparison* means the exact supremum over finite parameters compared with all reachable faces; a finite tie still counts as attained. This is an implicit condition, not an easily computed sample statistic.

| H | Class and condition / limit | Master |
|---|---|---|
| none | D: all zero → delta 0; otherwise finite iff some finite parameter point has likelihood at least the best NB or Poisson face (including equality); otherwise the best face is the unattained supremum. Positive constants and {0,1} samples escape to Poisson. | partial: numerical multistart and 1e-8 n face tie policy |
| size | D: all zero → delta 0; otherwise finite iff some finite point meets or exceeds the NB face of held size; otherwise that face is the supremum. | partial: numerical comparison |
| beta | D: same, with NB size=held beta by r/b symmetry. | partial: numerical comparison |
| alpha | D: finite iff any x>0; all zero → delta 0. | yes, numerical search not certified |
| size,alpha | D: finite iff any x>0; all zero → delta 0 by beta→0. | yes, numerical search not certified |
| alpha,beta | D: finite iff any x>0; all zero → delta 0 by size→0. | yes, numerical search not certified |
| size,beta | D: finite iff any x>0; all zero → delta 0 by alpha→infinity. | yes, numerical search not certified |

**Argument (proved closure; implicit classification):** [prior local analysis](2026-10-29-bnbinom.md) proves r/b interchangeability, all proper faces and compactness for positive-count samples when alpha or two coefficients are held. It proves positive constant and {0,1} all-free samples have a Poisson supremum. For an NB face with size r and fitted mean m, the first inward coefficient has sign `v-m-m²/r` (empirical variance v); positive sign proves interior improvement but negative sign proves only local boundary optimality. A necessary-and-sufficient explicit sample criterion and certification of global finite optima remain open; a tie to a finite point cannot be classified by the fitter's numerical tolerance alone.

## Design, restrictions and proposed metadata

The design assigns `zipf.N`, `dunif.min/max` endpoint; `tpois.a/b` design; all other coefficients regular. Agree on the endpoint/design intent subject to the strict-bound representation and singleton identifiability above. Dispute dlaplace location's integer domain under R7, and CMP's two open domains. No R5 restriction is listed for any family in this group and none is needed: every individual probability is <=1, so no likelihood diverges. Data-dependent unattained limits are R6, not R5. Their existence may still require endpoint conventions.

Proposed YAML fragments (regular kinds and absent fit_domain omitted). They describe the target, not current implementation. The conditional escapes above are not unconditional `holds: no-mle` exceptions. No exact hold subset here is never solvable for every sample.

CMP's reference domain should encode `lambda>=0`, `nu>=0` with the conditional normalizability constraint at nu=0; that constraint is a model domain, not an R5 restriction. Continuous dlaplace centres require resolution before adopting the proposed R7 interpretation. The tpois README bounds `(-inf, inf)` omit the ±Inf values actually accepted by the reference. The dgamma reference returns zero at every finite count when `rate=0`; although it accepts that numerical input, it is not a proper probability distribution and should not be treated as an attained model member without a policy decision.

## Independent numerical work and limitations

`oracle.R` runs from the repository root with `Rscript` and uses fixed family-specific seeds. It saves the samples as `samples-<family>.rds` and results as `oracle-<family>.csv`; an optional list of family ids runs only those families. Every family has default simulations at n=20, 100 and 1000, samples of sizes 2, 3 and 5, ties and constant samples. The additional Poisson, negative-binomial, geometric, Bernoulli, shifted-Bernoulli and point-mass samples cover the named reachable limits; zipf also has a zeta sample. All proper hold subsets are swept. For tpois the simulation is clipped to the held reference design when needed; the complementary singleton, zero-bound and `NULL` cases are checked by `boundaries.R`.

The oracle uses the external reference log densities for external-reference families; the five own families use independent formulas. It searches transformed continuous coefficients from three BFGS starts or scalar `optimize`, enumerates the appropriate discrete endpoint/median and explicitly checks attained CMP, Zipf and Skellam boundary members. `paths.R` checks the five own d/p/q/r implementations against those formulas: maximum absolute log-density differences for cmp, yulesimon, zeta, zipf and skellam were respectively 7.2e-15, 8.9e-16, 0, 0 and 4.5e-16. Formula checks and explicit likelihood sequences are in `formula-checks.csv` and `paths.csv`. Inapplicable fixed endpoints produce zero likelihood and are not positive-likelihood fits.

Across the sweep, **337 returned master fits** had both an independently maximized likelihood and the independent reference likelihood evaluated at the returned coefficients within `1e-6*n` of the reported likelihood. The numbers by family were: bnbinom 61, cmp 31, dgamma 34, dlaplace 39, dnorm 35, dunif 14, dweibull 33, skellam 42, tpois 11, yulesimon 11, zeta 11, zipf 15. All implemented non-all-held combinations have returned-fit comparisons; rejected cases and error messages are also recorded. Unsupported tpois bounds are identified in the script rather than passed as unsupported wrapper arguments; `boundaries.R` checks explicit `NULL`. Rejections on limiting samples are not numerical comparisons to finite estimates. Master CMP's rejection of attained geometric and zero-rate examples and tpois's rejection of singleton support are independently reproduced.

The sequences include fixed- and free-parameter approaches to point masses, adjacent-cell Bernoulli limits, the fixed-size NB and free Poisson BNB faces, and Zipf's zeta limit. They support the proved limits but cannot establish global nonexistence. `extraDistr::dtpois` becomes inaccurate at extremely small positive lambda with a positive truncation lower bound: the last path value is slightly positive, impossible for a log likelihood. The limiting conditional distribution and existence claim therefore use the analytic ratio argument, not that final numeric value. External density underflow, finite search boxes and multiple local optima also limit the numerical search; BNB and discretized gamma global optimality are not certified. The BNB comparison criterion is exact but implicit: finding a finite point meeting the best face is necessary and sufficient, while no simple explicit sample criterion is proved here. No R5 never case occurs, so there is no unbounded-likelihood path to demonstrate.
