## Zeta: infinite-support Zipf

**Reference and policy.** Write `P(X=k)=k^(-a)/zeta(a)`, `k>=1`, `a>1`. VGAM calls `a-1` its shape. Installed **poweRlaw 1.0.0** exports `dpldis(x,xmin,alpha,log)`, `ppldis(q,xmin,alpha,lower.tail)` and `rpldis(n,xmin,alpha,discrete_max=10000)`, but no q function; use `xmin=1, alpha=a`. Its source is directly inspectable through the installed namespace. [Package source](https://github.com/csgillespie/poweRlaw). Maintained distribution-fitting package; no claim about a later uninstalled release.

Installed recursive Imports are pracma plus base/recommended namespaces; following Depends as well gives methods, parallel, pracma, stats, utils, tools, compiler, graphics and grDevices. **No VGAM.** Existing **sads 0.6.5 imports VGAM directly**; following Imports/Depends/LinkingTo gives 25 installed namespaces including bbmle, poilog, GUILDS, poweRlaw, Matrix and nloptr. Thus “already uses sads” does not establish a VGAM-free dependency tree. No new sads zeta functions were found.

**tolerance 3.0.0** documents `d/p/q/rzipfman(...,s=a,b=NULL,N=Inf)`. Its release is dated 2024-04-17; a maintained tolerance-interval package, but these functions are unsuitable unchanged. Direct Imports are MASS, plotly, stats4; recursively plotly brings a large HTML/JSON/HTTP/tidyverse stack. No direct VGAM dependency; the entire current plotly closure was not independently resolved here, so a tree-wide negative assertion would be premature. [CRAN metadata](https://mirror.clarkson.edu/cran/web/packages/tolerance/index.html), [function source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/tolerance/master/R/zipfman.R).

**Checks performed.** Runtime poweRlaw densities at `a={1.01,1.1,2,10,100}`, `k={1,2,100}` agree with mpmath within about `1.1e-14` relative error. At `a=2`, `P(1)=0.6079271018540267`. `ppldis` sums to the requested integer, O(q); its upper-tail option returns **P(X>=q)**, not the usual P(X>q): runtime `ppldis(1,1,2,FALSE)=1`. Density source does not reject fractional x. Its r function uses exact table inversion through `discrete_max`, then a rounded continuous Pareto approximation. This is not an exact sampler near `a=1`; no p/q consistency test is possible because q is absent.

Transcribed tolerance's [Euler–Maclaurin zeta approximation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/tolerance/master/R/zetafun.R): the same density grid agrees to roughly double precision. But its q function enumerates one million values and extrapolates the remainder as proportional to `1/x`, although the correct tail is proportional to `x^(1-a)`. Reproducing that branch at `a=1.1,u=.9` gives `q=2373185`, whose independent CDF is **0.78233**, violating `F(q)>=u`. Worse, rzipfman reassigns sampled values to observed frequency ranks: `rzipfman(1,...)` always returns 1. These are source-derived defects; the whole package was not executed. Do not use this r as a distribution sampler.

**Existence and closure (derivation).** Let `L=mean(log x)`. The score per observation is `-L-zeta'(a)/zeta(a)` and its derivative is `-Var_a(log X)<0`. The model expectation of log X decreases continuously from infinity at `a↓1` to zero at `a→infinity`. Thus a unique finite MLE exists iff any observation exceeds 1, including a constant sample at an integer greater than 1. All ones escape to the point mass at 1; this event has positive probability under every finite a. At `a↓1`, mass escapes to infinity, not to a probability law in Hellinger closure. No geometric or continuous Pareto limit on the original support.

**Relation to mlzipf.** The existing finite model is `k^(-s)/H_N(s)`, with fitted `N=max(x)` and `s>=0`. For **fixed s>1**, letting N increase gives the zeta law in total variation/Hellinger distance. For fixed `s<=1`, it does not yield a probability limit on the positive integers. Fitting N does not make finite Zipf “the same fit”: at fixed s its likelihood decreases with N, so N is max(x), rather than infinity. Do not add a zeta escape message to mlzipf on that basis. At `s=0` the finite model is uniform. Runtime `sads::dzipf(1,10,2)=0.6452580`, distinct from zeta's 0.6079271.

**Fitter and decision.** One scalar a, optionally fixable; keep xmin fixed at 1. Cache `sum(log x)`, solve the strictly decreasing score by bracketed Newton; start `a≈1+1/L`, but use the exact discrete likelihood. Cost O(n) once plus zeta/derivative evaluations; use a controlled Euler–Maclaurin calculation at the Boost seam if derivatives are unavailable. All ones need a point-mass status, with no invented `mlX`. **Add later**, despite an easy fitter: poweRlaw can supply density, but a complete accurate external p/q/r route needs upstream work. An r.args map cannot manufacture a missing quantile function or repair an approximate sampler.

## Yule–Simon

**References and parameterisation.** `P(X=k)=rho*B(k,rho+1)`, `k>=1`, `rho>0`; `P(X>k)=k*B(k,rho+1)`. The classical source is Simon (1955), [doi:10.1093/biomet/42.3-4.425](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/42.3-4.425).

A useful non-VGAM alternative is **cpd 0.3.3 (2024-10-04)**: `debw/pebw/qebw/rebw` with `alpha=1,rho=rho` represent **X−1**, covering every positive rho. Do not confuse cpd's rho with its alternative gamma: `gamma=rho+2`. The density has no log argument. Required transformations are `d/p` at `x-1`, q/r plus 1. This needs an observation/output transformation beyond a coefficient-only argument map. [Density source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/cpd/master/R/dctp.R), [CDF source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/cpd/master/R/pctp.R), [quantile source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/cpd/master/R/qctp.R), [sampler source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/cpd/master/R/rctp.R).

cpd's direct Imports are hypergeo, Rdpack, dgof, graphics. Following their Imports adds elliptic, contfrac, deSolve, Rcpp, rbibutils, tools, utils and base namespaces; elliptic adds MASS. **No VGAM found in this resolved runtime dependency graph.** This is materially heavier than a beta formula; hypergeo also declares RcppArmadillo under LinkingTo. [cpd metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/cpd/master/DESCRIPTION), [hypergeo metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/hypergeo/master/DESCRIPTION), [Rdpack metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/Rdpack/master/DESCRIPTION).

**Other candidates.** gamlss.dist `d/p/q/rYULE` use zero-based support and `rho=1+1/mu`; this covers only **rho>1**, omitting infinite-mean Yule–Simon laws. Version 6.1-11 was released September 2026; Imports MASS and base namespaces, no VGAM. [YULE source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/R/YULE.R), [metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/gamlss.dist/master/DESCRIPTION). degreenet offers Yule sampling, not a full evaluation route. RTMBdist's development manual advertises `dyules/pyules/ryules`, but these are **absent from the inspected CRAN 1.1.0 NAMESPACE**, and no q is advertised. Treat the [development manual](https://janolefi.r-universe.dev/RTMBdist/doc/manual.html) as a future lead, not proof of a CRAN solution; compare the [CRAN exports](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/RTMBdist/master/NAMESPACE).

**Checks performed.** Source transcription of cpd's log-gamma density at `rho={.01,2,100}`, `k={1,2,10,10000}` agrees with independent mpmath beta values to `1.5e-11` in log-density. The returned density can underflow because there is no log API. Its probability recurrence `p(k+1)/p(k)=k/(k+rho+1)` matches the independent beta-tail CDF at 10000 within `4e-15` absolute. Transcribed qebw inversion at u=.1,.5,.9 gives `{1,1,4}` for rho=2 and `{1,1,1}` for rho=100, satisfying the discrete inverse inequalities. These are algorithm checks, not installed cpd calls.

The p/q/r source constructs CDF tables through the requested quantile, so cost is O(q) with repeated vector growth, and upper tails use subtraction. At rho=.01, **F(10000)=.0931645**: even the .1 quantile exceeds that table, and median/high quantiles become enormous. Summation can stagnate before reaching a requested probability. Thus a complete non-VGAM API exists algebraically, but **reliability over the full heavy-tail domain is not established**. No successful full-package extreme p/q/r test is claimed.

**MLE (derivation).** For an integer k, `log p(k)=log rho+log Gamma(k)-sum_{j=1}^k log(rho+j)`. In t=log rho the score is `n-sum_i sum_{j=1}^{x_i} rho/(rho+j)`, strictly decreasing from n to `n-sum x_i`. Thus there is a unique finite MLE whenever some x exceeds 1. All ones increase toward the point mass at 1 as rho→infinity. At rho↓0 every finite set loses its mass: escape to infinity, not a Hellinger probability limit. No other proper limits, nonidentifiability or unbounded likelihood. All-ones nonexistence has positive probability.

**Fitter and decision.** One optionally fixable rho; bracketed Newton in log rho with digamma/trigamma differences, avoiding O(sum x) loops. Cache unique observations/frequencies; start rho=1 or bracket by doubling, rather than using a mean that may be unstable. O(n) preprocessing and O(number of distinct x) per iteration. **Add later:** fitter S, integration M. Seek a CRAN beta-tail/bisection/rejection implementation or upstream improvements; do not silently restrict rho>1 merely to use gamlss.dist. cpd provides a credible moderate-range route if shifts and explicit range limitations are accepted.

## Conway–Maxwell–Poisson

**Definition and implementations.** `p(k)=lambda^k/(k!)^nu/Z`, `k>=0`, lambda>0, nu>0. Include nu=0 only when lambda<1 if deliberately extending the parameter space. **COMPoissonReg 0.8.2**, released 2026-07-22, provides `dcmp/pcmp/qcmp/rcmp` in these coordinates plus `ncmp(...,log=TRUE)`. Imports stats, Rcpp, numDeriv; recursive runtime graph adds only base namespaces: **small and VGAM-free**. [Release metadata](https://cran.univ-lyon1.fr/CRAN/web/packages/COMPoissonReg/index.html), [API](https://rdrr.io/cran/COMPoissonReg/man/CMP-Distribution.html).

**mpcmp** provides `dcomp/pcomp/qcomp/rcomp`, using mean mu and nu or alternatively lambda and nu; its density log flag is `log.p`. Inspected CRAN mirror source is 0.3.6 (2020), whereas the authors' site cites 0.3.8 (2022): version provenance needs pinning before a runtime comparison. Direct Imports include Rcpp, ggplot2, ggpubr, generics, tibble, dplyr, rlang, stringr, purrr and base namespaces. Recursive ggpubr/rstatix/car and plotting dependencies make it substantially heavier; this full current graph was not resolved, and tree-wide VGAM absence is **unverified**, though no direct VGAM import exists. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/mpcmp/master/DESCRIPTION), [authors' site](https://thomas-fung.github.io/mpcmp/), [distribution source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/mpcmp/master/R/comp_distribution.R).

**compoisson 0.3** is old (2008 code, 2012 CRAN packaging), with stats/MASS Depends and no further non-base runtime dependency or VGAM. It exposes dcom/rcom and a separate com.log.density, **no p/q in the inspected CRAN API**; do not substitute newer GitHub forks. dcom uses raw powers/factorials and scalar conditionals. [Metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/compoisson/master/DESCRIPTION), [CRAN source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/compoisson/master/R/com.R).

**Numerical comparison performed.** Independent 45-digit recurrence through at most 15495 terms, with relative remainder below `1e-38`; the following are **transcriptions of source normalizers**, not installed packages. A log-Z error changes every log-density by its negative. COMPoissonReg defaults: hybrid.tol=.01, truncate.tol=1e-6, ymax=1e6. It uses a leading asymptotic formula when `lambda^(−1/nu)<.01`; its summation has a remainder bound. mpcmp's C++ summation stops when the last term / sum is below `1e-10`, without a tail bound; I used a generous summax=20000. compoisson stops when consecutive log sums differ by at most .001. **p/q evidence and limitation.** COM's CDF and quantile source both force truncated normalization, whereas d may use the asymptotic branch; r uses quantile inversion. Thus p/q can agree with each other while disagreeing with d. No installed p/q test was possible. Source inspection verifies the common enumeration route, not extreme accuracy; large lambda/small nu can exceed ymax. [Evaluation source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/COMPoissonReg/master/src/cmp.cpp). Before adoption run the inverse inequalities `F(Q(u)-1)<u<=F(Q(u))` on actual packages with tightened summation controls, and compare density sums against the CDF. Literature likewise treats normalization as a computational problem: [Raim–Sellers computational report](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2022/adrm/RRC2022-01.html). **A usable full non-VGAM implementation exists, but defaults are not uniformly accurate.**

**MLE and complete boundary logic (derivation).** Put `m=mean x`, `t=mean log(x!)`, `h(k)=log(k!)`. The log likelihood per observation is `m*eta−nu*t−log Z`, eta=log lambda, strictly concave in `(eta,nu)` at finite parameters. For each nu>0 and m>0 solve `E X=m`; the profiled expected h decreases strictly with nu, since its derivative is `−[Var(h)−Cov(X,h)^2/Var(X)]<0`. Its limiting values are `g(m)=E_G h(X)` for geometric G with mean m at nu↓0, and the linear interpolation `b(m)` of h between floor(m) and ceil(m) at nu→infinity.

Consequently the **unique finite joint MLE exists iff `b(m)<t<g(m)`**. If `t>=g(m)`, the supremum is geometric, lambda=m/(1+m): stop “Use `mlgeom`” for the nu>0 family; if nu=0 was explicitly included it is an attained boundary. Equality still has no positive-nu maximizer. If `t=b(m)`, all observations lie on two adjacent integers (or one integer); the supremum is their empirical two-point law. For support `{j,j+1}`, set `lambda=(j+1)^nu*c`, then `P(j+1)/P(j)→c`. This generalizes the often-mentioned Bernoulli face `{0,1}`. Only j=0 maps directly to `mlbinom(x,size=1)`; other j need a shifted-Bernoulli message. Constants approach a point mass; m=0 is handled separately. Each nonexistence event has positive probability. This is a non-steep family: interior convex-support membership alone is insufficient; see the [non-steepness proof](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3964598/) and [original CMP paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9876.2005.00474.x). Poisson nu=1 is an **interior submodel**, never an escape requiring `mlpois`.

**Fitter and decision.** Estimate lambda,nu; either can be fixed. Cache sum x and sum lgamma(x+1); profile eta by mean matching, then solve the scalar dispersion score with covariance derivatives and bracketing. Start nu≈m/variance, lambda≈`(m+(nu−1)/(2nu))^nu` only when meaningful; Poisson is a safe fallback. Cost O(n) preprocessing plus O(K) normalization/moment recurrences per iteration; K can be enormous. Use mode-centred log sums, explicit tail bounds and no-throw failure statuses, not a hard silent cutoff. For fixed positive nu, eta fitting is simpler; for fixed lambda, constrain the nu↓0 domain correctly. **Add later, L:** COMPoissonReg is the preferred R evaluator, with an explicit control map, while the C++ normalizer and geometric/two-point status logic need dedicated work.

## Conway–Maxwell–binomial

**Reference.** For known integer size N, use `p(k)∝choose(N,k)^nu * p^k*(1−p)^(N−k)`, `0<=k<=N`. p is a canonical probability coordinate, **not the mean/N** except at nu=1. Finite normalization allows any real nu; nu>=0 is a separate restricted model. **RTMBdist 1.1.0 (2026-09-06)** exports `d/p/q/rcombinom(size,prob,nu)`, with stable log-sum normalization and finite table inversion. It excludes prob=0/1. [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/RTMBdist/master/R/combinom.R), [release metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/RTMBdist/master/DESCRIPTION).

Dependency weight is high: Depends RTMB; Imports Matrix, circular, sn, statmod, movMF, stats. Recursive branches include RTMB→TMB/Rcpp/Matrix/MASS (RcppEigen at build time), sn→quantreg/mnormt/numDeriv, circular→boot/mvtnorm, movMF→skmeans/clue/slam. No VGAM appears in inspected branches, but the complete quantreg/skmeans closure was not resolved here: **full-tree VGAM absence remains unverified**. [RTMB metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/RTMB/master/DESCRIPTION), [sn](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/sn/master/DESCRIPTION), [movMF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/movMF/master/DESCRIPTION). **COMMultReg** documents `d_cmb/r_cmb`, but no p/q on its [CMB page](https://rdrr.io/github/andrewraim/COMMultReg/man/cmb.html); that page is a GitHub build, not proof of a current CRAN release. glmmTMB documents dcombinom/pcombinom in mean coordinates, but no complete d/p/q/r route was established and its modelling stack is heavier. [Manual](https://cran.univ-lyon1.fr/CRAN/web/packages/glmmTMB/refman/glmmTMB.html).

**Checks performed.** Transcribed RTMBdist density arithmetic versus high-precision exact binomial coefficients on full supports `(N,p,nu)={(10,.3,1),(10,.3,2),(50,.01,−2),(100,.99,10)}`: maximum log-density errors `2.3e−15,6.3e−15,1.0e−13,9.7e−13`. Ordinary inverse-table checks at u=.1,.5,.9 give `{1,3,5}`, `{3,4,5}`, `{0,0,0}`, `{59,61,63}` and satisfy the discrete inequalities. Probability sums differ from 1 by at most `1.4e−13`. These validate the copied numerical kernels, **not RTMB dispatch or an installed package**.

Source defect: pcombinom masks with `ispos(q−k+.5)`, so for ordinary fractional q=.75 it includes k=1; the correct CDF floors q and includes only k=0. At N=10,p=.3,nu=1 this predicts F(1)=.1493083 instead of F(0)=.02824752. Upper-tail/log output is obtained by subtraction/log, losing extreme-tail accuracy. Therefore **all four non-VGAM functions exist, but I would not call the full API reliable unchanged**. A coefficient map cannot fix fractional-q semantics. Request an upstream correction and perform installed-package checks before declaring it a reference.

**Known-size MLE (derivation).** With eta=logit(p) and unrestricted real nu, this is a full finite exponential family with sufficient statistics `(x,log choose(N,x))`. For N>=2 the statistic points form a polygon: its upper edges join adjacent integers (strict concavity of log choose), and its lower edge joins 0 and N. The finite unique MLE exists exactly when the empirical statistic mean lies in its interior. On an upper edge, all data lie on adjacent integers and the limiting fit is their empirical two-point distribution (nu→+infinity with eta adjusted). On the lower edge, all data are endpoints 0/N and the limiting fit is an endpoint Bernoulli (nu→−infinity). Constant samples have point-mass limits. These events have positive probability. N=1 makes nu unidentifiable; N=0 makes both parameters unidentifiable. No unbounded likelihood.

If instead constraining nu>=0, the lower boundary is the **truncated geometric** `p(k)∝exp(eta*k)` on 0..N; eta=0 gives `mldunif` on 0..N, but generic eta has no package model. Compare the best nu=0 fit: the inward profile score is `mean(log choose)−E_0 log choose`; a nonpositive value puts the constrained MLE there. If nu>0 is required, report that face as unattained. Binomial nu=1 is interior, so no automatic “Use `mlbinom`” escape. These distinctions must be explicit in README domains/limits.

**Unknown-size limit (derivation).** For fixed nu>0, as `N→infinity` with `N^nu*p/(1−p)→lambda`, finite-k weights converge to `lambda^k/(k!)^nu`: CMP is the limit, including its Poisson nu=1 slice. For nu=0 a negative fixed eta gives the ordinary geometric limit. A free-N fitter must compare finite sizes with the CMP supremum, then CMP's own geometric faces; do **not** set N=max(x) by analogy with Zipf. No theorem that finite-N profiles are unimodal or that a finite cutoff certifies existence was established here. [Kadane's CMB paper](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bayesian-analysis/volume-11/issue-2/Sums-of-Possibly-Associated-Bernoulli-Variables--The-ConwayMaxwell-Binomial/10.1214/15-BA955.full) supplies the finite-family background; boundary and scaling arguments here are independent derivations.

**Fitter and decision.** Begin with **required fixed size N**, optionally fixed nu or p. Finite log-sum-exp gives means/covariances in O(N); profile eta to match the sample mean, then one-dimensional nu Newton/bracketing, with analytic face checks. Cache sufficient statistics in O(n); start nu=1,p=mean(x)/N away from endpoints. BFGS is unnecessary for this concave two-coordinate problem. If size later becomes estimable, it needs integer profiling, limit comparison, and an explicit stopping certificate. **Add later, M fixed size / L free size:** attractive finite computation, but repair/validate the external evaluator and accept its dependency cost first. A future free-size D1 message can name a CMP fitter only after one actually exists; do not invent `mlcmp` today.
