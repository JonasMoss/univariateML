# P0 summary: existence of the MLE under every hold (TASK-79)

Ten Codex jobs (gpt-6.1-terra, high effort), one per family group, analysed
every family and every hold subset against the rules in
`roadmap/2026-10-29-parameter-rules.md`. The computations ran on simbox.
Per-group notes, scripts and CSVs are in
`2026-10-29-p0-<group>.md`, `2026-10-29-p0-<group>/` and
`2026-10-29-p0-<group>-report.md`. The groups are locscale, skewreal,
positive, halfthreshold, genbeta, extreme, bounded, counts, discrete2 and
binomial.

Each note keeps three kinds of claim apart: proved, checked numerically,
and conjectured. Every job reports itself incomplete for the same reason:
- multistart optimization cannot certify global maxima;
- several exact existence criteria are proved only for some subsets (GB2
  and genbeta, sstd and sged, beta-binomial with free `size`, truncated
  binomial).

No job disputes the parameter kinds in the design. No group needs a new R5
restriction: the discrete likelihoods are bounded, and the bounded
families exist on their ordinary domains.

## Findings that amend the design (proposed)

**A1. Endpoint closure.** Several references define open supports:
- `dtnorm` at `a`, `dpower` at `alpha`;
- `dgpd` and `dgev` at the upper endpoint when $\xi = -1$;
- the strict tpois lower bound.

An endpoint estimated as a sample extreme is then never attained. R4
estimates endpoints, and C2 puts the shape bound at $-1$, so both rest on
attainment. Proposal: likelihoods use the upper semicontinuous closure,
i.e. the support is closed at a parameter-dependent endpoint and the
density there is its limit from inside. Effects:
- tnorm with estimated bounds and power `alpha = max(x)` become attained;
- the GPD at $\xi = -1$ is the uniform, attained;
- hnorm's closed-endpoint special case becomes the general rule;
- `dml` agrees with the fit.

**A2. Which reference values count (R7).** References accept degenerate
and extended values: `dnorm(sd = 0)`, `dlnorm(sdlog = 0)`, beta shapes 0
and ∞, gamma shape 0, `dht(nu = Inf)` (the half-normal), PIG `mean = Inf`
(Poisson–Lévy) and `shape = 0`, NB `size = 0`, `ddgamma(rate = 0)` (zero
everywhere), and uniform with infinite endpoints. Proposal: the domain is
the finite parameter values the reference accepts whose law is a proper
distribution dominated by the family's base measure (Lebesgue for
continuous, counting for discrete). Then:
- *excluded:* continuous point masses (sd 0, sdlog 0, beta 0/∞, gamma
  shape 0, nsbeta zero shapes), infinite parameters (ht `nu = Inf`, PIG
  `mean = Inf`: these stay escapes, "use `mlX`"), improper limits
  (`ddgamma` rate 0, infinite uniform endpoints);
- *included:* discrete point masses and other finite proper members (NB
  `size = 0`, PIG `shape = 0`, cmp `lambda = 0` and `nu = 0`, cmb prob 0/1,
  bhatt `a = 0`). Several current escapes become attained members (B7,
  B8).

**A3. Restriction scope (R5).** The real conditions do not fit
`while_free`:
- GED with `sd` held still spikes when more than 13.79 % of the
  observations tie at the mean (for distinct samples with free mean this
  covers every n from 2 to 7);
- GEV still needs $\xi \le 1$ with `sigma` held, and with `mu` held above
  the minimum;
- Fréchet needs $\lambda \ge 1$ with `sigma` held.

Proposal: a restriction on a coefficient applies whenever that
coefficient is free, whatever else is held. A held value outside the
restriction is accepted when that hold subset has an MLE (as Fréchet
with $\lambda$ and $\sigma$ held), otherwise it stops. This is simple and
conservative: some subsets get a restriction they do not strictly need,
and a flagged boundary fit tells the user. `while_free` is dropped from
the schema.

**A4. Estimated thresholds (`NULL`, M2).** The $c \ge 1$ idea does not
generalize:
- Pareto IV and Feller–Pareto collapse with $c$ = shape1·shape2 and have
  independent endpoint singularities (exponents shape2 and
  shape2·shape3);
- a free GPD threshold has no MLE even for $-1 \le \xi \le 1$;
- free-endpoint Pareto III/IV and Feller–Pareto escape to logistic laws,
  nsbeta to the normal, genbeta to the gamma;
- the fatigue threshold spikes unless $\alpha$ lies in a compact interval
  with no weakest endpoints, and fatigue also has a normal limit.

Proposal: `NULL` is supported for a threshold only where it is proved:
- hnorm (the MLE exists, ties included);
- hcauchy (tie multiplicity below n/2);
- Fréchet ($\lambda \ge 1$, today's free fit);
- ht ($\nu \ge 1$, with the half-normal escape).

For pareto2–4, fpareto, nsbeta, genbeta, gpd and fatigue, `NULL` stops
with the reason. The "new restricted threshold fits" leave P3.

**A5. huber `epsilon` (M4).** With `epsilon` free, normal samples escape
to the normal 60 %, 72 % and 64 % of the time at n = 20, 100, 1000, and
Cauchy samples 4 %, 10 %, 62 %. A free default would mostly answer "use
`mlnorm`". Proposal: `epsilon` is an analyst-chosen tuning constant, kind
`design`, so it is required (`mlhuber(x, epsilon = 1.345)`, or `NULL` to
estimate it).

**A6. Failed optimizers (R6, recorded in the design).** A failed BFGS
stops unless the score check passes, instead of returning its last point.

**A7. `holds:` holds structural exceptions only.** Sample-dependent
non-existence (most rows in the tables) is R6 behaviour, documented under
`limits:` and in the README prose, not as `holds:` entries.
