The Azzalini skew-t distribution combines asymmetric shape with Student tails.
The coefficient $\xi$ sets location, $\omega$ sets scale, $\alpha$ controls
skewness and $\nu$ controls tail weight. Zero skewness gives the ordinary
location-scale Student distribution. This skewing construction differs from
the Fernandez--Steel skew-t.

## d/p/q/r

For $|\alpha|\ge10^6$, the narrow angular wedge uses
$r=\theta e^{-v/k}$, $\theta=\arctan(1/|\alpha|)$ and
$k=1+\nu h(1-\theta^2)$, with
$h=t^2/(\nu\sin^2\theta+t^2)$. The kernel ratio is evaluated directly:
$[1+h\{(\sin\theta/\sin r)^2-1\}]^{-\nu/2}$.
This resolves the endpoint layer without subtracting large log kernels.
The transformed integrand has logarithmic decay at least one; truncation
at $v=64$ omits at most $e^{-64}/k$. The small-angle log-sinc expansion has
remainder $O(\theta^6)$, below $10^{-36}$ in this branch.
Adaptive quadrature must converge, and the propagated kernel margin plus
$2\cdot10^{-11}$ must satisfy the requested tail allowance; otherwise the
CDF returns NaN. This is a practical floating-point check, with independent
numerical evidence in the [frozen-reference replay](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-12-dpq-skew-data/check.py), not an outward-rounded quadrature enclosure.

On the positive broad side, the CDF is the sum of the central Student mass
and the thin wedge. Using that sum for a small CDF avoids complement
cancellation. Both previously archived large-skew/large-degree members now
have active truth tables. Quantile bracketing uses the corrected CDF and
retains its failed-evaluation checks.
The [subnormal rounding limit](../ged/README.md) also applies in this
angular branch. Both endpoints of the wedge error are transformed into the
requested tail before accepting a natural or logarithmic probability.

This angular scaling depends on the skew angle, including at ordinary
degrees of freedom. At $\nu=5$, $\alpha=10^{12}$ and $x=10^{-12}$
with location zero and scale one, the lower CDF is
$8.412994158666847\cdot10^{-13}$. The central-mass representation
preserves it without subtracting a rounded half probability. Independent
density and angular integrals agree in the [small-CDF check](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-12-dpq-skew-data/st-small-cdf.py).

Near the normal face, $h$ can underflow while $\nu h$ remains significant.
For $\nu>10^{32}$ and $\rho=|t|/\sin\theta\le2$, evaluation at
$\nu_0=10^{32}$ carries the relative probability allowance
$512(\nu^{-1/2}+\nu_0^{-1/2})$. The kernel is a Gamma mixture with
mean-one mixing variable and variance $2/\nu$. Its normalized Gaussian
angular integral has derivative bounded by $1.3$ and is above $0.02$
on this ray range; the central mass has the same coupling bound.
This gives the stated allowance, which participates in both tail checks.
The [normal-face check](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-12-dpq-skew-data/st-normal-face.py)
retains the finite-degree comparison bound for the normal reference.

## Details

The all-free interior accuracy check differentiates the Student probability through
its incomplete-beta continued fraction, including its degrees of freedom. The
location floor is omega; alpha has floor one. First and second derivative convergence
are checked along with the continued-fraction value. See the [accuracy
validation](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-10-acc-allfree-report.md).

When every coefficient is held, the likelihood is evaluated at those values
without optimization.

The full finite parameter domain has unbounded likelihood for every nonempty
sample when all coefficients are free. The fit restricts free $\nu$ to
$\nu\ge1$ and compares numerical interior candidates with skew-normal,
half-t and half-normal limits. Holding coefficients fixes those coordinates
throughout the search and the compatible face comparisons. A returned
stationary candidate does not establish a unique global maximum.

The arguments below distinguish boundedness, attainment and uniqueness.
They also explain why equality in the collapse exponent requires a separate
comparison rather than a declaration that no maximum exists.

## Collapse and restrictions

For fixed finite $\nu$ and $\alpha$, let $k$ observations equal $\xi$
and let all other residuals be nonzero. As $\omega\to0$, likelihood is
asymptotic to a positive finite constant times
$\omega^{\nu(n-k)-k}$. This includes $\alpha=0$ and $k=n$.
For each nonzero residual, the Student CDF argument tends to
$\pm\alpha\sqrt{\nu+1}$, whose CDF is strictly positive for finite
$\alpha$. A negative exponent therefore proves divergence. Equality gives
a finite positive path limit and does not alone establish non-existence.

For the unrestricted all-free model, choose $\xi$ at any observation and
$\alpha=0$. If $k<n$, choose $0<\nu<k/(n-k)$; if $k=n$, choose any
$\nu>0$. Letting $\omega\to0$ proves unboundedness for every nonempty
sample, including samples without repeated values. With $\xi$ held, this
construction works exactly when a datum equals that location. This is a
classification of this path, rather than of every joint escape.

The bound $\nu\ge1$ is the weakest constant lower bound that prevents
this fixed-shape divergence for every sample with largest tie count
$2k_{\max}<n$. Indeed, $\nu(n-k)-k>0$ then. Any smaller lower bound fails
for sufficiently large even $n$ with $k=n/2-1$. At $\nu=1$, a value with
$2k>n$ still causes divergence; $2k=n$ gives a finite nonzero path limit.
Thus restricting degrees of freedom cannot ensure attainment. The fitting
restriction applies whenever $\nu$ is free, including with scale held,
unless a held-set exemption has been proved. For free scale and a held
location away from all observations, a possible exemption remains open.
A tie at the held location retains the unrestricted collapse defect.

Likelihood is bounded for $\nu\ge1$ and $2k_{\max}\le n$.
Uniformly in $\nu\ge1$, the Student density satisfies
$t_\nu(z)\le C/(1+z^2)$ for a finite constant $C$. Its ratio to the Cauchy
density is maximized at $|z|=1$ (or constant at $\nu=1$), and its
normalizing constants are bounded. Consequently,
$f(x)\le 2C\omega/(\omega^2+(x-\xi)^2)$ regardless of $\alpha$.
Near a distinct sample value of multiplicity $k$, the other residuals stay
away from zero and the product is $O(\omega^{n-2k})$. Away from all sample
values, every residual stays away from zero. Positive scales bounded away
from zero give bounded densities, while $\omega\to\infty$ makes them
vanish uniformly. These bounds also control escaping location. They prove
boundedness, but neither attainment nor reliability of the numerical search.

With $\omega>0$ held, $f(x)\le2t_\nu(0)/\omega$ and $t_\nu(0)$ is
bounded uniformly for $\nu>0$. Thus likelihood cannot diverge, even with
ties. With $\xi$ held away from all data, maximizing the symmetric Student
density over scale bounds each density by a finite constant times
$1/|x_i-\xi|$, uniformly over $\nu>0$; skewing multiplies this bound by
at most two. These are boundedness results, not attainment results.
All-held finite proper coefficients give finite positive likelihood.
Other held-set exemptions and general boundary attainment remain open.

## Limits and joint paths

**Proved, fixed xi, omega, nu:** alpha -> +infinity converges weakly to a
translated right half-t, density 2 t_nu(z)/omega for x>xi and zero for x<xi;
alpha -> -infinity gives its reflection. At x=xi every finite-alpha density
equals t_nu(0)/omega, whereas the closed half-t density convention takes twice
that value. Weak convergence does not prescribe a density at a point. At an
observation exactly at xi, comparing the endpoint density of the closed half-t model without
analyzing the joint xi/alpha approach can overstate the actual path limit.
The reflected limit reverses the sign of the location. Held parameters must be mapped accordingly.

**Proved, fixed xi, omega, alpha:** nu -> infinity gives the Azzalini
skew-normal density 2 phi(z) Phi(alpha z)/omega, and weak convergence follows
by Scheffe's lemma. This is the skew-normal face. With $\alpha=0$, the model is the location-scale Student distribution.
If nu -> infinity and alpha -> either signed infinity with fixed xi, omega,
the weak limit is the corresponding translated half-normal, again by pointwise
convergence away from xi and Scheffe. The order of the two limits does not
settle paths with moving xi, omega, or observations at the moving boundary.

**Proved, nonuniform endpoint layer:** for nu -> nu0>0, alpha -> +infinity,
z -> 0 with alpha z -> c, the skewing factor tends to
2 T_(nu0+1)(c sqrt((nu0+1)/nu0)), which ranges strictly between 0 and 2.
Thus endpoint pointwise values and moving-boundary likelihoods depend on the
joint rate. For free nu restricted to at least one and $2k_{max}<n$, the
[uniform closure proof](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-07-skew-theory-data/proofs.md)
exhausts the joint escaping coordinates after nuisance exclusion. It also
controls the joint normal/skew corner. Quantitative face-neighborhood
profilers and global searches are still missing from the implementation;
nu tending to zero lies outside the free-df fitting restriction.

## Information at symmetry

The information at symmetry differs from that of the skew-normal. **Proved:** at
alpha=0 and finite nu, the xi and alpha scores, apart from nonzero constants,
are z/(nu+z²) and z/sqrt(nu+z²), respectively. They are not proportional as
functions of z; both have finite second moments under t_nu, so their 2-by-2
information block is positive definite. At the normal limit they become
proportional and the skew-normal direct-parameter information is singular.
This does not assert full four-parameter DQM, rank or MLE asymptotics. See also
Ley and Paindaveine's [generalized skew-elliptical information study](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/57896).

## Numerical fitting and remaining questions

The fitter restricts free degrees of freedom to nu >= 1, detects divergent
scale collapse and stops at equality. It standardizes the sample, profiles
location and scale at a grid of finite shape and df starting points, and
jointly refines the free coordinates. A separate nu=1 search checks the
one-sided df score, including transformed iterates that round to the bound. Successful candidates must pass a numerical free-score
check; failed searches stop. It compares the skew-normal, half-t, reflected
half-t and half-normal faces. Half-t face profiling with a held endpoint
retains the same df restriction. Face comparisons use numerical tolerance;
these are multistart searches, not global certificates. Location-only, symmetric scale-only and one-sign alpha/scale subsets use the conditional certificates below; other subsets retain the free-coordinate search. No penalized likelihood is used.

## Decision history

### Candidate positive-integral CDF route (2026-11-01, round 2)

For a >= 0 and t >= 0, the lower thin tail at z=-t has the representation

$$
 F(-t;a,\nu)=\frac1\pi\int_0^{\arctan(1/a)}
 \left(1+\frac{t^2}{\nu\sin^2 r}\right)^{-\nu/2}\,dr,
$$

with the upper endpoint pi/2 when a=0. This follows by conditioning the
skew-normal Gaussian wedge on an independent chi-square scaling variable and
using its Laplace transform. The integrand is nonnegative, avoiding a
subtraction of nearly equal CDFs on the thin side. Symmetry of the skewing
factor gives `1-F(t) = 2 T_nu(-t)-F(-t)`, where the subtraction is well
conditioned because the last term is at most `T_nu(-t)` for a >= 0. For
negative a, reflect both the observation and tail. This identity does not by
itself provide a tail-accurate floating-point quadrature algorithm, stable
extreme quantiles, or an implementation error bound.

The accompanying `cdf_wedge.py` compares this integral with independent
integration of the skew-t density in SciPy on 300 seeded parameter points;
seed 20261101, t in [0.01,8], a in [0.05,12], and nu in [0.5,50], drawn
log-uniformly. With absolute quadrature tolerance 1e-100 and relative
tolerances 1e-12 and 1e-11 respectively, the largest observed relative
difference was 8.16e-14. This is a finite numerical comparison, not a test
of a C++ implementation or of extreme tails. A first run using an absolute
tolerance of 1e-13 failed its 1e-7 relative threshold on a probability of
6.5e-14; the tolerance was too coarse to resolve relative error there.

For a numerical fitter, write `h = alpha*z*sqrt((nu+1)/(nu+z*z))`
and `R = t_(nu+1)(h)/T_(nu+1)(h)`. Differentiating the log density at
finite parameters gives

$$
g_z=-\frac{(\nu+1)z}{\nu+z^2}
 +R\frac{\alpha\nu\sqrt{\nu+1}}{(\nu+z^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
s_\xi=-g_z/\omega,\quad s_{\log\omega}=-1-zg_z,\quad
s_\alpha=Rz\sqrt{\frac{\nu+1}{\nu+z^2}}.
$$

These are pointwise algebraic derivatives, not a validated implementation;
the df score also differentiates the Student CDF with respect to its df.
A numerical score check must account for all free coordinates and for the
one-sided condition at the df=1 fitting restriction.

2026-11-01 (new-family numerical evidence): the `st` identifier was unused at revision d8a62683.

The family-local `spec.py` supplies the Student CDF to the quality harness.
Its df derivatives use multiprecision differentiation for mpmath and centered
finite differences for scipy; score and Hessian checks therefore also depend on
that numerical approximation and require their own validation.

At large df, the Student log-density normalizer uses the gamma-ratio expansion
through the inverse ninth power at nu >= 128; below that it uses the
no-throw backend gamma ratio. This avoids cancellation near the skew-normal
face and shares its normalizer with `ht` and `lst`; the score check and face
comparison remain numerical.
The new-family golden coefficient tolerance is 1e-5 because the df score is
finite-differenced and binary64 WebAssembly and native fits showed a small
coefficient drift. Log likelihood retains the tighter 1e-10 tolerance.

On the seed-7 scale-equivariance workload the df estimate is about 1273 and
the rescaled fits differed by up to 0.19 (relative 1.5e-4), while the first
three coefficients and per-observation log likelihood meet their tighter

2e-5 for its other coefficients. This is measured numerical sensitivity near
the normal face, not exact coefficient equivariance.

## Conditional skew certificate

When location, scale and df are held, `certificate.hpp` certifies the
alpha-only fit. Put $m=\nu+1$,
$q_i=z_i\sqrt{m/(\nu+z_i^2)}$ and $R_m=t_m/T_m$.
If every nonzero $q_i$ has one sign, the likelihood is strictly monotone;
the fitter stops at the corresponding half-t face. Each observation at the
held endpoint contributes its symmetric t density, so the face likelihood
is the closed half-t value divided by $2^k$. If all residuals vanish, alpha
is unidentifiable and a tie stop supplies two different finite maximizers.

With both signs present, all stationary points lie in $[-A_-,A_+]$.
For $n_-$ negative residuals, set
$C_+=2t_m(0)m^{(m+1)/2}\sum_{q_i>0}q_i^{-m}$ and
$A_+=\max\{\sqrt m/\min|q_i|,(4C_+/(mn_-))^{1/m}\}$;
reflection gives $A_-$. These are computed in logarithms.
The Student tail inequality $R_m(-a)\ge ma/(m+a^2)$ and
$R_m(a)\le2t_m(a)$ prove inward score signs on the entire exterior
half-lines. This does not assume that $\log T_m$ is concave.

On each interior interval the pdf's unimodality and the CDF's monotonicity
give lower and upper bounds for each $q_iR_m(\alpha q_i)$.
Exhaustive subdivision discards only intervals with a strictly signed score
range, and compares all remaining stationary enclosures using midpoint
likelihoods plus integrated score bounds. The coordinate width, grouping,
128-epsilon term-sum allowance and certificate work budget are those of the
sstd scalar search helper. No Student-CDF root-count assumption is made;
multiple or poorly separated stationary components can stop on tie or budget.
The weighted pdf/CDF ratios are formed in log space, with the working log
magnitudes included in the 128-epsilon allowance before exponentiation;
large thin-tail logs must not silently lose their difference. Nonfinite
ratio bounds stop numerically. At huge objective magnitudes, a lost
face gain retains the numerical tie stop and its finite/face witnesses.

The certificate is conditional on the computed ranges and comparisons in
exact arithmetic, with the stated practical binary64 allowance; it does not
claim outward rounding. Held df may be any positive value. The additional
conditional routes and symmetric Student reduction are described below. See the
[proof of the skew bracket](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-07-skew-theory-data/proofs.md)
and [implementation record](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-07-skew-impl-report.md).


## Conditional location and scale certificates

With omega, alpha and nu held, location fitting encloses every stationary
component on a compact interval. Write m=nu+1 and c=t_m(0). For alpha>0,
the log-density derivative g_z is positive on z<0. On z>0, the CDF ratio
is at most 2c, so g_z<0 once
z sqrt(nu+z²)>2c alpha nu/sqrt(m). Thus every location maximum lies between
min(x)-omega R and max(x), with R²=4c alpha nu/sqrt(m). Reflection gives
the interval for negative alpha; alpha=0 uses [min(x),max(x)]. These bounds
exclude whole exterior half-lines, not just selected probes. Rational-score
extrema at z=±sqrt(nu), Student pdf unimodality and CDF monotonicity give
interval score bounds. Subdivision retains connected stationary-component hulls to public location
width 4e-11 (1+abs(xi)); wider components stop. Point likelihoods plus integrated
score remainders supply the comparison bounds. No log-CDF concavity or uniqueness is assumed.
For x=(-2,2), omega=1, alpha=0 and nu=1, the two global location maxima
are exactly ±sqrt(3); the fitter stops with both witnesses.

With xi and nu held and alpha exactly zero, the log-scale score is
-n+(nu+1) sum d_i²/(nu omega²+d_i²), d_i=x_i-xi.
It is strictly decreasing whenever at least one residual is nonzero.
Its limits are nu(n-k)-k at zero scale and -n at infinite scale, where
k counts zero residuals. A positive first limit gives a unique finite
scale, bracketed with sign margins and bisected to log-scale width 2e-11.
A negative first limit proves unboundedness; equality gives a strictly
decreasing likelihood in scale and an unattained finite collapse supremum.
The equality stop reports likelihood bounds, rather than a zero-scale fit.

When alpha and omega are free, xi and nu are held, and all nonzero residuals
have the same sign, strict monotonicity in alpha reduces the global supremum
to that same symmetric scale problem plus (n-k) log(2). The finite-scale
supremum is a half-t limit stop; equality is an unattained collapse stop.
The held-endpoint observations retain factor one, including on reflection.
Mixed-sign alpha/scale fitting does not use this reduction.

For constant data and held omega,nu, freeing xi and alpha gives the sharp
upper bound n log(2 t_nu(0)/omega). No finite coefficients attain it:
at z=0 the skew factor is one, and at nonzero z the symmetric density is
strictly smaller than its mode. Approaching the datum from either side
while alpha z tends to positive infinity reaches the bound. The two
half-t faces therefore tie; both witnesses and finite global bounds are
reported. This moving-location endpoint factor is two per datum, whereas
the held-location factor above is one. If nu is also free, the sharp bound
is n log(2 phi(0)/omega), and the two half-normal faces tie. Indeed,
t_nu(0)=phi(0) E sqrt(V/nu) for V chi-squared with nu degrees of freedom;
strict Jensen gives t_nu(0)<phi(0) at every finite nu, with equality only
in the normal limit. The bound is approached jointly by nu tending to
infinity, xi approaching the datum from either side and alpha z tending
to positive infinity.

When alpha is held exactly at zero and xi,omega,nu are all free, the fitter
delegates to `lst_mle_certified` with the same nu>=1 restriction. Student
candidate models, budget/tie witnesses and collapse bounds are preserved;
the normal face is skew-normal with alpha=0, and the df=1 restriction is
retained. This does not certify arbitrary held Student
subsets or license reducing a small estimated alpha to zero.

General scale-only fitting at nonzero held alpha, mixed-sign alpha/scale,
nonconstant moving-location joint fits and other free-nu fits remain uncertified.


For mixed-sign residuals with xi,nu held and e=nu(n-k)-k>0, attainment
can be proved without a unimodality assumption: uniform density bounds
exclude both scale tails relative to a finite incumbent, and the conditional
alpha tail bounds can be made uniform on the remaining scale interval.
This gives a compact rectangle containing every maximizer. The
[conditional proofs](../../../../../dev/research/evaluations/2026-11-08-st-cert-data/proofs.md)

Held-subset refinement uses a coordinate system centred and scaled at the candidate. This prevents data spread many orders of magnitude below a held location from erasing the scale information. Held degrees of freedom are differentiated as constants, including values below one.

When only df is free, derivative convergence in the Student-CDF fractions
follows the packed df axis. Near the normal face its practical arithmetic
floor scales with df; the final information-weighted score check still
controls the public df error.
